**Qualified answer: yes, based on the available direct study.** In a cross-sectional cohort of 50 people with type 2 diabetes, OPG was higher among those with abnormal coronary artery calcification (CAC) and was associated with abnormal CAC in regression analysis (OR 3.324, 95% CI 1.321–8.359; P=.011). Osteocalcin and undercarboxylated osteocalcin were not significantly associated with CAC. CAC was measured by multidetector CT, with scores ≥10 Agatston units considered abnormal; 64% of participants met that threshold.

This supports OPG as the more promising **serum marker of CAC association in that study**, not as a proven superior clinical test: the sample was small and cross-sectional, and the abstract does not establish comparative diagnostic accuracy or prospective predictive value. **Source:** PMID 25100392, *Osteoprotegerin is a Better Serum Biomarker of Coronary Artery Calcification than Osteocalcin in Type 2 Diabetes.*
