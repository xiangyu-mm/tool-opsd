This child has subacute symptoms, is relatively well appearing, and has **multiple metaphyseal osteolytic lesions**. MRI findings of marrow edema and periosteal elevation are compatible with osteomyelitis but are not specific. The differential includes:

- Chronic nonbacterial osteomyelitis/chronic recurrent multifocal osteomyelitis
- Acute bacterial osteomyelitis
- Ewing sarcoma or another malignancy

Because treatment with antibiotics or anti-inflammatory agents could obscure the diagnosis, tissue should be obtained first. A bone biopsy allows histopathologic evaluation and cultures, helping distinguish infection from malignancy and sterile inflammatory disease.

CT-guided aspiration is more useful when a drainable abscess or a clearly accessible focal collection is present; here, the concern is the nature of the multifocal lytic bone lesions themselves.
