Yes. The study **“RNA-seq analysis of the rat placentation site reveals maternal obesity-associated changes in placental and offspring thyroid hormone signaling”** (PMID: 25449029; DOI: 10.1016/j.placenta.2014.09.015) reported maternal-obesity-associated thyroid-signaling changes.

In the rat model, the researchers found downregulation of multiple thyroid-hormone signaling components in the placental junctional zone and fetal liver, and persistent changes in thyroid-related gene expression in offspring. Placental **Trh, Dio2, Trα, and Ucp2** expression was negatively associated with maternal leptin. The study also reported reduced **TRH, TRβ, and UCP1** expression in term placenta from women with obesity.

**Qualification:** The offspring evidence concerns thyroid-related gene expression; it does not, by itself, establish altered thyroid hormone function or prove that these changes cause later obesity.

FINAL DECISION: YES
