Evidence supports an unfavorable prognostic role for high MSI2 expression in adult B-ALL:

- In 116 adults with B-ALL, high MSI2 expression was associated with inferior overall survival (P=0.004), event-free survival (P=0.001), and relapse-free survival (P=0.018), particularly among BCR-ABL-negative patients. MSI2 remained an independent adverse prognostic factor (PMID:23759245; search evidence: `call_oE7V53iDCSXQGy0JtejKxNgX`).
- In a separate cohort of 140 adults, high MSI2 was associated with lower complete-remission rates (P=0.03), inferior overall survival (P=0.018), and shorter disease-free survival (P=0.001), and was independently prognostic in adult BCR-ABL1-negative B-ALL (PMID:25090928; search evidence: `call_jmr8M0yXDetulog5Qo4tQJ1J`).
- Broader meta-analysis also supports an adverse prognostic direction for MSI2, although it was not specific to adult B-ALL (PMID:36530989).

Thus, the adult B-ALL evidence is consistent across two independent cohorts, though the data are observational and largely focused on BCR-ABL1-negative disease.
