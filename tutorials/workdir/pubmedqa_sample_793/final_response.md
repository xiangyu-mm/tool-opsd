**Qualified answer: yes.** In rats with biliary, CCl₄, or DMNA cirrhosis and portal-vein ligation, splenorenal shunt blood flow measured by transit-time ultrasound was substantially elevated in portal hypertension and correlated with microsphere estimates of mesenteric portosystemic shunting (**r = 0.61, p < 0.01**) and splenic shunting (**r = 0.54, p < 0.05**). Octreotide also reduced measured shunt flow (**−23 ± 20%**) versus placebo (**−6 ± 8%**).

However, the measure was **not uniformly interchangeable with microsphere-estimated shunt fractions**: with vapreotide, splenorenal flow decreased while microsphere estimates did not, and the changes were uncorrelated (**r = −0.1**). Thus, it is a useful index across the tested models and can detect some pharmacological changes, but results need care when interpreting drug-specific changes as changes in overall collateral burden.

Source: *Spleno-renal shunt blood flow is an accurate index of collateral circulation in different models of portal hypertension and after pharmacological changes in rats.* *Journal of Hepatology* (2000), PMID: 10735613.
