Yes. Available evidence indicates that variation in the **PCA3 long noncoding RNA promoter**, particularly the **(TAAA)n short tandem repeat**, is associated with prostate-cancer risk in Chinese males.

Key findings:

- **PMID:21655300 (2011)** studied 186 prostate-cancer cases and 135 healthy Chinese controls.
  - Compared with ≤10 total TAAA repeats:
    - 11 repeats: **OR 1.76**, 95% CI 1.07–2.89
    - ≥12 repeats: **OR 5.28**, 95% CI 1.76–15.89
- **PMID:25975686 (2015)** reported similar results in 186 prostate-cancer patients, 141 benign-prostatic-hyperplasia patients, and 135 healthy controls:
  - 11 repeats: **OR 1.74**, 95% CI 1.06–2.87
  - ≥12 repeats: **OR 5.63**, 95% CI 1.85–17.19
- **PMID:25445501 (2014)** also reported that longer PCA3 promoter repeats were associated with increased risk among Chinese males, although the accessible abstract did not provide numerical effect estimates.

Thus, longer PCA3 promoter TAAA repeat alleles were repeatedly associated with greater prostate-cancer risk in these studies. However, the evidence has important limitations: the studies appear related, some samples may overlap, and there is no clearly documented independent replication with complete statistics. The reported promoter AG/CA change is also insufficiently characterized because control frequencies and effect estimates were not provided.

Therefore, the PCA3 promoter region appears related to prostate-cancer risk in Chinese males, but the association should be considered preliminary rather than definitively established.

FINAL DECISION: YES
