Yes. In hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, high myocardial T2-weighted signal is associated with elevated high-sensitivity troponin T:

- In 101 patients, elevated hs-cTnT occurred in **54%** with high T2 signal versus **14%** without it (**p<0.001**); T2 extent correlated with hs-cTnT (**Spearman ρ=0.42, p<0.001**) [source: `call_9WmtuOZCH1KxtoQ5jKvKOhro`].
- An independent study of 44 patients found that increasing high-T2 segment burden was associated with higher hs-cTnT (**trend p=0.002**), and the percentage of high-T2 myocardium correlated with hs-cTnT (**r=0.388, p=0.009**) [source: `call_3QS2hOwuK8g4MvVHguLE8yK4`].
- Exercise-provocation data also support the relationship, though less directly: high T2 signal predicted a post-exercise hs-cTnT rise (**adjusted OR 7.9**) [source: `call_0FrURW06ARpkwDuKJFZ5Hcy2`].

These studies demonstrate an association, not necessarily causation, and do not imply that T2 signal alone is diagnostic of elevated troponin T.

FINAL DECISION: YES
