**Yes.** Pattison et al. reported that KLF5 binds a novel enhancer in the first intron of **CCNE1**. The enhancer contains **rs200996365**, a variant in high linkage disequilibrium with the bladder-cancer GWAS risk variant **rs8102137**; the study found that the polymorphism alters KLF5 binding. In heterozygous bladder cancer cells, CCNE1 transcription was **1.5-fold higher from the risk allele**. Luciferase assays and CRISPR-Cas9 editing further supported a functional role for the regulatory element.

This supports the proposed mechanism linking KLF5 binding and CCNE1 regulation to bladder-cancer susceptibility. It is mechanistic evidence from a study, not by itself definitive proof that this mechanism causes increased risk in every population.

**Source:** Pattison JM, Posternak V, Cole MD. *Mol Cancer Res.* 2016;14(11):1078–1086. PMID: 27514407. doi:10.1158/1541-7786.MCR-16-0123.
