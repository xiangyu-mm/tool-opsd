## Findings

Yes, **selective AURKA inhibitors enhance BRAF/MEK-pathway inhibitor activity in several preclinical melanoma models**:

- **MLN8054 + dabrafenib** or **MLN8054 + trametinib** produced greater antiproliferative effects than either agent alone in BRAFV600E A375 melanoma cells. The triple combination was strongest in a 3D human melanoma skin model, although residual tumor cells remained (PMID: 25074438).
- **Alisertib/MLN8237 + trametinib** showed strong antitumor activity in melanoma patient-derived xenografts, including BRAF-mutant and targeted-therapy-resistant models (PMID: 37003468).
- AURKA/MAPK combinations also overcame compensatory resistance programs in BRAF- and NRAS-mutant melanoma models, increasing antiproliferative and apoptotic effects (PMID: 26962685).

Important limitations:

- The studies generally demonstrate enhanced growth inhibition or tumor control, but **formal pharmacologic synergy scores were not consistently reported**.
- Evidence is predominantly **preclinical**.
- No clinical evidence currently establishes that adding an AURKA inhibitor to a BRAF/MEK regimen improves patient outcomes.
- Evidence specifically concerns selective AURKA inhibitors such as **alisertib/MLN8237 and MLN8054**, not necessarily pan-Aurora inhibitors.

Thus, AURKA inhibition is a promising strategy for enhancing BRAF/MEK inhibition and potentially addressing resistance, but clinical efficacy remains unproven.
