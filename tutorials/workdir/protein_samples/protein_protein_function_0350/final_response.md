## Functional identification

The sequence is **FliF, the bacterial flagellar M-ring/MS-ring protein**. Identification is highly confident because it is an **exact 560-residue sequence match** to reviewed *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium FliF (UniProt **P15928**), with a close *E. coli* homolog showing 86.3% identity.

### Functional role

FliF is a structural component of the **flagellar basal body**, the membrane-embedded foundation of the bacterial flagellum. It oligomerizes to form the **MS ring**—the membrane-associated ring connected to the flagellar rod and adjacent basal-body structures.

Its roles include:

- Assembly and stabilization of the flagellar basal body
- Providing a platform for attachment of the flagellar rod and other basal-body components
- Supporting construction of the flagellar motor
- Possible participation in transmission or coupling of energy used for flagellar rotation

FliF is therefore **not expected to be a transporter, receptor, or metabolic enzyme**. Its primary function is architectural and motor-associated.

### Sequence/domain evidence

The sequence contains characteristic FliF/YscJ-related regions:

- **FliF_N domain:** residues 51–105
- **YscJ_FliF domain:** residues 114–221
- **YscJ_FliF_C domain:** residues 253–437

It also contains strongly hydrophobic membrane-associated segments near the N terminus and in the later part of the sequence, consistent with insertion into the bacterial inner membrane. The intervening regions are largely soluble and contain conserved structural domains rather than recognizable catalytic motifs.

### Cellular processes

FliF is involved primarily in:

- **Bacterial flagellum assembly**
- **Flagellar motor organization**
- **Flagellum-dependent swimming or surface motility**
- Indirectly, motility behaviors such as chemotactic movement, through construction of the flagellar apparatus

The protein is part of a larger basal body containing the M/MS, S, P, and L rings and the central rod.

### Subcellular localization

FliF localizes to the **bacterial plasma/inner membrane**, where it forms part of the **flagellar basal body**. It is not a freely soluble cytoplasmic protein and is not a secreted periplasmic protein.

### Summary

**FliF is an inner-membrane flagellar basal-body protein that forms the MS/M ring and serves as a structural and motor-associated component of the bacterial flagellum. Its activity enables assembly of the flagellar apparatus and supports flagellum-dependent cell motility.**
