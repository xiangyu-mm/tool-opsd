### Functional identification

The 480-aa protein is most consistent with **bifunctional NAD(P)H-hydrate repair enzyme Nnr** (also called an **NAD(P)HX epimerase/dehydratase**).

This assignment is supported by:

- ~40–41% identity to reviewed Nnr proteins over ~97–98% of the sequence.
- N-terminal **YjeF_N** domain, associated with NAD(P)HX epimerization.
- C-terminal **Carb_kinase-like/YjeF C-terminal** domain, associated with ADP-dependent NAD(P)HX dehydration.
- Matching lengths and conserved architecture of archaeal Nnr proteins.
- Reported potassium-binding activity in close homologs.

### Catalytic activities

Nnr repairs NADH and NADPH that have undergone hydration at the nicotinamide ring, producing damaged cofactors called **NADHX** and **NADPHX**.

It catalyzes two related activities:

1. **Epimerization**

\[
(6R)\text{-NADHX} \rightleftharpoons (6S)\text{-NADHX}
\]

\[
(6R)\text{-NADPHX} \rightleftharpoons (6S)\text{-NADPHX}
\]

2. **ADP-dependent dehydration and cofactor regeneration**

For NADH:

\[
(6S)\text{-NADHX} + \text{ADP}
\rightarrow
\text{NADH} + \text{AMP} + \text{phosphate} + \text{H}^{+}
\]

For NADPH:

\[
(6S)\text{-NADPHX} + \text{ADP}
\rightarrow
\text{NADPH} + \text{AMP} + \text{phosphate} + \text{H}^{+}
\]

Thus, the principal chemical role is **metabolite repair**: removal of the hydration-derived damage from NADH/NADPH and restoration of functional NADH or NADPH. The dehydration step consumes ADP and produces AMP and inorganic phosphate.

### Annotation

- **EC 5.1.99.6:** NAD(P)HX epimerase  
- **EC 4.2.1.136:** ADP-dependent NAD(P)H-hydrate dehydratase  
- Likely cofactor/metal ion: **K⁺**, based on close reviewed homologs.

### Confidence and limitation

The identification is **strongly supported by sequence homology and conserved domain architecture**, but the precise catalytic activity has not been experimentally demonstrated for this exact sequence. The enzyme is therefore best reported as a **putative bifunctional Nnr NAD(P)H-hydrate repair enzyme**.
