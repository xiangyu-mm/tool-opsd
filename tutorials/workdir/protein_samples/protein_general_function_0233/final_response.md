### Protein assessment

**Identity:** The 539-aa sequence is an exact sequence match to *Drosophila melanogaster* **mushroom body miniature (MBM)**, UniProt **Q9VPM5**. This makes the functional annotation substantially stronger than a prediction based only on sequence similarity.

#### Primary characteristics

- **Length:** 539 amino acids  
- **Estimated molecular mass:** ~58.6 kDa  
- **Theoretical pI:** 8.76; moderately basic overall  
- **Net charge at pH 7:** approximately +7.5  
- **Hydropathy:** strongly hydrophilic; no evidence for transmembrane segments  
- **Composition:** extensive low-complexity and intrinsically disordered regions, including glycine/arginine-rich, lysine-rich, serine-rich, and acidic stretches  
- **Stability estimate:** high instability index (~71), consistent with a regulatory/disordered protein rather than a compact globular enzyme  
- **Recognizable motifs:** two annotated **CCHC-type zinc-finger regions** around residues **354–367** and **371–386**  
- **Pfam scan:** no domain result was returned; this does not exclude the presence of short or noncanonical domains

#### Likely function

MBM is a **nucleic-acid-associated nuclear/nucleolar protein** required for **small ribosomal-subunit biogenesis in neuroblasts**. It also contributes to **mushroom body development** in *Drosophila*.

Its basic and glycine/arginine-rich regions, together with the paired CCHC zinc-finger motifs, support a role in **RNA or ribosome-associated molecular interactions**. The extensive disordered regions likely mediate regulated protein–protein or protein–RNA interactions and may be subject to phosphorylation and other post-translational modifications.

#### Cellular localization

Reported localization includes:

- **Nucleus**
- **Nucleolus**
- **Cytoplasm**

The protein is nuclear during interphase and redistributes to the cytoplasm during mitosis when the nuclear envelope breaks down.

### Functional summary

This is a **basic, highly disordered, zinc-binding nucleic-acid-associated regulatory protein**, specifically *Drosophila* MBM. Its established role is in **small ribosomal-subunit biogenesis**, with additional developmental functions in the mushroom body. It is not predicted to be a membrane protein or metabolic enzyme.
