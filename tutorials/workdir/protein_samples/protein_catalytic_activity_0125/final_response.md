## Functional identification

The 123-residue protein is **holo-[acyl-carrier-protein] synthase (ACPS)**, also called **4′-phosphopantetheinyl transferase**.

This assignment is highly reliable because the sequence is an **exact match** to the reviewed UniProt protein **B2GJ30** and contains the characteristic **ACPS domain (Pfam PF01648)**.

- **EC:** 2.7.8.7  
- **Size:** 13.6 kDa  
- **Likely location:** Cytoplasm  
- **Cofactor:** Mg²⁺  
- **Biological role:** Activation of ACPs for fatty-acid and related acyl-chain biosynthesis  

## Catalytic activity

ACPS transfers the **4′-phosphopantetheine group** from coenzyme A to a conserved serine residue on apo-acyl carrier protein (apo-ACP). This converts inactive apo-ACP into holo-ACP, whose covalently attached phosphopantetheine arm carries growing acyl intermediates.

### Overall reaction

\[
\text{apo-ACP} + \text{CoA}
\rightarrow
\text{holo-ACP} + \text{adenosine 3′,5′-bisphosphate} + \text{H}^+
\]

More explicitly, the enzyme forms a phosphodiester linkage between the **4′-phosphate of phosphopantetheine** and the hydroxyl group of an ACP serine:

\[
\text{ACP-Ser-OH} + \text{CoA}
\rightarrow
\text{ACP-Ser-O-PO}_2\text{-pantetheine} + \text{3′,5′-ADP}
\]

## Summary

This is a small bacterial **holo-ACP synthase**, not an enzyme that directly elongates fatty acids. Its function is to **post-translationally install the phosphopantetheinyl prosthetic group on ACP**, enabling ACP to shuttle acyl intermediates during fatty-acid biosynthesis. The Mg²⁺ annotation and cytoplasmic localization are consistent with the curated reference annotation.
