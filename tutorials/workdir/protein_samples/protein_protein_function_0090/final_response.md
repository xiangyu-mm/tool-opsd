### Identification

The sequence is **cysteine- and glycine-rich protein 2 (CSRP2)**, also called **CRP2**. It is a small vertebrate LIM-domain protein.

The identification is highly reliable: the sequence is an **exact full-length match** to reviewed human CSRP2, UniProt **Q16527**, with 100% sequence identity and coverage. It also matches orthologous bovine and nearly identical mouse proteins.

### Sequence and domain architecture

- **Length:** 193 amino acids  
- **Predicted molecular mass:** approximately 20.95 kDa  
- **Theoretical pI:** approximately 8.95  
- **Overall character:** basic, cysteine-rich, glycine-rich, and hydrophilic  
- **Domains:** two LIM domains:
  - LIM domain 1: approximately residues **10–65**
  - LIM domain 2: approximately residues **119–175**

The repeated Cys/His-rich motifs are characteristic of **LIM domains**. These are compact zinc-coordinating protein-interaction modules. They are not, by themselves, evidence that this protein is a classical zinc-finger transcription factor. Instead, LIM domains commonly mediate interactions with cytoskeletal, nuclear, and signaling proteins.

The short central region between the LIM domains is relatively glycine-rich and likely provides a flexible linker.

### Function

CSRP2 is best described as a **LIM-domain-containing molecular adaptor or scaffold** involved in linking signaling, cytoskeletal, and muscle-associated protein complexes. It is not known primarily as an enzyme, membrane receptor, or conventional sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factor.

Reported functional associations include:

- **Actinin binding**
- Association with muscle structural complexes
- Participation in **sarcomere organization**
- Regulation or support of **smooth-muscle and vascular development**
- Possible involvement in cell differentiation and phenotypic remodeling

The protein’s two LIM domains likely allow it to bind multiple partner proteins and organize multiprotein complexes rather than catalyze a biochemical reaction.

### Subcellular localization

CSRP2 has been annotated in several cellular compartments:

- **Nucleus**
- **Cytoplasm**
- **Focal adhesions**
- **Z discs of muscle sarcomeres**

Its presence in both nuclear and cytoplasmic/cytoskeletal locations is consistent with a signaling and structural adaptor that may shuttle between compartments or function in multiple complexes depending on cell type and state.

### Biological processes

The strongest associated biological processes are:

1. **Muscle tissue development**
2. **Sarcomere organization**
3. **Cell differentiation**
4. **Embryonic vascular-system development**
5. Regulation of smooth-muscle phenotype

CSRP2 expression is reported to decrease strongly after **PDGF-BB stimulation or cell injury**. Those conditions promote smooth-muscle proliferation and dedifferentiation, suggesting that CSRP2 may be associated with maintenance of a differentiated, contractile smooth-muscle state.

### Protein interactions

Reported interaction partners include:

- **KAT14** — the first LIM domain is reported to be sufficient for this interaction.
- **GLRX3** — another reported binding partner.
- **Actinin-associated complexes** — consistent with localization at focal adhesions and muscle Z discs.

### Overall functional summary

**CSRP2/CRP2 is a small, basic, two-LIM-domain protein that functions primarily as a protein-interaction scaffold. It localizes to the nucleus, cytoplasm, focal adhesions, and muscle Z discs, where it participates in cytoskeletal and muscle-associated complexes. It is implicated in muscle differentiation, sarcomere organization, embryonic vascular development, and maintenance of smooth-muscle identity. Its cysteine-rich motifs form LIM domains that coordinate zinc and mediate protein binding; the sequence does not indicate a classical enzyme or canonical DNA-binding transcription factor.**

The functional assignment is supported by an exact reviewed UniProt sequence match and Pfam identification of two LIM domains. The precise molecular mechanism, oligomeric state, and any direct transcriptional activity are not established from the sequence alone.
