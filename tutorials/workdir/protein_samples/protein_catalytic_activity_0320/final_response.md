## Functional assignment

The 550-residue protein is most consistent with **CTP synthase** (**EC 6.3.4.2**), a bifunctional enzyme in de novo pyrimidine biosynthesis.

### Evidence

- Strong similarity to reviewed CTP synthases:
  - ~50–53% amino-acid identity
  - ~97% sequence coverage
  - E-values approximately \(10^{-184}\) to \(10^{-185}\)
- Expected two-domain architecture:
  - **CTP_synth_N domain**: residues ~9–270
  - **Glutamine amidase/GATase domain**: residues ~311–541
- Length and composition are typical of bacterial/archaeal CTP synthases.
- Homologs are annotated as homotetramers, although oligomerization of this specific sequence is inferred rather than experimentally demonstrated.

## Catalytic activity

CTP synthase converts **UTP to CTP**, using nitrogen supplied either by **L-glutamine** or, in the ammonia-dependent mode, directly by **ammonium**. The enzyme couples two activities:

1. The C-terminal glutaminase domain hydrolyzes glutamine to generate ammonia.
2. The N-terminal synthetase domain uses ATP to activate UTP and incorporates the ammonia-derived nitrogen into the uracil ring.

### Main glutamine-dependent reaction

\[
\mathrm{UTP + L\text{-}glutamine + ATP + H_2O}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{CTP + L\text{-}glutamate + ADP + P_i}
\]

A corresponding ammonia-dependent reaction is:

\[
\mathrm{UTP + NH_4^+ + ATP}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{CTP + ADP + P_i}
\]

The newly introduced nitrogen is the exocyclic amino group of cytosine. Thus, the enzyme changes the uracil base of UTP into the cytosine base of CTP.

## Biological role

The protein likely catalyzes the final step of **de novo CTP biosynthesis**, supplying CTP for RNA synthesis, phospholipid metabolism, and other nucleotide-dependent processes.

The glutamine-dependent activity and reaction assignment are strongly supported by homologous reviewed proteins. Exact substrate preference, regulation, and oligomeric state for this particular sequence remain sequence-based inferences rather than direct experimental findings.
