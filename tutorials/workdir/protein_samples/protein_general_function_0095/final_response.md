## Functional assessment

**Likely identity:** **FtsH-like ATP-dependent zinc metalloprotease**  
**Confidence:** High, based on domain architecture and strong homology.

### Primary characteristics

- **Length:** 655 aa  
- **Predicted molecular mass:** ~70.1 kDa  
- **Theoretical pI:** 6.03  
- **Overall topology:** Membrane-anchored protein with two N-terminal transmembrane helices, approximately:
  - TM1: residues ~29–56
  - TM2: residues ~129–165
- The large C-terminal region is predicted to face the cytoplasm.

The N-terminus is therefore unlikely to be a cleavable secretion signal peptide. Instead, it forms the membrane anchor characteristic of FtsH proteins.

### Conserved domains and motifs

- **FtsH extracellular/periplasmic domain:** residues ~29–128
- **AAA+ ATPase domain:** residues ~224–358
- **AAA lid region:** residues ~381–424
- **Peptidase M41 metalloprotease domain:** residues ~440–621
- Walker A-like ATP-binding motif: **IGAPGTGKT**
- Conserved metalloprotease motif: **HESGH**, consistent with the HExxH zinc-binding motif of FtsH proteases

### Functional interpretation

This protein is most likely a **membrane-bound, ATP-dependent protease involved in protein quality control**. The AAA+ domain uses ATP hydrolysis to unfold and translocate protein substrates, while the C-terminal M41 metalloprotease domain performs zinc-dependent proteolysis.

FtsH homologs commonly degrade misfolded or damaged membrane proteins and selected cytoplasmic proteins. The protein is likely to assemble as a **hexameric protease complex**, as observed for characterized FtsH proteins.

### Supporting evidence

The best reviewed homolog showed:

- **58.9% sequence identity**
- **91.1% query coverage**
- **E-value:** 4.35 × 10⁻²²⁴

Additional homologs gave comparable results, and Pfam scanning independently identified the characteristic **FtsH, AAA+, and Peptidase M41** domains.

### Summary

The sequence encodes a **two-pass membrane-anchored FtsH AAA+ zinc metalloprotease**, probably functioning on the cytoplasmic side of the membrane in ATP-dependent degradation and quality control of integral membrane and soluble proteins. Exact substrate specificity, oligomerization, and physiological pathway cannot be determined from sequence alone.
