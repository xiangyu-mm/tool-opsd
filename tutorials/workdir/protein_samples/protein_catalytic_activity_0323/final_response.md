## Functional assignment

The 82-amino-acid sequence is **DNA-directed RNA polymerase subunit omega** ( bacterial **rpoZ**-type protein), most likely from a cyanobacterium.

Evidence supporting this assignment:

- Exact sequence match to curated UniProt protein Q3AZJ0.
- 100% sequence identity and full-length coverage.
- Significant Pfam match to the **RNA_pol_Rpb6 domain** (PF01192), spanning residues 25–74.
- Strong similarity to several additional curated RNA polymerase omega-subunit proteins.

## Biological role

The omega subunit is a small, noncatalytic component of the bacterial RNA polymerase core. It helps assemble and stabilize the polymerase by interacting with the **β′ subunit** and facilitating its association with the **β and α subunits**.

Thus, this sequence is **not an independent metabolic enzyme** and should not be interpreted as directly catalyzing an isolated reaction on its own. Its role is structural and assembly-related within the multisubunit RNA polymerase complex.

## Catalytic reaction of the associated RNA polymerase

The assembled DNA-dependent RNA polymerase catalyzes RNA-chain elongation:

\[
\mathrm{RNA}_{n} + \mathrm{NTP}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{RNA}_{n+1} + \mathrm{PP_i}
\]

where NTP is ATP, GTP, CTP, or UTP, and the incoming ribonucleotide is selected according to the DNA template.

More explicitly:

\[
\mathrm{DNA\ template + NTPs}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{RNA\ transcript + pyrophosphate}
\]

This activity is associated with **EC 2.7.7.6** and Rhea reaction **RHEA:21248**, but the catalytic center resides primarily in the β and β′ subunits—not in omega.
