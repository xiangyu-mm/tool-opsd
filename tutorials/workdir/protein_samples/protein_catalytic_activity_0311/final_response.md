## Catalytic reaction

The direct chemical reaction is ATP hydrolysis:

\[
\mathrm{ATP + H_2O \rightarrow ADP + P_i + H^+}
\]

This reaction is mapped to **Rhea RHEA:13065** and supplies the energy for the helicase’s conformational cycle. DDX6 couples ATP binding and hydrolysis to **RNA binding, remodeling, and local unwinding**, rather than catalyzing a covalent modification of RNA.

A more functional description is:

\[
\text{ATP hydrolysis} \longrightarrow \text{RNA–protein conformational remodeling}
\]

The exact RNA substrate specificity and unwinding activity cannot be determined from sequence alone, although the DEAD-box architecture strongly supports this mechanism.

## Biological role

DDX6 is primarily associated with:

- Cytoplasmic **P-bodies**
- mRNA storage and degradation
- Translational repression
- mRNA decapping complexes
- Stress granules and cytoplasmic ribonucleoprotein granules
- Regulation of RNA fate and gene expression

It interacts with factors including DCP1/DCP2, EDC proteins, LSM14 proteins, PATL1, and EIF4ENIF1/4E-T.

## Cofactor note

DEAD-box helicases generally require a divalent metal ion, commonly **Mg²⁺**, to coordinate ATP hydrolysis. The supplied annotation directly establishes ATPase activity but does not independently specify a metal cofactor for this particular sequence.

### Bottom line

This protein is a **DDX6-like DEAD-box RNA helicase**. Its catalytic activity is **Mg²⁺-assisted ATP hydrolysis**, and the resulting energy is used to remodel RNA-containing complexes and regulate mRNA storage, translation, decapping, and degradation.
