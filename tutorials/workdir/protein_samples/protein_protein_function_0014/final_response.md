## Functional identification

The 483-aa protein is **NADH:quinone oxidoreductase subunit N**, also called **NuoN** or **NDH-1 subunit N**. It is a membrane-sector component of **bacterial respiratory complex I (NDH-1)**.

This assignment is highly reliable because the sequence is an **exact 100% sequence match** to the reviewed *Bacteroides fragilis* protein UniProt **Q64Y15**.

### Key sequence features

- Length: **483 amino acids**
- Molecular mass: approximately **53.5 kDa**
- Strongly hydrophobic, with numerous predicted transmembrane helices
- Pfam match: **Proton_antipo_M (PF00361)**, characteristic of complex-I membrane antiporter-like subunits
- No large soluble catalytic domain or obvious cofactor-binding domain

## Function

NuoN forms part of the membrane arm of complex I. It does not itself bind NADH or carry out the primary quinone-reduction chemistry; those functions occur in the peripheral, soluble arm of complex I.

Instead, NuoN contributes to the membrane proton-translocation machinery. During electron transfer from NADH to a quinone, complex I uses the released redox energy to move protons across the bacterial cytoplasmic membrane, generating a proton-motive force.

In the reference annotation, the respiratory quinone is believed to be **menaquinone**, consistent with many anaerobic bacteria.

## Subcellular localization

- **Bacterial cytoplasmic/inner membrane**
- **Integral multi-pass membrane protein**
- Part of the membrane sector of complex I

The membrane sector includes the antiporter-like subunits **NuoL, NuoM, and NuoN**, together with other membrane subunits such as NuoA, NuoH, NuoJ, and NuoK.

## Associated biological processes

The protein is involved in:

1. **Respiratory electron transport**
   - Complex I transfers electrons from NADH to quinones.

2. **Proton translocation**
   - NuoN contributes to coupling electron-transfer energy to proton movement across the membrane.

3. **Generation of the proton-motive force**
   - The resulting electrochemical gradient supports cellular energy conservation.

4. **ATP synthesis coupled to electron transport**
   - The proton gradient can drive the membrane ATP synthase.

### Summary

This sequence encodes **NuoN, a large multi-pass membrane subunit of bacterial respiratory complex I**. It is localized in the **cytoplasmic membrane** and participates in **quinone-linked NADH respiration, proton-gradient generation, and ATP synthesis coupled to electron transport**. The exact sequence match to a curated reference makes this identification substantially stronger than a prediction based only on membrane topology or homology.
