## Functional assignment

The 479-aa protein is most consistent with **UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate–meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase**, commonly called **MurE**.

### Evidence

- Closest reviewed homologs are annotated MurE enzymes.
- Sequence identity is approximately **45–48%**, with about **96% full-length coverage** and highly significant E-values (~10⁻¹¹⁶ to 10⁻¹¹⁹).
- The homologs are ATP-binding, Mg²⁺-dependent enzymes involved in bacterial peptidoglycan biosynthesis.
- The sequence length and conserved reference features, including the reported meso-diaminopimelate-recognition region and modified lysine, are consistent with MurE.

## Catalytic activity

MurE catalyzes the ATP-dependent incorporation of **meso-2,6-diaminopimelate (meso-DAP)** into the UDP-linked peptidoglycan precursor UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu. This forms the third peptide residue of the bacterial stem peptide.

### Overall reaction

\[
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu}
+ \text{meso-DAP}
+ \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu-}\textit{meso}\text{-DAP}
+ \text{ADP}
+ \text{P}_{i}
+ \text{H}^{+}
\]

More explicitly, the product is:

**UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-γ-D-glutamyl-meso-2,6-diaminopimelate**

The enzyme uses **Mg²⁺**, which coordinates ATP and supports the phosphoryl-transfer/ligation chemistry. Functionally, it is an **ATP-dependent peptide ligase**, not a hydrolytic enzyme.

## Biological role

The product is an intermediate in **peptidoglycan biosynthesis**, ultimately contributing to bacterial cell-wall construction, cell shape, and cell division. The protein is expected to be a soluble cytoplasmic enzyme.

**Confidence:** Moderate-to-strong sequence-based assignment. The catalytic function is supported by highly similar curated homologs, but direct biochemical activity has not been experimentally demonstrated for this exact sequence.
