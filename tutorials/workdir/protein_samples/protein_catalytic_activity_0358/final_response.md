### Functional identification

The 385-residue protein is most consistent with **RlmN (dual-specificity RNA methyltransferase)**, an **iron–sulfur-dependent radical-SAM enzyme**.

This assignment is supported by close matches to reviewed RlmN proteins:

- ~62% amino-acid identity
- ~91–95% sequence coverage
- E-values approximately \(10^{-160}\)
- Matching annotation: EC **2.1.1.192**
- Conserved association with a **[4Fe–4S] cluster**

### Catalytic activity

RlmN catalyzes **C2 methylation of adenosine residues in RNA**, producing 2-methyladenosine:

- **23S rRNA A2503 → 2-methyladenosine A2503**
- **tRNA A37 → 2-methyladenosine A37**

The overall transformation can be summarized as:

\[
\text{RNA-adenosine} + \text{SAM}
\rightarrow
\text{RNA-2-methyladenosine} + \text{SAH}
\]

Because this is a radical-SAM reaction, a second SAM molecule is used during catalysis to generate the 5′-deoxyadenosyl radical. The reaction therefore produces **5′-deoxyadenosine** in addition to the methylated RNA product and **S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine**.

The [4Fe–4S] cluster, coordinated by cysteine residues, activates SAM and enables the radical chemistry.

### Biological role

Modification of **23S rRNA A2503** is associated with optimization of ribosomal proofreading and translational fidelity. The sequence alone cannot establish whether this particular protein acts on rRNA, tRNA, or both, but its strong similarity to characterized dual-specificity RlmN proteins supports that assignment.

**Conclusion:** This sequence most likely encodes an **RlmN radical-SAM RNA methyltransferase that converts specific RNA adenosines to 2-methyladenosine using SAM and a [4Fe–4S] cofactor**.
