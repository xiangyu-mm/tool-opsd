## Functional role

eIF3i is a structural and interaction-associated component of the **eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 complex**. The eIF3 complex helps:

- Assemble the **43S preinitiation complex**
- Promote formation of the **48S initiation complex**
- Support recruitment of mRNA and initiator methionyl-tRNA to the 40S ribosomal subunit
- Coordinate interactions among translation-initiation factors, ribosomes, and selected mRNAs

Thus, this protein is best regarded as a **nonenzymatic scaffold/adaptor subunit**, rather than a metabolic enzyme.

## Potential cellular processes

The protein is associated with:

- **Cytoplasmic translation initiation**
- Formation of cytoplasmic translation-initiation complexes
- RNA binding and translation-initiation factor activity
- Regulation of translation of selected mRNA subsets

The matched reference annotation also describes eIF3-mediated preferential initiation of some mRNAs involved in cell proliferation. This should be interpreted as a property of the broader eIF3 complex and its regulatory context, not necessarily as an independently demonstrated activity of eIF3i alone.

## Subcellular localization

The primary predicted/curated location is the **cytoplasm**, where eIF3 participates in translation initiation. A **nuclear-periphery** annotation is also present in the reference data, suggesting that some of the protein or eIF3 complex may associate with the nuclear periphery under particular cellular conditions.

## Overall conclusion

This sequence encodes **eIF3 subunit I/eIF3i**, a WD-repeat-containing component of the eukaryotic translation-initiation machinery. It likely functions as a protein-interaction scaffold within eIF3, helping organize translation-initiation complexes and support regulated recruitment of mRNAs to the 40S ribosome. It is primarily cytoplasmic and is not expected to possess independent enzymatic activity.
