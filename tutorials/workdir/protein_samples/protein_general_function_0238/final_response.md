## Protein summary

The 102-amino-acid sequence is strongly identified as an **HIV-1 Tat (trans-activator of transcription) protein**. It matches reviewed HIV-1 Tat references with **85–87% sequence identity** and near-full-length coverage; a Pfam scan also detects the **Tat domain (PF00539)** at residues 2–62.

- **Likely function:** Tat promotes viral gene expression by binding the TAR RNA element in nascent HIV transcripts and recruiting P-TEFb (cyclin T1–CDK9), increasing RNA polymerase II elongation and production of full-length viral transcripts. This functional description comes from the close reviewed homologs, so it is a strong homology-supported assignment rather than an experimental characterization of this exact sequence.
- **Sequence features:** It is short, basic and acidic overall by composition, with a predicted pI of **9.51** and charge of about **+9.8 at pH 7**. Its cysteine-rich region and strongly basic segment are consistent with Tat-family features; the basic segment is also compatible with RNA/nuclear targeting.
- **Localization in reference annotations:** HIV-1 Tat is reported in host-cell nucleus, nucleolus, cytoplasm and extracellular space. These are reference-protein annotations, not experimentally established localization for this particular sequence.
- **Computed properties:** Predicted molecular mass **11.59 kDa**; GRAVY **−1.39**. The instability index is **65.1**, a sequence-based estimate and not a direct measurement of protein stability.

**Bottom line:** This is very likely an HIV-1 Tat protein, a small regulatory protein that enhances viral transcription elongation. Exact strain-specific activity, processing and localization cannot be established from sequence analysis alone.
