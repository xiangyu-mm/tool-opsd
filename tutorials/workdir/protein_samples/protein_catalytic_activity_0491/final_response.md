## Functional identification

The 136-residue protein is **nucleoside-diphosphate kinase (NDK)**, also called **nucleoside diphosphate kinase A**.

**Evidence:**

- Exact full-length sequence match to reviewed *Mycobacterium leprae* NDK proteins.
- NDK Pfam domain **PF00334** detected across residues 3–134.
- Conserved catalytic histidine identified at approximately **His117**.
- Assigned **EC 2.7.4.6**.

## Catalytic activity

NDK transfers the terminal, or γ-, phosphate of ATP to a nucleoside diphosphate. The general reaction is:

\[
\mathrm{NTP + NDP \rightleftharpoons NDP' + NTP'}
\]

More explicitly, for ATP as the phosphate donor:

\[
\mathrm{ATP + NDP \rightleftharpoons ADP + NTP}
\]

where NDP/NTP may be ribonucleotides or deoxyribonucleotides. Examples include:

\[
\mathrm{ATP + GDP \rightleftharpoons ADP + GTP}
\]

\[
\mathrm{ATP + CDP \rightleftharpoons ADP + CTP}
\]

\[
\mathrm{ATP + UDP \rightleftharpoons ADP + UTP}
\]

and corresponding reactions with deoxyribonucleotides.

## Mechanism

The enzyme operates by a **ping-pong phosphotransfer mechanism**:

1. ATP transfers its γ-phosphate to the active-site histidine, forming a phosphohistidine intermediate.
2. The phosphorylated histidine transfers that phosphate to a nucleoside diphosphate, producing the corresponding nucleoside triphosphate.

\[
\mathrm{E-His + ATP \rightarrow E-His\mathord{\sim}P + ADP}
\]

\[
\mathrm{E-His\mathord{\sim}P + NDP \rightarrow E-His + NTP}
\]

## Additional predicted properties

- **Cofactor:** Mg²⁺
- **Localization:** Cytoplasm
- **Likely oligomeric state:** Homotetramer, based on the exact reviewed reference annotation
- **Approximate molecular mass:** 14.7 kDa

### Summary

This is a small cytosolic **nucleoside-diphosphate kinase** that maintains cellular nucleotide-triphosphate pools by reversibly exchanging phosphate between ATP and other nucleoside diphosphates.
