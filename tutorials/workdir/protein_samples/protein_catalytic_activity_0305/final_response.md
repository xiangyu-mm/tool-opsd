## Functional identification

The 102-aa protein is most consistent with a **broad-specificity pyrimidine/purine nucleoside phosphorylase**.

Evidence:

- Strong match to the **Ppnp domain** (PF06865; residues 11–101).
- Up to **71% sequence identity over 98% coverage** to the reviewed UniProt protein A6QCX3, annotated as a pyrimidine/purine nucleoside phosphorylase.
- The homologous proteins are assigned **EC 2.4.2.1 and EC 2.4.2.2**.

## Chemical reaction

The enzyme catalyzes reversible phosphorolysis of nucleosides:

\[
\text{nucleoside} + \text{inorganic phosphate}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{free nucleobase} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

Representative reactions include:

\[
\text{guanosine} + P_i \rightleftharpoons \text{guanine} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

\[
\text{adenosine} + P_i \rightleftharpoons \text{adenine} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

\[
\text{uridine} + P_i \rightleftharpoons \text{uracil} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

Homolog annotations indicate activity toward several nucleosides, including **adenosine, guanosine, inosine, cytidine, uridine, thymidine, and xanthosine**. In the reverse direction, the enzyme can form nucleosides from a free base and ribose 1-phosphate.

## Additional interpretation

- **Likely cellular location:** cytosol, based on homolog annotations.
- **Cofactor:** no metal or organic cofactor is indicated; inorganic phosphate is a reaction substrate.
- **Catalytic residues:** specific catalytic residues cannot be assigned reliably from this sequence alone. The functional assignment is based primarily on the highly significant full-length homology and conserved Ppnp domain.
- **Confidence:** **high for the general nucleoside-phosphorylase activity**, but the exact substrate preference and relative catalytic efficiencies remain experimentally unverified for this particular sequence.
