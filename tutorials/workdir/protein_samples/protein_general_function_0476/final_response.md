## Functional summary

The 326-residue protein is **COQ9 (coenzyme Q/ubiquinone biosynthesis protein 9)**, a mitochondrial accessory factor involved in coenzyme Q synthesis.

### Key evidence

- **Exact sequence match:** The sequence matches the curated *Drosophila melanogaster* COQ9 protein (UniProt **Q8MKN0**) with 100% identity and coverage.
- **Length:** 326 amino acids
- **Predicted molecular mass:** ~36.3 kDa
- **Theoretical pI:** ~7.73
- **Hydropathy:** Overall moderately hydrophilic; the protein is not predicted to be a typical multipass transmembrane enzyme.
- **Mitochondrial targeting:** The positively charged, acidic-residue-poor N-terminal region is consistent with a mitochondrial targeting peptide of approximately residues 1–43/44. The mature protein likely begins near residue 44.
- **Domains:** Pfam analysis detected:
  - **COQ9_N** domain, approximately residues 109–136
  - **COQ9** domain, approximately residues 220–290

### Biological function

COQ9 is a **membrane-associated lipid/isoprenoid-binding protein** in the mitochondrial ubiquinone biosynthetic complex. It binds aromatic isoprenoid intermediates of coenzyme Q and helps present them to **COQ7**, the hydroxylase responsible for a later step in the pathway.

Its proposed role is therefore organizational and substrate-presenting rather than catalytic:

> **COQ9 binds and positions coenzyme Q intermediates at the mitochondrial membrane, facilitating COQ7-dependent hydroxylation during ubiquinone biosynthesis.**

### Localization and complex formation

Curated homolog annotations place COQ9 in:

- The **mitochondrion**
- The **mitochondrial inner membrane**
- The **ubiquinone biosynthesis complex**

COQ9 can form homodimers and participate in higher-order COQ7–COQ9 assemblies. In mammals, these assemblies can form lipid-associated oligomeric structures that help capture and expose hydrophobic coenzyme Q intermediates.

### Catalytic activity and cofactors

- No independent catalytic reaction or EC number was retrieved.
- COQ9 is best classified as a **non-enzymatic lipid/isoprenoid-binding and enzyme-supporting factor**.
- No specific metal, nucleotide, or redox cofactor requirement was identified.

### Overall conclusion

This sequence encodes a mitochondrial **COQ9 ubiquinone-biosynthesis factor**, with an N-terminal mitochondrial targeting peptide and conserved COQ9 domains. Its principal function is to bind hydrophobic coenzyme Q intermediates and facilitate their processing by COQ7 within the mitochondrial inner-membrane biosynthetic complex.
