## Identification
The 392-aa sequence is very likely **MUL1** (mitochondrial ubiquitin ligase activator of NF-κB 1). It matches reviewed human MUL1 (UniProt **Q969V5**) at **99.7% identity** over **79.8% of the query**. The annotation is therefore strongly supported by homology, though the sequence itself was not experimentally tested.

## Function and biological processes
MUL1 is an **E3 ubiquitin ligase** associated with protein ubiquitination; it is also proposed to have **SUMO E3 ligase** activity. Reported roles include:

- **Mitochondrial dynamics:** promotes mitochondrial fragmentation/fission, potentially by negatively regulating MFN1 and MFN2.
- **Protein turnover and signaling:** can promote degradation of phosphorylated AKT1 and cytoplasmic p53/TP53 through ubiquitination.
- **Antiviral innate immunity:** reported to ubiquitinate STING1 in a way that promotes type I interferon production, while also modulating RIG-I-dependent antiviral responses. Its effects may differ by pathway or context.

These are reported functions of the close annotated homolog, not all necessarily established directly for this exact sequence.

## Subcellular localization
The reference annotation places MUL1 primarily at the **mitochondrial outer membrane** and also reports localization to **peroxisomes**. It is annotated as a multi-pass membrane protein; transport from mitochondria to peroxisomes in mitochondria-derived vesicles has also been reported.

**Bottom line:** this sequence most likely encodes a mitochondrial membrane-associated E3 ligase involved in protein modification, mitochondrial fission, and regulation of innate immune signaling.
