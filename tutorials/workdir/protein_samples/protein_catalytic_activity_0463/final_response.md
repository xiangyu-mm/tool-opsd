### Catalytic activity

MsrA repairs oxidatively damaged proteins by reducing **protein-bound methionine-(S)-sulfoxide** back to methionine. The reducing equivalents are supplied by a thioredoxin system.

A simplified physiological reaction is:

\[
\text{Protein–Met-(S)-O} + \text{thioredoxin}_{\mathrm{red}}
\rightarrow
\text{Protein–Met} + \text{thioredoxin}_{\mathrm{ox}} + \mathrm{H_2O}
\]

Equivalently, the reaction can be written in the reverse oxidation direction as:

\[
\text{Protein–Met} + \text{thioredoxin}_{\mathrm{ox}} + \mathrm{H_2O}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{Protein–Met-(S)-O} + \text{thioredoxin}_{\mathrm{red}}
\]

In cells, reduced thioredoxin is typically regenerated by **thioredoxin reductase using NADPH**, so MsrA is indirectly coupled to NADPH-dependent redox metabolism.
