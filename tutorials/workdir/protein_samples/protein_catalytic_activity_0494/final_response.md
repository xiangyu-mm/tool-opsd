## Functional identification

The 485-residue protein is **Aspartyl/glutamyl-tRNA(Asn/Gln) amidotransferase subunit A**, commonly called **GatA**.

The assignment is highly reliable because the sequence is an **exact full-length match** to reviewed UniProt protein Q746Y7 and contains a matching **amidase domain (PF01425; residues ~24–466)**.

### Catalytic activity

GatA is the **glutaminase subunit** of the heterotrimeric **GatCAB amidotransferase complex**. It hydrolyzes glutamine to generate ammonia:

\[
\mathrm{L\text{-}glutamine + H_2O \rightarrow L\text{-}glutamate + NH_4^+}
\]

- **EC:** 3.5.1.2  
- **Rhea:** RHEA:15889  
- **Primary product:** glutamate  
- **Transferred nitrogen:** ammonia, which is channeled within GatCAB to the GatB subunit

GatA uses an amidase-type catalytic mechanism involving a conserved charge-relay system and an acyl-enzyme intermediate. The sequence annotations identify catalytic-region residues around positions **78, 153, and 177**.

## Role in the overall pathway

The GatCAB complex corrects mischarged tRNAs in organisms that lack one or both direct aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases:

- Asp-tRNA(Asn) → Asn-tRNA(Asn)
- Glu-tRNA(Gln) → Gln-tRNA(Gln)

The overall reaction uses **glutamine and ATP**. However, **ATP-dependent tRNA amidation is performed by the GatB-containing complex**, whereas GatA specifically supplies the ammonia by glutamine hydrolysis.

**Complex:** GatA–GatB–GatC
