## Functional identification

The 402-residue protein is most consistent with **CoaBC, the bifunctional coenzyme A biosynthesis protein**.

### Catalytic activities

It catalyzes two sequential reactions:

1. **Phosphopantothenate–cysteine ligase activity**  
   **EC 6.3.2.5; Rhea RHEA:19397**

\[
(R)\text{-4′-phosphopantothenate} + \text{L-cysteine} + \text{CTP}
\rightarrow
N\text{-[(R)-4-phosphopantothenoyl]-L-cysteine} + \text{CMP} + \text{diphosphate}
\]

2. **Phosphopantothenoylcysteine decarboxylase activity**  
   **EC 4.1.1.36; Rhea RHEA:16793**

\[
N\text{-[(R)-4-phosphopantothenoyl]-L-cysteine}
\rightarrow
(R)\text{-4′-phosphopantetheine} + CO_2
\]

Thus, the enzyme converts **4′-phosphopantothenate and cysteine into 4′-phosphopantetheine**, an essential intermediate in **coenzyme A biosynthesis**.

### Sequence evidence

- Length: **402 amino acids**
- N-terminal **Flavoprotein domain**, residues approximately 2–176
- C-terminal **DFP/CoaB domain**, residues approximately 185–396
- Strong similarity to curated CoaBC proteins:
  - *Haemophilus influenzae* P44953: **42.3% identity**, full-length coverage
  - *Bacillus subtilis* O35033: **40.4% identity**, near-full-length coverage
- Homolog annotations support binding of **one FMN per subunit** and use of **Mg²⁺**

### Summary

This is very likely a **bifunctional CoaBC enzyme**, not merely a cysteine decarboxylase. Its N-terminal domain performs the FMN-associated decarboxylation step, while its C-terminal domain performs CTP-dependent cysteine ligation. The assignment is supported by strong full-length homology and matching domain architecture, although it remains a sequence-based inference rather than direct biochemical characterization of this specific protein.
