## Functional identification

This 765-residue protein is **phosphoribosylformylglycinamidine synthase subunit PurL**, specifically the **large PurL enzyme** of the FGAM-synthase complex.

The assignment is highly secure: the sequence is an **exact sequence match** to reviewed UniProt entry **A5GHL4** and has the characteristic PurL domain architecture:

- N-terminal **FGAR-AT linker**
- Tandem **AIRS** and **AIRS_C** domains
- ATP- and magnesium-binding catalytic regions

Predicted properties include a molecular mass of approximately **80.9 kDa** and an acidic theoretical pI of approximately **4.8**.

## Catalytic activity

PurL catalyzes the ATP-dependent conversion of **formylglycinamide ribonucleotide (FGAR)** into **formylglycinamidine ribonucleotide (FGAM)** during de novo purine biosynthesis.

The overall reaction is:

\[
\text{FGAR} + \text{L-glutamine} + \text{ATP} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
\rightarrow
\text{FGAM} + \text{L-glutamate} + \text{ADP} + \text{P}_i + \text{H}^+
\]

More explicitly:

\[

+
\text{L-glutamine}
+
\text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{2-formamido-N}^1\text{-(5-O-phospho-}\beta\text{-D-ribosyl)acetamidine}
+
\text{L-glutamate}
+
\text{ADP}
+
\text{phosphate}
\]

## Mechanistic role in the complex

In organisms containing the three-component FGAM synthase system:

- **PurQ** hydrolyzes glutamine to generate ammonia.
- **PurL** uses that ammonia to amidate FGAR in an ATP-dependent reaction.
- **PurS** assists transfer of ammonia from PurQ to PurL.

Thus, the protein is an **ATP-dependent amidoligase**, not a glutaminase itself. It is expected to be a **cytoplasmic enzyme**, functioning as one PurL subunit in a complex containing **PurL, PurQ, and two PurS subunits**.

**Overall conclusion:** the sequence encodes the catalytic large PurL subunit of FGAM synthase, responsible for the ATP-powered conversion of FGAR to FGAM, a key committed amidation step in de novo purine nucleotide synthesis.
