## Functional identification

The 319-amino-acid protein is **protoporphyrin ferrochelatase** (**EC 4.98.1.1**), the terminal enzyme of bacterial heme biosynthesis.

This assignment is highly reliable because the sequence is an **exact 100% sequence match** to the reviewed *Actinobacillus pleuropneumoniae* ferrochelatase UniProt entry **B0BTL7**, with full-length coverage and an E-value of approximately **8.5 × 10⁻²³³**. It also contains the characteristic **Ferrochelatase Pfam domain PF00762**, spanning residues 5–318.

## Molecular function

Ferrochelatase inserts ferrous iron into protoporphyrin IX to produce **protoheme, or heme b**:

\[
\text{protoporphyrin IX} + \mathrm{Fe^{2+}}
\rightarrow \text{heme b} + 2\mathrm{H^+}
\]

Thus, the protein’s principal molecular function is:

- **Protoporphyrin ferrochelatase activity**
- **Metal ion insertion into a porphyrin ring**
- **Formation of heme b**

The retrieved reference annotations identify conserved functionally relevant binding-site positions corresponding to approximately **residues 193 and 274** in this sequence. The available evidence does not establish additional substrate specificities beyond the canonical protoporphyrin IX reaction.

## Subcellular localization

The protein is annotated as **cytoplasmic**.

This is consistent with its role in bacterial heme biosynthesis and with the sequence itself, which is a compact, soluble enzyme lacking an obvious N-terminal signal peptide or transmembrane segment. The localization is supported particularly strongly because it comes from the exact reviewed reference sequence match.

## Biological processes and pathways

The protein is involved in:

1. **Heme biosynthetic process**  
   It catalyzes the final enzymatic step in the production of heme b.

2. **Protoheme biosynthesis**  
   It converts protoporphyrin IX into heme by incorporating ferrous iron.

3. **Porphyrin-containing compound metabolism**  
   Its reaction links porphyrin metabolism to iron utilization and heme production.

Heme produced by this pathway is required for bacterial hemoproteins, including respiratory cytochromes, catalases, peroxidases, and other heme-dependent enzymes.

## Functional summary

This sequence encodes a **soluble bacterial ferrochelatase**, a ~36-kDa cytoplasmic enzyme that performs the terminal step of heme biosynthesis by inserting Fe²⁺ into protoporphyrin IX to generate heme b. Its identity is supported by an exact match to a reviewed ferrochelatase sequence, a highly significant ferrochelatase-domain match, and conserved pathway and Gene Ontology annotations.

**Confidence:** Very high for ferrochelatase function, cytoplasmic localization, and involvement in heme biosynthesis. The available evidence does not independently establish oligomeric state, detailed catalytic mechanism, or specific cofactor requirements beyond the metal substrate used in the reaction.
