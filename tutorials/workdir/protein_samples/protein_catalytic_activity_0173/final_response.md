## Functional identification

The 393-aa protein is most likely **RibBA**, a bifunctional enzyme in **riboflavin (vitamin B₂) biosynthesis**.

It contains two strongly supported domains:

- **Residues ~7–197:** DHBP synthase domain, PF00926  
- **Residues ~209–368:** GTP cyclohydrolase II domain, PF00925  

The sequence matches reviewed bacterial RibBA proteins at **75–77% identity across the full length**, providing strong homology-based support.

Here, DARP is **2,5-diamino-6-ribosylamino-4(3H)-pyrimidinone 5′-phosphate**, an early precursor in riboflavin biosynthesis. This activity is provided by the C-terminal domain.

## Additional evidence

Homolog annotations indicate binding of:

- **Two Mg²⁺ or Mn²⁺ ions**
- **One Zn²⁺ ion**

These metal assignments are transferred from closely related reviewed homologs rather than experimentally demonstrated for this exact sequence.

**Conclusion:** The protein is a bacterial **bifunctional RibBA riboflavin-biosynthesis enzyme**, catalyzing the first DHBP-synthase and GTP-cyclohydrolase-II steps of the pathway.
