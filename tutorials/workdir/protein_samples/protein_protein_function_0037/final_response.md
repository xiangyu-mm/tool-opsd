## Probable function

The 211-amino-acid protein is most likely a **Ham1p-like dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase**, corresponding to **EC 3.6.1.66**.

It likely hydrolyzes abnormal purine nucleoside triphosphates:

- **dITP → dIMP + pyrophosphate**
- **ITP → IMP + pyrophosphate**
- **XTP → XMP + pyrophosphate**

The reaction is expected to be **Mg²⁺-dependent**.

## Evidence

- The sequence contains a strong **Ham1p_like domain** hit, Pfam **PF01725**, spanning approximately residues 19–205.
- It shares **56.9% identity over 90.5% of the sequence** with the reviewed *Rhizobium meliloti* dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase Q92SK4.
- The homolog is annotated as a soluble, cytosolic enzyme involved in purine nucleotide triphosphate catabolism.
- The sequence is acidic and lacks obvious transmembrane segments or signal-peptide features, supporting a soluble intracellular role.

## Biological process

The likely biological role is **nucleotide-pool sanitization**. The enzyme removes noncanonical purine triphosphates—particularly dITP, ITP, and XTP—before they can be incorporated into DNA or RNA. This helps reduce:

- aberrant base pairing,
- mutagenic nucleotide incorporation,
- chromosomal lesions and replication-associated damage.

The protein may form a homodimer, as reported for close homologs, but this is not directly established for the provided sequence.

**Overall assignment:** a soluble, Mg²⁺-dependent **Ham1p-family purine-triphosphate pyrophosphatase involved in protection of genome integrity by cleansing abnormal nucleotides from the cellular pool**.
