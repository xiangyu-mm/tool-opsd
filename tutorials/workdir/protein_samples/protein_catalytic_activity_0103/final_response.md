## Functional assignment

The protein is **thymidylate synthase** (**ThyA**, EC **2.1.1.45**), a pyrimidine-biosynthesis enzyme.

### Evidence

- Length: **279 amino acids**
- Exact full-length sequence match to reviewed *Streptococcus pyogenes* thymidylate synthases, including UniProt **P67051**
- Contains the characteristic **Thymidylat_synt Pfam domain (PF00303)**
- Conserved catalytic nucleophile: **Cys154**
- Curated homologs are cytosolic, homodimeric thymidylate synthases
- Functional evidence grade: **exact sequence annotation**, not merely a weak homology prediction

## Catalytic reaction

\[
\text{dUMP} + \text{5,10-methylene-THF}
\rightarrow
\text{dTMP} + \text{dihydrofolate}
\]

More explicitly:

\[
\text{2′-deoxyuridine-5′-monophosphate}
+
\text{5,10-methylene-5,6,7,8-tetrahydrofolate}
\rightarrow
\text{2′-deoxythymidine-5′-monophosphate}
+
\text{7,8-dihydrofolate}
\]

The enzyme performs the **reductive methylation of dUMP**, adding a methyl group at C5 of the uracil ring to produce dTMP. The folate derivative supplies both the one-carbon unit and the reducing equivalent; it is converted to dihydrofolate.

Mechanistically, the conserved **Cys154** forms a transient covalent bond with the dUMP substrate, activating its C5 position for methylene transfer from 5,10-methylene-THF. Subsequent reduction and elimination release dTMP.

## Biological role

This reaction is the principal de novo source of **dTMP**, which is subsequently converted to dTTP for **DNA synthesis**. The protein is therefore involved in:

- Pyrimidine metabolism
- dTMP biosynthesis
- dTTP biosynthesis
- DNA precursor production

**Overall conclusion:** This sequence is confidently identified as a bacterial **thymidylate synthase (ThyA)**.
