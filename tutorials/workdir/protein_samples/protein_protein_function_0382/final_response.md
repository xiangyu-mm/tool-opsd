## Functional identification

The 366-amino-acid protein is **D-alanine--D-alanine ligase** (**Ddl; EC 6.3.2.4**), an ATP-dependent enzyme required for bacterial peptidoglycan biosynthesis.

This assignment is very strong because the sequence is an **exact sequence match** to reviewed UniProt entry **Q10X06**, annotated as D-alanine--D-alanine ligase from *Trichodesmium erythraeum*. Highly similar reviewed proteins also show approximately 72% identity across essentially the full sequence.

### Catalytic reaction

\[
2\ \text{D-alanine} + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{D-alanyl-D-alanine} + \text{ADP} + \text{phosphate}
\]

The product, **D-alanyl-D-alanine**, is incorporated into the stem peptide of bacterial peptidoglycan precursors. It is therefore essential for formation and maturation of the bacterial cell wall.

## Overall summary

This sequence encodes a highly conserved, cytosolic **D-alanine--D-alanine ligase** involved in bacterial cell-wall construction. It catalyzes ATP-dependent joining of two D-alanine molecules to produce D-alanyl-D-alanine, using Mg²⁺ or Mn²⁺ as catalytic metal cofactors. The identification is supported by an exact curated sequence match, full-length homologs, the expected D-Ala-D-Ala ligase domains, ATP-grasp architecture, and matching functional annotations.
