## Functional assessment

**Primary annotation:** **AMP phosphorylase** (**EC 2.4.2.57**), a soluble archaeal nucleoside-phosphorylase-like enzyme.

### Key characteristics

- **Length:** 505 amino acids  
- **Predicted molecular mass:** 53.3 kDa  
- **Theoretical pI:** 6.01  
- **Estimated charge at pH 7:** −3.9  
- **Hydropathy:** No membrane-spanning region or secretion signal is indicated; a **cytosolic** localization is most likely.
- **Sequence stability indicators:** Instability index ~30.8, consistent with a generally stable folded protein.

### Functional evidence

The sequence is an **exact sequence match** to reviewed UniProt entry **A2SPI9**, annotated as AMP phosphorylase. The primary reaction is:

\[
\text{AMP} + \text{phosphate}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{adenine} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1,5-bisphosphate}
\]

The enzyme is also reported to phosphorylyze **CMP and UMP**, producing cytosine or uracil plus ribose-1,5-bisphosphate:

- AMP phosphorylase reaction: **Rhea RHEA:36975**
- CMP phosphorylase reaction: **Rhea RHEA:36987**
- UMP phosphorylase reaction: **Rhea RHEA:36991**

This places the protein in an **archaeal AMP/nucleoside degradation pathway**, reported to function with ribose-1,5-bisphosphate isomerase and RubisCO.

### Domain architecture

Pfam analysis identifies:

- **Glycos_trans_3N** — residues ~91–151  
- **Glycos_transf_3** — residues ~159–388  
- **PYNP_C** — residues ~425–490  

This architecture is consistent with a large **phosphorolytic glycosyl-transferase/nucleoside phosphorylase family enzyme**.

### Cofactor and oligomerization

No specific metal, organic cofactor, or prosthetic group was identified by the available analysis. The oligomeric state is also not established from the sequence-based evidence.

### Overall conclusion

This is a ~53 kDa, likely cytosolic archaeal **AMP phosphorylase** with strong annotation support from an exact reviewed-sequence match. Its principal role is cleavage of AMP by inorganic phosphate to generate adenine and ribose-1,5-bisphosphate, with likely additional CMP and UMP phosphorylase activity.
