## Molecular function

NUG1 is a **guanosine triphosphate-binding protein with GTPase activity**. It acts as a ribosome-biogenesis factor rather than as a metabolic enzyme.

The protein is also annotated for:

- RNA binding
- mRNA binding
- Association with the large-subunit preribosome

No specific catalytic reaction equation or additional cofactor was retrieved. Its essential nucleotide cofactor is therefore **GTP**, which is bound and hydrolyzed by the GTPase domain.

## Subcellular localization

NUG1 is localized predominantly to:

- **Nucleus**
- **Nucleolus**
- **Large-subunit preribosomal particles**

The highly basic N-terminal region likely contributes to nucleolar localization and/or binding to nucleic acids or preribosomal assembly complexes. It is not a membrane or secreted protein: the sequence lacks a hydrophobic signal peptide and transmembrane segments.

## Biological processes

NUG1 participates in late nuclear ribosome biogenesis, particularly:

1. **Maturation of large-subunit rRNA** from the precursor rRNA transcript  
2. **Assembly of the large-subunit preribosome**  
3. **Export of the pre-60S ribosomal subunit from the nucleus to the cytoplasm**  
4. More generally, production and maturation of functional cytoplasmic 60S ribosomal subunits

Related fungal proteins, such as *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* Grn1, support conservation of this role in pre-rRNA processing and pre-60S particle export.

## Functional summary

**This protein is the yeast NUG1 nuclear GTPase, a nucleolar ribosome-biogenesis factor. It binds and hydrolyzes GTP and associates with large-subunit preribosomal particles, where it contributes to LSU rRNA maturation, pre-60S assembly, and export of the mature pre-60S ribosomal subunit from the nucleus to the cytoplasm.**
