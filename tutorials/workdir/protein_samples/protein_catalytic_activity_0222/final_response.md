## Functional assignment

The 350-residue protein is most consistent with **RlmN, a dual-specificity radical-SAM RNA methyltransferase** (**EC 2.1.1.192**).

### Evidence

- Strong homology to reviewed RlmN proteins:
  - ~43–45% amino-acid identity
  - ~97–99% sequence coverage
  - E-values approximately \(10^{-95}\)–\(10^{-98}\)
- Contains the characteristic:
  - **RlmN N-terminal domain**
  - **Radical-SAM domain**
  - Conserved **CxxxCxxC** motif for binding a **[4Fe–4S] cluster**
- Size and basic character are compatible with a bacterial, cytoplasmic RNA-modifying enzyme.

## Catalytic activity

RlmN methylates the **C2 position of adenine** in RNA. It can act on two substrates:

1. **A2503 of 23S rRNA**, producing **2-methyladenosine-2503 (m²A2503)**
2. **A37 of tRNA**, producing **2-methyladenosine-37 (m²A37)**

The principal reactions are:

\[
\text{A2503 in 23S rRNA} + 2\,\text{SAM}
\rightarrow
\text{m}^{2}\text{A2503 in 23S rRNA}
+ \text{SAH} + \text{5'-deoxyadenosine}
+ \text{other redox products}
\]

and

\[
\text{A37 in tRNA} + 2\,\text{SAM}
\rightarrow
\text{m}^{2}\text{A37 in tRNA}
+ \text{SAH} + \text{5'-deoxyadenosine}
+ \text{other redox products}
\]

Here, **SAM** is S-adenosyl-L-methionine and **SAH** is S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine.

## Mechanistic interpretation

RlmN uses an unusual two-SAM mechanism:

1. One SAM methylates a catalytic cysteine on the enzyme, generating a **methylcysteine intermediate**.
2. The [4Fe–4S] radical-SAM cluster reductively cleaves a second SAM to form a **5′-deoxyadenosyl radical**.
3. This radical activates the target adenine C2 position.
4. The methyl group is transferred from the methylated cysteine to the RNA substrate.

Thus, this protein is not a conventional single-step SAM-dependent methyltransferase; it is a **radical-SAM-dependent RNA C2 methylase**.

### Overall conclusion

The sequence most likely encodes a **cytoplasmic RlmN RNA methyltransferase**, modifying 23S rRNA A2503 and tRNA A37. The assignment is supported by domain architecture, the radical-SAM cysteine motif, and highly similar reviewed RlmN homologs, although the exact substrate preference has not been experimentally tested for this particular sequence.
