This makes the functional assignment substantially stronger than a prediction based only on sequence similarity.

## Function

Aqy2 is annotated as a **water channel** that facilitates water movement across cellular membranes. Its associated Gene Ontology annotations include:

- **Water channel activity** — GO:0015250  
- **Transmembrane transport** — GO:0055085  
- **Water transport** — GO:0006833  

Thus, the protein is expected to contribute to the controlled movement of water between cellular compartments and the extracellular environment. It is not predicted to be an ATP-driven pump or enzyme; rather, it likely provides a passive membrane pathway for water.

## Subcellular localization

The reference annotations place the protein in:

1. **Endoplasmic reticulum membrane**
2. **Plasma membrane / cell membrane**

This suggests that Aqy2 may function both during membrane trafficking or intracellular water equilibration in the endoplasmic reticulum and at the cell surface, where it can directly influence cellular water permeability.

## Biological processes and phenotypes

In *S. cerevisiae*, Aqy2 has been associated with:

- **Water transport**
- **Osmotolerance**
- **Freeze tolerance**
- **Cell flocculation in liquid cultures**

The connection to freeze tolerance and osmotolerance is biologically plausible because membrane water permeability affects the rate and extent of cellular volume changes during osmotic stress and freezing. Its reported association with flocculation indicates that Aqy2 may also influence multicellular aggregation or liquid-culture growth behavior, although this may be indirect or strain-dependent.

## Important strain-specific caveat

The reviewed annotation states that Aqy2 is **non-functional in most laboratory strains** of *S. cerevisiae*. This likely reflects strain-specific sequence variation, expression differences, or laboratory-associated loss of function. Therefore:

- The sequence identity supports the assignment to Aqy2 very strongly.
- The water-channel function is supported by curated annotation.
- The physiological effects should not automatically be assumed to occur in every yeast strain carrying an Aqy2-like sequence.
