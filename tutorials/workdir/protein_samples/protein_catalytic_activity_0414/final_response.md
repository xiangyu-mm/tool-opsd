### Functional assignment

The sequence is most consistent with **MurE ligase**—specifically, **UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate–meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase** (**EC 6.3.2.13**).

### Evidence

- **Length:** 480 amino acids, consistent with bacterial MurE proteins.
- **Domain architecture:** Contains the three characteristic Mur ligase domains:
  - Mur_ligase
  - Mur_ligase_M
  - Mur_ligase_C
- **Homology:** Matches reviewed MurE proteins with approximately **39–42% sequence identity** and **89–93% coverage**, with highly significant E-values.
- **Conserved features:** The homologs contain conserved ATP/substrate-binding residues and a conserved carboxylated lysine characteristic of Mur ligases.
- **Cellular role:** Expected to be a soluble, cytoplasmic enzyme involved in bacterial peptidoglycan biosynthesis.

### Catalytic reaction

The enzyme adds **meso-2,6-diaminopimelate (meso-DAP)** to the UDP-linked MurNAc tripeptide precursor:

\[
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu}
+ \text{meso-DAP}
+ \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-}\gamma\text{-D-Glu-meso-DAP}
+ \text{ADP}
+ \text{P}_{i}
+ \text{H}^{+}
\]

In expanded form:

**UDP-N-acetyl-α-D-muramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate + meso-2,6-diaminopimelate + ATP → UDP-N-acetyl-α-D-muramoyl-L-alanyl-γ-D-glutamyl-meso-2,6-diaminopimelate + ADP + phosphate**

### Catalytic role

MurE is an **ATP-dependent peptide ligase**. It uses ATP to activate the terminal D-glutamate carboxyl group of the UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu substrate, enabling formation of the peptide bond to meso-DAP. **Mg²⁺** is required for ATP utilization and catalysis.

The product is the **UDP-MurNAc-tripeptide precursor**, which is subsequently used to build the peptide stems of bacterial peptidoglycan. Thus, this enzyme contributes directly to **bacterial cell-wall assembly, cell division, and maintenance of cell shape**.

### Confidence and caveat

The assignment is strongly supported by domain architecture, sequence length, and multiple high-coverage matches to reviewed MurE homologs. The evidence supports **MurE ligase activity**, although the sequence analysis alone does not experimentally establish substrate specificity or kinetic properties.
