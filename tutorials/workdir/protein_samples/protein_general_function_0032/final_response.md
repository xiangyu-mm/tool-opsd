## Functional summary

The 768-aa protein is very likely a fungal **PalA/Rim20 pH-response regulator**, with high confidence.

### Evidence

- It is almost identical to the reviewed *Aspergillus oryzae* PalA/Rim20 protein:
  - **99.7% sequence identity**
  - **100% query coverage**
  - E-value 0
- It also matches PalA/Rim20 proteins from other filamentous fungi.
- Pfam analysis identifies the characteristic:
  - **BRO1 domain**, residues approximately **5–382**
  - **ALIX/LYPXL-binding domain**, residues approximately **418–719**

### Likely molecular role

PalA/Rim20 is a **nonenzymatic scaffolding/adaptor protein** in the fungal alkaline-pH response pathway. It helps assemble a proteolytic signaling complex containing:

1. The transcription factor **PacC/Rim101**
2. The calpain-like protease **PalB/Rim13**
3. The ESCRT-associated factor **Vps32**

This complex promotes proteolytic processing and activation of PacC/Rim101 in response to alkaline ambient pH. The ALIX-like domain is consistent with binding **YPX[LI]/LYPXL motifs** in PacC/Rim101.

- Length: **768 amino acids**
- Estimated molecular mass: **86.8 kDa**
- Theoretical pI: **5.92**
- Predicted charge at pH 7: mildly negative

- Middle region: ALIX-like motif-binding domain
- C-terminal region: likely more flexible/disordered and contains proline/glutamine-rich low-complexity sequence
- The sequence is highly charged and helical in many regions, consistent with a protein–protein interaction scaffold rather than a metabolic enzyme.

### Catalytic activity

No catalytic reaction, cofactor, or enzyme active-site annotation was identified. The strong homology and domain organization instead support a **regulatory adaptor/scaffold function**.

### Localization and complex association

Reviewed homologs are associated with:

- **Endosomes**
- The **ESCRT-III complex**
- pH-dependent proteolytic signaling complexes

### Overall conclusion

This protein is best annotated as:

> **Fungal pH-response regulator PalA/Rim20, an ESCRT-associated scaffold required for PacC/Rim101 proteolytic activation during alkaline pH adaptation.**

The family assignment and broad function are supported with high confidence. Exact species-specific interactions and substrate preferences should still be confirmed experimentally.
