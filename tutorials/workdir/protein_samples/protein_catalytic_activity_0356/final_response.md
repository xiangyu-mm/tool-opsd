## Functional identification

The sequence is **GroEL (Hsp60/Cpn60), an ATP-dependent molecular chaperone**, not a conventional small-molecule metabolic enzyme.

Evidence is decisive:

- **Length:** 541 amino acids  
- **Pfam:** Cpn60_TCP1 domain, PF00118, spanning approximately residues 22–521  
- **Reference match:** Exact sequence match to reviewed UniProt GroEL entry **A9KSJ1**
- **EC number:** **EC 5.6.1.7**, ATP-dependent protein-folding chaperone
- **Localization:** Cytoplasm
- **Complex:** Fourteen GroEL subunits arranged as two stacked heptameric rings; functions with the co-chaperone **GroES**
- **Calculated molecular mass:** ~57.1 kDa per subunit

## Catalytic activity

GroEL uses ATP hydrolysis to drive conformational changes that capture unfolded or misfolded proteins and provide an isolated environment in which they can refold.

A functional overall reaction can be represented as:

\[
\text{ATP} + \mathrm{H_2O} + \text{unfolded protein}
\rightarrow
\text{ADP} + \mathrm{P_i} + \text{folded protein}
\]

More precisely, GroEL catalyzes the ATP-dependent chaperone cycle:

1. An unfolded protein binds to the hydrophobic apical surface of GroEL.
2. ATP binds to GroEL and promotes a conformational change.
3. GroES binds, enclosing the client protein within the GroEL cavity.
4. The enclosed protein undergoes folding attempts in a protected environment.
5. ATP hydrolysis and subsequent nucleotide exchange drive release and reset of the complex.

Thus, the protein **does not chemically transform the client polypeptide**. Its enzymatic role is ATP hydrolysis coupled to mechanical conformational changes and protein-folding assistance.

## Functional summary

**This protein is the bacterial GroEL chaperonin, a cytoplasmic ATP-dependent protein-folding machine. It forms a tetradecameric double-ring complex with GroES and uses repeated ATP-binding and ATP-hydrolysis cycles to encapsulate and assist the folding of non-native proteins.**
