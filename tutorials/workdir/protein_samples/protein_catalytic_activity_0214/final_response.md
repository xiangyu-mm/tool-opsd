## Functional identification

The 506-residue protein is strongly identified as **MurE ligase**: UDP-*N*-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate—meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase (**EC 6.3.2.13**).

This assignment is supported by near-full-length matches to reviewed MurE proteins:

- **67–70% amino-acid identity**
- **~95–98% sequence coverage**
- Extremely significant homology E-values, approximately **10⁻²²¹ to 10⁻²²⁵**

## Catalyzed reaction

\[
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu}
+ \text{meso-DAP}
+ \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-}\gamma\text{-D-Glu-meso-DAP}
+ \text{ADP}
+ \text{P}_{i}
+ \text{H}^{+}
\]

In words, MurE uses ATP to activate the UDP-*N*-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate precursor and then adds **meso-2,6-diaminopimelate (meso-DAP)** to the terminal glutamate residue. The product is **UDP-*N*-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-γ-D-glutamyl-meso-DAP**, a key precursor of bacterial peptidoglycan.

## Biological role

This is an ATP-dependent ligase in **bacterial cell-wall peptidoglycan biosynthesis**. The reaction introduces the diamino acid that later contributes to peptide cross-linking in the mature peptidoglycan layer. **Mg²⁺** is the reported metal cofactor required for the ATP-dependent reaction.

**Confidence:** High for MurE identity and the stated reaction, based on strong homology to multiple reviewed MurE ligases and consistent EC/Rhea annotation (EC 6.3.2.13; Rhea RHEA:23676).
