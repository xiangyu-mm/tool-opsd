## Functional identification

The 104-residue protein is most consistent with a **pyrimidine/purine nucleoside phosphorylase** of the bacterial **Ppnp family**.

Evidence includes:

- A Pfam **Ppnp domain** spanning residues 11–102.
- 60–65% sequence identity over essentially the full length to reviewed bacterial nucleoside phosphorylases.
- Homolog annotations corresponding to **EC 2.4.2.1** and **EC 2.4.2.2**.

## Catalytic reaction

The enzyme accelerates reversible **phosphorolysis of nucleosides**:

\[
\text{nucleoside} + \text{inorganic phosphate}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{free nucleobase} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

Representative reactions include:

\[
\text{adenosine} + P_i
\rightleftharpoons
\text{adenine} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

and

\[
\text{guanosine} + P_i
\rightleftharpoons
\text{guanine} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

The homologs are reported to accept a broad range of substrates, including **adenosine, guanosine, inosine, uridine, cytidine, thymidine, and xanthosine**, although the exact substrate spectrum of this particular sequence has not been experimentally established.

**Summary:** This is most likely a small bacterial nucleoside phosphorylase that participates in nucleotide salvage by cleaving the N-glycosidic bond of nucleosides using inorganic phosphate, producing a free base and ribose-1-phosphate.
