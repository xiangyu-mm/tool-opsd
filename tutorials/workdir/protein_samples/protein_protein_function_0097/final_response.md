### Functional identification

The 289-aa protein is the **gamma subunit of bacterial F-type ATP synthase**.

This assignment is very strong: the sequence is an **exact match** to reviewed UniProt entry **A5EXJ6** from *Dichelobacter nodosus*, with complete sequence coverage, and contains the characteristic **ATP-synt domain (PF00231)** across nearly its entire length.

### Functional role

The gamma subunit is a central component of the soluble **F₁ sector** of ATP synthase. It forms part of the central rotary shaft within the α₃β₃ catalytic ring and:

- Couples rotation driven by proton translocation through the membrane F₀ sector to conformational changes in the catalytic α/β subunits.
- Helps regulate rotational ATPase activity and proton-coupled ATP synthesis.
- Supports production of ATP from ADP and phosphate using the proton-motive force.

It is **not the primary catalytic subunit**; ATP synthesis chemistry occurs mainly at the β subunits. The gamma subunit instead has a structural and mechanochemical/regulatory role.

### Cellular processes

The protein is involved in:

- **Proton motive force-driven ATP synthesis**
- **Rotational ATP synthase activity**
- Assembly and operation of the **F-type proton-transporting ATP synthase complex**
- Coupling of bacterial respiratory/electrochemical energy conservation to cellular ATP production

The F₁ complex is generally organized as **α₃β₃γδε**, with one gamma subunit per complex.

### Subcellular location

It is associated with the **bacterial plasma/inner membrane**, because the F₁ catalytic sector is attached to the membrane-embedded F₀ proton channel. The gamma subunit itself is annotated as a **peripheral membrane-associated protein**, rather than an integral transmembrane protein.

### Summary

This protein is the **central rotary-shaft gamma subunit of bacterial F-type ATP synthase**. It participates in proton-gradient-dependent ATP production by mechanically coupling the membrane F₀ motor to the F₁ catalytic head. It is a peripheral component of the inner-membrane ATP synthase complex and is expected to function primarily in structural rotation and regulation, not as an independent metabolic enzyme.
