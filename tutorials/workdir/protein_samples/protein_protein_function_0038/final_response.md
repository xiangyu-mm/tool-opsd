This 228-residue protein is most likely a **Ham1p-like dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase** (**EC 3.6.1.66**), a cytosolic nucleotide-“sanitizing” enzyme.

Strong supporting evidence includes:

## Likely catalytic activity

The enzyme probably hydrolyzes noncanonical purine nucleoside triphosphates:

\[
\text{dITP} + H_2O \rightarrow \text{dIMP} + PP_i
\]

\[
\text{ITP} + H_2O \rightarrow \text{IMP} + PP_i
\]

\[
\text{XTP} + H_2O \rightarrow \text{XMP} + PP_i

The characterized homologs are **Mg²⁺-dependent** and commonly form **homodimers**. Exact substrate preference cannot be established from sequence alone, but dITP, ITP, and XTP are the most plausible substrates.

## Biological process

The protein likely participates in **purine nucleotide-pool sanitization**. It removes abnormal inosine- and xanthosine-containing triphosphates before they are incorporated into DNA or RNA, thereby reducing nucleotide misincorporation, genome damage, and possible mutation.

**Functional summary:**  
*A soluble Ham1p-family Mg²⁺-dependent pyrophosphatase that eliminates noncanonical purine nucleotides—especially dITP, ITP, and XTP—from the intracellular nucleotide pool, contributing to nucleic-acid fidelity and genome maintenance.*
