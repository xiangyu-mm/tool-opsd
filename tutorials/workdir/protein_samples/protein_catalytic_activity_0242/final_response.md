## Catalyzed reaction

\[
\text{L-arginine} + \mathrm{H_2O}
\rightarrow
\text{L-citrulline} + \mathrm{NH_4^+}
\]

Thus, the enzyme hydrolytically removes the **guanidino imino group** of L-arginine and converts the substrate into **L-citrulline**, releasing ammonium.

## Catalytic mechanism

Arginine deiminase uses its conserved cysteine residue, corresponding here to **Cys392**, as a catalytic nucleophile:

1. Cys392 attacks the guanidino carbon of L-arginine.
2. A covalent **S-amidino-cysteine intermediate** forms.
3. Ammonia/ammonium is released.
4. Hydrolysis of the intermediate produces L-citrulline and regenerates the cysteine.

The reaction does not require a separately identified metal or organic cofactor in the retrieved annotation.

## Biological role

This enzyme catalyzes the first step of the **arginine deiminase pathway**, a cytoplasmic arginine-catabolic pathway:

\[
\text{Arginine} \rightarrow \text{Citrulline} \rightarrow \text{Ornithine} + \text{carbamoyl phosphate}
\]

It therefore contributes to arginine degradation and can support energy generation or nitrogen metabolism in bacteria.
