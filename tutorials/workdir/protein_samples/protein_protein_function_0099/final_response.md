### Functional summary

This 206-amino-acid sequence is **very likely a Rab-29 (RAB29) small GTPase**. The identification is supported by strong similarity to reviewed mammalian RAB29 proteins: about **73% identity** across roughly **98% of the sequence**, with a highly significant E-value. The sequence contains the conserved Ras-like nucleotide-binding region and switch regions characteristic of small GTPases.

**Likely function:** RAB29 acts as a molecular switch in intracellular membrane trafficking. Like other Rab proteins, it cycles between GDP-bound and GTP-bound states; the active form can recruit downstream trafficking effectors. RAB29 is implicated in maintaining endosome–trans-Golgi network organization and in retromer-dependent retrograde transport of cargo, including mannose-6-phosphate receptor, from endosomes/lysosomes toward the Golgi. It can also recruit LRRK2 to the Golgi and stimulate LRRK2 kinase activity; reported downstream effects include promoting LRRK2-dependent RAB10 phosphorylation. These specific roles are based on the close RAB29 homolog annotations and should be regarded as likely for this sequence rather than independently demonstrated for the submitted protein.

**Likely localization:** Primarily associated with the **Golgi apparatus and trans-Golgi network**, including Golgi-derived tubules, with reported presence at endosomal membranes and other cytoplasmic membrane compartments. Its association with membranes is consistent with Rab-family lipid anchoring. The sequence ends in `GCC`, but this alone does not establish the exact lipid modification or processing state.

**Biological processes it may participate in:**
- Intracellular vesicle formation, transport, tethering, and fusion
- Endosome–Golgi organization and retrograde trafficking
- Retromer-mediated recycling of cargo such as mannose-6-phosphate receptor
- Recruitment/regulation of LRRK2 and related signaling, including RAB10 phosphorylation
- Potential regulation of neuronal process morphology

**Evidence and caveats:** The reference annotations identify this sequence as RAB29 with strong homology support. The inferred molecular mass is approximately **23.2 kDa**. Exact localization, partners, and pathway roles can depend on cell type and experimental context; the analysis does not experimentally establish activity, nucleotide state, or specific trafficking cargo for this sequence.
