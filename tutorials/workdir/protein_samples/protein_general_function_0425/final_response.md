## Functional overview

This 768-amino-acid sequence is strongly identified as **LysX**, a bifunctional enzyme involved in bacterial lysyl-phosphatidylglycerol (LPG) synthesis. The closest reviewed reference match is *Mycolicibacterium vanbaalenii* LysX (UniProt A1TAC7), with **85.7% sequence identity and full query coverage**.

LysX is thought to carry out two linked reactions:

1. **Lysyl-tRNA formation:** attaches lysine to tRNA\(^\text{Lys}\), using ATP.
2. **LPG synthesis:** transfers lysine from lysyl-tRNA to membrane phosphatidylglycerol.

LPG modification gives the membrane a more positive charge and is associated with resistance to cationic antimicrobial peptides. The protein has predicted membrane-associated regions and Pfam matches to tRNA-synthetase and LPG-synthesis domains, consistent with this two-part role. Magnesium is annotated as a cofactor for reference LysX proteins.

**Sequence attributes:** 768 residues; estimated molecular mass **83.4 kDa**; theoretical pI **6.25**.

**Confidence and caveat:** The LysX assignment is strongly supported by sequence similarity and domain matches. The specific functional and resistance annotations are transferred from homologs, however; this analysis does not experimentally establish the activity or antimicrobial-peptide resistance phenotype of this exact protein.
