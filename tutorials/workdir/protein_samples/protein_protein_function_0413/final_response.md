### Functional summary

This 90-residue protein is best identified as a **secreted cone-snail conotoxin precursor**, specifically an exact sequence match to **Conotoxin Im6.2 from *Conus imperialis*** (UniProt **A0A125S9F2**).

#### Evidence

- **N-terminal signal peptide:** residues 1–18, indicating secretion through the secretory pathway.
- **Propeptide region:** approximately residues 19–29, typical of venom-peptide precursors.
- **Mature cysteine-rich peptide:** approximately residues 30–89, containing six cysteines that can form **three disulfide bonds**.
- **Curated annotation:** “probable neurotoxin.”
- **Localization:** extracellular/secreted.
- **Post-translational processing:** the reference annotation reports C-terminal modification, including amidation of a terminal glutamic-acid residue.
- **Related conotoxin homologs** are annotated with ion-channel inhibitor activity, supporting a likely neuroactive pharmacological role.

#### Likely function

The processed mature peptide is likely a **venom neuropeptide that modulates neuronal ion channels or related excitability targets**. It may act as an ion-channel blocker, inhibitor, or modulator, as is common for disulfide-stabilized conotoxins.

The precursor itself is not expected to function as a conventional intracellular protein, cytokine, growth factor, or enzyme. Its biological activity should reside in the **proteolytically released, disulfide-bonded mature toxin**.

#### Sequence-derived properties

- Length: **90 aa**
- Predicted precursor mass: **~10.5 kDa**
- Theoretical pI: **8.88**
- Cysteine-rich mature region, consistent with a compact venom-peptide structure

**Confidence: very high for conotoxin precursor identity**, because the sequence exactly matches the curated reference sequence. The precise ion-channel target and potency cannot be established from sequence alone.
