## Molecular function

ERCC1 is a **non-catalytic DNA-repair subunit**. It forms a heterodimer with **ERCC4/XPF**, which supplies the structure-specific nuclease activity. ERCC1 helps position and stabilize the complex on DNA and contributes to recognition and processing of DNA structures requiring incision.

Thus, the sequence is **not expected to encode an independent nuclease**, and no standalone catalytic reaction was retrieved for it.

## Cellular processes

The protein is implicated in:

- **DNA repair**, especially processing of damaged or branched DNA structures
- **Interstrand cross-link repair**, together with ERCC4/XPF and SLX4
- Processing of **anaphase-bridge-generating DNA lesions**
- **Homology-directed repair** of DNA double-strand breaks
- Binding or processing of damaged and single-stranded DNA structures
- Maintenance of chromosome and telomere-associated DNA integrity

ERCC1 dysfunction would therefore be expected to compromise genome stability and the resolution of certain DNA repair intermediates.

## Subcellular localization

The primary predicted localization is the **nucleus**, particularly the **nucleoplasm**, consistent with its role in DNA repair. Some curated annotations also report **cytoplasmic presence**, but the nuclear localization is the most functionally relevant and best-supported assignment.

The sequence lacks features suggestive of a secreted or membrane protein, supporting a soluble intracellular role.

## Concise summary

This 298-residue protein is strongly identified as **ERCC1**, a nuclear, non-catalytic component of the **ERCC1–ERCC4/XPF structure-specific DNA repair complex**. It acts as a DNA-binding and nuclease-partner subunit rather than as an enzyme itself, supporting interstrand cross-link repair, processing of abnormal DNA structures, homologous repair, and genome maintenance.
