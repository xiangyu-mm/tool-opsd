## Functional identification

The sequence is a **cysteine-type deubiquitinase (DUB)**, specifically a **ubiquitin-specific protease/ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase**.

The identification is highly confident because the sequence is an **exact full-length match** to reviewed UniProt protein **O96612, ubiquitin hydrolase B**, from *Dictyostelium discoideum*. It contains a near-full-length **USP/UCH catalytic domain** (Pfam PF00443; approximately residues 19–447).

## Catalytic reaction

The enzyme hydrolyzes the bond linking the C-terminal glycine of ubiquitin to a substrate:

\[
\text{Protein–Lys}^{\varepsilon}\text{–CO–NH–Gly}_{76}\text{–Ub} + H_2O
\rightarrow
\text{Protein–Lys}^{\varepsilon}\text{–NH}_2 + \text{Ub–COOH}
\]

In words, it removes ubiquitin from ubiquitinated proteins by cleaving the **C-terminal Gly76–substrate lysine isopeptide bond**. It can also process ubiquitin precursors and polyubiquitin chains by hydrolyzing analogous ubiquitin C-terminal peptide/isopeptide bonds.

**EC classification:** **EC 3.4.19.12**, ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase activity.

## Catalytic mechanism

The USP domain uses a catalytic cysteine nucleophile. The sequence annotation identifies:

- **Cys399** — catalytic nucleophile  
- **Residue 408** — proton-accepting catalytic residue  

The catalytic cysteine attacks the ubiquitin–substrate carbonyl, forming a thioester intermediate that is subsequently hydrolyzed to release free ubiquitin and the deubiquitinated substrate.

## Summary

This protein is **not a protein kinase or ATP-dependent enzyme**. It is a **USP-family cysteine protease that reverses ubiquitination**, thereby regulating the stability and turnover of ubiquitinated proteins.
