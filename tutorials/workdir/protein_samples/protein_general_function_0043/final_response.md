## Functional summary

The 578-residue protein is **Praja-1 (PJA1)**, an **E2-dependent RING-type E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase**. Its sequence is an exact match to reviewed mouse UniProt **O55176**.

### Key properties

- **Length:** 578 aa  
- **Calculated molecular mass:** ~63.9 kDa  
- **Theoretical pI:** 4.88  
- **Net charge at pH 7:** approximately −33  
- **Hydropathy:** strongly hydrophilic, GRAVY −0.895  
- **Predicted stability:** high instability index (~70.9), consistent with a regulatory protein containing extensive disordered regions  
- **Membrane topology:** no transmembrane segment is indicated  
- **Localization:** primarily cytoplasmic according to curated annotation  

### Domain architecture

- **Residues ~1–298:** largely disordered and low-complexity, enriched in acidic, basic, serine, and proline residues  
- **Residues ~332–397:** another disordered/low-complexity region  
- **Residues ~529–571:** conserved **RING-type zinc-finger domain**, detected by Pfam profiles **zf-RING_2** and **zf-C3HC4**  

The C-terminal RING domain is the key catalytic/recruitment module expected for E3 ubiquitin ligases. The extensive disordered N-terminal and central regions likely mediate regulatory interactions, substrate recognition, and post-translational control, although individual interaction motifs cannot be assigned confidently from sequence alone.

### Function

Curated annotation supports that PJA1:

1. Binds E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, including **UBE2D2**.
2. Promotes ubiquitination of **MAGED1**.
3. Targets MAGED1 for subsequent **proteasomal degradation**.
4. May contribute to protein sorting and cytoplasmic protein turnover.

### Overall interpretation

This is a **cytoplasmic, soluble RING E3 ubiquitin ligase with extensive intrinsically disordered regulatory regions and a C-terminal RING domain**. The exact sequence match makes the PJA1/Praja-1 identification highly reliable. The sequence does not provide evidence for an enzymatic domain other than the RING-based ubiquitin-ligase module, nor for DNA/RNA-binding or transmembrane activity. Exact substrate specificity beyond the curated MAGED1 interaction should be treated as context-dependent.
