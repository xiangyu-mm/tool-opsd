## Functional summary

The 281-amino-acid protein is the **F-type ATP synthase gamma subunit**.

### Evidence

- **Exact sequence match:** 100% identical to reviewed UniProt protein **Q6F203** from *Mesoplasma florum*.
- **Domain:** ATP-synt domain (**PF00231**) spans nearly the entire sequence, residues 3–279.
- **Size:** approximately **31.4 kDa**.
- **Homologs:** closely related ATP synthase gamma chains occur in several *Mycoplasma/Mesoplasma* species.

### Functional role

The gamma subunit is a central shaft component of the **F₀F₁ proton-translocating ATP synthase**. It mechanically couples proton-driven rotation in the membrane-associated F₀ sector to conformational changes in the catalytic F₁ sector.

Its main roles are to:

- Participate in rotational energy transduction.
- Regulate or influence ATP synthase activity.
- Couple the proton-motive force to ATP production from ADP and phosphate.
- Help coordinate the rotational movement of the catalytic complex.

The gamma chain is **not itself the primary catalytic ATP-producing subunit**; ATP synthesis occurs mainly at the alpha/beta catalytic core, while gamma transmits rotational motion and contributes to regulation.

### Cellular processes

This protein is involved in:

- **Oxidative or chemiosmotic energy conservation**
- **Proton-motive-force-driven ATP synthesis**
- **Rotary catalysis by the ATP synthase complex**
- Cellular energy metabolism and maintenance of ATP levels

### Subcellular location

It is annotated as a **peripheral component of the bacterial plasma membrane ATP synthase complex**. The sequence does not contain an obvious transmembrane helix, consistent with membrane association through binding to the other ATP synthase subunits rather than direct membrane insertion.

### Overall conclusion

This is a highly conserved bacterial **ATP synthase gamma subunit**, functioning as the rotating central stalk that couples proton flow across the plasma membrane to ATP synthesis in the F₀F₁ ATP synthase complex. The assignment is strongly supported because the query sequence exactly matches a reviewed reference protein.
