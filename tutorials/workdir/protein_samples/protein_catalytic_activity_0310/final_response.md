## Functional identification

The 544-residue protein is most consistent with **CTP synthase** (**EC 6.3.4.2**), a bifunctional enzyme in de novo pyrimidine biosynthesis.

### Evidence

- It matches reviewed bacterial CTP synthases with approximately **66% sequence identity over 99.6% of the sequence**.
- It contains the characteristic two-domain architecture:
  - **N-terminal CTP_synth_N domain**: residues ~3–267
  - **C-terminal glutaminase/GATase domain**: residues ~304–524
- The predicted molecular mass is approximately **60.3 kDa**.
- Homologous enzymes are annotated as **cytosolic homotetramers**.

## Catalytic reaction

Using glutamine as the nitrogen donor, the overall reaction is:

\[
\text{UTP} + \text{L-glutamine} + \text{ATP} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
\rightarrow
\text{CTP} + \text{L-glutamate} + \text{ADP} + \text{P}_{i} + 2\text{H}^+
\]

In this reaction:

1. The C-terminal glutaminase domain hydrolyzes glutamine:

\[
\text{L-glutamine}+\text{H}_2\text{O}
\rightarrow
\text{L-glutamate}+\text{NH}_4^+
\]

2. The generated ammonia is transferred internally to the N-terminal synthetase domain.
3. The synthetase domain uses ATP to aminate UTP, producing **CTP**.

The enzyme can also use free ammonia directly:

\[
\text{UTP}+\text{NH}_4^+ + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{CTP}+\text{ADP}+\text{P}_{i}+2\text{H}^+
\]

## Functional summary

This protein is a **glutamine-dependent CTP synthase**, supplying CTP for RNA synthesis, phospholipid metabolism, and other pyrimidine-dependent processes. The assignment is strongly supported by near-full-length homology and the characteristic CTP-synthase/glutaminase domain organization, although the specific sequence itself was not experimentally assayed.
