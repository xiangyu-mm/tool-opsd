## Functional assessment

The sequence is most consistent with **UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate—meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase**, commonly called **MurE**.

### Evidence

- **Length:** 511 amino acids; typical of bacterial Mur ligases.
- **Homology:** 43.7–46.8% identity across approximately 92–96% of the sequence to reviewed MurE proteins.
- **Domains:** All three characteristic Mur ligase regions are detected:
  - **Mur_ligase**: residues ~30–108
  - **Mur_ligase_M**: residues ~121–327
  - **Mur_ligase_C**: residues ~350–474
- **Functional motif evidence:** Homologs contain the conserved meso-diaminopimelate-recognition region and a catalytically important carboxylated lysine.
- **Likely localization:** Cytosol.
- **Likely cofactor:** Mg²⁺, which supports ATP binding and phosphoryl-transfer chemistry.

## Catalytic activity

MurE catalyzes the ATP-dependent addition of **meso-2,6-diaminopimelate (meso-DAP)** to the γ-carboxyl group of the glutamate residue in the peptidoglycan precursor UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu.

### Chemical reaction

\[
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu}
+ \text{meso-DAP}
+ \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu-meso-DAP}
+ \text{ADP}
+ \text{P}_{i}
+ \text{H}^{+}
\]

More explicitly, the product is:

**UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-γ-D-glutamyl-meso-2,6-diaminopimelate**

This corresponds to **EC 6.3.2.13** and Rhea reaction **RHEA:23676**.

## Biological role

The product is a late cytoplasmic intermediate in **bacterial peptidoglycan biosynthesis**. It is subsequently incorporated into the stem peptide of the cell-wall precursor, contributing to bacterial cell shape, mechanical strength, and cell division.

## Confidence and limitation

The assignment is **strongly supported by domain architecture and high-coverage homology to curated MurE proteins**, but the exact activity has not been experimentally demonstrated for this particular sequence. In particular, some Mur ligases use alternative amino-acid substrates, such as L-lysine, depending on the organism. Here, the close matches and conserved DAP-recognition evidence favor **meso-DAP specificity**, making MurE the best-supported annotation.
