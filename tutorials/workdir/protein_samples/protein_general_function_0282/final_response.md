## Protein overview

This 432-amino-acid sequence is **strongly supported as a bacterial seryl-tRNA synthetase (SerRS; EC 6.1.1.11)**. The closest reviewed reference is *Hyphomonas neptunium* SerRS (UniProt Q0C0W2), with 80.8% sequence identity and full-length coverage.

- **Likely function:** Uses ATP to attach L-serine to tRNA(Ser), producing seryl-tRNA(Ser), a key step in protein synthesis.
- **Possible additional activity:** The close homolog is also annotated to serylate tRNA(Sec), which can feed selenocysteine biosynthesis. This activity is plausible for the query but is not directly demonstrated for it.
- **Domains:** Matches the Seryl_tRNA_N domain (PF02403; residues 1–111) and tRNA-synt_2b domain (PF00587; residues 232–408).
- **Likely cellular context:** Cytoplasmic; close homologs are annotated as homodimers. These assignments are transferred from homologs, not measured directly for this sequence.
- **Estimated sequence properties:** 47.9 kDa; theoretical pI 5.16.

Overall, the sequence and domain matches support a conserved, ATP-dependent aminoacyl-tRNA ligase. The strongest direct evidence here is sequence homology and domain architecture; localization, oligomeric state, and possible tRNA(Sec) activity remain homology-based predictions.
