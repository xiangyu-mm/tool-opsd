### Functional identification

The 454-residue protein is **trigger factor**, a bacterial ribosome-associated molecular chaperone and **peptidyl-prolyl cis–trans isomerase (PPIase)**.

The sequence exactly matches the reviewed *Corynebacterium efficiens* trigger factor protein Q8FN35, with closely related reviewed matches in *Corynebacterium glutamicum*. This supports the annotation with high confidence.

### Catalytic reaction

It catalyzes the reversible isomerization of peptide bonds preceding proline:

\[
\text{Protein–Xaa–Pro}_{\text{trans}}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{Protein–Xaa–Pro}_{\text{cis}}
\]

Equivalently, the reaction is represented as:

\[
\text{peptidylproline }(\omega=180^\circ)
\rightleftharpoons
\text{peptidylproline }(\omega=0^\circ)
\]

- **Substrate:** a protein or peptide containing a peptidyl-proline bond  
- **Product:** the same protein or peptide with that proline peptide bond in the alternative cis/trans configuration  
- **EC number:** **5.2.1.8**  
- **Rhea:** **RHEA:16237**

This is an **isomerization reaction**, so there is no net addition or removal of atoms and no separate small-molecule product.

### Biological role

In addition to its PPIase activity, trigger factor binds near the ribosomal exit tunnel and assists nascent-protein folding. It helps maintain newly synthesized proteins in folding-competent conformations and is involved in protein export.
