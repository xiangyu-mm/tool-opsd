## Functional identification

The 409-residue protein is most likely **RibBA**, a bifunctional enzyme in **riboflavin (vitamin B₂) biosynthesis**.

This assignment is strongly supported by:

- Two conserved domains:
  - **N-terminal DHBP synthase domain**: residues ~11–202
  - **C-terminal GTP cyclohydrolase II domain**: residues ~216–378
- ~60–62% identity over nearly the full length to reviewed RibBA proteins.
- Conserved annotations for both enzymatic activities in homologous proteins.

## Catalytic activities

### 1. 3,4-Dihydroxy-2-butanone-4-phosphate synthase  
**EC 4.1.99.12; Rhea RHEA:18457**

\[
\text{D-ribulose-5-phosphate}
\rightarrow
\text{3,4-dihydroxy-2-butanone-4-phosphate}
+
\text{formate}
\]

This is the N-terminal activity and produces the DHBP precursor used in the riboflavin pathway.

### 2. GTP cyclohydrolase II  
**EC 3.5.4.25; Rhea RHEA:23704**

\[
\text{GTP} + \text{H}_2\text{O}
\rightarrow
\text{2,5-diamino-6-ribosylamino-4(3H)-pyrimidinone 5′-phosphate}
+
\text{formate}
+
\text{pyrophosphate}
\]

This is the C-terminal activity and generates the second major precursor branch for riboflavin synthesis.

## Cofactors and localization

Curated RibBA homologs indicate binding of:

- **Two Mg²⁺ or Mn²⁺ ions**
- **One Zn²⁺ ion per subunit**

The protein is predicted to be a soluble, cytosolic enzyme. Its calculated molecular mass is approximately **44.9 kDa**.

## Summary

This sequence encodes a **bifunctional RibBA riboflavin-biosynthesis enzyme**, catalyzing both the DHBP synthase reaction from **D-ribulose-5-phosphate** and the GTP cyclohydrolase II reaction from **GTP**. The domain architecture and high-confidence homology support this assignment substantially more strongly than either activity would be inferred from sequence motifs alone.
