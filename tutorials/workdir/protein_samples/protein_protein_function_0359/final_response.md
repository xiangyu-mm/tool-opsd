## Functional prediction

**Most likely function:** **Aspartate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Asd)**  
**EC number:** **EC 1.2.1.11**  
**Likely localization:** Cytosolic  
**Likely oligomeric state:** Homodimer  
**Confidence:** High based on sequence homology

### Evidence

- The protein is **337 amino acids** long, consistent with bacterial Asd enzymes.
- It matches reviewed Asd proteins with:
  - **48.5–57.6% sequence identity**
  - Approximately **97% sequence coverage**
  - Extremely significant similarity, with E-values down to **~8 × 10⁻¹²⁹**
- Curated homologs from *Aquifex aeolicus*, *Bacillus subtilis*, and *Legionella pneumophila* all have the same annotated function.
- The sequence contains the expected conserved catalytic region, including the active-site cysteine region characteristic of Asd enzymes.

### Catalytic reaction

The physiological reaction is:

\[
\text{L-aspartyl-4-phosphate} + \text{NADPH} + H^+
\rightarrow
\text{L-aspartate-4-semialdehyde} + P_i + \text{NADP}^+
\]

The enzyme proceeds through a catalytic **acyl-thioester intermediate** and uses **NADPH** as the reducing cofactor.

### Biological process

The product, **L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde**, is a central intermediate in **aspartate-family amino-acid biosynthesis**. Therefore, this protein is likely involved in the biosynthesis of:

- **Lysine**, through the diaminopimelate pathway
- **Methionine**
- **Threonine**
- Indirectly, related aspartate-derived metabolites

It performs an early branch-point step after aspartate kinase/aspartyl-phosphate formation and before the pathways diverge toward lysine, methionine, and threonine.

### Concise summary

This sequence most likely encodes a **cytosolic NADPH-dependent aspartate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase**, a homodimeric metabolic enzyme that converts **L-aspartyl-4-phosphate into L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde**. It is consequently expected to participate in **de novo biosynthesis of lysine, methionine, and threonine**. This assignment is strongly supported by near-full-length similarity to multiple reviewed bacterial Asd proteins, although biochemical confirmation would be required to establish activity experimentally.
