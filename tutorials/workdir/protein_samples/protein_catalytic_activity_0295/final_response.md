## Functional identification

The sequence is most consistent with an **ELOVL6-like very-long-chain fatty acid elongase**, an **endoplasmic-reticulum multi-pass membrane enzyme**.

### Evidence

- **385 amino acids** and strongly hydrophobic, consistent with a multi-pass membrane protein.
- Contains the characteristic **ELO elongase domain**: Pfam **PF01151**.
- Closely matches reviewed ELOVL6 proteins:
  - 96.1% identity to chicken ELOVL6
  - 91.5% identity to human ELOVL6
  - 91.2% identity to mouse ELOVL6
- The functional annotations of these homologs assign **EC 2.3.1.199**, fatty-acid elongase activity.

## Catalytic reaction

The protein catalyzes the **first and rate-limiting condensation step** of the four-step microsomal fatty-acid elongation cycle:

\[
\text{long-chain acyl-CoA} + \text{malonyl-CoA} + H^+
\rightarrow
\text{3-oxoacyl-CoA} + CO_2 + CoA
\]

For a representative ELOVL6 substrate:

\[
\text{hexadecanoyl-CoA} + \text{malonyl-CoA} + H^+
\rightarrow
\text{3-oxooctadecanoyl-CoA} + CO_2 + CoA
\]

Thus, the enzyme adds **two carbons** to a fatty-acyl chain by using malonyl-CoA as the two-carbon donor. The resulting 3-oxoacyl-CoA is subsequently processed by the other elongation-cycle enzymes to produce the elongated fatty acyl-CoA.

## Likely substrate preference

By analogy with ELOVL6 homologs, it likely acts preferentially on **C12–C16 acyl-CoAs**, especially **palmitoyl-CoA (C16:0-CoA)**, and may generate elongated saturated or low-desaturation C18 and longer fatty acids. Exact substrate specificity is inferred from homologs and was not experimentally measured for the supplied sequence.

## Cofactor and localization

- **Localization:** endoplasmic-reticulum membrane  
- **Complex:** fatty-acid elongase complex  
- **Cofactor:** no separately required metal, flavin, NAD(P)H, or other prosthetic cofactor is reported for this condensation reaction  
- **Substrate/co-substrate:** malonyl-CoA, not a catalytic cofactor

### Summary

This sequence encodes a highly likely **ELOVL6-family fatty-acid elongase**. Its enzymatic activity is the **malonyl-CoA-dependent condensation of long-chain acyl-CoAs**, producing **3-oxoacyl-CoAs while releasing CO₂ and CoA**, thereby initiating two-carbon extension of fatty acids in the ER. The assignment is strongly supported by domain architecture and close reviewed homologs, although it remains a sequence-based annotation rather than a direct biochemical assay of this exact protein.
