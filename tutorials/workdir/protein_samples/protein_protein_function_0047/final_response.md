## Functional identification

The sequence is **HIV-1 envelope glycoprotein precursor Env (gp160)**, with strong support:

- Length: **768 aa**
- ~**74% identity** to reviewed HIV-1 group M Env gp160 proteins
- Complete query coverage in homolog searches
- Pfam domains:
  - **GP120 domain:** residues ~36–500
  - **GP41 domain:** residues ~519–709
- Architecture matches a viral type-I membrane envelope protein.

### Processing and structure

Env gp160 is synthesized as a glycoprotein precursor in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi. It is cleaved by a host furin-like protease into:

1. **gp120**, the external receptor-binding subunit
2. **gp41**, the membrane-anchored fusion subunit

The mature Env complex is generally a **trimer of gp120–gp41 heterodimers**.

## Function

### gp120

gp120 mediates viral attachment by binding:

- **CD4**, the primary HIV receptor
- A chemokine coreceptor, usually **CCR5 or CXCR4**, after CD4-induced conformational rearrangement

The sequence contains the cysteine-rich, glycosylated extracellular architecture characteristic of HIV-1 gp120.

### gp41

gp41 contains:

- The fusion peptide
- Heptad-repeat/coiled-coil fusion machinery
- A single transmembrane helix
- A cytoplasmic tail

It functions as a **class-I viral membrane-fusion protein**, driving merger of the viral membrane with a host-cell membrane after receptor engagement.

## Subcellular and virion localization

The protein is expected to localize to:

- The **HIV virion envelope**
- The host-cell plasma membrane during viral assembly and budding
- Host endosomal membranes during entry

Its biosynthetic route is:

**endoplasmic reticulum → Golgi → furin cleavage → virion membrane incorporation**

The sequence has a large extracellular/luminal region followed by a C-terminal membrane anchor and cytoplasmic tail, consistent with this topology.

## Biological processes

The protein participates in:

- Virion attachment to host cells
- CD4 and coreceptor-dependent HIV entry
- Clathrin-dependent uptake of virions
- Fusion of the viral envelope with host plasma or endosomal membranes
- Viral protein processing
- Formation of trimeric Env spikes
- Delivery of the viral core into the host cytoplasm
- Interactions with dendritic-cell lectins such as DC-SIGN, which can facilitate viral capture and transmission

## Summary

**This is HIV-1 Env gp160, a trimeric viral envelope glycoprotein precursor. Host proteolysis produces gp120, which binds CD4 and CCR5/CXCR4, and gp41, which anchors the complex in the viral membrane and mediates membrane fusion. It is synthesized through the ER/Golgi pathway and functions primarily in HIV attachment, entry, and envelope–host membrane fusion.**

Confidence: **very high**, based on strong sequence homology, matching GP120/GP41 domains, and characteristic Env architecture.
