## Functional identification

The 142-residue protein is **peptide methionine sulfoxide reductase B (MsrB; SelR family)**.

This identification is highly confident because the sequence is an **exact match** to reviewed *Staphylococcus aureus* MsrB proteins, including UniProt entries **Q2FH15, P0A088, and A6QGX4**. The sequence also contains the characteristic **SelR/MsrB domain (PF01641; residues 9–124)**.

### Molecular function

MsrB reduces **peptide-bound methionine-R-sulfoxide** back to methionine. It therefore helps reverse oxidative damage to proteins:

\[
\text{peptide-Met-(R)-S(O)} + \text{reducing equivalents}
\rightarrow
\text{peptide-Met} + \text{water}
\]

This is distinct from MsrA, which acts on the opposite methionine-sulfoxide stereoisomer.

### Subcellular localization

- **Cytoplasm**
- The sequence is soluble and lacks features expected for secretion or membrane insertion:
  - no obvious signal peptide
  - no transmembrane segment
  - strongly hydrophilic overall

### Biological processes

The protein is involved in:

- **Protein repair**
- **Repair of oxidatively damaged methionine residues**
- **Response to oxidative stress**
- Protection of cellular proteins from damage caused by reactive oxygen species

### Summary

This is a small, acidic, soluble cytoplasmic **MsrB repair enzyme** from the SelR family. It protects proteins during oxidative stress by stereospecifically reducing peptide methionine-R-sulfoxide to methionine.
