### Functional identification

The sequence is most consistent with an **acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC) carboxyltransferase component**, annotated by close homologs as a fused **carboxyltransferase β/α subunit** (EC **2.1.3.15**).

It likely catalyzes the transfer of a carboxyl group from carboxylated biotin on the biotin-carrier protein to acetyl-CoA:

**N⁶-carboxybiotinyl-L-lysyl-[protein] + acetyl-CoA → N⁶-biotinyl-L-lysyl-[protein] + malonyl-CoA**

In other words, it produces **malonyl-CoA**, an important building block for fatty-acid biosynthesis. The carboxyl group is first added to biotin by the biotin-carboxylase component of ACC; this enzyme component performs the subsequent transfer to acetyl-CoA.

### Evidence and confidence

- The sequence is 587 amino acids and has close, reviewed Swiss-Prot homologs with **52.6–58.1% identity** and near-complete sequence coverage.
- Its predicted domains include **Zn_ribbon_ACC**, **Carboxyl_trans**, and **ACCA**, consistent with ACC carboxyltransferase architecture.
- Close homologs are annotated as zinc-binding ACC carboxyltransferase components and associated with malonyl-CoA biosynthesis.

This is a **strong sequence-based assignment**, but the specific sequence has not been experimentally validated in the results reviewed here; the catalytic assignment is transferred from homolog annotations.
