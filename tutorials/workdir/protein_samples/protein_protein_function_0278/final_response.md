## Functional identification

The 465-amino-acid protein is **argininosuccinate lyase (ASL)**, EC **4.3.2.1**.

This assignment is highly confident: the sequence is an exact 100%-identity, full-length match to the reviewed UniProt protein **Q3SUR0** from *Nitrobacter winogradskyi*. Strong homologs from other bacteria have the same annotation.

## Catalytic activity

ASL catalyzes the reversible cleavage of argininosuccinate:

**L-argininosuccinate ⇌ L-arginine + fumarate**

- **EC:** 4.3.2.1  
- **Rhea:** RHEA:24020  
- Reaction type: carbon–nitrogen lyase/elimination reaction  
- No metal ion or other obligatory cofactor was identified in the retrieved annotations.

## Biological processes

The most likely role is the final step of **bacterial L-arginine biosynthesis**:

1. Ornithine is converted toward citrulline/argininosuccinate.
2. Argininosuccinate lyase produces **L-arginine** and **fumarate**.

Thus, the protein contributes to:

- L-arginine biosynthesis
- Amino-acid biosynthesis
- Argininosuccinate metabolism
- Production of fumarate, which can enter central metabolic pathways

The available evidence supports this biosynthetic role rather than a specialized signaling or regulatory function.

## Summary

This protein is a **cytosolic bacterial argininosuccinate lyase**, a soluble enzyme that catalyzes the formation of **L-arginine and fumarate from argininosuccinate**. It most likely functions in the terminal step of the bacterial **arginine biosynthetic pathway**. The functional assignment is supported by an exact curated sequence match, conserved ASL domains, and consistent UniProt, EC, Rhea, GO, and localization annotations.
