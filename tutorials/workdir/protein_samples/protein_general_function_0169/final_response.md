### Functional summary

This 448-amino-acid protein is **MMM1/Mdm1 (maintenance of mitochondrial morphology protein 1)**, an essential component of the **ERMES/MDM complex** in fungi. The sequence is an exact match to reviewed *Debaryomyces hansenii* UniProt protein **Q6BH42**, providing very strong identification confidence.

### Key characteristics

- **Length:** 448 aa  
- **Estimated molecular mass:** ~50.2 kDa  
- **Theoretical pI:** 5.26; predicted acidic overall charge at neutral pH  
- **Topology:** Single-pass **type I ER membrane protein**
  - N-terminal lumenal region: residues 1–74
  - Transmembrane helix: approximately residues 75–95
  - Large C-terminal cytoplasmic region: residues 96–448
- **Domain:** MMM1 domain, Pfam **PF10296**, approximately residues 149–428; this region corresponds to the conserved lipid-transfer-associated MMM1/SMP-LTD region.
- **Sequence architecture:** Contains low-complexity and predicted disordered segments, particularly around residues ~119–144 and ~303–357.

### Molecular and cellular function

MMM1 is a structural component of the **ER–mitochondria encounter structure (ERMES)**. It helps:

1. **Tether the endoplasmic reticulum to mitochondria**
2. Support **nonvesicular phospholipid transfer** between the two organelles
3. Maintain **mitochondrial morphology and organization**
4. Contribute to **mitochondrial DNA nucleoid maintenance**
5. Participate in the assembly/insertion pathway for mitochondrial outer-membrane β-barrel proteins, including TOM40-related machinery

MMM1 forms a **homodimer** and associates with **Mdm12, Mdm34, and Mdm10**. The MMM1–Mdm12 subcomplex contributes to a continuous hydrophobic tunnel proposed to facilitate lipid transfer.

### Overall interpretation

The sequence is not consistent with a soluble enzyme. Its distinctive internal transmembrane helix, conserved C-terminal MMM1 domain, and exact match to a reviewed MMM1 protein strongly support the annotation:

> **ER membrane-resident ERMES tether and lipid-transfer scaffold required for ER–mitochondria contact, mitochondrial morphology, and mitochondrial membrane-protein biogenesis.**

Evidence is based on exact sequence matching to UniProt Q6BH42, reviewed homolog annotations, and Pfam domain detection.
