## Functional identification

The 221-residue protein is **PurQ**, the glutaminase subunit of **phosphoribosylformylglycinamidine synthase** in de novo purine biosynthesis.

Evidence is very strong: the sequence is an **exact 100% match** to a reviewed *Deinococcus radiodurans* PurQ protein, contains type-I glutamine amidotransferase domains (**GATase_5** and **GATase_3**), and has the conserved catalytic nucleophile at approximately **Cys87**.

## Direct catalytic reaction

PurQ hydrolyzes glutamine to generate ammonia:

\[
\mathrm{L\!-\!glutamine + H_2O \rightarrow L\!-\!glutamate + NH_4^+}
\]

This is the protein’s direct **glutaminase activity**.

## Role in the overall reaction

PurQ functions with the PurL and PurS subunits in the FGAM synthase complex. The ammonia produced from glutamine is transferred to formylglycinamide ribonucleotide (FGAR), producing formylglycinamidine ribonucleotide (FGAM):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{FGAR + L\!-\!glutamine + ATP + H_2O}\\
\mathrm{\rightarrow FGAM + L\!-\!glutamate + ADP + P_i + H^+}
\end{aligned}
\]

More explicitly, the substrate and product are:

- **FGAR:** N²-formyl-N¹-(5-phospho-β-D-ribosyl)glycinamide  
- **FGAM:** 2-formamido-N¹-(5-O-phospho-β-D-ribosyl)acetamidine

PurQ performs the glutamine-hydrolysis half-reaction, whereas **PurL** uses the resulting ammonia and ATP to convert FGAR to FGAM. **PurS** assists ammonia transfer within the complex.

### Summary

**Protein:** PurQ, phosphoribosylformylglycinamidine synthase glutaminase subunit  
**Enzyme class:** Type-I glutamine amidotransferase  
**Direct activity:** Glutamine hydrolysis to glutamate and ammonia  
**Biological role:** Supplies ammonia for FGAM formation during de novo IMP/purine biosynthesis
