### Functional identification

This 378-aa protein is most consistent with **RlmN, the dual-specificity radical-SAM RNA methyltransferase** (**EC 2.1.1.192**).

Evidence includes:

- Strong similarity to reviewed RlmN proteins: approximately **47–48% identity over ~88% of the sequence**.
- An N-terminal **RlmN_N domain**.
- A central **Radical_SAM domain**.
- A conserved cysteine cluster resembling the radical-SAM **CxxxCxxC** motif, supporting binding of a **[4Fe–4S] cluster**.
- Overall size and architecture typical of bacterial RlmN proteins.

### Catalytic reaction

RlmN catalyzes C2 methylation of specific adenosines in RNA:

\[
\text{Adenosine in RNA} + \text{SAM} \rightarrow \text{2-methyladenosine in RNA}
\]

The principal substrates are:

1. **A2503 in 23S rRNA**, producing **m²A2503**.
2. **A37 in tRNA**, producing **m²A37**.

A more complete radical-SAM reaction representation is:

\[
\text{RNA-A} + 2\,\text{SAM} + \text{reducing equivalents}
\rightarrow
\text{RNA-m²A} + 5'\text{-deoxyadenosine} + \text{methionine} + \text{SAH}
\]

The enzyme uses one SAM molecule to methylate an active-site cysteine, generating a **methylated cysteine intermediate**. A second SAM molecule is reductively cleaved by the [4Fe–4S] radical-SAM center to generate the radical chemistry that transfers the methyl group from the cysteine intermediate to C2 of the RNA adenine.

### Biological role

The **m²A2503 modification** helps optimize decoding and proofreading at the ribosomal peptidyl-transferase center. The tRNA modification at A37 is associated with accurate translation.

**Conclusion:** The sequence strongly supports a bacterial **RlmN dual-specificity RNA methyltransferase**, catalyzing radical-SAM-dependent C2 methylation of 23S rRNA and tRNA adenosines. The precise substrate assignment for this particular sequence remains a homology-based inference rather than a direct experimental demonstration.
