## Functional identification

The sequence is **growth hormone secretagogue receptor type 1 (GHSR1)**, most likely the **mouse receptor**:

- **UniProt:** Q99P50, *Mus musculus*
- **Length:** 364 amino acids
- **Sequence match:** 100% identity and 100% coverage to the reviewed mouse protein
- **Human homolog:** GHSR, UniProt Q92847, 95.4% identity

## Protein family and architecture

This is a **class A seven-transmembrane G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR)**. The sequence contains the expected seven hydrophobic transmembrane helices, supported by Pfam domains:

- **7tm_1 GPCR domain:** residues approximately 59–321
- Additional 7TM GPCR family hits spanning most of the membrane-protein region

The short N-terminal extracellular region and intracellular C-terminal tail are also consistent with a peptide-hormone GPCR.

## Molecular function

GHSR1 is the receptor for **ghrelin**, an octanoylated peptide hormone. Upon ghrelin binding, it:

1. Undergoes a conformational change.
2. Couples primarily to **Gq/11-type heterotrimeric G proteins**.
3. Activates downstream phospholipase C signaling.
4. Promotes production of **inositol trisphosphate (IP3)** and **diacylglycerol (DAG)**.

It can also bind other growth-hormone secretagogues and synthetic agonists.

## Subcellular localization

The protein is expected to localize to the:

- **Plasma membrane**
- **Cell surface**
- **Postsynaptic and synaptic membranes in neuronal contexts**
- Potentially membrane-raft domains

It is an integral **multipass membrane protein** with seven membrane-spanning helices.

## Biological processes

GHSR1 participates in:

- **Appetite regulation**
- **Stimulation of growth hormone secretion**
- **Ghrelin-mediated G-protein signaling**
- **Phospholipase C/DAG/IP3 signaling**
- **Neuronal signaling and synaptic plasticity**

In neurons, GHSR1 can form heteromeric complexes with dopamine receptors such as DRD1 and DRD2, influencing synaptic signaling and processes related to memory and learning.

## Summary

This sequence encodes a **ghrelin-responsive seven-transmembrane GPCR** located at the plasma membrane. Its primary role is to translate ghrelin binding into **Gq-dependent phospholipase C signaling**, contributing especially to **appetite control and growth hormone release**, with additional roles in neuronal and synaptic signaling.
