## Functional role

The protein is expected to be a **heme-dependent oxidative enzyme**. Cytochrome P450 reductase supplies electrons from NADPH, allowing the enzyme to activate molecular oxygen:

\[
\text{substrate} + O_2 + NADPH \rightarrow \text{oxygenated substrate} + H_2O + NADP^+
\]

CYP1B1-like enzymes are associated with oxidation of:

- **Steroid hormones**, including estrogen, testosterone, and progesterone
- **Fatty acids and arachidonic acid**, including formation of epoxyeicosatrienoic acid lipid mediators
- **Retinoids**, potentially contributing to retinoic-acid production
- Certain **xenobiotics and environmental procarcinogens**

Human CYP1B1 is particularly associated with hydroxylation of estradiol and estrone, including formation of 2- and 4-hydroxylated metabolites. However, the exact substrate profile of this sequence cannot be established from sequence similarity alone.

## Subcellular location

The N-terminal hydrophobic segment is consistent with a **microsomal membrane anchor**. The most likely localization is therefore:

- **Endoplasmic-reticulum membrane**, with the catalytic domain exposed toward the cytosolic side
- Possible association with microsomal membranes and interaction with **NADPH–cytochrome P450 reductase**
- Mitochondrial targeting has been reported for some CYP1B1 proteins under particular conditions, but mitochondrial localization should be considered **possible rather than firmly established** for this sequence

The N-terminal hydrophobic region is more consistent with membrane anchoring than with a classical cleavable secretory signal peptide, because the remainder of the protein has the architecture of a cytosolic-facing P450 catalytic domain.

This sequence encodes a **CYP1B1-family microsomal cytochrome P450 monooxygenase**. It is likely a membrane-anchored, heme-dependent enzyme that uses NADPH-derived electrons to oxidize endogenous lipids, steroid hormones, retinoids, and possibly xenobiotics.
