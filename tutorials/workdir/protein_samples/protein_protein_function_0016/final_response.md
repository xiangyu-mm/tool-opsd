## Functional role

IAPP is a **secreted glucoregulatory peptide hormone** involved in energy homeostasis. It functions as a ligand for **amylin receptors**, which are complexes of the calcitonin receptor (**CALCR**) with receptor activity-modifying proteins (**RAMPs**).

Reported roles include:

- Regulation of energy and glucose homeostasis.
- Inhibition of insulin-stimulated glucose utilization and glycogen deposition in muscle.
- Activation of amylin receptor signaling.
- Positive regulation of calcium-mediated signaling.
- Positive regulation of cAMP/PKA signaling.

## Cellular processes

The protein participates primarily in **extracellular peptide-hormone signaling**. Its activity is associated with:

- Receptor-ligand and signaling-receptor binding.
- Systemic metabolic regulation.
- Potential signaling effects in neuronal cell bodies, as reflected by the retrieved GO annotations.

## Subcellular location and processing

The protein is predicted and annotated as **secreted**:

1. The N-terminal hydrophobic region directs entry into the endoplasmic-reticulum/secretory pathway.
2. The precursor is processed proteolytically to generate mature amylin.
3. Mature IAPP is released into the **extracellular space**, where it engages CALCR–RAMP receptor complexes.

Thus, this sequence is not a membrane protein; it is a **secreted peptide-hormone precursor** whose mature product acts extracellularly.

**Overall summary:** This sequence encodes prepro-islet amyloid polypeptide/amylin, a secreted endocrine peptide that regulates glucose and energy homeostasis through CALCR–RAMP amylin receptors and downstream calcium- and cAMP/PKA-linked signaling.
