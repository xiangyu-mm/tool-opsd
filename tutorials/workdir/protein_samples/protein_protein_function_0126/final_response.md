## Functional summary

This 483-amino-acid protein is most likely **MurE (UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate—meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase)**, an ATP-dependent enzyme in bacterial peptidoglycan biosynthesis.

### Evidence

- Length: **483 aa**
- Predicted molecular mass: **~51.0 kDa**
- Strong similarity to reviewed MurE proteins:
  - **61.7% identity over 98.1% coverage** to *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* MurE Q59650
  - Additional reviewed homologs show approximately **56–60% identity** with near-complete coverage
- Contains the characteristic three-domain Mur ligase architecture:
  - **Mur_ligase domain:** residues ~18–92
  - **Mur_ligase_M domain:** residues ~105–302
  - **Mur_ligase_C domain:** residues ~325–451

## Likely enzymatic reaction

MurE adds meso-diaminopimelate to the UDP-linked peptidoglycan precursor:

\[
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu} + \text{meso-DAP} + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu-meso-DAP} + \text{ADP} + \text{phosphate}
\]

This corresponds to **EC 6.3.2.13** and Rhea reaction **RHEA:23676**.

The enzyme is expected to bind **ATP** and use **Mg²⁺** for nucleotide-dependent ligase activity. These assignments are supported by the closely matching reviewed homologs, although the exact query protein has not been experimentally characterized here.

## Biological processes

The protein likely participates in:

- **Peptidoglycan biosynthesis**
- **Bacterial cell-wall construction**
- **Cell division**
- **Maintenance of cell shape and envelope integrity**

MurE performs the third peptide-ligation step in the cytoplasmic assembly of the peptidoglycan stem peptide. Its product is subsequently used in later cell-wall precursor and glycan assembly steps.

## Cellular location

The most likely location is the **soluble bacterial cytoplasm/cytosol**.

The sequence is highly compatible with a soluble metabolic enzyme and lacks obvious features expected for:

- A secretory signal peptide
- A transmembrane protein
- An extracellular or periplasmic enzyme

## Overall confidence

**High confidence for MurE assignment.** The combination of near-full-length similarity to reviewed MurE proteins, conserved Mur ligase domains, appropriate size, and matching functional annotations strongly supports this identification.

The most concise annotation would be:

> **UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate—meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase (MurE), an Mg²⁺-dependent cytosolic enzyme involved in bacterial peptidoglycan biosynthesis.**
