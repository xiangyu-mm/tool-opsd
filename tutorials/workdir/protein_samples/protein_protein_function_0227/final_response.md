## Functional interpretation

The 140-residue sequence is identified with very high confidence as **TMEM242**, also called **transmembrane protein 242**:

- It is an **exact sequence match** to reviewed mouse TMEM242 (UniProt Q8VCR3).
- Human TMEM242 is a closely related ortholog with **85.8% sequence identity**.
- It contains the conserved **DUF1358 domain** and several strongly hydrophobic segments consistent with a small mitochondrial membrane protein.
- Predicted size is approximately **14.9 kDa**.

### Likely localization and role

TMEM242 is a small protein of the **mitochondrial inner membrane**. It is not predicted to be a transporter, receptor, or catalytic enzyme. Instead, it functions as a **membrane assembly scaffold** for mitochondrial ATP synthase, also known as respiratory complex V.

Specifically, TMEM242 helps:

1. Insert and oligomerize the ATP synthase **subunit c/ATP5MC3**.
2. Assemble the ATP synthase **c-ring**.
3. Incorporate additional membrane-associated ATP synthase components, including **MT-ATP6, MT-ATP8, ATP5MJ, and ATP5MK**.

### Biological-process hypothesis

The most likely biological process is:

> **Assembly and maturation of the mitochondrial F₁F₀ ATP synthase complex, particularly formation of its membrane-embedded c-ring.**

Through this role, TMEM242 indirectly supports **oxidative phosphorylation and mitochondrial ATP production**. Its effect is therefore structural and assembly-related rather than enzymatic or transport-related.
