## Functional identification

The 297-aa protein is **ribosomal RNA small-subunit methyltransferase A**, commonly called **RsmA/KsgA**.

Evidence is strong: the sequence is an **exact match to a reviewed Swiss-Prot RsmA protein** and contains the characteristic **RrnaAD domain (PF00398)**. It is assigned **EC 2.1.1.182**.

## Catalytic activity

RsmA/KsgA modifies two adjacent adenosines in 16S rRNA:

- **A1518**
- **A1519**

These residues occur in a conserved hairpin near the 3′ end of bacterial 16S rRNA within the 30S ribosomal subunit. The enzyme installs **two N6-methyl groups on each adenosine**, producing **N6,N6-dimethyladenosine** residues.

The overall reaction is:

\[
\text{16S rRNA-A1518/A1519}
+ 4\,\text{S-adenosyl-L-methionine}
\rightarrow
\text{16S rRNA-}m^6_2\text{A1518/}m^6_2\text{A1519}
+ 4\,\text{S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine}
+ 4\,\text{H}^+
\]

Thus, **S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)** is the methyl donor, and **S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine (SAH)** is produced.

## Biological role

The modification likely contributes to **30S ribosomal-subunit biogenesis and maturation**. The protein is expected to be a **cytoplasmic bacterial enzyme**.

**Summary:** This sequence encodes a SAM-dependent rRNA methyltransferase that dimethylates 16S rRNA nucleotides A1518 and A1519 during small-subunit ribosome assembly.
