### Functional identification

The 91-residue protein is very likely **MinE**, the bacterial **cell-division topological specificity factor**.

### Evidence

- **Exact sequence match** to reviewed UniProt protein **C4Z090** from *Lachnospira eligens*.
- Strong homologs in related bacteria show **72–71% identity** across nearly the full sequence.
- Contains a highly significant **MinE Pfam domain**: PF03776, residues 1–83, i-E-value \(1.1 \times 10^{-27}\).
- Compact size is characteristic of bacterial MinE proteins:
  - Length: **91 aa**
  - Estimated molecular mass: **10.1 kDa**
  - Theoretical pI: **5.03**

### Likely biological process

MinE participates in **bacterial division-site selection** through the **MinC–MinD–MinE system**. It counteracts MinC/MinD-mediated inhibition at internal membrane sites while allowing inhibition near the cell poles. This spatial pattern prevents misplaced septum formation and promotes assembly of the division septum near the **mid-cell position**.

**Conclusion:** The protein is best annotated as **MinE**, involved in **regulation of division septum assembly and bacterial cell division positioning**. This assignment is high confidence because it is supported by an exact reviewed sequence match and a strong MinE domain hit.
