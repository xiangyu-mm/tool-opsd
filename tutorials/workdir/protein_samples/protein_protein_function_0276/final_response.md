## Functional annotation

The 491-amino-acid protein is most likely **MurE**, the bacterial **UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate—meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase**.

### Molecular function

MurE is an ATP-dependent enzyme in peptidoglycan precursor biosynthesis. It catalyzes:

\[
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu} + \text{meso-DAP} + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu-meso-DAP} + \text{ADP} + P_i
\]

Thus, it adds **meso-diaminopimelate (meso-DAP)** to the UDP-linked peptidoglycan precursor. The predicted enzyme classification is **EC 6.3.2.13**.

The assignment is strongly supported by:

- 48–50% amino-acid identity to reviewed MurE proteins
- Approximately 95–99% alignment coverage
- Extremely significant homology scores, with E-values around \(10^{-131}\) to \(10^{-135}\)
- Detection of the characteristic:
  - **Mur_ligase_M domain**, residues approximately 111–310
  - **Mur_ligase_C domain**, residues approximately 335–461

The reaction is expected to require **Mg²⁺**, which supports ATP-dependent ligase activity.

### Cellular processes

This protein is involved in:

- **Peptidoglycan biosynthesis**
- **Bacterial cell-wall biogenesis**
- **Cell division**
- **Maintenance of bacterial cell shape**

By producing the DAP-containing peptidoglycan precursor, MurE contributes to construction and remodeling of the bacterial cell wall. Inhibition or loss of MurE would be expected to impair cell-wall assembly and bacterial growth, although mutation effects were not directly tested here.

### Subcellular location

The protein is most likely **cytoplasmic/cytosolic**.

This localization is supported by the reviewed MurE homolog annotations and by the sequence architecture: it is a large, soluble-looking enzyme with no evident N-terminal signal peptide or membrane-spanning region. It therefore likely acts on cytoplasmic, UDP-linked peptidoglycan precursors before later steps transport intermediates toward the membrane-associated cell-wall synthesis machinery.

### Sequence properties

- Length: **491 amino acids**
- Predicted molecular mass: **~51.7 kDa**
- Theoretical pI: **~5.63**
- Predicted architecture: multidomain Mur ligase
- No evidence from the available analysis for a secreted or integral membrane protein

### Overall conclusion

**This sequence encodes a soluble bacterial MurE ligase, most likely the meso-diaminopimelate-specific form. It uses ATP and Mg²⁺ to add meso-DAP to a UDP-MurNAc peptidoglycan precursor, an essential step in bacterial cell-wall synthesis, cell division, and shape maintenance.** The functional assignment is supported by strong full-length homology and conserved Mur ligase domains, although it remains a sequence-based annotation rather than a direct biochemical measurement.
