## Functional identification

The 192-residue protein is **HIV-1 Vif (virion infectivity factor)** with high confidence.

- Length: **192 aa**
- Estimated mass: **22.6 kDa**
- Predicted pI: **~10.0**, indicating a strongly basic protein
- Pfam: **Vif domain PF00559**, spanning the full sequence
- Homology: **84.9–87.5% identity over 100% of the sequence** to curated HIV-1 Vif proteins; E-values ~10⁻¹²⁷–10⁻¹²⁸

These results strongly support identification as an HIV-1 Vif protein rather than a generic cellular protein.

## Molecular function

Vif is a viral accessory protein that protects HIV-1 from host cytidine-deaminase defenses, especially:

- **APOBEC3G**
- **APOBEC3F**

Vif functions as a substrate-recruiting component of a hijacked host ubiquitin ligase complex. It recruits APOBEC3 proteins to a complex containing:

- Cullin-5
- RING-box protein/RBX2
- Elongin B/C
- The transcription cofactor **CBF-β/CBFB**

This promotes **polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation** of APOBEC3G and APOBEC3F. Vif can also inhibit APOBEC3G activity by mechanisms that do not require degradation, including interference with its cytidine-deaminase function.

The sequence contains features consistent with this role:

- A conserved **HCCH motif**, associated with metal-dependent structural interactions
- A **BC-box-like motif**, involved in recruitment of elongin proteins
- A C-terminal **SOCS-box-like region**, supporting assembly with the Cullin-5 ubiquitin-ligase machinery
- Basic regions compatible with **RNA and protein binding**

## Likely biological processes

The protein is expected to participate in:

1. **Antagonism of innate antiviral immunity**
2. **Host APOBEC3G/APOBEC3F degradation**
3. **Hijacking of host ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis**
4. **Promotion of the HIV-1 viral life cycle**
5. **Viral RNA and protein interactions**
6. **Virion incorporation and viral particle morphogenesis**

Curated Vif annotations also associate it with effects on viral nucleoprotein-core stability and, in some contexts, **G2 cell-cycle arrest**. These latter effects are more context-dependent than its APOBEC-counteracting function.

## Cellular localization

Vif is not predicted to be a transmembrane protein. Its expected localization is:

- **Host-cell cytoplasm**
- **Cytoplasmic face of cellular membranes**
- **Peripheral membrane-associated fraction**
- **Virions**

Its membrane association is likely mediated by protein, RNA, or viral structural-protein interactions rather than by a membrane-spanning helix. Vif can also interact with viral RNA and Gag-related components, supporting incorporation into virions.

## Overall summary

This sequence encodes **HIV-1 Vif**, a basic, multifunctional viral accessory protein. Its primary role is to neutralize host APOBEC3G and APOBEC3F by recruiting them to a Cullin-5-based E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, leading to their ubiquitination and degradation. It operates mainly in the host cytoplasm and at cytoplasmic membrane surfaces and is also packaged into virions. The identification is supported by full-length high-identity homology, a highly significant Vif Pfam domain match, and conserved interaction motifs.
