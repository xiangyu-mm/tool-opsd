## Functional identification

This 250-amino-acid protein is **1-(5-phosphoribosyl)-5-[(5-phosphoribosylamino)methylideneamino]imidazole-4-carboxamide isomerase**, commonly called **HisA**.

The assignment is highly confident because the sequence is an **exact full-length match** to reviewed UniProt protein **Q12NS9** from *Shewanella denitrificans*. It also contains the characteristic **His_biosynth Pfam domain (PF00977)** across nearly the entire sequence.

## Enzymatic function

HisA catalyzes **EC 5.3.1.16**, an intramolecular isomerization step in histidine biosynthesis:

\[
\text{ProFAR} \rightleftharpoons \text{PRFAR}
\]

More explicitly, it converts:

- **1-(5-phospho-β-D-ribosyl)-5-[(5-phospho-β-D-ribosylamino)methylideneamino]imidazole-4-carboxamide**

to

- the corresponding **phosphoribulosyl-imino isomer**

This is **step 4 of 9** in the bacterial pathway converting phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate-derived intermediates into **L-histidine**.

The curated reference annotation identifies catalytic roles for:

- **Residue 7:** proton acceptor  
- **Residue 129:** proton donor  

No metal, nucleotide, flavin, or other obligatory cofactor was reported.

## Subcellular localization

The protein is annotated as **cytoplasmic**. Its sequence is consistent with a soluble enzyme: it lacks an obvious N-terminal secretion signal and does not show characteristics expected for a membrane protein.

## Biological processes

The strongest assignment is involvement in:

- **L-histidine biosynthetic process**
- **Amino-acid biosynthesis**
- **Conversion of phosphoribosyl intermediates during histidine formation**

The retrieved annotation also includes **L-tryptophan biosynthesis**. This likely reflects the broader relationship between HisA/HisF-like isomerase enzymes and aromatic amino-acid biosynthetic pathways. However, based on the available evidence, the direct and most secure functional assignment for this sequence is **HisA activity in histidine biosynthesis**.

## Overall summary

This is a **small soluble bacterial HisA enzyme**, belonging to the His_biosynth family. It catalyzes the fourth reaction of **L-histidine biosynthesis** by isomerizing a phosphoribosyl imidazole-carboxamide intermediate. The exact sequence match to a reviewed protein, full-length His_biosynth domain, conserved catalytic residues, and cytoplasmic annotation provide strong evidence for this identification.
