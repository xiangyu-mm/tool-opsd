## Functional identification

The 355-residue protein is **UDP-3-O-acylglucosamine N-acyltransferase**, commonly called **LpxD**.

- **EC:** 2.3.1.191  
- **Pathway:** Lipid A biosynthesis / bacterial lipopolysaccharide production  
- **Predicted size:** ~37.4 kDa  
- **Localization:** Cytosol  
- **Oligomeric state:** Homotrimer  
- **Confidence:** Very high

The sequence is an **exact sequence match** to a reviewed UniProt LpxD protein, with additional reviewed homologs sharing 82–88% identity. Pfam analysis also detects an LpxD domain and characteristic Hexapep repeats.

## Catalyzed reaction

LpxD transfers a second 3-hydroxyacyl group to the amino group of UDP-3-O-acylglucosamine:

\[
\text{UDP-3-O-[(3R)-3-hydroxyacyl]-}\alpha\text{-D-glucosamine}
+
\text{(3R)-hydroxyacyl-ACP}
\]

\[
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-2-N,3-O-bis[(3R)-3-hydroxyacyl]-}\alpha\text{-D-glucosamine}
+
\text{holo-ACP}
+
\text{H}^{+}
\]

In simpler terms, it performs an **N-acylation reaction**, using **3-hydroxyacyl-acyl carrier protein (ACP)** as the acyl donor and **UDP-3-O-acylglucosamine** as the acceptor.

This is an early committed step in the construction of **lipid A**, the phosphorylated glycolipid anchor of Gram-negative bacterial lipopolysaccharide.

No separate metal ion or redox cofactor is indicated; the enzyme is an acyltransferase that directly transfers the acyl group from ACP to the UDP-linked glucosamine substrate.
