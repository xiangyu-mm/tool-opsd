### Likely function
MazG is a bacterial **nucleoside triphosphate pyrophosphohydrolase** that can hydrolyze nucleotide triphosphates and may also reduce cellular levels of **(p)ppGpp**, the bacterial stringent-response alarmone.

Its proposed physiological role is regulation of bacterial survival during nutritional stress:

1. Amino-acid starvation activates RelA and increases (p)ppGpp.
2. MazG lowers the nucleotide/alarmone pool, either by degrading NTPs or directly degrading (p)ppGpp.
3. This helps modulate the **MazE–MazF toxin–antitoxin system** and can protect cells from MazF-associated toxicity.

### Other annotations

- **Oligomerization:** annotated as a **homodimer**
- **Molecular activities:** ATP diphosphatase and nucleoside triphosphate diphosphatase activity
- **Associated processes:** nucleotide catabolism, response to starvation/stress, regulation of toxin–antitoxin-mediated survival

### Functional summary
This sequence encodes **MazG, a soluble bacterial nucleoside triphosphate pyrophosphohydrolase of approximately 30.5 kDa**. It likely functions as a homodimeric nucleotide-hydrolyzing enzyme that helps control NTP and possibly (p)ppGpp levels during nutritional stress, thereby modulating the MazE–MazF toxin–antitoxin response.

The assignment is highly reliable because the sequence exactly matches a reviewed UniProt protein. However, the precise substrate range and whether direct (p)ppGpp hydrolysis is the dominant activity should be confirmed experimentally rather than inferred solely from sequence similarity.
