## Functional assessment

The sequence is most likely **TMEM266 (transmembrane protein 266)**, a mammalian **voltage-sensor protein**.

### Evidence

- **Near-exact human match:** 100% identity across 98.5% of the query to reviewed human TMEM266 (UniProt **Q2M3C6**), with strong mouse and macaque homologs.
- **Membrane architecture:** The central region contains a strongly hydrophobic multi-pass membrane segment.
- **Domain:** An **Ion_trans/PF00520** domain occurs at residues approximately **102–217**, consistent with a voltage-sensor-like transmembrane module.
- **C-terminal region:** The remainder is largely charged and low-complexity, compatible with a cytoplasmic regulatory or scaffolding region rather than a conventional pore-forming transporter.
- **Reported behavior:** TMEM266 undergoes rapid and slow conformational rearrangements in response to membrane voltage. A zinc-binding site reportedly influences the slower transition.

### Likely molecular role

TMEM266 is best described as a **voltage-sensitive membrane protein that detects or responds to changes in membrane potential**. Current curated annotations indicate that it **does not form a functional ion-conducting channel**, despite its voltage-sensor-like domain.

It therefore is unlikely to be:

- a conventional ion channel,
- a classical transporter,
- or a ligand-activated receptor.

Its precise signaling or scaffolding function remains unresolved.

### Localization and assembly

Reported localization is the plasma membrane, particularly the **dendrites and soma/perikaryon of cerebellar granule neurons**, including postsynaptic regions. The protein is reported to form a **disulfide-linked homodimer**.

### Overall conclusion

**Most likely function:** neuronal plasma-membrane voltage sensor or voltage-dependent conformational regulator, corresponding to **TMEM266**. The sequence supports membrane voltage sensing, but not autonomous ion transport. Exact downstream signaling and physiological function remain uncertain.
