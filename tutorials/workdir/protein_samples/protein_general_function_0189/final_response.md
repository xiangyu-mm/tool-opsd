## Functional summary

This 456-amino-acid sequence is an **exact sequence match** to reviewed *Haemophilus influenzae* GlmU (UniProt Q4QMS5). It is therefore strongly identified as **GlmU, a bifunctional enzyme that catalyzes the final two steps of bacterial UDP-N-acetylglucosamine (UDP-GlcNAc) biosynthesis**.

- **N-terminal domain (residues 1–229):** UDP-N-acetylglucosamine pyrophosphorylase activity (EC 2.7.7.23). It uses UTP to convert N-acetylglucosamine-1-phosphate to UDP-GlcNAc, releasing diphosphate.
- **C-terminal domain (residues 251–456):** Glucosamine-1-phosphate N-acetyltransferase activity (EC 2.3.1.157). It transfers an acetyl group from acetyl-CoA to glucosamine-1-phosphate, producing N-acetylglucosamine-1-phosphate and CoA.
- **Cofactor and cellular context:** The reference annotation reports one Mg²⁺ ion per subunit, cytoplasmic localization, and a homotrimeric assembly.
- **Biological relevance:** UDP-GlcNAc supplies bacterial cell-wall and envelope biosynthesis, including peptidoglycan and lipid A pathways.
- **Sequence properties:** Calculated mass is about **49.2 kDa**, theoretical pI **6.04**, and GRAVY **−0.104**. These are sequence-derived estimates.

The N-terminal nucleotide-transferase and C-terminal acetyltransferase/hexapeptide-repeat domain evidence is consistent with the two activities. The glycine-rich motifs are part of the domain pattern; the exact-sequence match, rather than those motifs alone, is the strongest basis for assigning function.
