### Functional summary

The 527-residue protein is most consistent with a **flavin-containing monooxygenase (FMO)-like enzyme**, specifically an **FMO5-like Baeyer–Villiger monooxygenase**.

- **Domain evidence:** Strong FMO-like domain match, PF00743, spanning approximately residues 2–512.
- **Homology:** Best reviewed matches are mammalian FMO5 proteins, with approximately **48% sequence identity**, **94–97% coverage**, and E-values around **10⁻¹⁶⁴–10⁻¹⁶⁷**.
- **Cofactors:** Expected to use **FAD** as the prosthetic flavin and **NADPH** as the reducing co-substrate, with molecular oxygen as the oxygen source.

### Principal chemical reaction

The predicted catalytic activity is **Baeyer–Villiger oxidation**:

\[
\text{ketone} + \mathrm{NADPH} + \mathrm{O_2} + \mathrm{H^+}
\rightarrow
\text{ester} + \mathrm{NADP^+} + \mathrm{H_2O}
\]

Mechanistically, the enzyme inserts one oxygen atom into the **C–C bond adjacent to a ketone carbonyl**, thereby converting a ketone into an ester. FMO5 homologs are reported to accept diverse carbonyl compounds, including aliphatic ketones.

Representative reactions associated with the homologs include:

- Heptan-2-one → pentyl acetate
- Octan-3-one → pentyl propanoate or ethyl hexanoate

The protein may also exhibit secondary, uncoupled **NADPH oxidase activity**:

\[
\mathrm{NADPH} + \mathrm{O_2} + \mathrm{H^+}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{NADP^+} + \mathrm{H_2O_2}
\]

### Classification and confidence

- **Likely enzyme class:** FAD-dependent monooxygenase  
- **Likely activity:** Baeyer–Villiger monooxygenase  
- **Relevant EC annotations from homologs:** **EC 1.14.13.8** and secondary **EC 1.6.3.1**
- **Confidence:** **Moderate to high for the enzyme family and reaction class**; lower for the exact substrate range.

The exact substrate specificity cannot be established from sequence similarity alone. Also, although the homologs are often endoplasmic-reticulum-associated FMO5 proteins, this sequence lacks an obvious hydrophobic C-terminal membrane anchor, so its cellular localization should not be assigned confidently from the available evidence.
