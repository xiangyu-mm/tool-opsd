## Functional identification

The protein is most consistent with **UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate–meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase**, commonly called **MurE**.

Evidence:

- Length: **504 amino acids**
- Contains all three characteristic Mur ligase domains:
  - **Mur_ligase**
  - **Mur_ligase_M**
  - **Mur_ligase_C**
- Shows approximately **43–44% identity over 92–94% of its length** to reviewed MurE proteins.
- Homologs are assigned **EC 6.3.2.13**, with **Mg²⁺** as a cofactor.

## Catalyzed reaction

\[
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu}
+ \text{meso-2,6-diaminopimelate}
+ \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-}\gamma\text{-D-Glu-meso-DAP}
+ \text{ADP}
+ \text{P}_{i}
\]

In words, the enzyme uses ATP to attach **meso-diaminopimelate (meso-DAP)** to the γ-carboxyl group of D-glutamate in the UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu peptidoglycan precursor.

## Biological role

This is a cytosolic enzyme in **bacterial peptidoglycan biosynthesis**, producing the UDP-MurNAc-pentapeptide precursor required for bacterial cell-wall assembly.

The assignment is strongly supported by homology and domain architecture, although it remains a sequence-based inference rather than a direct biochemical assay.
