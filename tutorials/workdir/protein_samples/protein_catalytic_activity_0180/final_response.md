## Functional identification

The 496-residue protein is most consistent with **bifunctional NAD(P)H-hydrate repair enzyme Nnr** (also called an NAD(P)HX repair enzyme).

This assignment is supported by:

- Strong similarity to reviewed Nnr homologs, with about **31–34% identity over 94–99% of the sequence**.
- A characteristic two-domain organization:
  - **N-terminal YjeF_N domain**: NAD(P)HX epimerase module.
  - **C-terminal YjeF/Carb_kinase-like domain**: ADP-dependent NAD(P)HX dehydratase module.

## Catalytic activity

Nnr repairs hydrated, damaged forms of NADH and NADPH, collectively called **NAD(P)HX**. It has two activities:

### 1. NAD(P)HX epimerase activity

It interconverts the R and S stereoisomers:

\[
(6R)\text{-NADHX} \rightleftharpoons (6S)\text{-NADHX}
\]

\[
(6R)\text{-NADPHX} \rightleftharpoons (6S)\text{-NADPHX}
\]

This is assigned to **EC 5.1.99.6**.

### 2. ADP-dependent NAD(P)HX dehydratase activity

It restores the hydrated cofactor to functional NADH or NADPH. The best-supported reactions are:

\[
(6S)\text{-NADHX} + \text{ADP}
\rightarrow \text{NADH} + \text{AMP} + \text{phosphate} + H^+
\]

and

\[
(6S)\text{-NADPHX} + \text{ADP}
\rightarrow \text{NADPH} + \text{AMP} + \text{phosphate} + H^+
\]

This activity is assigned to **EC 4.2.1.136**. The reaction is unusual because **ADP is consumed and converted to AMP plus phosphate**, providing the energy or phosphoryl-transfer chemistry needed for dehydration and repair.

## Cofactor and biological role

Curated homologs indicate binding of **one K⁺ ion per subunit**. The enzyme participates in **metabolite repair**, removing NAD(P)H hydrates that can arise spontaneously or during enzymatic/heat-dependent hydration of NADH or NADPH.

### Summary

**Predicted function:** bifunctional NAD(P)H-hydrate repair enzyme Nnr, catalyzing both R/S epimerization of NAD(P)HX and ADP-dependent dehydration of S-NAD(P)HX back to NADH or NADPH. The assignment is strongly supported by sequence architecture and homolog annotation, but remains an inference for the supplied sequence rather than a direct biochemical measurement.
