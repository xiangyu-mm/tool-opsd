## Functional estimate

This 194-residue protein is most likely a **Ham1p-like dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase**, a small soluble nucleotide “house-cleaning” enzyme.

### Evidence

- **Pfam:** Strong full-length match to the **Ham1p-like domain** (PF01725), spanning residues 3–186; i-Evalue \(2.4 \times 10^{-62}\).
- **Homology:** Three reviewed homologs show approximately **45–47% sequence identity across ~96% of the protein**, with highly significant E-values around \(10^{-50}\)–\(10^{-54}\).
- These homologs are annotated as **dITP/XTP pyrophosphatases**, often also acting on **ITP**.
- The protein is predicted to be a compact, acidic, soluble protein of approximately **22.1 kDa**. Its homologs are cytosolic and form **homodimers**, which is a reasonable prediction for this sequence as well.

### Likely molecular activity

The enzyme likely hydrolyzes aberrant purine nucleoside triphosphates, especially:

- **dITP** — deoxyinosine triphosphate  
- **ITP** — inosine triphosphate  
- **XTP** — xanthosine triphosphate  

The general reaction is:

\[
\text{noncanonical purine NTP} + H_2O
\rightarrow
\text{corresponding NMP} + PP_i
\]

### Likely biological process

It probably participates in **purine nucleoside triphosphate catabolism and nucleotide-pool sanitization**. By removing noncanonical nucleotides, it helps prevent their accidental incorporation into DNA or RNA, thereby reducing mutagenesis and genome damage.

### Confidence and limitation

**Confidence: moderate to high for the protein family and broad function.** The Pfam match and multiple high-coverage curated homologs provide strong support. However, the exact substrate preference—whether dITP, ITP, XTP, or another related nucleotide is preferred—cannot be established from sequence similarity alone and would require biochemical testing.
