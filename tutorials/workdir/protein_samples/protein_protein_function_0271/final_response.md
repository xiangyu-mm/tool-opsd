## Functional summary

The sequence is most consistent with **DOT1L** (also called **DOT1**, histone H3 lysine-79 methyltransferase). The supplied-sequence analysis detected a highly significant DOT1-family domain, **PF08123**, at residues **115–317**. Its closest reported hits were human UniProt **Q8TEK3** and mouse **Q6XZL8**, with approximately **89% sequence identity**. Reviewed UniProt records for these proteins annotate H3K79-specific histone methyltransferase activity. Together, the domain match and close homologs support the identification, although the sequence-search output itself did not transfer functional annotations automatically.

### Function

DOT1L methylates **lysine 79 of histone H3 (H3K79)**, with a preference for histones in nucleosomes. This histone modification is associated with regulation of chromatin and gene transcription. The matched UniProt records also describe binding to DNA.

### Subcellular localization

The reviewed human and mouse homolog records annotate localization to the **nucleus**, including the **nucleoplasm**. They also report **cytoplasmic** localization; in those annotations, cytoplasmic localization depends on the interaction with **MLLT6/AF17**. These are homolog-record annotations, rather than a direct localization measurement of the exact supplied sequence.

### Biological processes and context

Annotations for the close homologs include **DNA-damage checkpoint signaling** and **DNA repair**. The mouse record additionally lists **gene expression**. The human record describes a specific context in which DOT1L, together with MLLT3/AF9, is associated with **SCNN1A transcriptional repression** following promoter H3K79 hypermethylation. That example should not be taken as a universal effect of H3K79 methylation.

The sequence is **768 amino acids** long, with a calculated molecular mass of about **87.6 kDa** and theoretical pI of **9.05**. These are sequence-derived estimates. Identification as DOT1L is strongly supported by the DOT1-domain match and high-identity homologs; localization and biological-process details are drawn from those reviewed homolog annotations, not directly established for this sequence.
