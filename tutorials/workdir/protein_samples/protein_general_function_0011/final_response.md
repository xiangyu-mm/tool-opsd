## Functional summary

This 646-residue protein is **lipoteichoic acid synthase (LtaS)**, a bacterial membrane enzyme involved in **lipoteichoic acid (LTA) biosynthesis**.

- **Strong identification:** The sequence is an exact match to reviewed UniProt protein **Q49VR4** from *Staphylococcus saprophyticus*; related staphylococcal LtaS proteins also show >84% identity.
- **Architecture:**  
  - The N-terminal ~200 residues are extremely hydrophobic and contain multiple membrane-spanning segments, consistent with a **multi-pass membrane anchor**.  
  - The C-terminal ~400 residues are largely soluble and contain an **LtaS_N domain** plus a **sulfatase-like domain** detected by Pfam.
- **Function:** Catalyzes polymerization of **polyglycerol phosphate**, forming the LTA backbone. The presumed lipid donor/substrate is **phosphatidylglycerol**.
- **Localization:** Bacterial plasma membrane, with the large catalytic region exposed toward the cell-envelope/extracellular side.
- **Biological role:** LTA production supports Gram-positive cell-envelope organization, growth, and cell division.
- **Calculated properties:** Approximate molecular mass **74.9 kDa**, theoretical pI **6.50**, and mildly negative predicted net charge at neutral pH.

Overall, this is a **membrane-tethered LTA polymerase**, not a conventional soluble sulfatase; the sulfatase-like Pfam hit describes part of its catalytic-domain architecture.
