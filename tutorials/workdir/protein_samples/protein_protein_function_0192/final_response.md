## Functional annotation

**Most likely identity:** **Ribosome-recycling factor (RRF)**  
**Biological process:** **Bacterial translation termination and ribosome recycling**

### Evidence

- The sequence is **187 amino acids** long and exactly matches the curated UniProt RRF protein **Q1WUG4** from *Ligilactobacillus salivarius*.
- It contains the characteristic **RRF domain, Pfam PF01765**, spanning residues **22–185**.
- Strongly matching RRF homologs occur in other bacteria, including *Enterococcus faecalis* and *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum*, with approximately **69–70% sequence identity** across nearly the full length.
- The predicted protein is soluble and lacks transmembrane segments or signal-peptide features; its likely localization is the **bacterial cytoplasm**.

### Functional hypothesis

This protein most likely binds the ribosome after translation termination and, together with other recycling factors such as **EF-G**, promotes dissociation of the post-termination ribosomal complex from mRNA. This frees the ribosomal subunits for another round of translation.

**GO-consistent functions:**

- **Ribosomal large-subunit binding**
- **Translational termination**
- **Ribosome recycling**
- **Cytoplasmic localization**

### Summary

The sequence is a high-confidence **bacterial ribosome-recycling factor**, involved in the transition from translation termination to initiation of a new translation cycle. Its function is supported by an exact curated sequence match and a highly significant RRF-domain annotation. The available evidence does not independently establish its oligomeric state or species of origin beyond the exact reference match.
