## Functional assignment

The 338-residue protein is most consistent with **ribose-phosphate pyrophosphokinase** (also called **ribose-phosphate diphosphokinase or phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate synthetase**, EC **2.7.6.1**).

### Catalytic reaction

\[
\text{D-ribose-5-phosphate} + \text{ATP}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{5-phospho-}\alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-diphosphate (PRPP)} + \text{AMP} + \text{H}^+
\]

The enzyme transfers the **pyrophosphoryl group of ATP** to the anomeric hydroxyl of ribose-5-phosphate, producing **PRPP**. PRPP is a central activated ribose-phosphate donor used in purine, pyrimidine, pyridine-nucleotide, and histidine biosynthesis.

- It matches reviewed cyanobacterial ribose-phosphate pyrophosphokinases with **79–84% sequence identity** and approximately **98% coverage**.
- Close homologs are annotated with:

- The detected Pfam regions—**Pribosyltran_N**, **Pribosyltran**, and **Pribosyl_synth**—are consistent with this enzyme family.
- Homologs are reported to bind **two Mg²⁺ ions per subunit**, which likely coordinate ATP and phosphate groups during catalysis.

### Additional likely properties

The protein is probably a soluble cytoplasmic enzyme and may form a **hexamer**, as reported for the closely related cyanobacterial homologs. These properties are inferred from homology rather than directly demonstrated for this exact sequence.
