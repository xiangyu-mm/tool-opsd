## Molecular function

Cif catalyzes the reaction:

**Protein-glutamine + H₂O → protein-glutamate + NH₄⁺**

Its principal known host substrate is **NEDD8**, which it deamidates at **Gln-40**. This modification disrupts NEDD8-dependent activation of **cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligases**.

Consequently, Cif can:

- Inactivate cullin-RING ubiquitin ligase complexes
- Stabilize cell-cycle inhibitors such as **p21/CDKN1A** and **p27/CDKN1B**
- Cause host-cell-cycle arrest, including G1 and G2 effects
- Perturb actin organization and programmed cell-death pathways
- Function as a bacterial toxin/host-manipulating effector

Cif can also deamidate ubiquitin in vitro, although **NEDD8 is the preferred in vivo substrate**.

## Localization and delivery

The curated annotation indicates that Cif is:

- Secreted through a **type III secretion system**
- Present extracellularly during delivery
- Ultimately active in the **host-cell nucleus**

## Overall summary

This is not a generic metabolic enzyme; it is a **bacterial type III-secreted virulence effector**. Its conserved CIF domain supports protein-glutamine deamidase activity, and the exact sequence match establishes the specific annotation as *Y. pseudotuberculosis* Cif. The most important biological effect is **NEDD8 deamidation and consequent inhibition of cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligases**, leading to disruption of host cell-cycle regulation.
