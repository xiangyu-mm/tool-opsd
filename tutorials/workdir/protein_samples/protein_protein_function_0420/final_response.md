## Functional interpretation

The 177-amino-acid sequence is most consistent with a **receptor activity-modifying protein (RAMP)-family member**, probably **RAMP2-like**.

### Key evidence

- **Strong RAMP domain match:** Pfam RAMP domain PF04901 was detected at residues **68–174** with a highly significant i-Evalue of **2.2 × 10⁻⁴¹**.
- **Closest curated homolog:** Human **RAMP2 (UniProt O60895)**, with:
  - **52.7% sequence identity**
  - **74% query coverage**
  - **E-value 5.94 × 10⁻⁴¹**
- **Compatible architecture:** The sequence has the characteristic organization of a small RAMP protein:
  1. N-terminal signal-peptide-like region
  2. Extracellular/luminal cysteine-containing domain
  3. One C-terminal transmembrane helix
  4. Very short intracellular C-terminal tail

### Predicted topology and localization

This protein is predicted to be a **single-pass type I membrane protein**:

- **N-terminus:** Secretory signal peptide or signal-peptide-like sequence
- **Large N-terminal domain:** Extracellular or lumen-facing
- **Transmembrane segment:** Approximately residues **145–169**, containing the strongly hydrophobic sequence  
  `AMIIAPICLIPFLVTLVIW`
- **C-terminus:** Short cytoplasmic tail of approximately 8 residues

Accordingly, the most likely localization is the **plasma membrane/cell surface**, although some protein may pass through intracellular secretory and endosomal compartments during biosynthesis and trafficking.

The extracellular domain contains several cysteines and a potential N-linked glycosylation motif, consistent with a glycosylated cell-surface accessory protein.

## Likely molecular function

RAMP proteins are not conventional signaling receptors or enzymes. They are **accessory proteins for class B G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs)**. They can:

- Promote GPCR transport to the plasma membrane
- Alter receptor folding and maturation
- Modify ligand-binding properties
- Determine receptor pharmacology and signaling behavior
- Form heteromeric receptor complexes with GPCRs

Based on the close relationship to human RAMP2, this sequence may function with **calcitonin receptor-like receptor (CALCRL)**. A RAMP2–CALCRL complex forms an **adrenomedullin receptor**, and RAMP2 can also associate with the calcitonin receptor.

The exact ligand specificity cannot be established from sequence alone, but the available homology strongly supports a **RAMP2-like GPCR coreceptor function**.

## Potential biological processes

If this protein is functionally equivalent to RAMP2, it may participate in:

- **Adrenomedullin receptor signaling**
- **GPCR trafficking and plasma-membrane delivery**
- **Regulation of adenylate cyclase-activating GPCR signaling**
- **Cell-surface receptor complex assembly**
- **Vascular signaling and angiogenesis**
- **Cell–cell adhesion or adherens-junction regulation**

RAMP2-mediated adrenomedullin signaling is particularly associated with vascular and endothelial biology. However, these process assignments are inferred from the RAMP2 homolog and should not be treated as experimentally demonstrated for this exact sequence.
