## Functional summary

The 541-residue protein is very likely a **bacterial CTP synthase (CTPS; EC 6.3.4.2)** involved in de novo pyrimidine biosynthesis.

### Evidence

- Strong similarity to reviewed CTP synthases:
  - Up to **70.6% amino-acid identity**
  - Approximately **98% sequence coverage**
  - E-value as low as **2.2 × 10⁻²⁷⁵**
- Matches the characteristic two-domain architecture:
  - **CTP_synth_N domain**: residues ~3–267
  - **Glutamine amidotransferase class-I domain**: residues ~303–527
- The overall length is highly consistent with bacterial CTP synthases.

### Predicted reaction

The enzyme catalyzes ATP-dependent conversion of UTP to CTP:

\[
\text{UTP} + \text{glutamine} + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{CTP} + \text{glutamate} + \text{ADP} + \text{phosphate}
\]

It may also use free ammonia as the nitrogen source:

\[
\text{UTP} + \text{NH}_4^+ + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{CTP} + \text{ADP} + \text{phosphate}
\]

The glutaminase domain hydrolyzes glutamine, while the N-terminal amidoligase domain activates UTP and incorporates the resulting ammonia.
