## Functional identification

The protein is most consistent with **MurE ligase**: **UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamate—meso-2,6-diaminopimelate ligase** (**EC 6.3.2.13**).

### Catalytic reaction

\[
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu}
+ \text{meso-DAP}
+ \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu-meso-DAP}
+ \text{ADP}
+ \text{P}_{i}
\]

More explicitly, it adds **meso-2,6-diaminopimelate (meso-DAP)** to the γ-carboxyl group of the D-glutamate residue in the UDP-MurNAc-L-Ala-D-Glu precursor. The product is **UDP-N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanyl-D-glutamyl-meso-diaminopimelate**, the UDP-MurNAc tripeptide intermediate in bacterial peptidoglycan synthesis.

### Evidence from the sequence

- Length: **525 amino acids**, consistent with bacterial MurE proteins.
- Contains the characteristic three-domain Mur ligase architecture:
  - **Mur_ligase** domain
  - **Mur_ligase_M** domain
  - **Mur_ligase_C** domain
- Closely matches reviewed MurE proteins:
  - approximately **46–47% sequence identity**
  - approximately **93–96% sequence coverage**
  - extremely significant homology scores
- Matched homologs have the same **EC number, Rhea reaction, ATP-binding annotation, and Mg²⁺ requirement**.

### Mechanistic role

MurE is an **ATP-dependent peptide ligase**. ATP activation of the UDP-MurNAc-bound D-glutamate carboxyl group enables formation of the peptide bond to meso-DAP. **Mg²⁺** is expected to coordinate ATP and support catalysis.

### Biological significance

This enzyme functions in **bacterial cell-wall peptidoglycan biosynthesis**, specifically the cytoplasmic stage that builds the UDP-MurNAc peptide precursor before its incorporation into the cell wall.

**Confidence:** strong sequence-based assignment, supported by conserved domain organization and high-coverage homology to curated MurE enzymes; the exact substrate specificity is inferred from these homologs rather than experimentally demonstrated for this particular sequence.
