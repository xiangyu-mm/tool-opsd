## Functional annotation

The protein is most likely **RibBA**, a bifunctional enzyme in **bacterial riboflavin (vitamin B₂) biosynthesis**.

### Evidence

- **Length:** 405 aa; predicted soluble enzyme, with no transmembrane segments or signal peptide.
- **Domain architecture:**
  - **DHBP synthase domain**: residues ~7–197, Pfam **PF00926**
  - **GTP cyclohydrolase II domain**: residues ~206–372, Pfam **PF00925**
- **Homology:** 58.7–63.5% identity across ~97% of the sequence to reviewed RibBA proteins, with highly significant similarity.
- The protein therefore appears to be a fusion of the activities usually associated with **RibB** and **RibA**.

### Predicted molecular functions

1. **3,4-Dihydroxy-2-butanone-4-phosphate synthase**  
   **EC 4.1.99.12; Rhea RHEA:18457**

   Converts D-ribulose 5-phosphate into 3,4-dihydroxy-2-butanone 4-phosphate and formate.

2. **GTP cyclohydrolase II**  
   **EC 3.5.4.25; Rhea RHEA:23704**

   Converts GTP into a pyrimidine precursor of riboflavin, with release of formate and phosphate-containing products.

### Biological process

The protein is involved in the early steps of **riboflavin biosynthesis**. Its two domains independently generate:

- the **dihydroxybutanone-phosphate branch** from ribulose 5-phosphate, and
- the **pyrimidine branch** from GTP.

These products are subsequently combined and processed to produce **riboflavin**, which is used to form the flavin cofactors **FMN** and **FAD**.

### Cofactor and localization prediction

Based on the curated homologs, RibBA proteins commonly bind:

- two divalent metal ions, typically **Mg²⁺ or Mn²⁺**
- one **Zn²⁺** ion per subunit

The protein is expected to be **cytosolic**. Metal binding is strongly supported by homologous annotations but has not been experimentally demonstrated for this exact sequence.

### Concise summary

**This 405-aa protein is a likely cytosolic bifunctional RibBA enzyme that catalyzes the DHBP synthase and GTP cyclohydrolase II steps of bacterial riboflavin biosynthesis, supplying precursors for vitamin B₂ and the derived cofactors FMN and FAD.**
