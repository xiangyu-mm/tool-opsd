## Functional summary

This 768-amino-acid protein is most consistent with a **SLC6/NSS-family sodium- and chloride-dependent neurotransmitter transporter**, closely matching the **Drosophila melanogaster GABA transporter “ine”** (UniProt **Q9VR07**).

### Key characteristics

- **Length:** 768 aa  
- **Estimated molecular mass:** ~83.7 kDa  
- **Theoretical pI:** ~7.13  
- **Domain:** Strong Pfam **SNF transporter domain** signal, residues ~338–767  
- **Topology:** A large hydrophobic C-terminal region consistent with approximately **12 transmembrane helices**, characteristic of SLC6 neurotransmitter transporters  
- **N-terminal region:** Long, low-complexity, serine/proline-rich cytosolic segment, likely involved in regulation, trafficking, or protein interactions  
- **Localization:** Plasma membrane / multipass membrane protein  
- **Architecture:** **Not a seven-transmembrane GPCR**; it is a 12-transmembrane solute transporter

### Likely function

The strongest homology supports a role as a **Na⁺/Cl⁻-coupled neurotransmitter transporter**, likely mediating uptake of **GABA and/or related amino-acid neurotransmitters**. The reference Drosophila protein is annotated with:

- Neurotransmitter:sodium symporter activity  
- Amino-acid:sodium symporter activity  
- Sodium and chloride-dependent transport  
- GABA-related neurotransmitter transport  
- Glycine import  
- Regulation of neuronal excitability and osmotic/water homeostasis  

The sequence also shows significant similarity to human **GAT1/SLC6A1**, a sodium- and chloride-dependent GABA transporter, supporting assignment to the same transporter family.

### Interpretation and confidence

The family-level assignment is strong. The best reference match is a reviewed Drosophila protein with **100% identity across the query sequence**, although the reference protein is longer, suggesting that the query may represent an isoform or a truncated/alternative sequence. Therefore:

> **Most likely identity:** a Drosophila-like SLC6 neurotransmitter transporter, probably the GABA transporter **ine** or a very closely related isoform.

Exact substrate specificity should be treated as **high-confidence homology-based inference rather than direct experimental evidence for this particular sequence**.
