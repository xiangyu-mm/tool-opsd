### Functional assignment

The 105-aa protein is most likely a **Ppnp-family pyrimidine/purine nucleoside phosphorylase**.

Evidence includes:

- **67% sequence identity over ~98% of the sequence** to reviewed *Leptospira* nucleoside phosphorylases.
- A highly significant **Ppnp domain** match: Pfam **PF06865**, residues 12–102.
- Homologs are annotated with **EC 2.4.2.1 and EC 2.4.2.2**, corresponding to purine- and pyrimidine-nucleoside phosphorylase activities.

### Catalyzed reaction

The enzyme catalyzes reversible phosphorolysis of nucleosides:

\[
\text{Nucleoside} + \text{orthophosphate}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{free nucleobase} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

Examples include:

\[
\text{Adenosine} + P_i
\rightleftharpoons
\text{Adenine} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

and analogous reactions involving **guanosine, inosine, cytidine, uridine, thymidine, and xanthosine**.

In the reverse direction, the enzyme can form nucleosides from a free base and ribose-1-phosphate:

\[
\text{Nucleobase} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{Nucleoside} + P_i
\]

### Summary

This protein is best assigned as a **broad-specificity nucleoside phosphorylase involved in nucleoside salvage and interconversion**. It cleaves the N-glycosidic bond of purine and pyrimidine nucleosides using inorganic phosphate, producing the corresponding nucleobase and ribose-1-phosphate. The broad substrate range is strongly supported by close homologs, although the exact substrate specificity of this particular sequence remains inferred rather than experimentally demonstrated.
