## Functional identification

The sequence is **matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9; gelatinase B)**. It exactly matches the reviewed rat MMP-9 sequence **UniProt P50282** and is also strongly homologous to mammalian MMP-9 proteins, including human MMP-9.

MMP-9 is a **secreted, zinc-dependent extracellular matrix metalloprotease** that is synthesized as an inactive zymogen and activated outside the cell.

## Domain architecture

The sequence has the characteristic MMP-9 organization:

1. **Signal peptide, residues 1–19**  
   Directs secretion through the endoplasmic-reticulum/Golgi pathway.

2. **Propeptide, approximately residues 20–107**  
   Contains the conserved cysteine-switch region, including the motif around **PRCGVPD**. This cysteine helps maintain the enzyme in an inactive zymogen state by coordinating the catalytic zinc.

3. **Catalytic M10 metalloprotease domain, approximately residues 108–220 and extending into the downstream catalytic region**  
   Contains the conserved catalytic machinery of matrix metalloproteinases, including the zinc-binding catalytic region.

4. **Three fibronectin type-II repeats, approximately residues 226–390**  
   These modules contribute to binding and recognition of gelatin, collagens, and other extracellular-matrix substrates.

5. **Proline-rich/disordered hinge region, approximately residues 440–520**  
   Provides flexibility between the catalytic and C-terminal domains.

6. **C-terminal hemopexin-like domain, approximately residues 521–707**  
   Supports substrate recognition, interactions with extracellular-matrix components, regulation, and protein–protein interactions.

## Likely biological function

MMP-9 cleaves extracellular-matrix proteins, especially:

- Type IV and type V collagen
- Gelatin and collagen-derived material
- Fibronectin
- Other extracellular substrates in a context-dependent manner

It is involved in extracellular-matrix remodeling and can contribute to processes such as:

- Leukocyte migration
- Tissue remodeling
- Inflammation
- Basement-membrane degradation
- Bone resorption
- Cell invasion and tissue repair

The specific substrate list and biological effect depend on species, tissue, activation state, and experimental context.

## Processing and regulation

The precursor is expected to undergo:

- Removal of the N-terminal signal peptide during secretion
- Proteolytic removal or disruption of the propeptide for activation
- Possible extracellular regulation by endogenous metalloprotease inhibitors, particularly TIMPs

The mature enzyme is therefore smaller than the full 708-residue precursor. MMP-9 can exist as a monomer or dimer and has also been reported to form complexes with **LCN2/NGAL**.

## Overall conclusion

This is a **canonical secreted mammalian MMP-9 precursor**: a modular extracellular zinc metalloprotease containing an inhibitory cysteine-switch propeptide, catalytic M10 domain, three fibronectin type-II repeats, a flexible hinge, and a C-terminal hemopexin domain. The sequence evidence and exact reviewed-sequence match make the identification highly confident.
