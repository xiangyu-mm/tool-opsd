### Functional identification

The 190-aa protein is most consistent with a **Ham1p-like dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase** (**EC 3.6.1.66**).

Evidence:

- Strong similarity to curated dITP/XTP pyrophosphatases: up to **66% sequence identity** over ~99% of the sequence.
- Contains the characteristic **Ham1p-like domain** (Pfam **PF01725**).
- Size and acidic, soluble-protein composition are consistent with this enzyme family.

### Catalytic activity

The enzyme hydrolyzes noncanonical purine nucleoside triphosphates, particularly:

\[
\mathrm{dITP + H_2O \rightarrow dIMP + PP_i + H^+}
\]

It can also catalyze:

\[
\mathrm{ITP + H_2O \rightarrow IMP + PP_i + H^+}
\]

\[
\mathrm{XTP + H_2O \rightarrow XMP + PP_i + H^+}
\]

Thus, it is a **nucleoside-triphosphate pyrophosphatase** that removes the diphosphate group from aberrant purine nucleotides. The reaction is **Mg²⁺-dependent**, with homologous enzymes reported to bind one Mg²⁺ ion per subunit.

### Biological role

This is likely a **nucleotide-pool sanitizing enzyme**. By converting dITP, ITP, and XTP to their corresponding monophosphates, it limits incorporation of abnormal bases into DNA or RNA and helps prevent genome or transcriptome damage. Homologous proteins are generally annotated as **cytosolic homodimers**.

The assignment is strongly supported by homology and domain evidence, although the exact substrate preference of this particular sequence would require direct biochemical testing.
