## Functional summary

**This 101-amino-acid protein is very likely HIV-1 Tat**, the viral transcriptional activator. It matches reviewed HIV-1 Tat proteins at **80.8–83.2% identity across nearly the entire sequence**, and the Tat domain (PF00539) was detected at residues 2–62.

Tat’s primary role is to increase production of full-length viral transcripts. It recognizes the **TAR RNA** structure on nascent HIV transcripts and recruits **P-TEFb (cyclin T1–CDK9)**. CDK9 then promotes RNA polymerase II phosphorylation and more efficient transcription elongation. Tat is also annotated as promoting proviral chromatin remodeling and influencing host-cell gene expression.

**Localization:** Reference annotations place Tat in the host-cell **nucleus and nucleolus**, as well as the **cytoplasm**. It can also be **secreted** and taken up by neighboring cells. These localizations describe known Tat proteins; they are not direct measurements for this exact sequence.

**Associated biological processes** include positive regulation of HIV transcription, RNA polymerase II transcription elongation, proviral chromatin remodeling, and modulation of host-cell responses.

**Evidence note:** The identification is strongly supported by sequence homology and the detected Tat domain. The functional and localization details are transferred from highly similar, reviewed Tat references, so they should be regarded as likely annotations rather than experiments on this specific sequence.
