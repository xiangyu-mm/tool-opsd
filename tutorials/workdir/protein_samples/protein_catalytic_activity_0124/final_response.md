## Functional identification

The 329-aa protein is most consistent with **prostaglandin reductase 1 (PTGR1)**, an NAD(P)H-dependent oxidoreductase in the **zinc-dependent alcohol dehydrogenase/reductase superfamily**.

Evidence:

- 329 residues; predicted mass ≈ **35.8 kDa**
- Full-length similarity to reviewed PTGR1 homologs:
  - **63.1% identity**
  - **100% coverage**
  - E-value **7.2 × 10⁻¹⁴⁹**
- Pfam domains characteristic of the family:
  - ADH_N_2
  - ADH_zinc_N
  - ADH_zinc_N_2

## Principal catalytic reaction

The enzyme most likely catalyzes NADPH-dependent reduction of the 13,14 double bond of 15-oxo-prostaglandins:

\[
\text{15-oxo-prostaglandin} + \text{NADPH} + H^+
\rightarrow
\text{13,14-dihydro-15-oxo-prostaglandin} + \text{NADP}^+
\]

For example:

\[
\text{15-oxo-PGE}_2 + \text{NADPH} + H^+
\rightarrow
\text{13,14-dihydro-15-oxo-PGE}_2 + \text{NADP}^+
\]

This activity is commonly annotated as **15-oxoprostaglandin 13-reductase activity**, associated with **EC 1.3.1.48**.

## Additional likely activities

Based on the reviewed homologs, the enzyme may also:

- Reduce 15-oxo-PGE₁, 15-oxo-PGF₁α, and 15-oxo-PGF₂α
- Reduce 15-oxo-lipoxin A₄ to 13,14-dihydro-15-oxo-lipoxin A₄
- Reduce α,β-unsaturated aldehydes and ketones, including medium-chain alkenals
- Inactivate lipid-derived electrophiles such as 4-hydroxy-2-nonenal
- Oxidize leukotriene B₄ at C12 to produce 12-oxo-leukotriene B₄, likely as a secondary reaction

## Summary

**This sequence encodes a cytosolic NADPH-dependent prostaglandin/lipid-aldehyde reductase, most likely PTGR1. Its principal role is reductive metabolic inactivation of inflammatory eicosanoids, especially the NADPH-dependent conversion of 15-oxo-prostaglandins into their 13,14-dihydro products.**

The family assignment is strongly supported by sequence homology and domain architecture, while the exact substrate range and relative catalytic efficiencies remain predictions from homologs rather than direct measurements for this specific sequence.
