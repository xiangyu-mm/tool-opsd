## Functional assignment

The 193-residue protein is most likely a **Ham1p-like dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase**, corresponding to **EC 3.6.1.66**.

### Evidence

- It contains a nearly full-length **Ham1p_like domain (Pfam PF01725)**.
- It is highly similar to reviewed bacterial dITP/XTP pyrophosphatases:
  - **72.6% identity** over 98.4% of the sequence to UniProt A6H0L2.
  - Additional homologs show 60–72% identity over essentially the full length.
- These homologs are annotated as **Mg²⁺-dependent purine nucleoside-triphosphate pyrophosphatases**.
- The sequence is small and acidic, consistent with a soluble nucleotide “house-cleaning” enzyme.

## Catalytic activity

The enzyme hydrolyzes unusual or noncanonical purine nucleoside triphosphates, especially **dITP** and **XTP**, producing the corresponding monophosphate and inorganic pyrophosphate:

\[
\mathrm{dITP + H_2O \rightarrow dIMP + PP_i + H^+}
\]

\[
\mathrm{XTP + H_2O \rightarrow XMP + PP_i + H^+}
\]

It may also hydrolyze ITP:

\[
\mathrm{ITP + H_2O \rightarrow IMP + PP_i + H^+}
\]

The reaction is reported to require or be supported by **Mg²⁺**, which coordinates the nucleotide phosphate groups and facilitates cleavage of the terminal phosphoanhydride bond.

## Biological role

This enzyme likely functions as a **nucleotide-pool sanitation enzyme**. By removing dITP, XTP, and related noncanonical purine triphosphates, it reduces their accidental incorporation into DNA or RNA and thereby helps prevent mutagenic or genome-damaging lesions.

A **homodimeric organization** is also supported by the characterized homologs, although the oligomeric state of this particular sequence has not been experimentally established.

### Confidence and limitation

The assignment is strongly supported by full-length homology and the characteristic Ham1p-like domain. However, the exact substrate preference of the queried protein has not been directly measured; dITP/XTP/ITP specificity is inferred from closely related, curated homologs.
