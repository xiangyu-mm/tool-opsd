## Functional assessment

The 594-aa sequence is most consistent with **NADPH-dependent diflavin oxidoreductase 1 (NDOR1)**. It matches a reviewed zebrafish NDOR1 over its full length (65% identity) and human NDOR1 at 57.1% identity; flavodoxin-, FAD-binding-, and NAD-binding-domain matches are also consistent with this assignment.

NDOR1 is an **electron-transfer enzyme**: it uses **NADPH** as the electron donor and transfers electrons through its **FAD and FMN** cofactors to a protein-bound **[2Fe–2S] cluster**. In the cytosolic iron–sulfur (CIA) assembly machinery, this supports reduction of the CIAPIN1 cluster and, in homolog annotations, NDOR1 can also reduce the CISD1 cluster.

**Overall reaction:**

\[
2\,\text{oxidized [2Fe–2S]-protein} + \text{NADPH}
\rightarrow
2\,\text{reduced [2Fe–2S]-protein} + \text{NADP}^{+} + \text{H}^{+}
\]

This is annotated broadly as **EC 1.18.1.-** (Rhea mapping reported as RHEA:67716). The immediate acceptor is a protein-bound iron–sulfur cluster, rather than a defined small-molecule substrate.

**Evidence caveat:** The activity and reaction are supported by close, reviewed homologs—not demonstrated experimentally for this exact query sequence. Thus NDOR1 is a strong sequence-based assignment, while exact acceptor specificity for this protein remains unverified.
