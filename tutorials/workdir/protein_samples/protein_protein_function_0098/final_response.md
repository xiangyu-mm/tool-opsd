## Functional summary

The 150-residue protein is a **D-aminoacyl-tRNA deacylase** (**EC 3.1.1.96**), also known as a D-Tyr-tRNA(Tyr) deacylase.

### Evidence

- The sequence is an **exact full-length match** to reviewed UniProt protein **A1BI65** from *Chlorobium phaeobacteroides*.
- It contains the characteristic **Tyr_Deacylase domain** (**Pfam PF02580**, residues 4–145).
- The conserved **Gly–cisPro motif near residues 138–139** is associated with discrimination against correctly charged L-aminoacyl-tRNAs.

### Molecular function

The enzyme hydrolyzes mischarged aminoacyl-tRNAs:

\[
\text{D-aminoacyl-tRNA} + H_2O \rightarrow \text{D-amino acid} + \text{tRNA}
\]

It also acts on **glycyl-tRNA(Ala)** and related mischarged species, producing free glycine and regenerating tRNA(Ala). This is an **editing/proofreading reaction**, not conventional protein-substrate catalysis: the enzyme recognizes the tRNA-linked substrate and removes inappropriate amino acids.

### Localization and assembly

- **Subcellular localization:** cytoplasm  
- **Likely oligomeric state:** homodimer  
- The sequence lacks features expected for a secreted or membrane protein and is consistent with a small soluble cytoplasmic enzyme.

### Biological processes

The protein likely participates in:

- **Aminoacyl-tRNA quality control**
- **Correction of D-aminoacyl-tRNA and glycine-mischarged tRNA species**
- **Protection against mistranslation**
- **Maintenance of protein L-amino-acid homochirality**
- **D-amino acid catabolic/recycling processes**

Overall, this is best classified as a **small cytoplasmic tRNA-editing enzyme that safeguards translational fidelity by removing D-amino acids and other inappropriate amino acids from mischarged tRNAs**.
