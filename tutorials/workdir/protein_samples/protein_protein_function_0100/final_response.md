## Functional summary

The sequence is **canine aldehyde dehydrogenase 3A1 (ALDH3A1)**. It is 453 amino acids long and exactly matches the reviewed *Canis lupus familiaris* UniProt entry **A3RF36** (100% sequence identity and coverage). The human ortholog, **ALDH3A1/P30838**, is 88.5% identical across the full sequence.

ALDH3A1 is an NAD(P)-dependent aldehyde dehydrogenase. It oxidizes aldehydes to their corresponding carboxylic acids, helping detoxify reactive aldehydes. Reference annotations describe activity toward medium- and long-chain fatty aldehydes and a preference for aromatic aldehyde substrates, but those substrate details should be treated as annotation-based rather than established specifically for this query by the sequence analysis.

- **Subcellular localization:** Primarily **cytoplasmic**; the human ortholog is also annotated in the cytosol. Other locations reported for the human ortholog should not automatically be assigned to this sequence.
- **Biological processes:** Most directly supported are **aldehyde metabolism** and **xenobiotic metabolism**. Broader ALDH3A1 annotations also connect the family to metabolism of alcohol-derived acetaldehyde, corticosteroids, biogenic amines, neurotransmitters, and lipid-peroxidation products. In corneal tissue, ALDH3A1 has been proposed to help protect against ultraviolet-associated damage; this is a contextual, similarity-based annotation rather than evidence from this sequence analysis.
- **Sequence/domain evidence:** The sequence contains a strong aldehyde-dehydrogenase domain signature (PF00171), consistent with the identification.

**Confidence and caveat:** The protein identification is high confidence because of the exact match to a reviewed reference sequence. The specific physiological substrates and protective roles are broader functional annotations and are not independently demonstrated by sequence matching alone.
