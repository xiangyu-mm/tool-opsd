## Functional summary

The 104-residue protein is most consistent with a **pyrimidine/purine nucleoside phosphorylase** of the **Ppnp family**.

### Evidence

- It contains a highly significant **Ppnp Pfam domain** (`PF06865.18`, residues 11–102; i-E-value \(4.4\times10^{-39}\)).
- It shows approximately **59–62% sequence identity** across nearly the full length to reviewed bacterial nucleoside-phosphorylase proteins.
- The closest curated homologs are annotated with **EC 2.4.2.1** and **EC 2.4.2.2**.

### Catalyzed reaction

The enzyme catalyzes reversible **phosphorolysis of nucleosides**:

\[
\text{D-ribonucleoside} + \text{phosphate}
\rightleftharpoons
\text{free nucleobase} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

Examples include:

\[
\text{adenosine} + P_i
\rightleftharpoons
\text{adenine} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

\[
\text{guanosine} + P_i
\rightleftharpoons
\text{guanine} + \alpha\text{-D-ribose-1-phosphate}
\]

Related reactions may involve **uridine, cytidine, thymidine, inosine, and xanthosine**.

### Interpretation

The best assignment is therefore:

> **Broad-specificity nucleoside phosphorylase involved in nucleoside salvage, cleaving the glycosidic bond by phosphate-dependent phosphorolysis.**

The family-level catalytic activity is strongly supported by domain architecture and full-length homology. However, the exact substrate preference of this particular sequence—whether it favors purine, pyrimidine, or multiple nucleoside classes—cannot be established from sequence evidence alone and would require biochemical testing.
