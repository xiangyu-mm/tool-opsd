## Functional identification

The 703-residue protein is **polyribonucleotide phosphorylase (PNPase)**, also called **polyribonucleotide nucleotidyltransferase**. This assignment is very strong: the sequence is an exact match to the reviewed *Clostridium acetobutylicum* PNPase UniProt entry Q97I45.

PNPase is a bacterial RNA-processing enzyme that performs **processive 3′→5′ phosphorolysis of single-stranded RNA**:

\[
\text{RNA}_{n+1} + \text{phosphate}
\rightarrow
\text{RNA}_n + \text{ribonucleoside diphosphate}
\]

It therefore functions primarily as a **3′→5′ RNA exonuclease** and participates in **mRNA degradation and RNA maturation**. The reaction requires **Mg²⁺**.

## Sequence and domain evidence

The architecture is characteristic of PNPase:

- Two **RNase PH-related domains**, forming the catalytic core
- A PNPase-associated central domain
- A **KH RNA-binding domain** near residues ~555–612
- A C-terminal **S1 RNA-binding domain** near residues ~618–689

The KH and S1 domains likely help recognize, bind, and channel RNA substrates into the catalytic core.

The protein is expected to contribute to:

- Bacterial mRNA turnover and degradation
- General RNA processing
