## Functional assignment

The 425-residue protein is most consistent with a **pyridoxal-5′-phosphate (PLP)-dependent kynureninase** (**EC 3.7.1.3**).

Evidence includes:

- ~51–54% sequence identity to reviewed bacterial kynureninases across nearly the full protein length.
- Conserved **Aminotran_5** and **KYNU_C** domains.
- Homolog annotations specifying PLP dependence and kynureninase activity.
- Corresponding curated Rhea reactions for kynurenine cleavage.

## Catalyzed reactions

The primary reaction is:

\[
\text{L-kynurenine} + \mathrm{H_2O}
\rightarrow \text{anthranilate} + \text{L-alanine} + \mathrm{H^+}
\]

It can also act on 3-hydroxy-L-kynurenine:

\[
\text{3-hydroxy-L-kynurenine} + \mathrm{H_2O}
\rightarrow \text{3-hydroxyanthranilate} + \text{L-alanine} + \mathrm{H^+}
\]

In mechanistic terms, kynureninase uses PLP to facilitate cleavage of the kynurenine substrate, releasing the corresponding anthranilate derivative and the amino-acid fragment L-alanine.

## Biological context

This activity participates in:

- **L-tryptophan/kynurenine degradation**
- **Anthranilate production**
- In microorganisms, the kynurenine route toward **quinolinate and NAD⁺ biosynthesis**

The assignment is strongly supported by homology and domain evidence, but the exact substrate preference of this particular sequence has not been experimentally tested here.
