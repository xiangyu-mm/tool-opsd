## Functional identification

This protein is almost certainly the **alpha subunit of bacterial F-type ATP synthase** (**AtpA**).

### Evidence

- **Length:** 501 amino acids; approximately **54.4 kDa**, matching bacterial ATP synthase alpha subunits.
- **Exact homology:** The sequence is an exact full-length match to reviewed UniProt entry **P95787**, annotated as ATP synthase subunit alpha from *Streptococcus mutans*.
- **Conserved Pfam domains:**
  - ATP-synt_ab_N: residues 26–92
  - ATP-synt_ab: residues 149–364
  - ATP-synt_ab_C: residues 371–492
- The sequence lacks an apparent transmembrane segment, consistent with the alpha subunit being part of the soluble/peripheral **F₁ sector**, rather than the membrane-spanning F₀ proton channel.

## Likely cellular function

The protein forms part of the membrane-associated **F₀F₁ proton-translocating ATP synthase**. Three alpha subunits alternate with three beta subunits to form the central **α₃β₃ ring**.

Its main roles are to:

- Provide structural support for the F₁ catalytic head.
- Bind ATP/ADP at regulatory nucleotide-binding sites.
- Couple conformational changes and rotation of the central stalk to the catalytic beta subunits.
- Participate in proton-gradient-driven ATP synthesis.

The **beta subunits perform most of the nucleotide phosphorylation chemistry**; therefore, the alpha subunit should be considered primarily a **regulatory and structural nucleotide-binding component**, not an independently acting ATP synthase enzyme.

### Complex-level reaction

The associated F-type ATP synthase catalyzes, in the ATP-producing direction:

**ADP + phosphate + proton motive force → ATP**

The reported EC/Rhea reaction applies to the complete multisubunit ATP synthase complex, not to this alpha subunit alone.

## Conclusion

**Most likely function:** bacterial F-type ATP synthase subunit alpha (**AtpA**), a soluble F₁-sector structural/regulatory subunit involved in ATP synthesis and ATP/ADP binding. The assignment is supported by exact sequence identity and the characteristic three-domain ATP-synthase-alpha architecture, so confidence is **very high**.
