## Functional identification

The 193-residue protein is strongly identified as **HIV-1 Vif (virion infectivity factor)**.

Evidence:

- **85.5% sequence identity** to reviewed HIV-1 Vif proteins across the full sequence.
- Complete match to the **Vif Pfam domain (PF00559)**.
- Length and highly basic character are consistent with HIV-1 Vif:
  - Predicted mass: **~22.4 kDa**
  - Theoretical pI: **~9.87**
  - Estimated charge at pH 7: **~+14**

## Functional role

Vif is a viral immune-evasion protein that counteracts host **APOBEC3F and APOBEC3G** cytidine deaminases. It acts as an adaptor/substrate-recognition component by recruiting a host **Cullin-5/RING E3 ubiquitin-ligase complex**, including elongin B/C and CBFB.

This leads to:

1. **Polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation** of APOBEC3F and APOBEC3G.
2. Reduced APOBEC-mediated cytidine deamination and mutagenesis of viral DNA during reverse transcription.
3. Enhanced HIV replication and production of infectious virions.
4. Additional inhibition of APOBEC3G activity that may occur independently of degradation.

Vif may also affect viral particle assembly, viral nucleoprotein-core stability, RNA interactions, and host-cell signaling, although these effects are secondary to its APOBEC-antagonist function.

## Cellular and viral processes

The protein is involved primarily in:

- Viral immune evasion
- HIV replication and infectious-virus production
- Host ubiquitin/proteasome pathways
- Regulation of APOBEC3 antiviral activity
- Viral particle assembly and incorporation
- Potential host-cell-cycle effects, including G2 arrest

## Subcellular localization

Vif is expected to be a **soluble/peripheral membrane-associated protein**, rather than a transmembrane protein. Reported locations include:

- **Host-cell cytoplasm**
- Cytoplasmic face of cellular membranes
- Association with viral assembly components
- **Virion-associated** after incorporation into HIV particles

Its basic sequence may support interactions with RNA and acidic protein partners. The available annotations also associate Vif with RNA binding and metal-ion binding, but it should not be interpreted as a conventional catalytic enzyme.

## Overall summary

This sequence encodes **HIV-1 Vif**, a small, basic viral accessory protein that hijacks the host Cullin-5 E3 ubiquitin-ligase machinery to eliminate APOBEC3F and APOBEC3G antiviral restriction factors. It functions mainly in the cytoplasm and at peripheral membranes, with a fraction incorporated into virions. The identification is highly reliable from full-length homology and domain evidence, although detailed interaction or localization behavior can vary among HIV-1 strains.
