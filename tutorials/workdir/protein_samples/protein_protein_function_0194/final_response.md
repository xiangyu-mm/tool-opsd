## Functional identification

The sequence is identified with very high confidence as **NADH dehydrogenase subunit 2**, also called a mitochondrial **Complex I membrane-arm subunit**. It is an exact sequence match to the reviewed *Neurospora crassa* protein Q35140.

The many hydrophobic segments are consistent with a protein embedded extensively in a biological membrane rather than a soluble enzyme.

## Functional role

This protein is a **core membrane component of mitochondrial respiratory-chain Complex I**, also known as NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase.

Complex I:

1. Accepts electrons originating from NADH.
2. Transfers those electrons through the respiratory chain toward ubiquinone.
3. Uses the released energy to contribute to proton translocation across the mitochondrial inner membrane.
4. Helps generate the proton-motive force used by ATP synthase to produce ATP.

The individual subunit is not expected to carry out the complete NADH dehydrogenase reaction independently. Its role is structural and membrane-associated within the larger Complex I assembly, contributing to the membrane arm and its coupling to respiratory electron transfer.

## Cellular processes potentially involving this protein

The protein is therefore expected to participate in:

- **Mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation**
- **Respiratory electron transport**
- **NADH-dependent ubiquinone reduction by Complex I**
- **Generation of the mitochondrial proton gradient**
- **ATP synthesis coupled to electron transport**
- **Maintenance of mitochondrial energy metabolism**

Loss or dysfunction of this subunit would be expected to impair Complex I assembly or activity, reduce respiratory-chain electron flow, and compromise mitochondrial ATP production. That consequence is inferred from its Complex I assignment rather than directly demonstrated for this particular sequence.
