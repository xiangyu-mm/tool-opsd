## Functional assignment

The 346-residue protein is **phosphoribosylformylglycinamidine cyclo-ligase**, also known as **PurM**.

**Confidence: very high.** The sequence is an exact match to reviewed UniProt protein Q7N3F7, with 100% identity and complete sequence coverage. It also contains the characteristic **AIRS** and **AIRS_C** domains:

- AIRS/PF00586: residues 60–164

The enzyme is predicted to be a soluble cytosolic protein of approximately **36.9 kDa**.

## Catalytic reaction

PurM catalyzes the ATP-dependent cyclization of **FGAM**—formylglycinamidine ribonucleotide—during de novo purine biosynthesis:

\[
\text{FGAM} + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{AIR} + \text{ADP} + \text{P}_{i} + \text{H}^{+}
\]

More explicitly:

\[
\text{2-formamido-N}^{1}\text{-(5-O-phospho-}\beta\text{-D-ribosyl)acetamidine}
+ \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{5-amino-1-(5-phospho-}\beta\text{-D-ribosyl)imidazole}
+ \text{ADP} + \text{phosphate} + \text{H}^{+}
\]

The product, **AIR** (5-aminoimidazole ribonucleotide), is an early imidazole-containing intermediate in the pathway to IMP and subsequently AMP/GMP.

## Pathway role

This is the **second and final step of the FGAM-to-AIR segment** of de novo IMP biosynthesis. Mechanistically, ATP supplies the energy needed to promote intramolecular cyclization and formation of the imidazole ring.

This protein is **PurM, an ATP-dependent phosphoribosylformylglycinamidine cyclo-ligase that converts FGAM into AIR by forming the imidazole ring during de novo purine biosynthesis**.
