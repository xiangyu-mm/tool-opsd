## Functional annotation

The protein is most likely a **bacterial RibA-like GTP cyclohydrolase II**, probably a **RibBA-type fusion protein**.

### Evidence

- **Length:** 301 aa; predicted mass ≈34.4 kDa.
- **C-terminal domain:** Strong match to **GTP_cyclohydro2 (PF00925)** at residues **118–277**.
- **Homology:** The C-terminal region matches annotated RibA proteins at approximately **50–56% amino-acid identity**, with highly significant E-values.
- **N-terminal domain:** A **PRA-CH domain (PF01502)** is detected at residues **27–101**, indicating an additional fused catalytic or accessory module.
- The strongest homologs are annotated as **RibA/GTP cyclohydrolase II** proteins.

### Likely molecular function

The C-terminal RibA domain likely catalyzes the first committed step of **riboflavin/vitamin B₂ biosynthesis**:

\[
\text{GTP} \rightarrow \text{riboflavin-pathway intermediates}
\]

Specifically, GTP cyclohydrolase II produces intermediates including **3,4-dihydroxy-2-butanone 4-phosphate** and a pterin-derived compound. The precise role of the N-terminal PRA-CH-like domain cannot be established confidently from the available evidence alone, but its fusion with RibA suggests a multifunctional or lineage-specific riboflavin-biosynthesis protein.

### Subcellular localization

Most likely **cytosolic/soluble**.

- No obvious N-terminal signal peptide was detected.
- The sequence lacks long hydrophobic segments characteristic of transmembrane helices.
- Its size and enzyme-like domain architecture are consistent with a soluble bacterial metabolic enzyme.

### Biological processes

Likely involved in:

- **Riboflavin biosynthetic process**
- **GTP metabolic process**
- **Biosynthesis of flavin cofactors**, including FMN and FAD indirectly

### Confidence and limitations

- **High confidence:** The C-terminal region is a RibA/GTP cyclohydrolase II domain.
- **Moderate confidence:** Participation in riboflavin biosynthesis and cytosolic localization.
- **Lower confidence:** Exact function of the N-terminal PRA-CH domain and whether the complete protein is formally annotated as a RibBA fusion.

**Overall summary:** This is most plausibly a soluble bacterial **RibA-like GTP cyclohydrolase II fusion protein**, functioning in the early steps of **riboflavin biosynthesis**.
