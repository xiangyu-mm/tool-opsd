## Functional identification

The 294-residue protein is **acetylglutamate kinase** (**EC 2.7.2.8**), an **AA-kinase family** enzyme.

### Catalytic reaction

\[
\text{N-acetyl-L-glutamate} + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow
\text{N-acetyl-L-glutamyl 5-phosphate} + \text{ADP}
\]

The enzyme transfers the terminal phosphate of ATP to the **5-carboxyl group of N-acetyl-L-glutamate**, forming the activated intermediate **N-acetyl-L-glutamyl 5-phosphate**.

### Biological role

This is step 2 of the bacterial/archaeal route from glutamate to arginine:

- It participates in **L-arginine biosynthesis**.
- The product is subsequently used to form **N-acetylornithine**.
- The protein is annotated as **cytoplasmic** and ATP-binding.

### Evidence and confidence

- The sequence is **294 aa** long.
- It has an **AA_kinase domain** (Pfam PF00696).
- It is an **exact full-length sequence match** to the reviewed acetylglutamate kinase A6UR56, with 100% identity and full coverage.
- Close reviewed homologs independently have the same EC number and reaction annotation.

**Conclusion:** This protein is confidently identified as **acetylglutamate kinase**, catalyzing ATP-dependent phosphorylation of N-acetyl-L-glutamate to N-acetyl-L-glutamyl 5-phosphate.
