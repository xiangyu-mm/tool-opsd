The 214-residue protein is most likely a **Ham1p-like nucleotide pyrophosphatase**, specifically a **dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase** involved in sanitizing the cellular nucleotide pool.

- **Theoretical pI:** 4.83

- **Likely localization:** cytosolic  
- **Likely oligomeric state:** homodimer, based on characterized homologs

### Likely enzymatic activity

The protein probably hydrolyzes noncanonical purine nucleoside triphosphates:

- dITP → dIMP + diphosphate  
- ITP → IMP + diphosphate  
- XTP → XMP + diphosphate  

These reactions correspond to **EC 3.6.1.66** and are associated with Rhea reactions **RHEA:28342, RHEA:29399, and RHEA:28610**.

### Biological role

This is likely a **nucleotide-pool quality-control enzyme**. By removing aberrant purine nucleotides such as dITP, ITP, and XTP, it helps prevent their incorporation into DNA or RNA and thereby reduces potential mutagenic or genome-damaging events.

The family assignment is strong because both the Pfam match and the high-coverage similarity to reviewed enzymes agree. The exact substrate preference and catalytic properties are inferred from homologs rather than experimentally demonstrated for this sequence, so the safest annotation is:

> **Putative Mg²⁺-dependent Ham1p-like dITP/XTP/ITP pyrophosphatase involved in sanitizing the intracellular nucleotide pool.**
