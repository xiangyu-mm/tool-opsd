## Functional identification

The protein is **magnesium chelatase subunit ChlI**, also called the **38-kDa magnesium-chelatase subunit**.

### Evidence

- **Length:** 346 amino acids; approximately 38 kDa.
- **Exact full-length sequence match:** reviewed UniProt entry **O50312** from *Chlorobaculum parvum*.
- **P-loop NTP-binding motif:** `GHRGTGKST`, consistent with an ATPase.
- **Detected domains:**
  - **Mg_chelatase domain** (Pfam PF01078)
  - **AAA_lid_2 domain** (Pfam PF17863)
- **Curated function:** ATP-binding subunit of magnesium chelatase; EC **6.6.1.1**.

### Molecular role

ChlI is the ATP-dependent motor/regulatory subunit of the magnesium-chelatase complex. The complete complex uses ATP to insert **Mg²⁺ into protoporphyrin IX**, producing **Mg-protoporphyrin IX**:

\[
\text{protoporphyrin IX} + \mathrm{Mg^{2+}} + \mathrm{ATP}
\rightarrow \text{Mg-protoporphyrin IX} + \mathrm{ADP} + \mathrm{P_i}
\]

The protein itself is not expected to perform the entire reaction independently; it works with the other magnesium-chelatase subunits, commonly ChlD and ChlH.

### Biological process

The strongest assignment is contribution to **bacteriochlorophyll biosynthesis**, particularly in photosynthetic bacteria such as *Chlorobaculum*. More generally, ChlI homologs participate in **chlorophyll or bacteriochlorophyll biosynthesis** by catalyzing the first committed magnesium-insertion step in tetrapyrrole pigment formation.

**Summary:** This sequence encodes the ATP-utilizing **ChlI subunit of magnesium chelatase**, a component of the Mg-chelatase complex required for converting protoporphyrin IX into Mg-protoporphyrin IX during chlorophyll/bacteriochlorophyll biosynthesis.
