## Functional prediction

This 393-amino-acid protein is **PurT, formate-dependent phosphoribosylglycinamide formyltransferase** (**EC 6.3.1.21**).

The assignment is highly reliable because the sequence is an **exact full-length match** to reviewed Swiss-Prot PurT proteins, with 100% identity and coverage. It also contains the expected **ATP-grasp domain** and C-terminal PurT-related region.

### Catalytic reaction

PurT catalyzes the ATP-dependent formylation of GAR:

\[
\text{GAR} + \text{formate} + \text{ATP}
\rightarrow \text{FGAR} + \text{ADP} + \text{phosphate}
\]

where:

- **GAR** = 5-phosphoribosylglycinamide  
- **FGAR** = 5-phosphoribosyl-N-formylglycinamide  

The enzyme likely uses **Mg²⁺** for ATP-dependent catalysis. In the formate route, formate is supplied by **PurU**, which hydrolyzes 10-formyltetrahydrofolate.

### Likely biological pathway

The protein participates in **de novo purine biosynthesis**, specifically the early conversion of GAR to FGAR in the pathway leading to **IMP**, the precursor of AMP and GMP.

### Summary

The protein is a bacterial **ATP-dependent GAR formyltransferase (PurT)** involved in **formate-dependent de novo purine/IMP biosynthesis**. It activates and formylates GAR using ATP and formate, producing FGAR for subsequent purine-ring assembly.
