## Functional assignment

The 428-residue protein is most likely a **bacterial kynureninase**, a **pyridoxal-5′-phosphate (PLP)-dependent lyase**.

### Evidence

- Length: **428 aa**
- Predicted molecular mass: **47.8 kDa**
- Strong similarity to curated kynureninases:
  - **67–69% amino-acid identity**
  - Approximately **96% sequence coverage**
  - E-values below **3 × 10⁻²⁰²**
- Conserved domain architecture:
  - **Aminotran_5 domain:** residues ~48–256
  - **KYNU_C domain:** residues ~328–418

This combination strongly supports kynureninase rather than a general aminotransferase.

## Catalytic activity

The predicted activity is **EC 3.7.1.3, kynureninase**. It likely catalyzes PLP-dependent cleavage of:

\[
\text{L-kynurenine} + H_2O
\rightarrow
\text{anthranilate} + \text{L-alanine} + H^+
\]

It may also act on 3-hydroxy-L-kynurenine:

\[
\text{3-hydroxy-L-kynurenine} + H_2O
\rightarrow
\text{3-hydroxyanthranilate} + \text{L-alanine} + H^+
\]

The enzyme is expected to contain a **PLP-linked catalytic lysine**, as observed in closely related curated kynureninases.

## Cofactor

- **Pyridoxal 5′-phosphate (PLP)**

## Subcellular localization

The most likely localization is the **bacterial cytoplasm**.

This is inferred from the localization of closely related reviewed kynureninases and from the absence of obvious sequence features suggesting secretion or membrane insertion. Localization is not directly experimentally established for this query protein.

## Biological processes and pathways

The protein may participate in:

1. **L-kynurenine degradation**
2. **L-tryptophan catabolism**
3. **Anthranilate production**
4. **Bacterial NAD⁺ biosynthesis**, through conversion of kynurenine-pathway intermediates toward quinolinate
5. **Quinolinate biosynthesis**

## Oligomeric state

Closely related kynureninases are annotated as **homodimers**, so a homodimeric structure is plausible, but this remains an inference for the query sequence.

## Summary

**Most likely function:** cytoplasmic, PLP-dependent bacterial kynureninase that cleaves L-kynurenine—and potentially 3-hydroxy-L-kynurenine—to produce anthranilate or 3-hydroxyanthranilate plus L-alanine. It likely functions in kynurenine/tryptophan degradation and may feed bacterial quinolinate and NAD⁺ biosynthesis. The assignment is strongly supported by near-full-length similarity to curated kynureninases and the characteristic Aminotran_5/KYNU_C domain architecture, although direct biochemical confirmation of substrate specificity and oligomeric state is still needed.
