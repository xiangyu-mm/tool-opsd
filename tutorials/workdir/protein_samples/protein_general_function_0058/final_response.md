The 761-aa protein is **mitochondrial elongation factor G (EF-G; mitochondrial translational GTPase)**, most likely from a yeast such as *Candida dubliniensis*.

This assignment is highly confident because the sequence is an **exact full-length match** to curated UniProt protein **B9W9T4** and is 98.2% identical to the curated *Candida albicans* mitochondrial EF-G.

## Function

Mitochondrial EF-G uses the energy of **GTP hydrolysis** to drive the translocation phase of mitochondrial translation. During this process, it promotes:

1. Movement of peptidyl-tRNA from the A site to the P site  
2. Movement of deacylated tRNA from the P site to the E site  
3. Coordinated movement of mRNA  
4. Conversion of the mitochondrial ribosome from the PRE- to the POST-translocation state  

Thus, the protein is required for **mitochondrial protein biosynthesis and polypeptide-chain elongation**.

## Overall conclusion

This is a **mitochondrial EF-G homolog**, a soluble, multidomain GTPase that functions as the mitochondrial ribosomal translocase. The exact sequence match and conserved EF-G domain architecture make the functional assignment highly reliable. The available evidence does not establish a specific oligomeric state or provide a separately retrieved catalytic reaction equation, although GTP-dependent ribosomal translocation is strongly supported.
