## Functional assignment

The sequence is most consistent with a **dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase**, also called a **noncanonical purine nucleoside-triphosphate pyrophosphatase**.

- **Likely EC:** 3.6.1.66  
- **Length:** 191 residues  
- **Best homologs:** reviewed dITP/XTP pyrophosphatases  
- **Similarity:** 51–54% identity over ~99% of the sequence  
- **Likely cofactor:** Mg²⁺  
- **Likely oligomeric state:** homodimer, based on homolog annotations  

## Catalytic activity

The enzyme hydrolyzes the diphosphate bond of abnormal purine nucleoside triphosphates, producing the corresponding monophosphate and inorganic pyrophosphate:

\[
\text{NTP} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \text{NMP} + \mathrm{PP_i} + \mathrm{H^+}
\]

The principal predicted reactions are:

\[
\text{XTP} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \text{XMP} + \mathrm{PP_i} + \mathrm{H^+}
\]

\[
\text{dITP} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \text{dIMP} + \mathrm{PP_i} + \mathrm{H^+}
\]

\[
\text{ITP} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \text{IMP} + \mathrm{PP_i} + \mathrm{H^+}
\]

Here, XTP is xanthosine triphosphate, dITP is deoxyinosine triphosphate, and ITP is inosine triphosphate.

## Biological role

This is likely a **nucleotide-pool sanitizing enzyme**. By removing noncanonical purine triphosphates before they are incorporated into DNA or RNA, it helps reduce mismatches, mutations, and other forms of genome damage.

## Confidence and limitation

The assignment is strongly supported by near-full-length similarity to several reviewed proteins with the same function and by matching reaction annotations (Rhea RHEA:28342, RHEA:28610, and RHEA:29399). However, the sequence contains several ambiguous `X` residues, so exact catalytic-residue analysis and physicochemical calculations were not possible. The specific substrate preference of this particular protein remains an inference from homology rather than a direct biochemical measurement.
