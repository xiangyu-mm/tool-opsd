## Functional identification

The 138-residue protein is strongly predicted to be **interleukin-4 (IL-4)**, a small secreted cytokine.

Evidence includes:

- **N-terminal signal peptide-like hydrophobic region**, consistent with secretion through the classical secretory pathway.
- **Pfam IL-4 domain** (PF00727) spanning approximately residues 28–134.
- Strong similarity to reviewed IL-4 proteins, including **71–74% sequence identity across ~98% of the sequence** and extremely significant E-values.
- Predicted molecular mass: approximately **15.5 kDa**; theoretical pI: **9.78**, consistent with the basic character of IL-4-family cytokines.

## Functional role

IL-4 is an extracellular cytokine and growth factor that binds the **IL-4 receptor complex** and activates receptor-associated **JAK–STAT signaling**, particularly STAT6-dependent responses.

Likely cellular effects include:

- Promotion of **B-cell activation and differentiation**
- Induction of **IgE and IgG1 production**
- Increased expression of **MHC class II** and the low-affinity IgE receptor **CD23**
- Regulation of **macrophage activation and polarization**
- Effects on **dendritic-cell differentiation and function**
- Modulation of inflammatory and immune-response gene expression

These biological functions are inferred from the strong match to curated IL-4 homologs; the exact effects may vary with species and cellular context.

## Subcellular location

The protein is expected to be:

1. Synthesized into the **endoplasmic reticulum** through its N-terminal signal peptide.
2. Processed and transported through the secretory pathway.
3. Released as a **soluble extracellular cytokine**.

It is therefore most likely localized to the **extracellular region**, where it can interact with the IL-4 receptor complex on target cells.

## Overall summary

This sequence most likely encodes a **secreted interleukin-4-family cytokine**. Its primary role is extracellular immune signaling through the IL-4 receptor and JAK–STAT pathway, influencing B-cell responses, antibody-class switching, macrophage and dendritic-cell biology, and broader immune regulation. Exact species identity, signal-peptide cleavage, and species-specific activity cannot be established from the sequence alone.
