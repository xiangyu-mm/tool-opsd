## Functional identification

The sequence is **histidine ammonia-lyase** (**HAL**, EC **4.3.1.3**), also called histidase.

This assignment is highly reliable: the 512-residue sequence is an **exact sequence match** to the reviewed *Streptomyces coelicolor* protein Q9EWW1, with additional close reviewed homologs showing 88–92% identity.

### Catalytic reaction

\[
\text{L-histidine} \rightarrow \text{trans-urocanate} + \mathrm{NH_4^+}
\]

The enzyme catalyzes a **nonoxidative β-elimination/deamination**:

- The amino group of L-histidine is eliminated as ammonium.
- A double bond is formed in the product.
- The resulting product is **trans-urocanic acid (trans-urocanate)**.
- This is an ammonia-lyase reaction, not a hydrolysis or redox reaction.

### Enzyme family and catalytic chemistry

- **Pfam:** Lyase_aromatic, PF00221  
- **Length:** 512 amino acids  
- **Molecular mass:** approximately 52.8 kDa  
- **Predicted pI:** approximately 5.67  
- **Localization:** cytoplasm  
- **Oligomerization:** homologs are annotated as homotetramers  

Histidine ammonia-lyases use an autocatalytically generated **MIO-type electrophilic prosthetic group**. The curated homolog annotation identifies the corresponding modified serine/cross-linked region, supporting the characteristic lyase mechanism of this enzyme family.

### Biological role

This enzyme catalyzes the first step of **L-histidine degradation**:

\[
\text{L-histidine} \rightarrow \text{urocanate} \rightarrow
\text{N-formimidoyl-L-glutamate} \rightarrow \text{L-glutamate}
\]

The sequence also carries an annotation for phenylalanine ammonia-lyase activity, which is common in this aromatic-lyase family; however, the strongest and most specific assignment for this exact sequence is **histidine ammonia-lyase**.

**Overall conclusion:** this is a soluble, likely tetrameric bacterial histidine ammonia-lyase that converts L-histidine to trans-urocanate and ammonium using an MIO-dependent ammonia-elimination mechanism.
