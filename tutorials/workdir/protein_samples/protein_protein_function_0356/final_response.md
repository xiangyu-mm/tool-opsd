## Molecular function

FecI is an alternative bacterial sigma factor. Sigma factors associate with the bacterial RNA polymerase core enzyme and redirect it to promoters recognized by that sigma factor.

Specifically, FecI:

1. **Binds the bacterial RNA polymerase core enzyme.**
2. **Recognizes specific promoter sequences** through its C-terminal DNA-binding region.
3. **Initiates transcription of the `fecABCDE` operon.**
4. Promotes production of the ferric citrate transport system.

The `fecABCDE` gene cluster encodes components involved in uptake of ferric citrate, an iron source. Thus, FecI functions as the transcriptional activator of a specialized iron-acquisition response.

## Subcellular localization

FecI is expected to be a **soluble cytoplasmic/nucleoid-associated protein**, where it associates with RNA polymerase and promoter DNA.

The sequence does not show a long hydrophobic transmembrane segment or a classical secretion signal, supporting a non-membrane localization. However, FecI is functionally coupled to the membrane-associated regulatory protein **FecR**. FecR interacts with the cytoplasmic N-terminal portion of FecI and regulates its activity.

Thus:

- **FecI:** soluble cytoplasmic transcription factor  
- **FecR:** membrane-associated anti-sigma regulatory partner  
- **RNA polymerase–FecI complex:** acts at target promoters in the bacterial chromosome

The strongest associated biological processes are:

- **Iron acquisition**
- **Intracellular iron ion homeostasis**
- **DNA-templated transcription initiation**
- **Regulation of DNA-templated transcription**

Functionally, FecI links environmental or extracytoplasmic iron-related signals to expression of the ferric citrate transport machinery. FecR serves as the regulatory interface controlling FecI activity; when FecI is available to associate with RNA polymerase, it activates transcription of the ferric citrate uptake genes.

This 173-residue protein is **FecI, an ECF-family alternative sigma factor involved in ferric citrate transport regulation**. It is a small soluble transcription factor that binds bacterial RNA polymerase and directs it to promoters controlling the `fecABCDE` operon. Its C-terminal sigma-70 region 4-like domain contains the promoter DNA-recognition module, while its N-terminal region participates in RNA polymerase and FecR interactions. The protein is therefore involved primarily in **iron acquisition, ferric citrate uptake, transcription initiation, and cellular iron homeostasis**.
