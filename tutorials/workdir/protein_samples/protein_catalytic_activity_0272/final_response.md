### Functional identification

The 197-residue protein is most consistent with a **dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase** (**EC 3.6.1.66**), a small bacterial “house-cleaning” enzyme that removes noncanonical purine nucleotides from the cellular nucleotide pool.

### Catalytic activity

It accelerates Mg²⁺-dependent hydrolysis of nucleoside triphosphates, cleaving the phosphoanhydride bond to produce the corresponding nucleoside monophosphate and inorganic diphosphate:

- **dITP + H₂O → dIMP + diphosphate + H⁺**  
  Rhea: **RHEA:28342**

- **XTP + H₂O → XMP + diphosphate + H⁺**  
  Rhea: **RHEA:28610**

- **ITP + H₂O → IMP + diphosphate + H⁺**  
  Rhea: **RHEA:29399**

Its strongest expected substrates are **dITP, XTP, and ITP**, with preference for the noncanonical purine nucleotides dITP and XTP.

### Evidence

The sequence shows strong similarity to reviewed dITP/XTP pyrophosphatases, including:

- **UniProt Q9S2H9** from *Streptomyces coelicolor*: 49% identity, 95% query coverage, E-value 2.15 × 10⁻⁴¹
- Additional reviewed homologs from *Streptomyces avermitilis* and *Corynebacterium efficiens*: approximately 46–49% identity and 95–99% coverage

These homologs are annotated as **Mg²⁺-dependent pyrophosphatases**, binding approximately one Mg²⁺ ion per subunit.

### Biological role

By hydrolyzing aberrant purine triphosphates before they are incorporated into nucleic acids, the enzyme likely helps prevent replication or transcription errors and associated DNA damage.

The assignment is supported by strong homology and curated annotations of multiple close homologs; the exact substrate preference has not been experimentally tested for the supplied sequence itself.
