## Functional identification

The 416-residue protein is most consistent with **N-acyl-phosphatidylethanolamine-hydrolyzing phospholipase D (NAPE-PLD)**, also known as an **NAPE-specific phospholipase D**.

This assignment is supported by strong sequence homology to reviewed mammalian NAPE-PLD proteins:

- **70–72% sequence identity**
- **83–85% query coverage**
- E-values approximately **10⁻¹⁹⁴**
- Homologs include rat, bovine, and human NAPE-PLD
- Functional annotation: **EC 3.1.4.54**
- Predicted cofactor requirement: **two Zn²⁺ ions per subunit**

### Catalyzed reaction

The enzyme hydrolyzes the terminal phosphodiester bond of an N-acyl phosphatidylethanolamine:

\[
\text{N-acyl-1,2-diacyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine}
+ H_2O
\rightarrow
\text{N-acylethanolamine}
+
\text{1,2-diacyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphate}
+ H^+
\]

In general terms:

\[
\text{NAPE} + H_2O \rightarrow \text{N-acylethanolamine} + \text{phosphatidic acid}
\]

Thus, the enzyme produces bioactive **N-acylethanolamines**, including fatty-acid ethanolamides such as signaling lipids, while generating **phosphatidic acid** from the phospholipid substrate.

### Substrate scope

The matched homologs hydrolyze:

- Diacyl NAPEs
- Alkenylacyl NAPEs
- Mainly long-chain saturated and monounsaturated NAPEs
- More limited activity toward N-acyl lyso-NAPEs

### Sequence interpretation

The sequence contains an N-terminal low-complexity, charged region followed by a conserved, globular catalytic domain. Its overall properties are compatible with a soluble/peripheral membrane-associated lipid enzyme rather than a classical multipass membrane protein. The sequence itself does not establish the exact lipid-chain specificity or cellular localization, but the very strong similarity to curated NAPE-PLD homologs supports this functional assignment.

## Functional summary

**This protein is a zinc-dependent NAPE-specific phospholipase D that converts membrane N-acyl phosphatidylethanolamines into bioactive N-acylethanolamines and phosphatidic acid.** The identification is supported by high-confidence homology to reviewed mammalian NAPE-PLD proteins, although direct biochemical confirmation for this exact sequence would still be required.
