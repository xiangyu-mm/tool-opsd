## Functional identification

The 499-residue protein is most consistent with a **pyridoxal-5′-phosphate (PLP)-dependent kynureninase**, EC **3.7.1.3**.

### Evidence

- Strong similarity to reviewed fungal kynureninases:
  - 59–61% sequence identity
  - >92% sequence coverage
  - E-values around \(10^{-197}\) to \(10^{-203}\)
- Contains domains characteristic of kynureninases:
  - **Aminotran_5 domain**: residues 113–289
  - **KYNU_C domain**: residues 367–464
- The homologous enzymes are PLP-dependent, cytosolic kynureninases involved in tryptophan degradation and quinolinate/NAD\(^+\) biosynthesis.
- Predicted size is approximately **54.9 kDa**, consistent with this enzyme family.

## Catalyzed reaction

The principal reaction is the PLP-dependent cleavage of **L-kynurenine**:

\[
\text{L-kynurenine} + \mathrm{H_2O}
\rightarrow
\text{anthranilate} + \text{L-alanine} + \mathrm{H^+}
\]

In words, kynureninase cleaves the kynurenine carbon–carbon bond, producing:

- **Anthranilate** — the aromatic product
- **L-alanine** — the amino-acid fragment

The enzyme can also act on **3-hydroxy-L-kynurenine**:

\[
\text{3-hydroxy-L-kynurenine} + \mathrm{H_2O}
\rightarrow
\text{3-hydroxyanthranilate} + \text{L-alanine} + \mathrm{H^+}
\]

## Functional summary

This protein is likely a **fungal kynureninase**, a PLP-dependent carbon–carbon bond-cleaving enzyme in the kynurenine pathway. It converts kynurenine derivatives into anthranilate derivatives and alanine, contributing to **L-tryptophan catabolism** and, for the hydroxy-substrate reaction, **quinolinate/NAD\(^+\) biosynthesis**.

The assignment is strongly supported by homology and domain architecture. However, the exact substrate preference, oligomeric state, and cellular localization of this specific sequence remain inferred from homologs rather than directly experimentally established.
