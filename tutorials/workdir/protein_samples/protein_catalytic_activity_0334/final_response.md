## Functional summary

The 192-residue protein is most consistent with a **Ham1p-like dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase** (**EC 3.6.1.66**).

### Evidence

- Contains a highly significant **Ham1p_like Pfam domain (PF01725)** spanning nearly the full sequence.
- Matches reviewed dITP/XTP pyrophosphatases with **57.7–74.1% sequence identity** and approximately **96–99% coverage**.
- Closest reviewed homologs are annotated as **Mg²⁺-dependent, homodimeric nucleotide pyrophosphatases**.
- Predicted size is approximately **21.2 kDa**.

### Catalytic activity

This enzyme hydrolyzes noncanonical purine nucleoside triphosphates, helping “sanitize” the cellular nucleotide pool and reduce their incorporation into DNA or RNA.

The general reaction is:

\[
\text{nucleoside triphosphate} + H_2O
\rightarrow
\text{nucleoside monophosphate} + \text{diphosphate} + H^+
\]

The best-supported substrate reactions are:

\[
\mathrm{dITP + H_2O \rightarrow dIMP + PP_i}
\]

\[
\mathrm{XTP + H_2O \rightarrow XMP + PP_i}
\]

\[
\mathrm{ITP + H_2O \rightarrow IMP + PP_i}
\]

where **PPᵢ** is inorganic diphosphate. The reaction is expected to require or be stimulated by **Mg²⁺**, which coordinates the nucleotide phosphate groups and supports phosphoanhydride-bond cleavage.

### Interpretation

The strongest assignment is therefore:

> **Mg²⁺-dependent dITP/XTP pyrophosphatase involved in the removal of aberrant purine nucleotides.**

The exact substrate preference of this particular sequence has not been experimentally tested here; it is inferred from its domain architecture and very strong similarity to curated dITP/XTP pyrophosphatases.
