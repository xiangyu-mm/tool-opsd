## Functional identification

The 317-residue protein is **porphobilinogen deaminase**, also called **hydroxymethylbilane synthase** (**EC 2.5.1.61**).

The assignment is highly reliable because the sequence is reported as an **exact full-length match** to reviewed UniProt protein Q3ALS9, with additional homologs sharing approximately 87–89% identity. Pfam analysis also identifies the characteristic **Porphobil_deam** domains.

## Catalyzed reaction

The enzyme polymerizes four molecules of porphobilinogen (PBG) to form hydroxymethylbilane:

\[
4\ \text{porphobilinogen} + H_2O
\rightarrow
\text{hydroxymethylbilane} + 4\ NH_4^+
\]

This is a multistep **tetrapolymerization/deamination** reaction. The product, hydroxymethylbilane, is the linear tetrapyrrole precursor that is subsequently cyclized during heme and chlorophyll biosynthesis.

## Cofactor and catalytic chemistry

- The enzyme carries one **dipyrromethane cofactor** covalently attached to a conserved cysteine.
- The dipyrromethane group acts as a primer, allowing sequential addition of four porphobilinogen units.
- The reaction releases four ammonia/ammonium equivalents.
- No metal ion, ATP, or external redox cofactor is required according to the curated annotation.

## Biological role

This enzyme catalyzes **step 2 of tetrapyrrole biosynthesis**, downstream of 5-aminolevulinate formation:

5-aminolevulinate → porphobilinogen → **hydroxymethylbilane**

It therefore contributes to:

- **Heme/porphyrin biosynthesis**
- **Protoporphyrin IX and coproporphyrinogen III formation**
- **Chlorophyll biosynthesis** in cyanobacteria

The protein is annotated as a **soluble, monomeric cytoplasmic enzyme**.

### Summary

This sequence encodes a dipyrromethane-dependent **porphobilinogen deaminase/hydroxymethylbilane synthase** that converts four porphobilinogen molecules into hydroxymethylbilane, releasing four ammonium ions. The exact sequence match and conserved domain architecture make this identification substantially stronger than a motif-only prediction.
