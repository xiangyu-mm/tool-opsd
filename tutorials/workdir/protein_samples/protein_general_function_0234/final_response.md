### Functional summary

The 471-amino-acid protein is **FERM domain-containing protein 8 (FRMD8)**, also known as **FRMD8/FERM domain-containing protein 8**. The sequence is an **exact match to a reviewed Danio rerio FRMD8 protein** and shows strong homology to mammalian FRMD8 orthologs.

#### Likely molecular function

FRMD8 is best characterized as a **nonenzymatic membrane-associated regulatory/scaffold protein**. Its FERM-related domains are consistent with protein–protein interactions and association with membrane-proximal complexes.

Curated vertebrate annotations indicate that FRMD8:

1. **Stabilizes iRhom proteins at the cell surface**
   - Interacts with **RHBDF1/iRhom1** and **RHBDF2/iRhom2**.
   - Limits their degradation through the endolysosomal pathway.
   - This affects the availability and activity of iRhom–ADAM17 signaling complexes.

2. **Participates in ADAM17-dependent ligand shedding**
   - Through iRhom stabilization, it influences shedding of ligands including:
     - TNF
     - Amphiregulin/AREG
     - HBEGF
     - TGF-alpha/TGFA

3. **Negatively regulates canonical Wnt signaling**
   - Mammalian annotations suggest that FRMD8 may influence the interaction of **LRP6** with **AXIN1**, thereby reducing canonical Wnt/β-catenin pathway activity.

#### Localization

Annotated or inferred localization includes:

- Cytosol/cytoplasm
- Plasma-membrane-associated compartments
- In some mammalian annotations, nucleus and nucleoplasm have also been reported

The sequence lacks an obvious enzymatic signature or transmembrane segment, supporting a **cytosolic/peripheral membrane role** rather than an integral membrane protein.

#### Overall interpretation

This sequence encodes **FRMD8, a FERM-domain-containing regulatory adaptor/scaffold** involved in membrane-proximal trafficking and signaling. Its principal established role is to stabilize iRhom proteins and thereby modulate **ADAM17-mediated ligand release**, with an additional role in suppressing **canonical Wnt signaling**. The sequence and domain organization strongly support a protein-interaction function rather than that of a metabolic enzyme.

**Evidence strength:** high for protein identity and domain architecture; strong for the general regulatory role based on reviewed ortholog annotations; exact interaction and pathway effects may vary by species and cellular context.
