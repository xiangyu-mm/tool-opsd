## Functional identification

The 133-residue protein is almost certainly the **epsilon subunit of bacterial F-type ATP synthase (AtpC)**.

### Evidence

- **Exact full-length sequence match** to a reviewed ATP synthase epsilon-chain protein.
- Contains both characteristic epsilon-subunit domains:
  - **ATP-synt_DE_N**: residues 3–85
  - **ATP-synt_DE**: residues 86–131
- Small size (~14.5 kDa) and basic character (predicted pI ~9.4) are typical of this subunit.
- Lacks transmembrane segments, consistent with a **peripheral component** of the membrane-associated ATP synthase complex.

### Likely cellular role

The epsilon subunit is a **structural and regulatory component of the F₁ sector** of the proton-driven F₀F₁ ATP synthase. It associates with the central gamma shaft and helps couple rotational motion from the membrane F₀ motor to the catalytic alpha₃beta₃ headpiece. In many bacteria, it can also modulate ATP synthase activity by adopting different conformations.

It is **not expected to be an independent ATP-hydrolyzing enzyme**; ATP synthesis and hydrolysis occur primarily at the beta subunits of the ATP synthase complex.

**Overall confidence: very high**, based on the exact sequence match and domain architecture.
