## Functional assignment

This 768-residue protein is strongly supported as a **PEX6 peroxisomal AAA+ ATPase** and component of the **PEX1–PEX6 protein-dislocase complex**.

### Evidence

- It matches the reviewed *Kluyveromyces lactis* PEX6 protein **Q6CPV1** with **100% sequence identity** across the query.
- It contains a characteristic C-terminal P-loop NTP-binding motif:

  **FYGPPGTGKT**

  consistent with a Walker A/AAA+ ATPase nucleotide-binding site.
- Pfam detects a **PEX6-specific region** and an **AAA domain**.
- Curated PEX6 homologs are annotated with ATP binding, ATP hydrolysis, protein unfolding, and peroxisomal protein-import functions.

## Catalytic activity

The enzyme catalyzes ATP hydrolysis:

\[
\mathrm{ATP + H_2O \rightarrow ADP + P_i + H^+}
\]

This corresponds to **EC 3.6.4.-** and Rhea **RHEA:13065**.

PEX6 is therefore not primarily a biosynthetic enzyme acting on a small-molecule substrate. It is an **ATP-powered molecular motor**. ATP hydrolysis drives conformational changes in the PEX1–PEX6 complex, which mechanically extracts and unfolds protein cargo.

## Biological role

PEX6 forms a heterohexameric complex with PEX1, typically described as a trimer of PEX1–PEX6 dimers. The complex:

1. Recognizes ubiquitinated PEX5 at the peroxisomal import machinery.
2. Extracts PEX5 from the peroxisomal membrane/import channel.
3. Uses ATP-dependent mechanical force to unfold or dislocate the receptor.
4. Recycles PEX5 for additional rounds of peroxisomal matrix-protein import.

The protein is expected to be mainly cytosolic and associated with the cytoplasmic face of the peroxisomal membrane, generally through partner or membrane-anchor proteins rather than through a transmembrane helix.

### Summary

**Likely function:** PEX6 AAA+ ATPase/protein dislocase  
**Reaction:** ATP + H₂O → ADP + inorganic phosphate + H⁺  
**Main role:** ATP-driven extraction and unfolding of PEX5 during peroxisomal protein-import receptor recycling  
**Confidence:** High, based on exact curated-homolog matching and the conserved AAA+ P-loop motif.
