## Functional summary

The 248-amino-acid protein is most likely **UbiE**, a **SAM-dependent ubiquinone/menaquinone C-methyltransferase**.

### Evidence

- Strong Pfam match to the **UbiE methyltransferase family**: PF01209, residues 19–247, i-E-value \(2.2\times10^{-58}\).
- Approximately **42–43% sequence identity** across most of the protein to reviewed bacterial UbiE homologs.
- Size and predicted properties are consistent with bacterial UbiE proteins:
  - Length: **248 aa**
  - Molecular mass: approximately **29.0 kDa**
  - Basic predicted pI: **9.4**
- The sequence lacks an obvious signal peptide or transmembrane segment, supporting a **soluble intracellular enzyme**.

### Probable molecular function

UbiE transfers a methyl group from **S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)** to quinone intermediates. The likely reactions include:

\[
\text{demethylmenaquinol} + \text{SAM}
\rightarrow \text{menaquinol} + \text{S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine}
\]

It may also methylate a methoxy-polyprenyl benzoquinol intermediate in the ubiquinone pathway.

### Biological process

The protein likely participates in:

- **Menaquinone biosynthesis**, particularly the terminal methylation step
- Potentially **ubiquinone biosynthesis**, depending on the organism and pathway context
- More broadly, **quinone/cofactor biosynthesis and quinone-dependent respiratory electron transport**

### Overall assignment

**Probable annotation:**  
**UbiE, ubiquinone/menaquinone biosynthesis C-methyltransferase; EC 2.1.1.163 / EC 2.1.1.201.**

The UbiE family assignment is strongly supported by sequence and domain evidence. However, whether this particular protein primarily acts in the menaquinone or ubiquinone branch cannot be determined with certainty from the sequence alone.
