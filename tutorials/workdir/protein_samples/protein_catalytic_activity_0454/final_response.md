## Functional assignment

The 450-residue protein is most consistent with **GlmU**, a bacterial **bifunctional enzyme** in UDP-*N*-acetylglucosamine (UDP-GlcNAc) biosynthesis.

This assignment is supported by close, full-length reviewed homologs showing approximately **51–67% sequence identity** and nearly complete sequence coverage.

## Catalytic activity

GlmU catalyzes two sequential reactions:

### 1. Glucosamine-1-phosphate acetyltransferase  
**EC 2.3.1.157; Rhea RHEA:13725**

\[
\text{α-D-glucosamine-1-phosphate} + \text{acetyl-CoA}
\rightarrow
\text{N-acetyl-α-D-glucosamine-1-phosphate} + \text{CoA} + \text{H}^+
\]

This acetylation reaction is assigned to the **C-terminal acetyltransferase domain**.

### 2. UDP-*N*-acetylglucosamine diphosphorylase  
**EC 2.7.7.23; Rhea RHEA:13509**

\[
\text{N-acetyl-α-D-glucosamine-1-phosphate} + \text{UTP} + \text{H}^+
\rightarrow
\text{UDP-N-acetyl-α-D-glucosamine} + \text{diphosphate}
\]

This uridylation reaction is assigned to the **N-terminal UDP-GlcNAc diphosphorylase domain**.

## Overall role

The enzyme converts glucosamine-1-phosphate into **UDP-GlcNAc**, an essential precursor for bacterial **peptidoglycan**, **lipid A**, and other cell-envelope glycoconjugates. Close homolog annotations indicate a cytoplasmic, likely homotrimeric enzyme that binds **Mg²⁺**.

**Summary:** This sequence encodes a bifunctional **GlmU enzyme**, catalyzing acetyl-CoA-dependent acetylation of glucosamine-1-phosphate followed by UTP-dependent formation of UDP-*N*-acetylglucosamine.
