### Functional summary

The 400-aa protein is **nicotinate phosphoribosyltransferase (NaPRTase; EC 6.3.4.21)**. Its sequence is an **exact full-length match** to reviewed *E. coli* NaPRTase entries, providing very high-confidence identification.

- **Function:** Catalyzes the ATP-dependent conversion of nicotinate and 5-phospho-D-ribose 1-phosphate into **nicotinate D-ribonucleotide (NaMN)**, the first committed step of the NAD⁺ salvage pathway.
- **Pathway:** NAD⁺ biosynthesis via nicotinate salvage.
- **Domains:** N-terminal and catalytic NaPRTase domains: **NAPRTase_N** (residues 13–130) and **NAPRTase** (residues 169–395).
- **Catalytic feature:** A conserved histidine near position 220 is annotated as an **autocatalytically generated phosphohistidine**.
- **Localization:** Cytosolic, consistent with the *E. coli* annotation.
- **Size/properties:** Approximately **45.9 kDa**, predicted pI **6.21**, mildly acidic at neutral pH; no signal peptide or membrane-spanning region is apparent.
- **Complex formation:** The available evidence does not establish a specific oligomeric state.

Overall, this is a soluble bacterial **NaPRTase involved in NAD⁺ cofactor biosynthesis**, rather than a generic phosphoribosyltransferase.
