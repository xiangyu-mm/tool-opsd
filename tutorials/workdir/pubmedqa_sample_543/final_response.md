## Conclusion

Yes—passage number can affect both proliferation and osteoblastic differentiation of human adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ASCs), particularly at later passages.

Evidence includes:

- **P2 vs P4 vs P6:** proliferation slowed, with population-doubling time increasing from **3.2 days at P2 to 4.8 days at P6**. ALP activity and calcium deposition were highest at P2 and absent at P4 under those conditions (source: `call_7CsT2W7A6xXU1cRASiWtLPBZ`).
- **P4 vs P10:** late-passage cells showed reduced proliferation and osteogenic differentiation, consistent with replicative senescence (source: `call_YGKpCAZYQqlrDjAqmGTeOhJB`).
- However, the relationship is **not universally monotonic**. Some studies found relatively preserved ADSC function through intermediate passages, while another observed calcium deposition decreasing from P4 to P6 but recovering by P10 (sources: `call_7iZNK1cdKuYHHGCpgCAl3JIx`, `call_6Xy8vvwPmDXVLMCIWFh3lf1y`).

Thus, proliferation is generally more consistently passage-sensitive, whereas osteoblastic differentiation depends more strongly on donor, culture conditions, passage range, and assay endpoint. Exact passage number should therefore be reported and controlled in ASC experiments.

FINAL DECISION: YES
