**Yes.** The available human evidence indicates that increased urinary IL-22 binding protein (IL-22BP) is associated with greater lupus nephritis activity.

- In an adult SLE study (112 patients), urinary IL-22BP was higher with active renal disease and positively associated with overall SLEDAI, SLICC renal activity, and biopsy histologic activity. Levels also fell after six months in patients achieving complete response (PMID: **25086075**).
- In a pediatric juvenile SLE study (51 patients, 31 controls), levels were higher in active lupus nephritis than in active SLE without nephritis or non-active disease, and positively correlated with SLEDAI-2K, renal SLEDAI, and SLICC renal activity (PMID: **28887660**).

The findings are consistent across two human cohorts, though the available reports do not provide correlation coefficients, and this association alone does not establish that urinary IL-22BP is a validated standalone activity test or that it causes renal inflammation.
