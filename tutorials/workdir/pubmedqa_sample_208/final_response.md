**Yes, with a qualification.** A study of 808 university students without Williams syndrome found that the common GTF2I variant **rs13227433** was associated with socioemotional measures: the AA genotype predicted lower threat-related amygdala reactivity, and this response mediated an association with greater “warmth” (an extraversion facet) in women, but not men. The paper also notes prior association of this variant with lower social anxiety in the general population. (Swartz et al., *Biological Psychiatry*, 2017; PMID: **26853120**; DOI: **10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.12.007**.)

This supports an association between GTF2I variation and **some socioemotional phenotypes in healthy people**. It does not show that healthy carriers have the full cognitive-behavioral profile of Williams syndrome, nor does one study establish causality.
