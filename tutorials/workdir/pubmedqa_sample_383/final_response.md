The study identified is **PMID: 27489082**, a large cross-sectional population-based analysis of **45,540 ostensibly healthy Chinese men aged 55–69 years**.

Key findings:

- Men with metabolic syndrome had larger prostate volumes.
- Before accounting for prostate size, the reduction in serum PSA associated with metabolic syndrome appeared smaller.
- After adjustment using PSA density and PSA mass density—accounting for prostate volume and plasma volume—PSA levels were significantly lower in men with metabolic syndrome.
- Multivariable analysis estimated an independent **11.3% decline in serum PSA** associated with metabolic syndrome.
- Increasing numbers of metabolic-syndrome components were linearly associated with lower PSA.

Thus, the study supports the conclusion that prostate enlargement in men with metabolic syndrome partly concealed the underlying PSA-lowering association. However, because the study was observational and cross-sectional, it establishes an association rather than definitive causation.
