## Conclusion

Yes. Lysophosphatidylserine (lysoPS) exhibits **bilateral, context-dependent effects on macrophages relevant to atherosclerosis**:

- **Potentially pro-atherogenic:** In RAW264.7 and mouse peritoneal macrophages, lysoPS increased oxLDL-associated cholesterol accumulation and upregulated CD36, MSR1, LOX1, and TLR4, consistent with enhanced foam-cell formation [PMID 25445889; `call_8febWbfdZqlVrrmqevBmKiL6`].
- **Potentially anti-atherogenic:** In LPS-stimulated macrophages, lysoPS suppressed inflammatory mediators and reduced endoplasmic-reticulum stress in some settings [PMID 25445889; `call_8febWbfdZqlVrrmqevBmKiL6`].
- **Resolution-promoting effects:** In separate inflammatory models, lysoPS signaling through macrophage G2A enhanced efferocytosis, promoted CD206-high macrophage responses, and reduced inflammatory mediators [ `call_QFYbFmGkHke0dWZyS96yYNF0`; `call_uA9q8OFJZ0nkN15t6Bokczqm`].
- **Species-dependent effects:** Very-long-chain lysoPS species promoted TLR2-dependent inflammatory responses, whereas long-chain species activated distinct cAMP, calcium, and ERK pathways [ `call_8P9IHtD8yNSf26yUDrMRtWx5`; `call_7Z468pqVgY6aDZJvz03SuTx7`].

Thus, lysoPS is not functionally uniform: its effects depend on acyl-chain composition, concentration, macrophage activation state, receptor pathway, and experimental context. However, direct evidence that lysoPS changes plaque growth, stability, or regression in ApoE−/−, LDLR−/−, or other atherosclerosis models remains insufficient. Therefore, the evidence supports **bilateral macrophage effects**, but not yet a definitive net pro- or anti-atherogenic effect in vivo.

FINAL DECISION: YES
