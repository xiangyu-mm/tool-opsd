The evidence supports **yes**.

A primary comparative study (Margineanu et al., *CNS Neuroscience & Therapeutics*, 2015; **PMID: 25444522**) found that brivaracetam:

- Reduced peak voltage-gated Na⁺ current by about **30% in N1E-115 cells** and **21% in cultured cortical neurons**.
- Produced **no detectable Na⁺-current effect in adult mouse CA1 neurons** under the tested conditions, indicating cell-system-dependent effects.
- Modulated fast inactivation, including a hyperpolarizing shift in the voltage dependence of inactivation.
- **Did not impair sustained repetitive firing** in cultured cortical neurons or adult mouse CA1 neurons.

By contrast, carbamazepine caused stronger Na⁺-current inhibition and markedly reduced sustained repetitive firing. However, the study did not directly establish selectivity among molecular NaV subtypes or assess persistent/resurgent Na⁺ currents.

FINAL DECISION: YES
