Yes. Evidence from two 2015 studies supports strong antitumor activity of PP242 in the rat pheochromocytoma PC12 model:

- In PC12 xenografts in nude mice, PP242 significantly reduced tumor volume compared with comparator groups.
- It markedly increased apoptosis by TUNEL staining.
- PP242 inhibited phosphorylated Akt, reduced Bcl-2 and VEGF, and increased Bax.
- Its tumor-growth inhibition was stronger than that of rapamycin.
- A second study found that dual mTORC1/mTORC2 inhibition promoted apoptosis and that PP242 was more potent than rapamycin in PC12 xenografts.

The main limitation is that the available abstracts do not provide detailed PP242 dose-response data or quantitative in-vitro viability values. Nevertheless, the direct in-vivo xenograft findings strongly support the claim, particularly from PMID:25440763 and PMID:25666752.

FINAL DECISION: YES
