Yes. The directly relevant rat study, PMID **10731001** (“Vitamin E regulates changes in tissue antioxidants induced by fish oil and acute exercise”), found that after 8 weeks of supplementation:

- Fish oil increased catalase, glutathione peroxidase, and glutathione-S-transferase activities in liver and red gastrocnemius muscle.
- Fish oil decreased hepatic total glutathione.
- Vitamin E counter-regulated these changes, reducing the fish-oil-associated enzyme increases and increasing total glutathione in liver, heart, and skeletal muscle.
- Vitamin E also reduced oxidative damage, including lipid peroxidation and protein carbonyl formation.

A companion study (PMID **9216963**) similarly found that vitamin E reduced fish-oil-associated lipid and protein oxidation. However, its effects on oxidative damage induced specifically by the acute exercise bout were less consistent, with some exercise outcomes similar between vitamin-E and control groups.

Thus, vitamin E did regulate tissue antioxidant changes induced by fish oil, while its modification of acute-exercise responses was endpoint- and tissue-dependent.

FINAL DECISION: YES
