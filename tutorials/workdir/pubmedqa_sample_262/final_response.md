**Conclusion:** Yes—rotavirus vaccination can contribute to UHC when it is included in a publicly funded, broadly accessible immunization program. The clearest evidence comes from India’s Universal Immunization Programme (UIP), in a setting where private providers also deliver immunization.

- A real-world evaluation of India’s UIP across 31 hospitals in nine states estimated that three doses of Rotavac were **54% effective against severe rotavirus gastroenteritis** (95% CI 45–62%; PMID: 41057654). Preventing severe illness is a meaningful contribution to population health.
- After rotavirus vaccine was introduced in the UIP, private-sector vaccine sales declined in several states: by 37.4% in Rajasthan and 72.2% in Uttar Pradesh-East, with smaller or statistically unclear changes in other states. This is consistent with a free public program reducing reliance on paid private doses, though sales are only a proxy for vaccine use and do not establish who gained access (PMID: 39394601).
- Research documenting private-sector vaccine utilization in India confirms that private provision is relevant to the overall immunization landscape (PMID: 32189688). A separate Indonesian analysis assessed national-program introduction from health-system and societal perspectives, but its cost-effectiveness results are modeled and depend on assumptions (PMID: 39500219).

**Qualification:** The evidence supports a contribution through disease prevention and expansion of publicly provided vaccination; it does not show that vaccination alone achieves UHC. The studies reviewed do not directly establish equitable coverage across income or geographic groups, comprehensive coordination with private providers, or reduced household out-of-pocket spending. Those outcomes depend on sustained vaccine supply, affordable and convenient delivery, monitoring of missed or undervaccinated children, and appropriate engagement with private providers.

FINAL DECISION: YES
