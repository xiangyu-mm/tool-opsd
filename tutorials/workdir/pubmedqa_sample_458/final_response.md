Yes—based on the directly relevant study (PMID: 27505325), HMOX1/HO-1 promoter (GT)n repeat length materially influences cilostazol responsiveness in vascular smooth muscle cells.

- Cilostazol induced HO-1 expression through transcriptional mechanisms.
- Cells with shorter GT-repeat alleles showed greater HO-1 induction than cells with longer alleles.
- The shorter-repeat group also showed stronger cilostazol-mediated inhibition of VSMC proliferation.
- HO-1 knockdown weakened the anti-proliferative effect, supporting HO-1 as a mediator.
- The proposed mechanism involved CREB-dependent regulation and promoter chromatin changes.

The evidence supports repeat-length dependence for HO-1 induction and anti-proliferative effects, but does not establish that repeat length determines every cilostazol effect—particularly VSMC migration, which was not clearly tested in a genotype-stratified assay.

FINAL DECISION: YES
