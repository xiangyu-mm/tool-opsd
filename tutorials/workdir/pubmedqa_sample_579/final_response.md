Yes. The evidence supports a causal, context-dependent pathway:

- In the central study (PMID:25088053), FcRγ/FCER1G-associated receptors on dendritic cells induced IL-33 and promoted ST2/IL1RL1-dependent TH2 airway inflammation.
- IgG immune complexes acted through FcγRIII/FCGR3, while house-dust-mite extract acted through Dectin-2/CLEC6A; both required FcRγ.
- Genetic loss of FcRγ or ST2 disrupted the response.
- Exogenous IL-33 rescued TH2 responses in FcRγ-deficient mice.
- Transfer of allergen-pulsed FcRγ-sufficient dendritic cells restored inflammation in FcRγ-deficient recipients.
- An independent study (PMID:23585480) confirmed the FcγRIII–IL-33–ST2 pathway in allergic lung inflammation.

The conclusion is receptor- and context-specific. Dectin-1/CLEC7A should not be treated as interchangeable with Dectin-2: Dectin-1 can induce IL-33 in some settings, but it has also been reported to suppress TSLP-driven TH2 responses or promote TH9 responses.

Thus, signaling through particular FcRγ-associated receptors on dendritic cells—especially FcγRIII and Dectin-2—can drive IL-33-dependent TH2-type responses, particularly in allergic airway models.

FINAL DECISION: YES
