Yes. A 2015 fMRI study directly examined this question in 17 patients with avoidant personality disorder and 21 healthy controls.

- Patients with avoidant personality disorder showed greater amygdala reactivity during anticipation of cognitive reappraisal.
- Greater anticipatory amygdala activity was positively associated with higher self-reported state and trait anxiety.
- The finding was cross-sectional, so it supports prediction of concurrent anxiety levels—not necessarily future anxiety or causality.
- The authors cautioned that the activity might reflect anxiety about the upcoming reappraisal, the expected negative image, or anticipated scrutiny.

Evidence: Denny et al., *Journal of Affective Disorders* (2015), PMID: 25451388.

FINAL DECISION: YES
