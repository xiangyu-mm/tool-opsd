Yes. The paper is **“Catecholamine and Volume Therapy for Cardiac Surgery in Germany—Results from a Postal Survey”** (*PLOS ONE*, 2014;9(7):e103010; PMID: 25084362; DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0103010).

It surveyed German cardiac-surgery anaesthesia departments: 51 of 81 returned questionnaires (63%), and 50 were analyzed. Norepinephrine was the preferred treatment for vasoplegia at 96% of responding centers. For low cardiac output, choices varied, with dobutamine (32%) and epinephrine (30%) most common. Colloids were used regularly by 88% of centers, and hydroxyethyl starch was the first-choice colloid at 64%.

This was a survey of reported practice, not a trial testing which treatment was most effective.
