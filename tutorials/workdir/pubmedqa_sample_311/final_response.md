Yes. The evidence indicates that **1,4-benzoquinone induces autophagy in human HL-60 cells**, and that **reactive oxygen species (ROS) contribute to this response**.

- In the primary study (**PMID: 27514409**), HL-60 cells treated with **10 μmol/L 1,4-benzoquinone for 24 hours** showed:
  - Increased acidic/autophagic vesicles by acridine-orange staining
  - Increased LC3 immunofluorescence
  - Increased LC3-II and the LC3-II/LC3-I ratio
  - Increased Beclin-1 expression  
  These findings support induction of autophagy.

- ROS involvement was tested using **N-acetylcysteine (NAC; 5 mmol/L)**. NAC pretreatment reduced the benzoquinone-associated acidic vesicles, LC3 changes, and Beclin-1 expression. This supports the conclusion that ROS participate in, and are at least partly required for, the induced autophagy.

- A related follow-up study (**PMID: 29567065**) further reported activation of **PINK1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy** after 1,4-benzoquinone exposure in HL-60 cells, with measurements of ROS, mitochondrial dysfunction, LC3, Beclin-1, p62, and mitophagosomes.

The main limitation is that the primary study’s ROS evidence relies substantially on antioxidant inhibition with NAC; the available record does not clearly establish ROS causality using multiple independent ROS-specific approaches. Nevertheless, the overall answer is affirmative.

FINAL DECISION: YES
