A prospective cohort study in rural Cameroon followed 300 treatment-naïve adults starting first-line ART for 12 months (PMID: 25428920; DOI: 10.1093/jac/dku470).

- Among 238 participants with Month-12 viral-load results, 38 (16%) experienced virological failure, defined as HIV RNA ≥1,000 copies/mL.
- Baseline minority drug-resistance mutations were detected by next-generation sequencing in:
  - 6/30 (20%) participants with virological failure
  - 6/35 (17%) matched controls without failure
- This difference was not statistically significant (P=0.77).
- Lower baseline CD4 count and poorer adherence—particularly interruptions due to ART unavailability—were associated with failure.
- Although 63% of those failing developed detectable resistance mutations by Month 12, this reflects resistance emerging during treatment and does not establish baseline minority resistance as a predictor.

Thus, in this study, virological failure after one year was **not associated with baseline HIV minority drug resistance**.
