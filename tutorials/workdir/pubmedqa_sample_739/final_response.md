The evidence supports the statement that systemic estrogens have **no conclusively established beneficial effect** on human skin connective tissue.

- A 2023 meta-analysis of 15 studies involving 1,589 women found improved skin elasticity, thickness, and collagen with oral or transdermal menopausal hormone therapy. However, the studies were heterogeneous, included nonrandomized designs, and the authors stated that better-conducted trials are needed.
- A small randomized trial found a 6.49% increase in biopsy-measured collagen after six months of combined oral estradiol/progestogen therapy, but no significant changes in elastic fibers or several epidermal measures.
- Reviews report inconsistent findings for elastin, wrinkling, skin blood flow, sebum, and other clinically relevant outcomes. A later review characterized the effects of low-dose systemic hormone therapy as not well established.
- Evidence from topical estrogen cannot establish a systemic effect because topical treatment has different exposure and some studies combined it with systemic therapy.

Thus, systemic estrogen may improve certain surrogate measures—especially collagen, dermal thickness, and possibly elasticity—but the overall evidence is insufficiently consistent and robust to establish a definitive, clinically meaningful benefit for human skin connective tissue.

FINAL DECISION: YES
