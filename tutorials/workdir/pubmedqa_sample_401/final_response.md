Yes—imaging-genomics can reveal candidate biological pathways underlying MRI-derived volumetric tumor phenotypes in glioblastoma.

The key study, **“Imaging-genomics reveals driving pathways of MRI derived volumetric tumor phenotype features in Glioblastoma” (PMID: 27502180)**, analyzed:

- **141 patients** with presurgical MRI and survival data
- Gene-expression data for **91 patients**
- MRI-derived volumes including necrotic core, contrast-enhancing tumor, tumor bulk, FLAIR edema, total tumor volume, and component ratios

Using preranked gene-set enrichment analysis, the study found:

- **Necrotic core and tumor bulk:** enrichment of immune-response and apoptosis pathways
- **Contrast-enhancing volume:** enrichment of signal-transduction and protein-folding pathways
- **Peritumoral edema:** enrichment of homeostasis and cell-cycle pathways
- Edema predicted molecular subtype modestly (**AUC 0.61**)
- Contrast enhancement predicted overall survival modestly (**C-index 0.60**)

Related radiogenomic studies also linked MRI phenotypes to specific biological programs, including **POSTN-associated invasion**, **mesenchymal subtype**, **angiogenesis**, **tumor proliferation**, **mTOR/EGFR signaling**, hypoxia, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition.

However, the evidence primarily demonstrates **statistical pathway associations and biological interpretation**, not definitive causal “drivers.” The studies generally used retrospective imaging–gene-expression correlations and enrichment analyses without perturbing the implicated pathways or validating them experimentally.

Thus, imaging-genomics does reveal pathways that plausibly drive or characterize MRI volumetric phenotypes, but causal confirmation requires functional experiments and prospective validation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
