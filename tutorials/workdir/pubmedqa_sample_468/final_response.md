## Conclusion

Yes—**aortic prosthesis–patient mismatch (PPM) appears to strongly affect early outcomes after double valve replacement**, based on the directly relevant study identified.

### Evidence

A retrospective cohort of **196 patients undergoing combined mitral–aortic valve replacement** evaluated aortic PPM, defined as indexed effective orifice area (EOAI) **≤0.85 cm²/m²** (PMID: **25076543**).

Compared with patients without PPM:

- **In-hospital mortality:** 16.4% vs 2.8%  
  - Absolute increase: **13.6 percentage points**
  - Approximate unadjusted risk ratio: **5.9**
- **Overall postoperative complications:** significantly more frequent with PPM
- **Cardiac complications:** significantly more frequent
- **ICU stay:** significantly longer

Multivariable analyses retained PPM as a predictor of hospital mortality and postoperative complications, alongside age and, for complications, cardiopulmonary bypass time.

### Important limitations

- The evidence comes primarily from **one retrospective, single-center cohort**.
- The EOAI threshold combined what are commonly classified as **moderate and severe PPM**; separate effects by severity were not reported.
- Adjusted odds ratios and confidence intervals were unavailable in the abstract.
- The evidence directly addresses early outcomes, but not long-term survival or functional status.

Thus, the observed association is large and clinically important, but the certainty is limited by the observational design and narrow evidence base.

FINAL DECISION: YES
