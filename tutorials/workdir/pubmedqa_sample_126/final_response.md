The study **“Older age does not limit postbariatric surgery cognitive benefits: a preliminary investigation”** (PMID: **25443078**) examined 95 bariatric-surgery patients from the Longitudinal Assessment for Bariatric Surgery cohort.

Key findings:

- Participants completed computerized cognitive testing before surgery and at **12 weeks and 12 months** afterward.
- **Attention/executive function and memory improved significantly** after surgery at both follow-ups.
- Age was **not associated with baseline cognitive performance**.
- After adjusting for baseline cognition, age was **not a significant predictor** of cognitive performance at either follow-up (**P > .05 for all analyses**).
- The authors concluded that older age **did not preclude cognitive benefits** after bariatric surgery.

However, the evidence is preliminary: the sample was relatively small, the study was not designed to establish effects in very old adults, and the authors specifically called for studies including more participants up to approximately age 70.

FINAL DECISION: NO
