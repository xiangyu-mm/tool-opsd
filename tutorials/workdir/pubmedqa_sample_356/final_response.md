**Yes—preclinical evidence shows that noninvasive CEUS molecular imaging can detect myocardial inflammatory activity in experimental autoimmune myocarditis.**

In a mouse model induced with α-myosin peptide, microbubbles targeting CD4+ lymphocytes, endothelial P-selectin, or leukocytes produced stronger signals in moderate and severe myocarditis than isotype-control microbubbles. The CD4-targeted signal correlated with myocardial CD4+ T-cell infiltration. In that study, CEUS detected inflammatory changes when conventional cardiac functional measures showed little change ([PMID: 27502060](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27502060/)).

The conclusion is limited to **animal proof-of-concept**: these findings do not establish diagnostic performance in people. A follow-up study found only weak correlations between inflammatory CEUS signals and later fibrosis, and no correlation with later functional measures ([PMID: 31658281](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31658281/)). Results also depend on the target: a study targeting activated platelets did not detect selective binding by ultrasound ([PMID: 34943922](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34943922/)).

**FINAL DECISION: YES**
