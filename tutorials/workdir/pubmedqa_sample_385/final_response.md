**Yes.** A 2017 mouse study used SPECT/CT and biodistribution assays to track radiolabeled *E. coli* asparaginase. It reported rapid accumulation in bone marrow and other macrophage-rich tissues. Depleting phagocytic cells with liposomal clodronate reduced uptake in those organs and prolonged asparaginase’s serum half-life; additional experiments implicated macrophage cathepsin B in drug degradation.

This supports rapid macrophage-mediated clearance **in the tested mouse model**; it does not by itself establish the same kinetics in patients.

Source: *In Vivo Imaging of Antileukemic Drug Asparaginase Reveals a Rapid Macrophage-Mediated Clearance from the Bone Marrow.* **J Nucl Med.** 2017. PMID: 27493268; DOI: 10.2967/jnumed.116.177741.
