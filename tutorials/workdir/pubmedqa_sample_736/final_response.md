The evidence indicates that a continuous electrode-paste band connecting precordial electrodes can significantly distort the ECG, although direct experiments specifically testing a V1–V6 paste bridge were not identified.

### Electrical mechanism

A conductive paste bridge creates an unintended low-resistance connection between neighboring electrode–skin interfaces. This can:

- Partially equalize the electrical potentials of adjacent precordial electrodes.
- Cause cross-talk and mixing between neighboring V-lead signals.
- Reduce the apparent spatial differences normally recorded across V1–V6.
- Alter electrode impedance and, in severe cases, produce baseline instability, signal attenuation, or amplifier saturation.

The magnitude and direction of the changes depend on paste conductivity, bridge dimensions, electrode-skin impedance, amplifier input impedance, and which electrodes are connected. Therefore, the effect is not necessarily a uniform increase or decrease in QRS amplitude.

### Expected ECG changes

The directly connected neighboring precordial leads are most likely to show:

- Abnormal or flattened R-wave progression.
- Unusually similar QRS morphologies or amplitudes in adjacent V leads.
- Redistribution of R- and S-wave amplitudes.
- Distorted transition-zone behavior.
- ST-segment displacement or altered ST-T amplitude/polarity.
- Baseline shifts or apparent QT prolongation if the T-wave end is distorted.
- Possible lead-quality alarms or saturation if the paste creates a very low-impedance path.

Normal precordial leads show characteristic spatial variation, usually with increasing R-wave amplitude from V1 toward V4 and subsequent reduction in V5–V6. A conductive bridge can disrupt this pattern and make the tracing look physiologically implausible.

### Diagnostic implications

Contact and impedance artifacts are documented to mimic ischemia. One reported precordial artifact caused pseudo-ST depression and apparent QT prolongation, while another described pseudo-ST elevation associated with electrode, skin, or lead-contact problems. These artifacts can trigger unnecessary ischemia evaluation or treatment if not recognized.

### Practical recommendation

If a paste band is visible or suspected:

1. Remove the electrodes.
2. Clean and dry the skin thoroughly.
3. Apply a small, isolated amount of paste or gel under each electrode.
4. Ensure there is a dry gap between adjacent precordial electrodes.
5. Repeat the ECG before diagnosing ischemia, STEMI, abnormal R-wave progression, or QT prolongation.

A suspicious tracing includes abrupt changes confined to adjacent precordial leads, nearly identical neighboring-lead morphology, disrupted R-wave progression, baseline movement, or disagreement with symptoms, limb leads, prior ECGs, or serial recordings.

The evidence supports a real artifact-producing effect, but the exact waveform change is circuit-dependent and cannot be predicted uniformly for every patient or lead.
