Yes. The primary study (PMID: 27515282) tested recombinant human biglycan core protein in neonatal rat cardiomyocytes subjected to simulated ischemia/reperfusion.

Key findings:

- Biglycan core protein reduced cardiomyocyte death, apoptosis, necrosis, mitochondrial membrane-potential loss, and abnormal autophagy.
- Pharmacologic inhibition of **TLR4** abolished this cytoprotective effect.
- Inhibiting downstream **IRAK1/4, ERK, JNK, or p38** also eliminated protection.
- Biglycan core protein induced early ERK1 phosphorylation and increased Akt phosphorylation and nitric-oxide production.
- Glycosaminoglycan chains were not required: deglycanated biglycan and recombinant core protein were protective, whereas dermatan sulfate was not.

Thus, the evidence supports a mechanism involving **TLR4-dependent IRAK/MAPK signaling with downstream Akt/nitric-oxide responses**. However, the evidence is based on pharmacologic inhibition in an **in-vitro cardiomyocyte model**; genetic TLR4 knockout and definitive in-vivo infarct studies were not reported.

FINAL DECISION: YES
