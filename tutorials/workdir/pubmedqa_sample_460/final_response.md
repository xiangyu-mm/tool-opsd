**The evidence suggests that leukocyte-poor PRP (LP-PRP) is more effective than acetaminophen for short-term symptom improvement in early knee osteoarthritis.**

The most directly relevant study randomized 65 people with grade 1–2 knee OA to acetaminophen (500 mg every 8 hours for 6 weeks) or three LP-PRP injections. Through 24 weeks, the study reported greater pain reduction and better sustained WOMAC function and SF-12 quality-of-life outcomes with LP-PRP (PMID: 27506585). A smaller preliminary randomized study of 42 patients with grade I OA also reported clinical improvement with PRP versus paracetamol, although both groups received supervised rehabilitation (PMID: 25312315).

**Caveat:** These were small studies with relatively short follow-up. The abstracts do not provide enough detail to judge the size of the benefit, how it compares with a clinically meaningful threshold, or adverse-event rates. Thus, the findings favor LP-PRP but do not establish that it is definitively superior over the long term.
