Yes. Yang et al. (2014, PMID: 25447994) directly addressed this question in *Drosophila* spermatids.

They found that an MKS transition-zone module—including Cep290, Mks1, B9d1, and B9d2—moves distally away from the centriole as the axoneme elongates. The module remains associated with the growing axoneme tip, indicating that the ciliary gate migrates with and defines the distal axoneme-assembly compartment rather than remaining fixed at the basal body.

Loss of Cep290 disrupted transition-zone organization, axoneme-tip compartmentalization, and axoneme integrity. Thus, the evidence supports **functional compartmentalization** of the axoneme assembly site. The study does not establish that the gate is an absolutely impermeable barrier, but it does support its role as a molecularly restricted boundary.

FINAL DECISION: YES
