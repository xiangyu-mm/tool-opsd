Yes. The evidence supports that the P2Y2 nucleotide receptor, encoded by **P2RY2**, mediates arteriogenesis in a murine hind-limb ischemia model.

The key study (**PMID: 25088742**) used femoral artery ligation in wild-type and **P2RY2-null mice**:

- P2RY2-null mice had persistent ischemia and substantially poorer perfusion recovery—approximately **40% of wild-type recovery by day 28**.
- Collateral vessels were underdeveloped, reaching only about **65% of wild-type size** (**P = .011**).
- Baseline collateral formation was unchanged, indicating that P2Y2 primarily supports **collateral enlargement/maturation**, the defining process of arteriogenesis.
- Knockout mice showed reduced early macrophage recruitment and vascular-cell proliferation, consistent with impaired remodeling.
- Increased angiogenesis in knockout limbs did not compensate for the defective arteriogenesis; it was interpreted as a response to ongoing ischemia.

Thus, loss-of-function evidence indicates that P2Y2 is necessary or strongly contributory to effective collateral maturation and perfusion recovery. However, the evidence is primarily from knockout experiments; an independent P2Y2 gain-of-function or cell-specific study was not identified.

FINAL DECISION: YES
