**Yes—promising evidence suggests the collagen-to-smooth-muscle ratio is associated with prognosis after pyeloplasty.** In the studies retrieved, a lower ratio generally corresponded to better postoperative recovery:

- In 65 patients, lower ratios were associated with greater improvement in hydronephrosis and renal function; the lowest-ratio group had better renal-function improvement than the highest-ratio group (PMID: 10737527, *“Collagen-to-smooth muscle ratio helps prediction of prognosis after pyeloplasty”*).
- In a 31-child study, the ratio at the ureteric anastomosis correlated strongly with one-year outcome; a ratio below 1.2 was associated with success (PMID: 31500874).
- Another cohort found the association with hydronephrosis improvement was weaker (p=0.049), and elastin content also related to recovery (PMID: 15711350).

**Caveat:** These are relatively small studies, and results depend on where tissue is sampled and how outcomes are defined. The proposed cutoffs should not be treated as universally validated clinical prediction rules. Overall, the ratio appears useful as a prognostic indicator, but the evidence does not establish it as a stand-alone or definitive predictor.

FINAL DECISION: YES
