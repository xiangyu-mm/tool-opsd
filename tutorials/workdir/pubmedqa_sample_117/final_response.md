**Yes—blood transfusion has been associated with recurrence after hepatectomy in Child-Pugh A patients, but the evidence is observational and mixed.**

The most directly relevant study included 479 Child-Pugh A patients with HCC undergoing curative hepatectomy. After inverse-probability weighting, transfusion was associated with worse recurrence-free survival (adjusted HR 1.45, 95% CI 1.0–2.1; *P*=0.038). The adjusted overall-survival difference was not significant (PMID 25446481; source: `call_ca86r0II1QioAIlTJt4QHnaG`).

Broader evidence points in the same direction but does not settle the question: a meta-analysis of propensity-matched studies reported worse recurrence-free survival with perioperative allogeneic transfusion (HR 1.29, 95% CI 1.07–1.56), while several individual propensity-matched cohorts found no significant recurrence difference. Its BCLC-A subgroup is not equivalent to Child-Pugh A (PMID 37854678; source: `call_PdW5J81HyjU38DEAWDFY3lv3`; null cohort findings: `call_KJ3ruu2lwjzYqtggE0UIV9kN`, `call_XZ6efD0XD6vymFWkHZSBuok2`, `call_TlpleXU2sjarbY9pHCT0bwxY`).

Thus, the evidence supports an **association**, not proof that transfusion itself causes recurrence. Confounding by blood loss, surgical complexity, tumor characteristics, or other patient factors remains possible.

FINAL DECISION: YES
