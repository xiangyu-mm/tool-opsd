**Yes—early AFP decline appears to predict better survival**, particularly when AFP falls by more than 20% within about six weeks of starting sorafenib.

- In a retrospective cohort of 126 patients with advanced HCC, patients with a >20% AFP decline at 6–8 weeks had longer median overall survival (10.9 vs 5.2 months) and progression-free survival (5.3 vs 2.9 months). The study reported that AFP response independently predicted both outcomes (PMID: 27508193).
- A second study found that a >20% decline at six weeks was independently associated with longer progression-free survival in its exploration cohort (HR 0.31, 95% CI 0.13–0.76). Its overall-survival finding was described as marginal (PMID: 21885876).

**Interpretation:** The evidence supports early AFP decline as a favorable prognostic marker, but it comes from observational studies, and the OS evidence is less consistent than the PFS evidence. It should inform assessment, not be treated as proof that sorafenib is working or as a substitute for clinical and imaging evaluation.
