Yes. A Swedish sibling study (Kendler et al., 2016; PMID: 27507659) found that later-born siblings had higher hospitalization rates for alcohol and narcotics use than first-born siblings, especially before age 20:

- **Alcohol:** approximately 47% higher for second-borns (HR ≈ 1.47) and 65% higher for third-borns (HR ≈ 1.65) compared with first-borns.
- **Narcotics:** a similar monotonic increase with later birth order was reported, though exact hazard ratios were not available in the retrieved abstract.
- Associations were weaker after age 20.

However, a later Swedish midlife study (PMID: 36091296) found that these associations disappeared after adjustment for familial background, suggesting that the relationship may be age-dependent and partly explained by shared family factors.

FINAL DECISION: YES
