Yes. A primary study (PMID:25431913) found that stable human dermal fibroblasts heterologously expressing mutated HLA-G1 showed reduced in-vitro alloreactivity:

- **73% reduction** in NK-cell-mediated cytolysis.
- **30% reduction** in phytohemagglutinin-activated PBMC proliferation.

The construct modified HLA-G1 regulatory regions, including the endoplasmic-reticulum retrieval motif and 3′-UTR miRNA-binding sites. However, the abstract does not provide the exact mutation sequences or clearly establish a direct comparison with wild-type HLA-G1; the reductions were reported relative to control fibroblasts. Thus, the evidence supports reduced alloreactivity, but not necessarily superiority over unmodified HLA-G1.

FINAL DECISION: YES
