**Yes—clinical studies on CPFA for burn sepsis have been published.**

- **Exact-title study:** Meng et al., *“Clinical study on continuous plasma filtration absorption treatment for burn sepsis”* (2014; PMID: [25429809](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25429809/)). Thirty patients received two CPFA sessions alongside routine care. Inflammatory markers and APACHE II scores improved, and all patients survived to discharge. This was an uncontrolled study, so it cannot establish that CPFA caused the improvements.
- **Comparative study:** *“Effect of continuous plasma filtration adsorption on treatment of severely burned patients with sepsis”* (2018; PMID: [29961295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29961295/)). In 86 patients assigned to routine care with or without CPFA, reported 28-day mortality was 6.98% (3/43) with CPFA versus 25.58% (11/43) with routine care alone (P=0.023).
- **Additional observational evidence:** A retrospective cohort of burn patients with septic shock and acute kidney injury reported lower mortality with CPFA plus renal replacement therapy than with renal replacement therapy alone (PMID: [31787473](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31787473/)); the authors noted that prospective studies are needed.

These reports support the existence of clinical research and suggest potential benefit, but the evidence is limited and does not by itself establish CPFA as standard treatment.
