Yes. In rat dental pulp cells, AGE treatment increased **S100A8 and S100A9 mRNA**. An anti-RAGE antibody prevented both increases; the MEK/ERK-pathway inhibitor PD98059 suppressed the S100A8 response, while the p38 inhibitor SB203580 suppressed the S100A9 response. These results support involvement of RAGE-MAPK signaling.

The evidence is from one in-vitro study and measures mRNA, not protein expression; it supports pathway involvement without establishing every signaling step conclusively.

**Source:** Nakajima Y, et al. *Advanced glycation end products increase expression of S100A8 and A9 via RAGE-MAPK in rat dental pulp cells.* *Oral Diseases.* 2015;21(3):328–334. PMID: 25098709. DOI: 10.1111/odi.12280.

FINAL DECISION: YES
