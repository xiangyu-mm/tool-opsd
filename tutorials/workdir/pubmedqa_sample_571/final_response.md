High coach intent does **not reliably translate into effective implementation** of ACL injury-prevention programs.

- The FIFA 11+ intention study found favorable expectations but limited intention to use the program; it was cross-sectional and did not measure later delivery (PMID:27399746; source: `call_0Jt2fdLYjpmhPO6cbEUoXmnl`).
- In a prospective FIFA 11+ trial, actual session completion varied from **73.5% to 85.6%** depending on implementation support, but adjusted differences were not statistically significant. The study did not measure baseline coach intention, so it cannot demonstrate that intention predicted adherence (PMID:23486935; source: `call_w5AwsatM6M78c4oaLpPbCbD4`).
- The strongest attitude-to-behavior evidence came from Knee Control. High-compliance teams averaged **95% compliance**, compared with **50%** in low-compliance teams, yet the groups did **not differ in baseline coach prevention expectancies** (PMID:34213586; source: `call_J8LmOipzx3PM8UBWdiSQ1Uwp`).
- Educational interventions can improve perceived feasibility, but perceived feasibility has not consistently been followed by measurement of real-world delivery, dose, or fidelity (PMID:34501680; source: `call_YNoX63I5SD9rBp4CSjjcJeyi`).

Thus, coach intent may be necessary or helpful, but it is insufficient by itself. Effective implementation appears to require practical supports such as training, supervision, monitoring, time integration, and reinforcement. The evidence base is limited because few studies directly measure intention before observing later implementation.

FINAL DECISION: NO
