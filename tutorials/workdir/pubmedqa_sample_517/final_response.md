## Findings

Yes—available evidence indicates that methanolic extracts of both *Parmelia sulcata* Taylor and *Usnea filipendula* Stirt can induce apoptosis-like cell death and DNA damage in cancer-related cell models.

- **Primary evidence:** PMID **25081971** examined methanolic extracts of both lichens in A549, PC3, Hep3B, and C6 cells over **1.56–100 μg/mL**.
- **Apoptosis-like cell death:** The study reported dose-dependent growth inhibition, pyknotic nuclei, caspase-3 activation, caspase-cleaved cytokeratin-18, and PARP cleavage.
- **DNA damage:** A comet assay showed a significant increase in genetic damage for the tested extracts/cell lines.
- **Interpretation:** The authors proposed that DNA damage could contribute to the apoptosis-like cell death.
- **Limitations:** The accessible record does not provide species-specific IC₅₀ values, detailed comet-assay measurements, treatment durations, or comparative potency data. The evidence is based principally on one study, and the comet assay was the main direct DNA-damage test identified.
- A separate study of *P. sulcata*-extract-derived silver nanoparticles reported apoptosis-associated effects in MCF-7 cells, but did not establish direct DNA damage from the lichen extract itself (PMID **34048745**).
- A potentially relevant record for *U. filipendula* was excluded because it actually studied *Usnea diffracta*, not *U. filipendula*.

FINAL DECISION: YES
