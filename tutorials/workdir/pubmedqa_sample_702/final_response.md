**Yes.** A 2014 *BMC Cancer* study reports that ΔNp63α directly regulates GLI2 transcription in osteosarcoma cells and that ΔNp63α–GLI2 crosstalk contributes to oncogenic properties. In engineered SaOS-2 cells, the study assessed phenotypes including proliferation, colony formation, and wound healing. This supports the proposed mechanism in vitro; it does not by itself establish the effect in patients or tumors in vivo. (PMID: 25085524, “ΔNp63α enhances the oncogenic phenotype of osteosarcoma cells by inducing the expression of GLI2.”)
