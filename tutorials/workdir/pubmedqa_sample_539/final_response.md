**Yes.** In a human venous-occlusion study, short-term stasis increased tissue-type plasminogen activator and plasmin–α2-antiplasmin complexes, indicating fibrinolytic activation. Prothrombin fragment 1+2 and thrombin–antithrombin complexes did not change; most samples had no quantifiable free thrombin. This pattern was reported in healthy participants and in cohorts with inherited thrombophilia or symptomatic atherosclerosis (PMID: **25069814**).

The conclusion is specific to the study’s conditions and assays: unchanged measured thrombin markers support “no detectable thrombin formation,” not proof that no thrombin formed at all.
