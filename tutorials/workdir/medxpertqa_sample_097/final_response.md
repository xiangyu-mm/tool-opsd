The best explanation is a **large extracardiac left-to-right shunt**, most classically a **vein of Galen aneurysmal malformation**.

A high-flow cerebral arteriovenous shunt sends arterial blood directly into the cerebral venous system, then through the jugular veins and SVC to the right heart. This produces:

- Marked SVC and right-heart dilation  
- RV dysfunction and high-output heart failure  
- Secondary pulmonary hypertension  
- Cardiomegaly with relatively clear lung fields  
- Elevated BNP  
- Carotid and anterior-fontanelle bruits  

These cranial bruits strongly favor a cerebral extracardiac arteriovenous shunt over isolated TAPVR or an atrial septal defect. Thus, the relevant circulation is **left-to-right shunting across a large extracardiac shunt**.
