### Findings

**Yes—within the limits of the available evidence.**

- In a rat model given repeated oral infection with *Porphyromonas gingivalis*, *Treponema denticola*, and *Tannerella forsythia*, serum oxidative stress increased approximately five-fold versus sham animals. Total oxidant status and the oxidative stress index increased, while total antioxidant status decreased (**P < 0.001**).  
  **Source:** `call_ZDGFR9UzHd5W2oPj7N1dNx1e`

- In that same direct study, the bone-targeted agents **alendronate** and **bis-enoxacin** inhibited the infection-associated serum oxidative-stress increase; bis-enoxacin was more effective than alendronate.  
  **Source:** `call_ZDGFR9UzHd5W2oPj7N1dNx1e`

- Independent ligature-induced periodontitis studies support systemic or extraoral oxidative injury: rat periodontitis increased liver ROS and MDA and reduced liver SOD and GSH, with associated liver injury.  
  **Source:** `call_hPmhUpL3shzK58mZrcTrF5IZ`

- A separate ligature model showed increased kidney ROS and MDA with reduced GSH, SOD, mitochondrial membrane potential, Nrf2, and PGC-1α, although serum kidney-function measures were unchanged. No antiresorptive was tested in that study.  
  **Source:** `call_3Ubb6Nj4pSn9iRKVuwTzu647`

### Qualification

The evidence supports the combined claim primarily for **alendronate and bis-enoxacin in one direct rat infection model**. It does **not** establish that all bone-targeted antiresorptives control systemic oxidative stress. I found no direct rat-periodontitis evidence for oxidative-stress reduction by **zoledronic acid, pamidronate, or denosumab**.
