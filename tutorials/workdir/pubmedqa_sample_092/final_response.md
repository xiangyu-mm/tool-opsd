Yes. Direct mouse orthotopic liver-transplant studies support a protective recipient T-cell TIM-3/hepatocyte galectin-9 pathway against cold ischemia-reperfusion injury.

- In syngeneic mouse liver transplantation after 20 hours of cold storage, enhanced **recipient TIM-3** signaling reduced serum ALT and Suzuki histologic injury. Ischemia-stressed hepatocytes released **galectin-9**, and recombinant galectin-9 improved graft resistance (source: `call_Tkw7HaEUBhzmRMTKKJxHnJ1L`).
- **TIM-3 blockade** worsened graft injury, increased Th1/Th17 responses, and promoted neutrophil/macrophage infiltration. Adoptive transfer of wild-type CD4⁺ T cells transferred injury, whereas TIM-3-transgenic CD4⁺ T cells did not; recombinant galectin-9 was hepatoprotective (source: `call_VySTWLqVMrCRpbJICCmU3m`).
- Mechanistically, TIM-3 signaling suppressed T-bet/IFN-γ responses, promoted GATA3 and IL-4/IL-10, increased PD-1/LAG-3 exhaustion features and CD4⁺ T-cell apoptosis, and reduced secondary innate inflammation.
- Independent warm hepatic ischemia-reperfusion experiments also support this protective direction, although they were not transplantation studies (`call_8HhZHehO5prAVfSERdKei1hG`).

The principal limitation is that the transplant studies did not use hepatocyte-specific galectin-9 knockout or donor-liver galectin-9 loss-of-function. Thus, they strongly support the functional TIM-3–galectin-9 axis, but do not prove that hepatocyte-derived galectin-9 is indispensable.

FINAL DECISION: YES
