Yes. The direct study (PMID:25446530) used **Notch2^fl/fl;Alfp-Cre** mice, an early hepatic/hepatoblast-lineage conditional knockout—not an inducible mature-hepatocyte-only model.

- **Perinatally:** fetal and neonatal livers lacked CK19-, DBA-, and SOX9-positive ductal structures, consistent with intrahepatic bile duct agenesis.
- **After weaning:** secondary cholangiocyte differentiation and formation of disorganized bile duct-like tubules began around weaning. Some ducts connected to the extrahepatic biliary system.
- **Persistence of deficiency:** postnatally formed cholangiocytes lacked nuclear NOTCH2, arguing that the ducts arose despite persistent Notch2 deficiency rather than from unrecombined Notch2-positive cells.
- **Functional recovery:** approximately 30% of knockout mice were no longer jaundiced by 6 months.

A separate injury-repair study (PMID:23500150) should not be conflated with this developmental result; it found that Notch2 deficiency impaired biliary tubule maturation after chemical injury.

FINAL DECISION: YES
