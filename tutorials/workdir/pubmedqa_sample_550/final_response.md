**Yes, longitudinal evidence supports a relationship, but it is heterogeneous and does not establish causation.**

In the Lifelines cohort, covering 104,766 labour-market-active adults aged 18–50, continuous unemployment and multiple long unemployment spells were associated with greater likelihood of binge drinking (relative risk ratios 1.43 and 1.49, respectively). Recent unemployment was associated with heavy drinking (RRR 1.26), but also with abstinence (RRR 1.23), suggesting the association is not simply that unemployment uniformly increases drinking. **(PMID: 36661293)**

A second cohort study found that heavy-drinking prevalence increased around retirement, with changes varying by sex and occupational group. **(PMID: 22028898)**

**Important qualification:** These studies connect employment histories or transitions with drinking categories or changes in drinking. They do not, based on the evidence retrieved, demonstrate a joint model of labour-market and alcohol-use trajectories. The findings are observational, and the available summaries do not establish that all associations are independent of relevant confounders.
