A direct human whole-blood study supports combining synthetic CMV pp65 peptides with selected TLR agonists—particularly lipoteichoic acid (LTA) and poly(I:C)—to enhance CMV-specific IFN-γ release.

Key evidence from PMID 25450001:

- Synthetic pp65 alone produced 6,418 pg/mL IFN-γ in CMV-seropositive versus 13 pg/mL in seronegative controls.
- Synthetic pp65 alone already showed reported 100% sensitivity and 100% specificity against CMV serology.
- LTA and poly(I:C) further increased IFN-γ responses to synthetic pp65, native CMV lysate, and recombinant pp65.
- Seronegative controls were reportedly unaffected.
- However, the study did not provide numeric combination-effect sizes, coefficients of variation, or formal reproducibility analyses.
- It therefore demonstrates enhanced cytokine signal, but not definitive improvement in diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, or assay precision over peptide alone.
- Evidence was specific to pp65; direct evidence for IE-1 or overlapping CMV peptide pools combined with TLR agonists was not identified.

Thus, the combination is supported as a cytokine-signal optimization strategy, but broader assay-performance optimization remains incompletely established.

FINAL DECISION: YES
