**Yes—available evidence suggests weight-based IV unfractionated heparin is associated with earlier attainment of therapeutic anticoagulation in surgical patients**, though the evidence is limited and primarily measures time to a therapeutic PTT/aPTT rather than clinical outcomes.

- In a 40-patient study of **orthopedic patients with postoperative DVT**, 75% receiving a weight-based nomogram reached therapeutic PTT within 24 hours and 95% within 48 hours, compared with 30% and 60%, respectively, with a standard nomogram (PMID: **10392521**).
- In a retrospective study of 98 neurology and vascular-surgery patients, a higher weight-based UFH regimen reached therapeutic aPTT sooner than a lower-dose regimen. At 6 hours, more patients were therapeutic (36.0% vs 16.7%), but supratherapeutic results were also more frequent (30.0% vs 12.5%) (PMID: **24831654**).
- A neurocritical-care quality-improvement study, including patients with recent neurosurgery, also reported shorter time to therapeutic aPTT after increasing the weight-based infusion rate; it was small and not a randomized comparison (PMID: **40421164**).

These findings support earlier laboratory anticoagulation with weight-based dosing, but the small, nonrandomized studies and potential for overshooting mean the conclusion is suggestive rather than definitive.
