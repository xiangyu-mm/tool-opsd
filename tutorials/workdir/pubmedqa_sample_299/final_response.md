The ADAMTS13–von Willebrand factor (VWF) axis appears to be involved in kidney ischemia–reperfusion injury (IRI), based mainly on preclinical evidence.

- Renal IRI decreases ADAMTS13 and increases renal VWF expression and VWF dimers. ADAMTS13-deficient mice develop greater endothelial and tubular injury, inflammation, and VWF accumulation; anti-VWF treatment partially reduces injury [call_6NVk7wCmQcahU3XaysKM03EW].
- VWF knockout, VWF-blocking antibody, and recombinant ADAMTS13 each improve renal blood flow, serum creatinine, tubular injury, and neutrophil infiltration after renal IRI [call_UhEICXy2AuMiznAPJOPncdam].
- Administering recombinant ADAMTS13 after IRI also reduces BUN, creatinine, proteinuria, oxidative stress, and morphological injury; VWF can impair some of these protective effects [call_fFb1wkkNVNunK5QJ617ChaZA].
- Persistent elevation of the VWF:ADAMTS13 ratio after IRI is associated with oxidative stress and proteinuria, suggesting a possible role in progression toward chronic kidney damage [call_Tq7uRSl1s7BOQ19mLrY8MzFS].

The best-supported mechanisms are endothelial injury, impaired renal microvascular perfusion, inflammation, oxidative stress, and tubular damage. However, important limitations remain: most evidence is from mouse models, direct human evidence in isolated renal IRI is sparse, and standard renal IRI studies have not definitively demonstrated that ultra-large VWF and platelet-rich microthrombi are the dominant mechanism. Evidence from trauma-associated AKI and transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy is supportive but indirect [call_hris09agkEnLao674N1gDE4U, call_PTjREfViV8z3vymN4jF5U0eW, call_He3a6KmGlnNsiZDIwsLfdmWW].

Thus, the axis is likely a genuine contributor to renal IRI pathophysiology, but its clinical importance and precise thrombotic mechanism require further study.
