Yes. Wei et al. reported that *H. pylori* CagA-associated degradation of p53 was mediated by the host E3 ubiquitin ligases HDM2 and ARF-BP1, and was **dependent on p14ARF status**. The important qualification is that p14ARF acts protectively: low or absent p14ARF allowed efficient p53 degradation, whereas high p14ARF counteracted the signaling and protected p53. Thus, p14ARF is a regulator of the effect, not a factor that promotes degradation.

**Evidence:** Wei et al., *Gut* (2015), “Bacterial CagA protein induces degradation of p53 protein in a p14ARF-dependent manner,” PMID: 25080447.

FINAL DECISION: YES
