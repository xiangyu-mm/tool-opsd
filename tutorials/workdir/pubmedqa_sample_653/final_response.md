Yes. The primary study by Du et al. examined two *Populus tomentosa* S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine hydrolase genes, **PtoSAHHA** and **PtoSAHHB**, in association and validation populations.

Key findings:

- **PtoSAHHA_2203** and **PtoSAHHB_1065**—both noncoding SNPs—and their corresponding haplotypes were significantly associated with **α-cellulose content**.
- **PtoSAHHB_410**, a nonsynonymous SNP located within an SAHH signature motif, was significantly associated with **fiber length**.
- The reported associations explained an average of approximately **3.14% of phenotypic variance**.
- The study also found high SAHH expression in developing and mature xylem, supporting a possible role in secondary cell-wall formation.

These results demonstrate an association between allelic variation in the SAHH gene family and wood properties in Chinese white poplar, although they do not by themselves establish causality. Evidence source: Du et al., 2014, PMID **25079429**, DOI **10.1186/1471-2156-15-S1-S4**, PMC **PMC4118623**.
