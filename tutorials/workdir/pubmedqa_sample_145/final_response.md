The best-supported answer is **no**.

In the most directly relevant multicenter cohort of 259 solitary-kidney patients undergoing partial nephrectomy without preoperative renal insufficiency, neither vascular clamping nor warm-ischemia time independently predicted long-term renal-function deterioration or chronic renal failure after multivariable adjustment. Preoperative GFR and blood loss were the significant predictors ([source](call_IsPP2ivX7cRzK7OzzidEybsz)).

This finding is supported by a larger 660-patient cohort: ischemia duration lost significance after adjustment for the percentage of preserved renal parenchyma, while baseline GFR and preserved parenchymal volume were the main determinants of long-term renal function ([source](call_Ff4MnsBLGZvgTHS2IaxnrBHn)).

There is a qualification: in one 362-patient cohort, warm ischemia longer than 25 minutes was marginally associated with new stage-IV CKD after adjustment (HR 2.27, P=0.049), although continuous ischemia time was not independently predictive of this chronic outcome ([source](call_I3WokON3U1BV7pHrz3ipGlsV). Thus, prolonged ischemia may contribute to risk in selected circumstances, but ischemia overall is not consistently an independent predictor of chronic renal failure.

FINAL DECISION: NO
