The evidence supports **yes**.

The key study (PMID:25424393) used adult mice expressing slow skeletal troponin I (ssTnI/TNNI1) and subjected them to transverse aortic constriction, a model of chronic pressure overload.

Compared with control mice, ssTnI hearts showed:

- Much less hypertrophy: heart size increased approximately **7% versus 25%** in controls.
- Less wall thickening and ventricular dilation.
- Protection from diastolic dysfunction.
- Preserved cardiac performance and phosphocreatine:ATP levels.
- Increased glucose oxidation, reduced palmitate oxidation, and prevention of excessive anaplerotic flux.
- Reduced PDK4 expression, favoring pyruvate oxidation and more efficient energy production.

Thus, improved metabolic efficiency occurred alongside preservation of myocardial energetics and protection from pressure-overload remodeling and dysfunction. The findings support a mechanistic link rather than a purely incidental correlation.

However, the study did not independently manipulate PDK4, glucose oxidation, or substrate selection. Therefore, it did not prove that metabolic efficiency alone is necessary or sufficient for protection. The strongest interpretation is that metabolic efficiency is a plausible and important mediator of ssTnI-associated cardioprotection.

FINAL DECISION: YES
