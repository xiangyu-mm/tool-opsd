**Answer: No—not reliably when “institutional patient-specific IMRT QA” means routine measurement-based QA interpreted mainly by conventional gamma pass rates.** A passing result does not guarantee that delivery is acceptable; its predictive value depends on the error type, plan, measurement system, and analysis criteria.

- In induced-error testing of 21 IMRT QA measurements, commonly used **3%/3 mm** criteria with a 10% dose threshold and a 90% passing-rate cutoff could miss errors as large as **15% in monitor units** and **±1 cm random MLC errors** in some cases. Some penumbra changes were difficult to detect even with stricter criteria (Steers & Fraass, 2016; **PMID: 27036593**).
- A clinically focused workflow study found that tighter **3%/2 mm** criteria, separate pass/fail thresholds, and DVH review for intermediate results could improve identification of deliberately introduced, clinically relevant errors (Stambaugh & Ezzell, 2018; **PMID: 29481698**).
- Newer portal-dose image features combined with machine learning classified induced delivery-error types with high test accuracy, but this was a controlled error-injection study—not evidence that routine QA predicts actual patient harm or clinical outcomes (Huang et al., 2023; **PMID: 36988665**).

**Interpretation:** PSQA can detect problems and support a safer workflow, particularly when locally validated and supplemented by clinically relevant dose/DVH review. But a conventional gamma pass alone is not a dependable predictor that a plan will be delivered acceptably. Evidence cited here primarily tests detection of induced errors; it does not establish direct prediction of patient outcomes.
