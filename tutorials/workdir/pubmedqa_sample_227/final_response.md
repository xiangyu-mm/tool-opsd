**Yes—there is evidence of an association with HbA1c, but it is limited and does not establish causation.**

A 2015 study of 100 men with Type 1 diabetes younger than 40 found modest correlations between higher HbA1c and worse PE measures: PEDT score (*r* = 0.27, *P* = 0.006) and estimated intravaginal ejaculatory latency time (*r* = −0.30, *P* = 0.01). PE prevalence itself was similar to controls (24% versus 23.5%). [PMID 25424355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25424355/)

A 2021 study of 112 young men with Type 1 diabetes found no association between PE and high versus low short-term glucose variability; PE prevalence was 13% in each group. This addresses glucose variability, not the HbA1c correlations reported in the earlier study. [PMID 33928529](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33928529/)

**Bottom line:** The available direct evidence supports a modest association between poorer HbA1c control and PE symptom measures, but results are sparse, and they do not show that poor glycemic control causes PE.

FINAL DECISION: YES
