Yes—although the hierarchy is **conditional and modular rather than a single fixed linear sequence**, and stoichiometry is **variable**.

- **Temporal hierarchy:** In epithelial cells, phospho-Cas/Crk clusters containing inactive bent β1-integrin appear before integrin activation and recruitment of talin, kindlin, vinculin, and paxillin (PMID: 37489578).
- **Parallel recruitment:** Talin and vinculin can be recruited concurrently at nascent adhesions; preformed talin–vinculin complexes contribute to maturation, arguing against a strict “talin first, vinculin second” sequence (PMID: 33783351).
- **Spatial hierarchy:** Super-resolution studies support nanoscale layering: integrin/FAK/paxillin near the membrane, talin/vinculin in an intermediate force-transduction layer, and actin-regulatory proteins toward the cytoskeletal side. Kindlin follows a distinct membrane-diffusion route rather than simply acting downstream of talin (PMID: 34035280).
- **Variable stoichiometry:** Quantitative imaging shows that integrin:talin ratios change during maturation and with contractility (PMID: 28446705). Single-molecule imaging also finds broad variation in adhesion-island size, composition, copy number, and stoichiometry (PMID: 37278764).
- **Adhesion-to-adhesion variability:** FAK, vinculin, paxillin, and zyxin respond heterogeneously across individual adhesions and contractility conditions (PMID: 29391434).
- **Mechanochemical feedback:** Integrin signaling, actomyosin force, talin–vinculin coupling, and nanoclustering reinforce one another, producing multiple assembly routes rather than a deterministic sequence (PMID: 31104842).

Thus, nascent adhesions are best viewed as **dynamic, heterogeneous assemblies with hierarchical spatial and temporal organization, flexible protein ratios, and feedback-driven parallel recruitment**.

FINAL DECISION: YES
