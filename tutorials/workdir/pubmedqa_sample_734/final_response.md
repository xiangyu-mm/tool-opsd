Yes. Direct in-vitro co-culture studies provide evidence that primary human conjunctival fibroblasts can enhance peripheral-blood eosinophil survival and functional activity:

- **Survival:** In the key study (PMID: **10752939**), conjunctival fibroblast monolayers increased eosinophil viability by **35.9% on day 4** and **12.8% on day 8** compared with medium alone. Blocking GM-CSF, IL-3, or IL-5 partially reduced this effect.
- **Functional activity:** After four days of co-culture, eosinophils showed greater **LPS-induced eosinophil peroxidase release** (*P* = 0.01) and supported adherence, indicating enhanced secretory responsiveness or priming.
- **Mechanistic limitation:** Fibroblast-conditioned medium alone did not improve survival, suggesting that direct contact or a short-lived/membrane-associated signal may be required.
- **Context dependence:** A second study (PMID: **12036989**) found increased adherence and eosinophil peroxidase release but no viability increase with fibroblasts from inflamed juvenile conjunctival nevi.

Overall, the evidence supports enhancement of eosinophil survival and functional responsiveness by conjunctival fibroblasts, particularly in direct co-culture, although the survival effect is not universal across fibroblast sources or experimental contexts.

FINAL DECISION: YES
