## Conclusion

**Yes—stemness can be derived from thyroid cancer cells**, meaning malignant thyroid cells can acquire or reacquire cancer-stem-like properties. The evidence is strongest for a **plastic, inducible phenotype**, not for a permanently distinct lineage.

Key evidence includes:

- **Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma:** CD133-enriched cells showed self-renewal and tumor initiation in immunodeficient mice, with higher OCT4 expression in the most tumorigenic subset (source: `call_9rbw0zUPeYBzlnGAh5Ukjl72`).
- **Papillary thyroid carcinoma:** Both ALDH-positive and ALDH-negative populations could self-renew and initiate tumors, indicating that stemness is not restricted to one fixed marker-defined population (source: `call_9ejgloh9Cy1FJjwCneHWbgeP`).
- **Reversibility:** B-CPAP thyroid cancer cells formed long-term thyrospheres with stemness/EMT features and could revert toward the parental differentiated phenotype, directly supporting acquired and reversible stemness (source: `call_dpTgWgx7trYoB1uLBNjA0i6z`).
- **Signaling dependence:** Shh/Gli1, PI3K–AKT, neuronal acetylcholine, and related pathways can increase self-renewal or stemness-associated programs such as SOX2, BMI1, and OCT4 (sources: `call_kzOm0drfE88LysKY6dMWO3cd`, `call_WDZ67WJxZyYIZbjm6MND1Een`).

However, these results do **not** prove that thyroid cancer stem-like cells originate exclusively from normal thyroid stem cells. Marker expression alone is insufficient, and sphere assays, cell lines, and xenografts have important limitations. The most supported model is therefore:

> Thyroid cancer contains tumor-initiating subpopulations, while ordinary malignant cells can also acquire stemness through dedifferentiation, EMT, genetic/epigenetic changes, and tumor-microenvironmental signals.

FINAL DECISION: YES
