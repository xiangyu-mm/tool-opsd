**Yes—according to the study, the association differed between Slovak and Dutch cities.** A cross-sectional EURO-URHIS 2 study of residents aged 65 and older (665 in Slovak cities; 795 in Dutch cities) measured neighbourhood deprivation using unemployment rates.

In Slovakia, **none of the measured health-risk behaviours** was associated with neighbourhood unemployment. In the Netherlands, older people in the least favourable neighbourhoods had higher odds of **daily smoking** (OR 2.32, 95% CI 1.25–4.30) and **overweight** (OR 1.84, 95% CI 1.24–2.75) than those in the most favourable neighbourhoods. The behaviours assessed also included binge drinking, physical activity, and fruit and vegetable consumption.

The study was cross-sectional, so it supports a difference in **association**, not proof that neighbourhood unemployment causes these behaviours. Individual socioeconomic position explained some country differences, but not the difference for overweight.

Source: *The effect of neighbourhood unemployment on health-risk behaviours in elderly differs between Slovak and Dutch cities* (2015), PMID: 25085473.

FINAL DECISION: YES
