**Yes.** A direct mouse-cell co-culture study found different effects on pancreatic islets: both hair-follicle and boundary-cap neural crest stem cells migrated extensively when islets were present, but only boundary-cap cells showed directed migration toward islets, induced β-cell proliferation, and increased cadherin at contacts with β-cells. Hair-follicle cells did not show those effects. The authors suggested that inducing cadherin expression might help hair-follicle cells support β-cells.

This is an in-vitro finding, not evidence that the same difference necessarily occurs in humans or in clinical treatment. Source: *Neural crest stem cells from hair follicles and boundary cap have different effects on pancreatic islets in vitro* (PMID: 25077520).
