**In general, yes: chronic pancreatitis pain pattern and experience are not reliably predicted by abdominal imaging findings.** Imaging shows structural disease; it is a poor proxy for how much pain a person has or whether it is continuous or intermittent.

- In a nationwide longitudinal cohort of **1,131 patients**, continuous and intermittent pain groups did not differ in imaging morphology. Pain patterns often changed over time; changes tracked pain intensity, not a distinct imaging phenotype (PMID: **33158979**).
- In a CT cohort of **158 patients**, imaging findings generally correlated with pancreatic functional abnormalities, but not with the presence of pain (PMID: **31630919**).
- The broader pain review likewise describes imaging features as not correlating with pain (PMID: **33762844**).

This is **not absolute independence**: a separate cross-sectional study of 742 patients reported that severe calcifications were negatively associated with abdominal pain (PMID: **35396813**). Also, the studies do not all directly measure pain severity against imaging in the same way. The practical conclusion is that imaging alone should not be used to infer an individual patient’s pain pattern or severity.
