**Conclusion: Yes, in experimental models.** The principal study (PMID: 27503326) found that in human brain microvascular endothelial-cell BBB cultures, the ADAM10 inhibitor GI254023X blocked Aβ42-induced LRP1 shedding and increased Aβ transcytosis. Endothelial-specific ADAM10 knockout in mice also reduced LRP1 shedding and enhanced BBB Aβ clearance.

The interpretation has important limits: the evidence is mainly from one cellular and mouse study; ADAM10 is not established as the only LRP1 sheddase; and, in the AD mouse inhibitor experiment, increased plasma Aβ40 was consistent with enhanced efflux, but reductions in brain Aβ were not statistically significant. No direct human evidence demonstrating this mechanism or clinical benefit was identified.

**Bottom line:** ADAM10 inhibition appears to promote Aβ passage across the BBB, at least partly by reducing LRP1 ectodomain shedding, but clinical relevance and the precise contribution of LRP1 remain uncertain.
