**Yes.** Human genetic studies report overlap between chronic central serous chorioretinopathy (cCSC) and genetic variants implicated in age-related macular degeneration (AMD).

- In a 2015 case-control study of 292 cCSC patients, 1,147 AMD patients, and 1,311 controls, **ARMS2 rs10490924** was significantly associated with cCSC after correction (OR 0.64). **CFH** variants also showed associations, including a protective CFH-H3 haplotype (OR 0.54). The authors noted that some alleles that raise AMD risk may instead be protective for cCSC. (PMID: 25439433)
- A 2023 three-cohort genetic study replicated overlap at the **CFH** locus and reported an AMD polygenic score associated with *lower* CSC risk (OR 0.76 per standard deviation). The CFH and NOTCH4 associations were in opposite directions for CSC and AMD. (PMID: 37079300)

Thus, the evidence supports a genetic association, but not a uniform shared direction of risk: variants implicated in AMD may have different or opposing effects in cCSC.

FINAL DECISION: YES
