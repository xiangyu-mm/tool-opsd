Yes—**the study identified evidence consistent with immune crosstalk between Parkinson’s disease (PD) and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) loci**, but it did not prove a causal disease-to-disease interaction.

Chan et al. (Neurology: Genetics, 2016; **PMID: 27504496**) tested 24 PD risk variants against AD-relevant myeloid proteins in human monocytes:

- **rs12456492 near RIT2**, a PD risk locus, was associated with increased **CD33** expression; this trans-pQTL association was independently validated.
- **rs11060180 at CCDC62/HIP1R** showed a strongly suggestive association with decreased **PTK2B** expression.
- Other AD-related immune proteins assessed included **TREM1, TREM2, TREML2, and TYROBP**.

These findings support convergence of PD and AD genetic risk on **innate immune/myeloid pathways**. However, the study measured and validated protein-expression associations; it did **not** directly demonstrate immune-cell communication, causal mediation, or a definitive PD–AD interaction.
