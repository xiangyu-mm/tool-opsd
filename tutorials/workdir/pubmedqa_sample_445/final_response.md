## Evidence summary

The strongest direct comparison found was a pooled analysis of 120 patients with stage II–III oesophageal cancer receiving curative-intent chemoradiation [source: `call_W5BQTvxqMrKdDkXx78AkleVc`].

Pretreatment patient-reported quality of life was assessed using FACT-E and its oesophageal cancer subscale (ECS), and compared with physician-rated ECOG performance status:

- **ECS:** AUC 0.69 (95% CI 0.60–0.79)
- **FACT-E:** AUC 0.63 (95% CI 0.52–0.73)
- **ECOG:** AUC 0.56 (95% CI 0.45–0.66)

FACT-E and ECS were independently associated with overall survival, whereas ECOG was not. Thus, in this cohort, pretreatment QoL—particularly oesophageal-specific QoL—discriminated survivors from nonsurvivors better than ECOG.

A second prospective study of 96 patients with incurable oesophageal or gastro-oesophageal-junction cancer found that selected EORTC QLQ-C30/QLQ-OES18 domains—especially physical functioning, fatigue, and reflux—added prognostic information beyond disease stage [source: `call_VgKpxouqyU0Z0W4q183D7Zxc`]. However, it did not provide formal AUC or C-statistic comparisons demonstrating superiority over Karnofsky performance status.

### Interpretation

The evidence supports pretreatment QoL as a better survival discriminator than ECOG performance status in the most direct study. This may be because performance status was concentrated mostly at ECOG 0–1, whereas patient-reported QoL captured more subtle differences in fatigue, physical function, symptoms, and overall wellbeing.

Confidence is moderated by:

- Small sample sizes.
- Limited direct comparisons.
- Heterogeneous treatment settings.
- Restriction of the direct comparison largely to patients with good ECOG status.
- Lack of formal head-to-head discrimination statistics against Karnofsky in the EORTC study.
- Reliance on abstract-level evidence for the identified studies.

Therefore, QoL appears superior in the studied setting, but this should not be interpreted as proving that every QoL instrument or domain universally outperforms every performance-status measure.
