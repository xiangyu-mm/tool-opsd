Yes—**the study’s findings are consistent with, and suggest, adaptation**, but they do not establish a genetic cause.

Pizarro-Ortiz et al. reviewed records from **5,295 deliveries**. They reported that higher altitude was associated with poorer perinatal measures overall, while newborns of mothers with greater estimated Aymara ancestry had better gestational age, birth weight, and head circumference. Ancestry was estimated from surnames, however, and the study did not measure genetic variants. Its observational results therefore cannot separate inherited effects from environmental, social, or other maternal factors.

**Reference:** Pizarro-Ortiz M, et al. *Revista Médica de Chile*. 2014;142(8):961–965. PMID: 25424667. https://doi.org/10.4067/S0034-98872014000800002
