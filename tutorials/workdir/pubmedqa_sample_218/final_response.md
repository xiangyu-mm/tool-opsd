**Yes—available evidence suggests these indices are increased, with the clearest reported elevations among people with mood disorders who also have tobacco use disorder (TUD).**

- A **2015 case–control study** reported higher AIP and AC in people with unipolar depression or bipolar disorder than in controls. Its TUD subgroup analysis reported that the mood-disorder-plus-TUD group had higher AIP and AC than the other comparison groups (PMID: **25451396**).
- A **2025 meta-analysis** covering 85 studies and 70,856 participants reported significantly increased pooled AIP across mood disorders (**p < 0.001**). The retrieved summary did not report a pooled AC finding or analyze tobacco use as a modifier (PMID: **39793682**).

**Qualification:** The tobacco-comorbidity result comes from a single case–control study, and the available report does not provide subgroup effect sizes or adjusted estimates. Thus, the evidence supports an increase—particularly in the TUD-comorbid group—but does not establish how large or independent that additional elevation is. These are associations, not evidence that depression, bipolar disorder, or tobacco use causes the lipid-index changes.

FINAL DECISION: YES
