Yes. The strongest direct evidence is from **PMID: 31361973**, which studied intestinal epithelial VDR knockout mice (**VDRΔIEC**) and organoids. VDR deletion:

- Reduced **ATG16L1** and **Beclin-1** in intestinal epithelium.
- Increased epithelial apoptosis and disrupted the apoptosis–autophagy balance.
- Produced impaired autophagy-related function in both small-intestinal and colonic epithelium.

An independent study of **Paneth-cell-specific VDR deletion** also reported reduced autophagic responses and greater intestinal inflammation (**PMID: 33217447**), supporting the connection but representing a narrower epithelial subtype.

Thus, intestinal epithelial VDR loss is associated with defective autophagy and increased susceptibility to intestinal inflammation/colitis. The main caveat is that the evidence is strongest for reduced autophagy machinery and autophagic responses; direct measurements of autophagic flux specifically during chemically induced colitis were limited.

FINAL DECISION: YES
