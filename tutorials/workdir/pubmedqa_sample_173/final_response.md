Yes. Excessive medial femoral bowing can complicate intramedullary nailing in elderly femoral insufficiency or atypical fractures.

Evidence indicates that marked bowing may cause:

- **Nail–femur mismatch**, with difficulty advancing the nail and cortical impingement or perforation.
- **Poor fracture reduction**, including medial/posterior fracture gapping and residual deformity.
- **Intraoperative iatrogenic fractures**, particularly in markedly bowed femora.
- Need for **longer, smaller-radius, curvature-matched, contralateral-side, elastic, or custom-bent nails**, or plate fixation when a standard nail cannot safely follow the canal.

The most direct evidence for medial bowing comes from a small elderly insufficiency-fracture series in which excessive medial bowing specifically complicated nail insertion. Broader atypical-fracture studies—more often describing anterior or lateral bowing—also demonstrate increased mismatch, cortical perforation, reduction difficulty, and iatrogenic fracture risk. Direct evidence specifically quantifying medial cortical impingement is limited, so preoperative full-length radiographs and/or CT templating are important.

**FINAL DECISION: YES**
