## Evidence summary

Yes—**in a preclinical mouse model**, both haptoglobin and hemopexin reduced acute adverse effects of resuscitation after prolonged red-cell storage.

The key study was:

- **PMID: 27515135, Circulation (2016)**  
  Mice underwent 2 hours of hemorrhagic shock and were resuscitated with red blood cells stored for 14 days, analogous to prolonged storage in humans.
  - **Haptoglobin** coinfusion improved survival, reduced inflammation, retained cell-free hemoglobin in plasma, and prevented hemoglobinuria and kidney injury.
  - **Hemopexin** coinfusion improved survival and reduced inflammation.
  - Albumin control did not provide the same protection.

Supporting work also found that recombinant hemopexin prevented mortality and lung injury associated with stored-RBC transfusion in a mouse trauma/hemorrhage model with subsequent bacterial pneumonia (**PMID: 29522519**).

### Important limitation

These findings demonstrate **animal-model efficacy**, not proven clinical benefit in humans. The therapies remain investigational for preventing complications of transfusion with prolonged-storage red cells, and clinical dosing, safety, and effectiveness have not been established by these studies.

FINAL DECISION: YES
