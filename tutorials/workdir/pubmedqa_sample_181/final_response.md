Yes. The strongest evidence is from PMID 25423567, which studied human granulosa-derived SVOG cells:

- The NO donor SNAP increased **COX-2/PTGS2 mRNA and protein** and increased **PGE2 production**.
- The membrane-permeable cGMP analog **8-bromo-cGMP reproduced these effects**.
- Both SNAP and 8-bromo-cGMP activated **CREB signaling**.
- **CREB knockdown reduced COX-2 induction and PGE2 production**, supporting a causal role for CREB rather than simple association.
- COX-1 was not affected, suggesting relative specificity for COX-2.

Therefore, the supported model is:

**NO → cGMP-dependent signaling → CREB activation → COX-2/PTGS2 induction → increased PGE2**

However, the evidence has limitations. The direct experiments used an immortalized human granulosa-derived cell line rather than freshly isolated primary granulosa cells. Also, direct involvement of soluble guanylyl cyclase and PKG was not established by specific inhibitors, knockdown, or rescue experiments. A separate observational study in human IVF granulosa cells found coordinated increases in iNOS, pCREB, COX-2, and PGE2, but it did not prove causality.

Thus, NO and cGMP do induce COX-2 expression and PGE2 production through CREB signaling in the tested human granulosa-cell model, while the precise upstream sGC/PKG steps remain incompletely demonstrated.

FINAL DECISION: YES
