Yes. In one directly relevant longitudinal study, 192 older adults (mean age 73.8) in post-acute rehabilitation were assessed during rehabilitation and six months after hospital discharge. Decreased actigraphy-measured daytime sleep was associated with improved MMSE scores after adjustment for several health and demographic factors (β = −0.01, *P* = .002; McCrae et al., 2014, PMID: 25093233; search evidence: `call_vLq3feXneIHJYH0vTBPiLsbh`).

This was observational: it does **not** show that deliberately reducing naps causes cognitive improvement. The evidence identified was limited, so the result should be interpreted as an association rather than a recommendation to restrict daytime sleep.

FINAL DECISION: YES
