### Evidence summary

Yes. The most directly relevant study was a retrospective cohort of 815 men undergoing RARP, defining a small prostate as specimen weight **<31 g** (PMID:25098818). In this subgroup:

- Having **≥3 positive preoperative biopsy cores** independently predicted positive surgical margins.
- Adjusted odds ratio: **2.52**
- **P = 0.043**
- Preoperative clinical T3 disease was also independently predictive.

Supporting studies found that small prostate volume itself increases the risk of margin positivity, particularly at the basal margin, although they did not consistently demonstrate that total positive-core number remains independently predictive. Another cohort found more positive cores among patients with PSM, but the independent association was more strongly attributed to biopsy location.

Therefore, the best available evidence supports positive biopsy-core number—particularly **≥3 positive cores**—as a predictor of PSM in small prostates after RARP. However, evidence is limited, based largely on one directly relevant retrospective cohort, and does not establish a continuous dose-response relationship.
