Yes—available evidence supports an antidepressant-like effect of vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) in the kainic-acid model of temporal lobe epilepsy.

A direct 2015 rat study (PMID: 25444592) randomized kainic-acid- and saline-treated animals to VNS or sham stimulation for two weeks. Kainic acid reduced saccharin preference, interpreted as an anhedonia-like phenotype. VNS significantly restored saccharin preference in kainic-acid-treated rats, without changing quinine aversion, suggesting the effect was not due to altered taste perception.

However, the evidence is limited: the conclusion is based primarily on one study and one reward-preference assay. It demonstrates an anti-anhedonic or antidepressant-like effect, but does not establish a broad antidepressant profile across multiple behavioral tests or clinical antidepressant efficacy.

FINAL DECISION: YES
