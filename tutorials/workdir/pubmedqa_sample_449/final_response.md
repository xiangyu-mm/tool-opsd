Yes. Direct human evidence supports the proposed correlations.

- In a study of 27 patients with interstitial lung disease (PMID:27501892), pulmonary blood flow measured by phase-contrast MRI correlated positively with CT-derived lung volume:
  - Both lungs: **r = 0.52, p = 0.006**
  - Left lung: **r = 0.61, p = 0.001**
  - Right lung: **r = 0.54, p = 0.004**
  
  Therefore, lower pulmonary blood flow was associated with lower lung volume. These associations remained significant after adjustment for age, height, and weight.

- The same study found an inverse correlation between pulmonary blood flow and CT visual abnormal/fibrotic lung area:
  - Both lungs: **r = −0.39, p = 0.04**
  - Left lung: **r = −0.48, p = 0.01**
  - Right lung: **r = −0.38, p = 0.04**
  
  Adjusted correlations also remained significant, including **r = −0.58** for both lungs.

- A supporting study in pulmonary fibrosis (PMID:21927998) similarly found an inverse relationship between phase-contrast MRI pulmonary flow and CT visual severity score (**r = −0.60, p = 0.031**), although it did not report CT-derived lung volume.

Thus, decreased pulmonary blood flow on phase-contrast MRI is correlated with both decreased lung volume and increased CT-defined abnormal/fibrotic lung area. The evidence is observational, based mainly on a small mixed-ILD cohort, and does not establish causation or confirm the relationship separately for every ILD subtype.

FINAL DECISION: YES
