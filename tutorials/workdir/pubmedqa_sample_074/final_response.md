Yes—central adenosine receptors mediate neuropeptide S (NPS) antinociception in the mouse formalin test, with phase-specific contributions:

- **Formalin phase 1:** Both **A1 and A2A receptors** contribute. Intracerebroventricular DPCPX (A1 antagonist) and ZM241385 (A2A antagonist) blocked NPS antinociception.
- **Formalin phase 2:** **A2A receptors**, but not A1 receptors, contribute. ZM241385 blocked the effect, whereas DPCPX did not.
- Systemic caffeine, a nonselective adenosine antagonist, prevented NPS antinociception in both phases.

The key study was **PMID:25447449**, supported by the earlier central-NPS formalin study **PMID:20603169**. NPS was administered intracerebroventricularly; the evidence supports a central/supraspinal mechanism, while PAG involvement was suggested by c-Fos activation rather than direct PAG injection.
