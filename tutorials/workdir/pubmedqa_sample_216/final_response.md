**Conclusion:** Available evidence does **not show that beta-blockers worsen limb outcomes** in patients with critical limb ischemia/chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLI/CLTI) undergoing revascularization. In the largest directly relevant observational study, beta-blocker use was not significantly associated with 30-day mortality (adjusted OR 1.22, *p*=0.19) or major adverse limb events—including patency loss, reintervention, or amputation (OR 0.99, *p*=0.88). However, the adjusted estimates covered both open and endovascular procedures, rather than the endovascular subgroup alone.

That study did find higher short-term MACE and myocardial infarction among beta-blocker users (adjusted OR 1.27 and 1.36, respectively). This association does not establish that the medication caused harm: the evidence is observational and may be affected by confounding from underlying cardiovascular disease and other differences between users and nonusers. Endovascular cohorts retrieved did not provide clear beta-blocker-specific limb-outcome estimates.

**Practical interpretation:** There is no convincing evidence to stop beta-blockers solely because a patient has CLI/CLTI or is receiving endovascular therapy. Decisions should account for the patient’s cardiac indication and individual clinical risks.
