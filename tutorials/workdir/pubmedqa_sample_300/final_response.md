Yes—fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) has been demonstrated for rapid identification of *Xanthomonas citri* subsp. *citri* (Xcc).

- A 2016 study (PMID: 27492577) used 23S rRNA-targeted FISH to detect Xcc directly in citrus canker pustules.
- The method required neither bacterial culture nor DNA extraction.
- Reported turnaround time was approximately 2–3 hours.
- Specificity was evaluated against multiple *Xanthomonas* species and strains.
- Sensitivity was reported as comparable to an existing PCR assay.

However, the evidence is limited to essentially one direct study. The available report does not provide complete numerical sensitivity/specificity estimates, analytical limit of detection, a comprehensive cross-reactivity panel for all citrus-associated bacteria, independent laboratory replication, or extensive blinded field validation. Therefore, FISH is supported as a rapid and apparently accurate method for symptomatic citrus tissue, but it is not as extensively validated as PCR, qPCR, or LAMP-based diagnostics.

FINAL DECISION: YES
