Yes. In the primary mouse study (PMID:25445398), deletion of the murine TRAIL receptor (TRAIL-R/DR5, generally mapped to **Tnfrsf10b**) protected mice fed a high-fat/high-cholesterol/high-fructose diet from nutrient-excess inflammation. Receptor-deficient mice showed reduced inflammatory macrophage accumulation and activation in the liver and adipose tissue, reduced NF-κB activation and chemotaxis in macrophages, and less steatohepatitis and metabolic dysfunction.

This finding is specific to deletion of the **single murine TRAIL receptor**. TRAIL-ligand deletion produced a more limited phenotype, reducing liver inflammation but not adipose inflammation (PMID:29124251).

FINAL DECISION: YES
