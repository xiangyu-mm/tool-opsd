**Yes.** Herrmann et al. studied pedunculate oak (*Quercus robur*) challenged with the plant-parasitic nematode *Pratylenchus penetrans*. Their transcriptome analysis found that endogenous shoot-versus-root growth flush strongly changed the oak’s systemic gene-expression response. Without the ectomycorrhizal fungus *Piloderma croceum*, the response during shoot flush involved **about 16 times more differentially expressed genes** than during root flush. The fungal context altered the pattern, further indicating that growth phase modulated the interaction-associated response. The authors concluded that endogenous rhythmic growth is an important driver of these interactions. (Herrmann et al., 2016; PMID: 27520023.
