Yes. The directly relevant study was:

- **Study:** PMID 25451608, *“Negative affectivity predicts decreased pain tolerance during low-grade inflammation in healthy women.”*
- **Participants:** 25 healthy women, mean age 22.3 years.
- **Inflammation:** Salmonella typhi vaccination, producing low-grade inflammation with increased leukocytosis and IL-6.
- **Pain outcomes:** Pressure pain thresholds and thumbnail pain tolerance measured before and 6 hours after vaccination.
- **Finding:** Higher baseline negative affectivity predicted lower post-vaccination pain tolerance (**β = −0.57, p = .007**). This association was not present in the saline placebo condition (**β = .25, p = .26**).

Thus, the evidence supports an inflammation-specific predictive relationship. However, it comes primarily from one small study, and the significant finding was for pain tolerance rather than all pain-threshold measures.

FINAL DECISION: YES
