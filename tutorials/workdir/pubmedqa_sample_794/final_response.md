The available evidence supports **yes**, with important qualifications.

A direct study (PMID: **10735610**) evaluated **80 consecutive patients with alcoholic cirrhosis and suspected alcoholic hepatitis**, using transvenous liver biopsy as the reference standard. Histologic severity was graded from polymorphonuclear infiltration, hepatocyte injury, and Mallory bodies.

At prespecified cutoffs:

- **Serum laminin ≥4.1 UI/mL:** 90% sensitivity and 77% specificity
- **Serum type IV collagen ≥150 ng/mL:** 89% sensitivity and 77% specificity
- Combining the two markers did **not** improve diagnostic performance.
- Maddrey’s score was less accurate for severe histologic disease, with 66% sensitivity and 69% specificity.

Thus, in this cohort, both markers were reasonably accurate for identifying **histologically severe alcoholic hepatitis**, particularly because of their high sensitivity. However, the specificity was only moderate, and the evidence appears to come primarily from a single historical cohort without demonstrated external validation. They should therefore be viewed as useful noninvasive indicators—not definitive replacements for biopsy or comprehensive clinical assessment.

FINAL DECISION: YES
