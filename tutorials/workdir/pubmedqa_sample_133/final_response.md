The matching study was **“Alteration of inflammatory response by shock wave therapy leads to reduced calcification of decellularized aortic xenografts in mice”** (2015; **PMID:25422292**).

- Decellularized porcine aortic grafts were implanted subcutaneously in C57BL/6 mice.
- Shock wave therapy was applied immediately after implantation.
- Treated grafts showed **smaller areas of calcification** and increased recruitment of osteoclastic cells.
- Shock wave therapy **modulated**, rather than simply suppressed, inflammation: macrophage infiltration and M2 polarization increased, while some inflammatory/complement mediators decreased.
- The authors attributed the lower calcification to modulation of the acute macrophage-mediated inflammatory response.

Thus, in this mouse xenograft model, the evidence supports that altering the inflammatory response with shock wave therapy was associated with reduced graft calcification. This is evidence from a single experimental study and does not by itself establish clinical efficacy.

FINAL DECISION: YES
