## Findings

Yes, the available human evidence supports a correlation between HPV infection and inflammatory STAT3/IL-17 activity in breast cancer, although the evidence is limited and does not establish causation.

- **Direct tri-marker evidence:** A study of 379 breast-cancer patients (PMID: **27508043**) detected HPV16/18 in 34/325 tumors (10.5%). High phosphorylated STAT3 (p-STAT3) occurred in 38.5% of tumors and high IL-17 staining in 61.5%. HPV positivity was significantly associated with:
  - High p-STAT3: **P = 0.018**
  - High IL-17: **P = 0.021**

- **Independent partial support:** Another human case-control study (PMID: **30642295**) reported HPV DNA in 35/72 breast-cancer specimens and found increased IL-17 and other inflammatory mediators in HPV-positive cancers. However, it did not measure STAT3 or p-STAT3.

- **Important limitations:**
  - Only one identified patient study directly assessed HPV, p-STAT3, and IL-17 together.
  - Effect sizes and confidence intervals for the HPV–pSTAT3 and HPV–IL-17 associations were not available in the reported abstract.
  - IL-17 staining may reflect infiltrating immune cells or macrophages rather than breast-cancer cells.
  - HPV prevalence in breast cancer is highly variable across studies and remains methodologically controversial.
  - The relationship is observational; it does not prove that HPV causes STAT3 activation or IL-17 expression.

Thus, HPV-positive breast tumors appear more likely to show inflammatory pSTAT3 activity and IL-17 expression in the available evidence, but this should be described as a **limited, preliminary correlation**, not a confirmed causal HPV–STAT3–IL-17 pathway.

FINAL DECISION: YES
