The study **“Initiation of nutritional support is delayed in critically ill obese patients: a multicenter cohort study”** (PMID: 25080456) evaluated 3,257 ICU stays from the multicenter OUTCOMEREA database.

Key findings:

- Obese patients were defined as having BMI ≥30 kg/m².
- Delay before nutritional support differed significantly across BMI groups (**P=0.035**).
- Nutritional support was initiated later in obese patients than in normal-weight patients.
- Adjusted relative risk of delayed initiation in obese patients was **1.06 (95% CI 1.00–1.12)**.
- The association persisted after adjustment for age, sex, diabetes, and other chronic diseases.
- The authors explicitly concluded that nutritional support initiation was delayed in critically ill obese patients.

This was an observational cohort study, so it demonstrates an association rather than proving that obesity caused the delay.

FINAL DECISION: YES
