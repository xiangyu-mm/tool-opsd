The available evidence supports **YES, but with important limitations**.

- A directly matching study, **“Immunotherapy prevents recurrent abortion without influencing natural killer receptor status” (PMID: 10735601)**, reported that husband-derived lymphocyte/PBMC immunotherapy was associated with prevention of recurrent abortion.
- The study found no apparent treatment-related change in **CD158a, CD158b, CD159, or CD94 expression**, nor in the killer-activating/killer-inhibitory receptor balance. [Sources: `call_MI0PtlCUGK9IZTCiG1d3qCxO`, `call_IiS4dcyoRBCKORPv0ZYBwkCl`]
- However, the abstract did not provide robust numerical efficacy estimates or a clear control-group comparison.
- This conclusion applies specifically to measured **NK-receptor expression/repertoire**, not necessarily to NK-cell numbers, activation, or cytotoxic function. Another study found altered NK-cell populations after lymphocyte therapy but did not assess KIR/NK-receptor status. [Sources: `call_mUDB9WblHWLOKY8BuGaXrjMc`, `call_EqS7ZAaz0LgpVlN8DGwhYSgN`]

Therefore, the exact study supports prevention of recurrent abortion **without changing measured NK-receptor status**, but the clinical evidence is limited and does not establish broad immunological neutrality.

FINAL DECISION: YES
