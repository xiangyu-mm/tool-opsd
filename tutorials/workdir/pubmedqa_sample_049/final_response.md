## Answer

Yes—**limited evidence suggests that 24-hour in-hospital congenital cardiac surgical coverage improves perioperative ECMO outcomes**, particularly operative survival and certain complications.

The key study was a single-center retrospective before/after cohort of 128 children requiring perioperative ECMO after congenital heart surgery. After implementation of 24-hour in-hospital coverage by advanced congenital cardiac surgical fellows:

- Mortality decreased from **68% to 43%** (*p* = 0.007).
- Cardiac arrhythmias decreased from **36% to 16%** (*p* = 0.012).
- Pulmonary complications decreased from **32% to 8%** (*p* < 0.001).
- Twenty-four-hour coverage independently predicted operative survival in multivariable analysis (*p* = 0.009).  
  **Source:** `call_TAZsvaMPeErtSyiu3w2aNSGz`

However, the evidence is not definitive. It comes primarily from one retrospective, single-institution historical comparison. Improvements may also reflect changes in ECMO protocols, ICU staffing, surgical practice, institutional experience, or patient selection. The study did not establish whether coverage reduced time to cannulation or improved survival to decannulation or hospital discharge specifically. No additional comparative studies directly evaluating surgeon-coverage models were identified.  
**Sources:** `call_tououvhPqcKusTIuWPmuzsLb`, `call_upiXNvYuBVZOexpja65gPqVW`, `call_Y8nifUXQTwaMxSJk2zMHEbsC`

Thus, 24-hour coverage is associated with better perioperative ECMO outcomes, but the strength of evidence is limited and causal certainty is moderate-to-low.

FINAL DECISION: YES
