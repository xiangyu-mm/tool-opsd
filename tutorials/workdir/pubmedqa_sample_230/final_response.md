Yes. The evidence indicates that canals of Hering (CoH) loss is associated with progression of the histological stages of primary biliary cholangitis (formerly primary biliary cirrhosis).

- In a study of 62 untreated PBC biopsies, CoH density was already reduced in early disease and declined further with increasing histological stage. Lower CoH density correlated with fibrosis, bile-duct loss, and hepatitis activity (PMID: 25430494).
- A separate study found near-complete periportal CoH loss in patients with nearly normal or minimally inflamed biopsies, suggesting that CoH loss can be an early lesion, potentially preceding classic destructive cholangitis (PMID: 22911653).
- In 53 biopsies classified by Ludwig stage, c-Kit staining—used to identify CoH—was absent in early stages and only focally present in advanced stages. However, focal CoH-like structures could still be detected in some advanced specimens (PMID: 16963314).

Thus, CoH loss is not exclusively a late-stage phenomenon. It may begin very early and become more extensive as PBC progresses histologically. The relationship is best described as **early depletion with stage-associated worsening**, rather than universal complete disappearance in advanced disease. Interpretation is also somewhat marker-dependent because K19, c-Kit, and morphometric CoH counts do not measure exactly the same feature.
