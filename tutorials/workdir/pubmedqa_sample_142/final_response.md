Yes. The primary rat study (PMID: 25451626) found that intramuscular activation of group I mGluRs with DHPG produced masseter muscle mechanical hyperalgesia. The mechanism was supported by:

- Attenuation with the TRPV1 antagonist AMG9810.
- Suppression by a TRPV1–AKAP interaction-disrupting decoy peptide.
- Increased TRPV1 serine phosphorylation at S800 after DHPG exposure.
- Inhibition of this phosphorylation and the associated TRPV1 sensitization by a PKC inhibitor.

Thus, the evidence supports a PKC-dependent TRPV1 phosphorylation pathway linking peripheral group I mGluR activation to muscle mechanical hyperalgesia in rats. The experiment primarily tested combined mGlu1/5 activation rather than isolating each receptor subtype individually.

FINAL DECISION: YES
