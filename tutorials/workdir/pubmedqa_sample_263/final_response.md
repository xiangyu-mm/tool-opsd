**Yes.** The clinical evidence indicates that ADRB1 Arg389Gly can modify flecainide’s antiarrhythmic efficacy when β-blockers are coadministered.

In a study of 159 patients with supraventricular tachyarrhythmia, tachyarrhythmia was controlled in **60% of Arg389Arg**, **30% of Arg389Gly**, and **0% of Gly389Gly** patients receiving flecainide with a β-blocker (**P=0.001**). Without a β-blocker, control rates were **64%, 70%, and 60%**, respectively, with no genotype difference. Response was assessed for at least two months using symptoms, ECG, and Holter monitoring (PMID: [27500822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27500822/)).

A separate study in patients with atrial fibrillation also found higher flecainide cardioversion rates for Arg389Arg than for Arg389Gly or Gly389Gly, but it did not report results stratified by β-blocker use (PMID: [20625396](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20625396/)).

The direct coadministration result is notable, but comes from a relatively small clinical cohort; it supports an effect, not a definitive basis for genotype-guided treatment decisions on its own.

FINAL DECISION: YES
