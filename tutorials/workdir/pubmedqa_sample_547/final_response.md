Yes. The best direct evidence is a retrospective study by Ohnishi et al. (2014; PMID: 25098177), which compared summer and winter serum KL-6 levels in patients with IPF, NSIP, collagen-vascular ILD, CPFE, house-related HP, and bird-related HP.

Key findings:

- Seasonal KL-6 variation was greater in both house-related and bird-related HP than in the comparator ILDs.
- Bird-related HP showed significantly higher KL-6 in winter.
- House-related HP showed significantly higher KL-6 in summer.
- Variation was also greater in acute than chronic HP.
- A newer 2025 longitudinal study supports disease-specific seasonal KL-6 patterns in HP, although it did not establish that the amplitude was greater than in IPF.

The exact between-group effect sizes and p-values were not available in the retrieved text, and the findings appear dependent on the relevant seasonal antigen exposure.

FINAL DECISION: YES
