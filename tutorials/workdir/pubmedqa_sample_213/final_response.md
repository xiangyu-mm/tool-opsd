In a retrospective cohort of patients undergoing pancreaticoduodenectomy for nonmalignant disease, severe nutritional risk was associated with significantly poorer long-term survival among geriatric patients (p<0.001). Reported five-year mortality was approximately one-third for patients with severe nutritional risk versus one-fourteenth for those without. The study also reported severe nutritional risk as an independent survival factor in geriatric patients.

This supports a predictive association, not proof that nutritional risk causes reduced survival. The evidence is from a single-center observational study, and the available report did not provide the adjusted effect size or risk thresholds. (PMID: 25442378)
