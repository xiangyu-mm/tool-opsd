The available primary evidence supports the statement.

In the Noble–Collip drum trauma rat model, investigators observed:

- Spontaneous thrombin generation and markedly increased soluble fibrin
- Consumption of platelets, coagulation factors, and antithrombin
- Endothelial injury and pronounced fibrinolysis
- Shock, hypotension, and lactic acidosis
- No increase in activated protein C, a mechanism associated with ACoTS

The principal study therefore classified the model as **trauma-associated DIC with a fibrinolytic phenotype**, explicitly concluding that it did **not** induce acute coagulopathy of trauma-shock. Although the model includes shock and transient hypocoagulable/fibrinolytic changes, these do not establish ACoTS as the dominant mechanism.
