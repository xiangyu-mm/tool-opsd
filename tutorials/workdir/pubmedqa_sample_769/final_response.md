The evidence indicates that **stent design can favorably influence vascular response in normal porcine coronary arteries**, particularly with thinner struts, optimized geometry/surface finish, and selected coatings or polymer-free platforms.

Key findings:

- A 65-µm cobalt-chromium stent produced substantially less angiographic stenosis, neointimal thickness, and histologic area stenosis than a 91-µm cobalt-alloy stent at 1 month: 2±1% versus 17±5% angiographic stenosis, and 0.11±0.01 versus 0.23±0.03 mm intimal thickness (source: `call_EvgARYSYL6DpLduGS8jYoqYX`).
- A 71-µm cobalt-chromium stent with Polyzene-F coating reduced neointimal thickness at 28 and 90 days without reducing endothelial coverage; inflammation was numerically lower but not statistically significant (source: `call_TNnOmDm70M45fmLT8AR0pzox`).
- Polymer-free drug-delivery designs showed potentially better endothelialization and less fibrin or inflammation than polymer-based sirolimus stents, but effects varied with drug and dose (sources: `call_0Rrr4TGo1JeS73KVq16Asld6`, `call_nCuyzqmBr52QgvJLeGQlraLg`).
- The evidence is not universal: different bioabsorbable, drug-eluting, and thicker-strut designs may have greater thrombogenicity or delayed healing. Moreover, many comparisons bundled several design variables, so the benefit cannot always be attributed to strut thickness alone (source: `call_1S2OitfIn1UhV39kzYAHNVZr`).

Thus, in normal porcine coronary arteries, **specific stent designs improve aspects of vascular healing and reduce neointimal response, but design effects are platform- and formulation-dependent rather than uniformly beneficial**.
