**Yes—but mainly as a marker of an early, short-lived inflammatory phenotype, not of ongoing RA activity over time.**

- In a cohort of **773 patients with RA**, anti–type II collagen (anti-CII) positivity was associated with higher CRP, ESR, swollen-joint counts, and DAS28 at diagnosis and through approximately **6 months**. Over five years, anti-CII-positive patients tended to improve and more often achieved a good or moderate EULAR response (PMID: [28336519](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28336519/)).
- In an early-RA cohort of **274 patients**, anti-CII-positive patients had higher CRP and ESR at baseline, but not later in follow-up. Among the nine patients with very high baseline anti-CII levels, eight showed antibody levels declining in parallel with CRP, ESR, and immune-complex-induced cytokine responses (PMID: [17040962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17040962/)).
- A separate early-RA study also found higher CRP and ESR among anti-CII-positive patients at diagnosis, but not during follow-up (PMID: [22548748](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22548748/)).

**Interpretation:** Anti-CII can reflect inflammation around RA onset, but it is uncommon and the association fades; it should not be treated as a reliable marker for monitoring persistent disease activity. These observational findings do not establish that the antibodies cause the inflammation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
