Yes. In isolated rat hearts, urodilatin administered during the first 15 minutes of reperfusion reduced acute ischemia–reperfusion injury, particularly at 0.05 μM.

Evidence from the primary study (PMID: 10728355):

- Improved contractile recovery after 40 minutes of ischemia (P < 0.0309).
- After 60 minutes of ischemia, reduced the rise in LV end-diastolic pressure (P = 0.0139).
- Reduced LDH release, indicating less myocardial cell injury (P = 0.0263).
- Reduced contraction-band necrosis (P = 0.0179).

A follow-up dose-response study (PMID: 11476750) found that 0.05 μM reduced peak LDH release by 40% and improved function, whereas 0.01 μM was ineffective and 1 μM produced no benefit. Thus, the protective effect was concentration-dependent and most evident at an intermediate dose.

FINAL DECISION: YES
