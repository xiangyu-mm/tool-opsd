The exact study was a single-blind randomized controlled trial by Picelli et al. (PMID: **25082957**, *Clinical Rehabilitation*, 2015; DOI: **10.1177/0269215514544041**).

- **Participants:** 66 patients with Parkinson’s disease, Hoehn and Yahr stage 3.
- **Interventions:** 12 sessions over 4 weeks of either robot-assisted gait training or targeted balance training.
- **Primary outcome:** Berg Balance Scale (BBS).
- **Immediately post-treatment:**  
  - Robot-assisted gait training: **51.58 ± 3.94**  
  - Balance training: **51.15 ± 3.46**  
  - No significant between-group difference.
- **At 1-month follow-up:**  
  - Robot-assisted gait training: **51.03 ± 4.63**  
  - Balance training: **50.97 ± 4.28**  
  - No significant between-group difference.
- Secondary measures—including balance confidence, Timed Up and Go, and Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale—also showed no significant advantage for robot-assisted gait training.

Thus, robot-assisted gait training improved or maintained postural stability, but it was **not superior to balance training** in this trial.

FINAL DECISION: NO
