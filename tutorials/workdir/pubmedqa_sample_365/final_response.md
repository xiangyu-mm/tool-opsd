## Findings

**Yes—genomic prediction was evaluated both within and across generations in maritime pine.** The key study is Bartholomé et al. (2016), which assessed circumference, height, and stem straightness in *Pinus pinaster*.

- The study used a reference population of **818 trees spanning G0, G1, and G2**, genotyped with **4,436 SNP markers**.
- In the across-generation test, models trained on **G0 and G1** were used to predict **G2 progeny**. Prediction accuracies ranged from **0.70 to 0.85** across the three traits.
- Within-generation accuracy depended substantially on how related the training and validation individuals were: the mean difference between low- and high-relatedness validation scenarios was **0.17**. Including progenitors in the calibration set reduced that difference to **0.03**.
- **Pedigree-based ABLUP performed similarly to, or better than, the genomic models** in this study. Thus, the results support cross-generation prediction potential, but do not show genomic models outperforming pedigree prediction.

These are encouraging results from a selected, closely connected breeding population; they should not be assumed to transfer unchanged to less-related populations or other traits.
