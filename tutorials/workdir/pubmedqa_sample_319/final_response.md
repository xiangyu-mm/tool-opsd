## Conclusion

Yes—a virtually ¹H-free birdcage coil for ZTE MRI is technically feasible and has been demonstrated.

### Evidence

- A 7 T transmit/receive birdcage coil for human joint ZTE MRI was constructed using proton-avoiding materials, mechanical connections instead of adhesives, and ferromagnetic signal-spoiling elements.
- Under normal ZTE acquisition, no coil-derived ¹H signal was detectable above the noise floor.
- With extreme signal averaging, weak residual signal appeared near conductors adjacent to proton-containing glass surfaces.
- Phantom imaging showed only negligible background artifacts for dead times up to 3.8 Nyquist dwells, and high-quality in-vivo images were obtained.
- A subsequent ZTE study also reported largely ¹H-free birdcage coils at 3 T, supporting broader applicability.

### Important qualification

“Without background signal” should be interpreted as **no detectable background under the intended imaging conditions**, not absolute mathematical absence of all proton signal. Residual signal can arise from:

- Adhesives, cable insulation, coatings, plastic fixtures, or capacitor packages
- Nearby glass, contamination, adsorbed water, or scanner hardware
- Proton-bearing materials outside the nominal coil structure

A practical design would therefore use copper conductors, mechanically fastened hydrogen-free or carefully verified low-proton supports, and thoroughly screened components. PTFE and selected ceramics may be preferable to hydrogen-containing polymers, but the retrieved evidence does not establish material-specific Q, SNR, or transmit-efficiency advantages. Ferromagnetic signal spoiling may also reduce Q or introduce susceptibility effects.

Thus, the demonstrated target is a **virtually ¹H-free birdcage coil with background below the noise floor**, rather than a physically perfect zero-proton coil.

FINAL DECISION: YES
