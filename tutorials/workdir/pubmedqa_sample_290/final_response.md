## Answer

Yes. The study *“Centre characteristics determine ambulatory care and referrals in patients with spondyloarthritis”* (PMID: 27498017) found substantial centre-to-centre variation after adjustment for patient and clinical characteristics.

- **Study:** 1,168 patient records from 45 centres in a nationwide retrospective audit.
- **Ambulatory rheumatology care:** Adjusted between-centre variability was **15.7%**; variation was partly explained by the numbers of faculty members and trainees.
- **Referrals:** Adjusted variability was **12.3%**, with referral probability differing by up to **25%** between centres.
- Referral variation was associated with **urban versus non-urban setting, number of procedures, and the presence of a dedicated SpA clinic**.

Thus, centre-level organizational and structural characteristics were associated with both rheumatology ambulatory-care use and referrals. Because this was an observational audit, it demonstrates an important influence rather than definitive causation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
