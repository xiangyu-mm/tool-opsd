## Findings

Available rat studies generally report that apitherapy products improve CCl4-induced hepatic injury compared with CCl4-only controls:

- **Propolis:** Improved ALT, AST, ALP, lipid peroxidation, glutathione depletion, and hepatocyte histology. Benefits were reported in both preventive and post-treatment designs; post-treatment propolis at 200 mg/kg was described as particularly effective. Evidence includes PMID 15511003 and PMID 35153395.
- **Royal jelly:** Reduced liver enzymes and lipid peroxidation, restored antioxidant defenses, and improved histology. A later study also reported improvement in cholesterol-related markers, oxidative-DNA damage, PON1, and liver telomere shortening. Evidence includes PMID 20637822 and PMID 37477674.
- **Bee pollen:** When given after CCl4 exposure, chestnut bee pollen improved AST/ALT, oxidative stress, and histopathologic damage, supporting a recovery effect rather than prevention alone. Evidence includes PMID 24250716.
- **Honey:** Carob honey improved liver enzymes, LDH, lipid/protein oxidation, glutathione, ascorbate, CAT, and GPx. Sider honey also improved biochemical and histologic outcomes, although that study emphasized pretreatment. Evidence includes PMID 30342848 and PMID 21330121.
- **Mixed apitherapy diets:** Diets containing honey, pollen, propolis, Apilarnil, and sometimes royal jelly improved biochemical and histologic abnormalities after repeated CCl4 injury. However, effects of individual products cannot be separated.
- **Bee venom:** Honey plus bee venom improved liver markers and histology in an LPS+CCl4 model, but this is less directly applicable because the injury was not caused by CCl4 alone.

## Interpretation

The direction of evidence is consistently favorable: several products improved biochemical markers of hepatocellular injury, oxidative stress, antioxidant depletion, and liver histology. Some studies specifically administered products after CCl4 injury, supporting enhanced amelioration or recovery.

However, the evidence is **preclinical and limited**:

- Studies were generally small and heterogeneous.
- Many experiments tested prevention or concurrent treatment rather than recovery after established damage.
- Abstracts often lacked numerical effect sizes.
- Mixed-product formulations prevent attribution to a specific apitherapy product.
- No study establishes that apitherapy accelerates true liver regeneration or has relevance to human treatment.

Thus, the best-supported conclusion is that apitherapy products **appear to enhance recovery or reduce ongoing injury in rat CCl4 models**, but the evidence is not definitive regarding optimal products, doses, or regenerative effects.
