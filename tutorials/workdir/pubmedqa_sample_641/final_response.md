The available experimental evidence supports **normotopic cortex as the major contributor** to epilepsy in experimental double cortex/subcortical band heterotopia models.

- In the strongest localization study, Dcx-knockdown rat cortical slices recorded with a 60-channel microarray showed that most interictal-like discharges began in the overlying **normotopic cortex** and propagated into the heterotopic band; the band was not a primary source ([call_u33qS43tAyYCRk19jy5sy9mc]).
- In vivo, suppressing heterotopic-band excitability did not eliminate the increased seizure susceptibility, further arguing against the band being necessary as the principal seizure generator ([call_u33qS43tAyYCRk19jy5sy9mc]).
- Heterotopic cortex is nevertheless important: it participates in abnormal reciprocal circuitry and can modify excitability and excitation/inhibition balance in normotopic cortex ([call_dX6u2XPD751W7IodfDp377g0]).
- Bilateral models demonstrate spontaneous seizures and occasional onset from the hemisphere with the larger band, but these recordings do not distinguish the heterotopic band from adjacent normotopic cortex ([call_cOgkiXay7wQW5nyB9kQjj97Y]).
- A separate Eml1/tish model confirms epileptogenicity of the overall malformation but does not localize seizure onset to either compartment ([call_qfZkLOy4HboQhcGSc90VeCD]).

Thus, the best-supported interpretation is a **normotopic-cortex-dominant epileptic network**, with heterotopic cortex acting as a network-modifying and propagation-related component rather than usually being the primary seizure-onset site.
