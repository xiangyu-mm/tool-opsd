**Yes.** Chachi et al. reported that airway smooth muscle (ASM) cells from people with severe asthma were less responsive to fluticasone and dexamethasone than cells from healthy subjects: corticosteroids were less able to suppress TNF-α-induced CCL11 and CCL5, and fluticasone-induced glucocorticoid-receptor-α (GRα) signaling was impaired. PP5 levels were higher in severe-asthma ASM cells and in ASM bundles in endobronchial biopsies. In patient-derived ASM cells, **PP5 knockdown restored** fluticasone’s chemokine-suppressing effect, GRα nuclear translocation, and GRE-dependent GILZ expression.

This supports PP5 as a mediator of corticosteroid insensitivity in ASM in severe asthma. The causal intervention evidence is from cell experiments; increased PP5 in biopsies supports relevance in patients but does not itself show that targeting PP5 improves clinical outcomes.

**Source:** Chachi L, et al. *Protein phosphatase 5 mediates corticosteroid insensitivity in airway smooth muscle in patients with severe asthma.* PMID: 27501780.
