Yes. The 2016 study of 2-cyclohexylamino-5,8-dimethoxy-1,4-naphthoquinone (R6) in LPS-stimulated BV2 microglia reported reduced nitric oxide, ROS, and pro-inflammatory cytokine expression. It also reported reduced phosphorylation of ERK, JNK, and p38, and less IκB-α degradation—findings consistent with inhibition of MAPK and NF-κB pathway activation.

For the reported nitric-oxide experiment, cells were pretreated with 30 μM R6 for 30 minutes before LPS exposure (1 μg/mL, 24 hours). The evidence retrieved supports the stated mechanism, though it does not establish direct measurement of NF-κB nuclear translocation or DNA binding.
