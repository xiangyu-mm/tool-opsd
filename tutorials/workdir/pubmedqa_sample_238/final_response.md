Yes—particularly for **CT imaging in U.S. emergency departments during the 2000s and early 2010s**.

- A U.S. claims study of **33.1 million ED visits (2005–2013)** found that CT use increased **59.9%**, with 17.8% of visits involving CT (PMID: 28874935).
- A longitudinal U.S. practice-based study found that overall CT utilization rose only **10% from 2000–2010**, while ED CT use increased **81%**—from 41.1 to 74.4 scans per 1,000 patients (PMID: 24080717).
- National U.S. data from **2003–2008** likewise showed ED CT use increasing over time and faster than in Ontario, Canada (PMID: 23683773).

Thus, ED CT growth did appear to diverge from the broader trend of relatively stable imaging utilization. However, this is not universal or indefinitely sustained: pediatric ED CT was flat or declining in some settings, and later data show CT stabilization or decreases in selected populations, alongside modality substitution such as increased ultrasound (PMIDs: 22331345, 31339541). The strongest conclusion is therefore historical and modality-specific rather than a claim that all ED imaging continues to rise today.

FINAL DECISION: YES
