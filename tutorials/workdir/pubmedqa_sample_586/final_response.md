Yes. One directly relevant study (PMID:25082847, *Cardiovascular Research*, 2014) evaluated human PBMCs mobilized with G-CSF and then primed ex vivo with erythropoietin (10 IU/mL for 6 hours), compared with unprimed cells.

Reported improvements included:

- Increased expression of vasculogenic mediators, including IL-8, IL-10, bFGF, PDGF, and MMP9.
- Increased integrin expression and adhesion to endothelial cells/fibronectin.
- Enhanced endothelial-cell migration and tube formation in conditioned-medium assays.
- Greater vasculogenic activity in Matrigel plug, ischemic hindlimb, and myocardial-infarction mouse models.
- Direct incorporation of mobilized human cells into mouse vasculature, along with paracrine effects from primed-cell supernatant.

The evidence is therefore positive for improved vasculogenic potential. However, the accessible report provided qualitative rather than numerical effect sizes, and the evidence comes primarily from one experimental study rather than clinical validation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
