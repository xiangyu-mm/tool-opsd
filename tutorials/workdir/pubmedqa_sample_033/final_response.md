A primary study by Maléth et al. reported that alcohol-associated exposures impaired pancreatic duct CFTR function and reduced CFTR expression or stability, alongside impaired ductal fluid and bicarbonate secretion. In mice, CFTR deficiency made pancreatitis more severe after alcohol or fatty-acid exposure—supporting CFTR impairment as a contributor or susceptibility factor (PMID: 25447846).

The human findings are supportive but not definitive: acute alcohol exposure was associated with increased sweat chloride, and pancreatic tissue from people with alcohol-associated pancreatitis showed lower CFTR levels. These observations do not establish how much CFTR dysfunction causes human pancreatitis, or how it compares with other alcohol-related injury mechanisms.

FINAL DECISION: YES
