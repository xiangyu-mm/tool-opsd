**Yes—preclinical evidence supports this mechanism.** In a 2016 mouse study, ex-vivo carbon monoxide (CO) preconditioning improved mesenchymal stromal cell (MSC) efficacy in polymicrobial sepsis: treated animals had improved survival, bacterial clearance, organ injury, and resolution of inflammation. CO-exposed MSCs supplied with docosahexaenoic acid produced specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators, particularly D-series resolvins. Silencing the 5-lipoxygenase and 12/15-lipoxygenase pathways removed the CO-associated therapeutic benefit, supporting a role for SPM biosynthesis.

This is evidence from cell and animal experiments, not proof of benefit in people. A later mouse sepsis study also reported improved function after CO-conditioning human MSCs, but described an autophagy/extracellular-vesicle mechanism rather than establishing the SPM mechanism clinically.
