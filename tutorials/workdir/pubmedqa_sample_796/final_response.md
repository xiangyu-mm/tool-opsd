Yes. A directly relevant human study (PMID 10751853, *Journal of Urology*, 2000) evaluated 15 patients with interstitial cystitis and 13 controls before and after bladder hydrodistension—three distensions to 80 cm water—with urine sampled immediately before, 2–4 hours afterward, and at 2 weeks.

Findings in interstitial cystitis patients:

- **Urinary HB-EGF increased**, moving toward control levels. The initial difference from controls was significant (**P=0.003**) but was no longer significant at 2 weeks (**P=0.67**).
- **Urinary antiproliferative factor (APF) activity decreased significantly** after hydrodistension. It remained different from controls at 2 weeks, although the difference was reduced (**P=0.04** versus **P=0.0000004** before distension).

Thus, in this study, bladder stretch/hydrodistension altered both urinary biomarkers: it increased HB-EGF and reduced APF activity. Evidence is based primarily on this single small clinical study. Supported by `call_lMRnaVpGhA9xsJL1ttX6gULJ` and `call_gsUImybNA1jKe4j2c4KOb0Bb`.
