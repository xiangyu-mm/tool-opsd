The study supports the conclusion that initial management strategy and early revisions are important predictors of fascial closure after open abdomen:

- **Study:** Lambertz et al., *International Journal of Surgery*, 2015;13:12–16. PMID: **25447607**.
- **Design:** Single-center retrospective cohort, 355 adults treated with open abdomen from 2003–2013.
- **Closure rate:** Fascial closure was achieved in **137/355 patients (39%)**.
- **Initial open-abdomen strategy:** An open abdomen performed during the initial operation was more common among patients who achieved closure (**71% vs 54%; p=0.002**).
- **Early revisions:** Patients who achieved closure underwent earlier:
  - Second-look operation: **2.7 vs 4.2 days; p=0.021**
  - Third-look operation: **5.6 vs 8.5 days; p=0.006**
- **Authors’ interpretation:** Fascial closure probability increased when the open abdomen was used initially and second- and third-look operations were performed early.

However, this was a retrospective observational study, and the available report does not establish causality or fully separate the effect of the precise initial indication from disease severity and treatment selection. Therefore, these factors were **strongly associated with closure and considered decisive by the authors**, but should not be regarded as definitively causal.
