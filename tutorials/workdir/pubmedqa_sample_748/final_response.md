Yes—**in carefully selected patients with discogenic pain arising from a residual disc at a level that had already achieved solid posterolateral fusion**, anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF) appears to improve pain and function.

The strongest direct evidence is a retrospective study of 20 such patients; 18 had follow-up averaging 58 months:

- Pain improved from **7.9 to 4.7** (*P*<0.001).
- Oswestry Disability Questionnaire scores improved from **56.3 to 47.9** (*P*=0.04).
- **16/18 patients (89%)** were satisfied.
- **5/15** patients unable to work preoperatively returned to work.
- All achieved radiographic anterior fusion.

Source: PMID 10751297; search evidence tool call `call_65r2LGNHOP2gz3z27v4DAL9R`.

However, this is **low-level evidence**: it was retrospective, uncontrolled, involved only 20 relevant patients, and had limited complication reporting. Broader revision-ALIF evidence is also observational; in one 22-patient series, 50% achieved a clinically meaningful improvement in back pain, but the population was heterogeneous and not specific to prior posterolateral fusion (`call_9NzOC81exUPUjs3RemLICz62`).

The conclusion should be limited to **same-level residual discogenic pain after a solid posterolateral fusion**. Evidence is insufficient to claim the same benefit for:

- Same-level pseudarthrosis/nonunion after failed posterolateral fusion.
- Adjacent-segment disease.
- Patients without convincing diagnostic localization of pain to the residual disc.

Thus, anterior fusion can help, but the evidence supports it as a **selected salvage option**, not a predictably effective treatment for all pain after posterolateral fusion.

FINAL DECISION: YES
