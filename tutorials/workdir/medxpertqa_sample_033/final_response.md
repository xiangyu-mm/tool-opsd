The most likely cause is **initiation of oral estradiol**.

Oral estrogen undergoes hepatic first-pass metabolism and increases production of **thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG)**. Consequently:

- More thyroxine becomes protein-bound.
- **Total T4 rises**, as seen here from 7.2 to 10.3 μg/dL.
- The free, biologically active T4 fraction decreases or becomes insufficient.
- TSH increases, indicating that the patient now requires more levothyroxine.

This explains the otherwise paradoxical combination of **increased total T4 and increased TSH**. Calcium can impair levothyroxine absorption if taken close to it, but this would not explain the increase in total T4 through TBG elevation. Omeprazole, progesterone, atorvastatin, seasonal variation, and Hashimoto progression are less likely.
