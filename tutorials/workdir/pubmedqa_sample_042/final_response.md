Yes. A directly relevant systematic review (PMID:25441702) examined 76 studies using administrative health data and concluded that multiple comorbidity indices are valid, although performance depends on the population, data source, and outcome.

Key findings:

- **Elixhauser** and **Romano-adapted Charlson** indices generally performed better for **mortality prediction**.
- Medication-based indices, such as the **Chronic Disease Score**, performed better for some **healthcare-utilization outcomes**.
- Reported c-statistics ranged from **≤0.69** to **>0.80**, indicating substantial variation in predictive validity.
- A systematic review in orthopedic populations found:
  - Elixhauser AUCs of **0.70–0.92**
  - Charlson AUCs of **0.68–0.89**
  for in-hospital mortality, supporting use of either index for mortality adjustment in that setting.
- A maternal-health review found that only the **Maternal Comorbidity Index** had been specifically derived and validated for pregnancy/postpartum populations, emphasizing that validity cannot automatically be generalized across settings.

Therefore, systematic reviews do identify valid administrative-data-derived comorbidity indices, but they do **not** establish one universally valid index. The appropriate choice should reflect the target population, coding system, available administrative data, and outcome.
