Yes—baseline right atrial pressure (RAP) predicted the hemodynamic response to brief apneic positive airway pressure in a human study of 22 postoperative patients with acute lung injury.

- Airway pressure was increased from 0 to 30 cmH₂O during 15-second apneic inspiratory holds.
- Baseline RAP correlated with the change in cardiac index (**r² = 0.68, p < 0.01**), more strongly than pulmonary artery occlusion pressure or right-ventricular end-diastolic volume.
- Patients with **RAP ≤10 mmHg** had a substantially greater cardiac-index decrease than those with RAP >10 mmHg (**30% ± 9% vs 8% ± 7%, p < 0.01**).
- The proposed mechanism was impaired venous return under positive airway pressure.

A second human study confirmed that apneic positive airway pressure can increase RAP and reduce stroke volume, but it did not test RAP as a predictor (PMID:10710387; evidence from `call_b1BD8XYlKjoaoY9kHSO51uOH`). Thus, the predictive finding is strongest for acute lung injury, high airway pressures, and cardiac-output response—not necessarily for routine CPAP used solely for apneic oxygenation or for predicting blood-pressure changes (PMID:10752813; evidence from `call_qDAP4eJH5BjeqEFM55AdbOXI`).

FINAL DECISION: YES
