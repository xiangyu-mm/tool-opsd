**No—within the conditions tested, cyclosporine-inhibitable cerebral transport did not measurably influence clinical methadone pharmacodynamics.**

In a crossover study of healthy volunteers receiving oral methadone (n=16) or IV methadone (n=12), cyclosporine—a P-glycoprotein inhibitor—did not alter miosis, the R-methadone concentration–miosis relationship, or measured thermal analgesia. It did produce a small change in methadone plasma pharmacokinetics. The study therefore found no corresponding change in the measured pharmacodynamic effects (PMID: 25072223, *Cyclosporine-inhibitable Cerebral Drug Transport Does Not Influence Clinical Methadone Pharmacodynamics*).

This conclusion is limited to the study’s healthy volunteers, dosing protocols, and endpoints; it does not rule out effects in other clinical settings or on outcomes that were not measured.

FINAL DECISION: NO
