Yes. A primary study reported that male Sprague–Dawley rats given timosaponin A3 orally at 100 mg/kg/day for 14 days had increased serum total bile acids and decreased bile-acid components in bile. In cultured rat hepatocytes, timosaponin A3 increased reactive oxygen species and HO-1, while reducing expression of the bile-acid transporters Ntcp, Bsep and Mrp2 and impairing taurocholate accumulation and biliary excretion. N-acetylcysteine ameliorated several of these effects, supporting a role for oxidative stress.

Important qualification: the rat findings establish liver/bile-related effects, while the oxidative-stress and transporter mechanism was primarily tested in cultured hepatocytes. Thus the study supports the proposed mechanism, but does not establish that these pathways are the sole cause of liver injury in vivo.

**Source:** PMID 25087997, “Timosaponin A3 induces hepatotoxicity in rats through inducing oxidative stress and down-regulating bile acid transporters.” https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25087997/

FINAL DECISION: YES
