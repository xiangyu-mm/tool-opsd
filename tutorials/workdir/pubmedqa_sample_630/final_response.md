**Yes.** In a mouse model, slow-pressor angiotensin II infusion (600 ng/kg/min for two weeks) increased the tubuloglomerular feedback (TGF) response from **3.4 ± 0.2 to 5.2 ± 0.2 μm**. At the same time, macula-densa superoxide rose from undetectable to **21.4 ± 2.5 units/min**, while nitric oxide fell from **138.5 ± 9.3 units/min to undetectable**. The authors concluded that TGF is mainly influenced by macula-densa NO under physiological conditions, but by macula-densa superoxide during Ang II hypertension. They measured these signals in isolated, perfused juxtaglomerular apparatuses. **This supports redox-dependent modulation of TGF responsiveness**, although the comparison alone does not establish that changing redox status is the sole cause of the TGF change.

Supporting context: separate studies found increased macula-densa superoxide and increased Nox2/Nox4 expression in Ang II–infused mice (PMID: 24285500), and identified Nox2 as a primary source of Ang II–induced macula-densa superoxide in cell experiments (PMID: 20053956).

**Key study:** [PMID: 25089004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25089004/) — *Oxidative status in the macula densa modulates tubuloglomerular feedback responsiveness in angiotensin II-induced hypertension.* *Acta Physiologica* (2015).
