The evidence supports **yes**.

The relevant study was:

- **Tièche R, da Costa BR, Streit S.** *Patient satisfaction is biased by renovations to the interior of a primary care office: a pretest-posttest assessment.* **BMC Health Services Research.** 2016;16:373. PMID: **27515540**.

### Findings

- Single primary-care practice in Switzerland.
- Patient surveys were conducted before and after renovation, with **153 patients in each period**.
- The questionnaire assessed:
  - Office appearance
  - Medical assistants
  - General practitioners
  - Overall satisfaction
- Ratings increased significantly after renovation across **all four domains**—including perceptions of physicians and staff, whose clinical work was not directly altered.
- Reported significance levels ranged from **p < 0.01 to p < 0.001**, and adjusted analyses remained significant.

### Interpretation

The pattern suggests a **contextual or perceptual bias**: an improved physical environment may have positively influenced patients’ evaluations of care quality beyond their assessment of the renovated space itself.

The study was observational and conducted in one practice, so it cannot establish universal causality. Nevertheless, its pretest–posttest findings directly support the conclusion that interior renovations can bias patient-satisfaction ratings upward.

FINAL DECISION: YES
