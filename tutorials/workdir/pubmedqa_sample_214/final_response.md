Yes. The directly relevant study (PMID: 25447298, *Peripherally administered oxytocin modulates latent inhibition in a manner consistent with antipsychotic drugs*) tested subcutaneous oxytocin in Brown Norway rats, which normally show weak latent inhibition.

Oxytocin doses of 0.02, 0.1, and 0.5 mg/kg enhanced latent inhibition in a conditioned taste-aversion paradigm, chiefly by reducing aversion in rats pre-exposed to the conditioned stimulus. The authors explicitly characterized this facilitation as consistent with the effects of antipsychotic drugs in latent-inhibition models.

This supports an antipsychotic-like modulation, although it is based on an animal study and does not establish clinical antipsychotic efficacy.

FINAL DECISION: YES
