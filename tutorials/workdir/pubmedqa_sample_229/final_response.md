The STAR*D study by Fried and Nesse investigated whether major depression presents as a consistent symptom syndrome:

- **Paper:** Fried EI, Nesse RM. *Depression is not a consistent syndrome: An investigation of unique symptom patterns in the STAR*D study.*
- **Citation:** *Journal of Affective Disorders*. 2015;172:96–102.
- **PMID:** 25451401.
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jad.2014.10.010.

The study found substantial heterogeneity in the combinations of depressive symptoms experienced by participants. The most common symptom profile represented only a small minority of the sample, suggesting that people meeting criteria for major depression often do not share one uniform symptom pattern.

This supports the conclusion that depression is **not a consistent syndrome at the symptom-configuration level**. However, the finding does not mean that depression lacks clinical usefulness or that every symptom pattern represents a distinct disorder. It indicates that the diagnostic category encompasses diverse presentations, and that symptom-level assessment may provide clinically important information beyond the overall diagnosis.

The accessible records verified the paper and its central conclusion, although the exact numerical profile counts could not be independently confirmed from the retrieved abstract-level sources.
