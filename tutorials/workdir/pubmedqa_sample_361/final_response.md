Yes. The primary study by Liu et al. found that pPCP1-encoded HmsA (also called sR084) contributes to enhanced *Yersinia pestis* biofilm formation. Deleting **hmsA** reduced cellular c-di-GMP, and HmsA positively regulated biofilm-synthesis-associated genes, including **hmsHFRS**, **hmsT**, and **hmsCDE**. The authors therefore proposed HmsA as a biofilm activator (PMID: 27492011; doi:10.1186/s12866-016-0793-5).

A later HmsA study focused on virulence and does not report a contrary biofilm result (PMID: 33026884).

FINAL DECISION: YES
