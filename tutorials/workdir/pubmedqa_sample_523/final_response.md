Yes—limited direct evidence suggests caffeine modifies blood glucose during exercise in adults with type 2 diabetes, but it does **not** show that caffeine improves glucose availability.

A small pilot study of eight adults tested caffeine (1.5 mg/kg) before 40 minutes of exercise at 40% heart-rate reserve, followed by 10 minutes of recovery. The study abstract reports a significant blood-glucose reduction of 75 mg/dL (65%) with caffeine compared with the other treatment groups (p<0.05). It does not provide detailed time-point values or establish what the lower glucose means for glucose availability.

This finding comes from a single small study, so the effect needs confirmation. Lower measured blood glucose should not be interpreted as proof of better glucose supply to working muscles. **Source:** PMID: 25100892.

FINAL DECISION: YES
