**Yes—gut microbiome composition has been associated with temperament in some human studies during infancy and early childhood.** Studies using temperament measures such as the IBQ-R, ECBQ, CBQ, and Lab-TAB have reported associations involving microbial diversity, community structure, or particular taxa and dimensions including surgency/extraversion, negative emotionality, and fear reactivity. Examples include studies of toddlers and infants (PMIDs **25449582, 31132457, 34108055, 37974473**) and preschoolers (PMID **39237483**).

The evidence is **observational and mixed**: specific taxa and even the direction of diversity associations differ across studies, ages, sexes, and temperament dimensions. One prospective cohort found that temperament–microbiome cluster associations did not remain significant after correction for multiple testing (PMID **38276199**). Thus, the findings indicate possible associations, not that the microbiome causes temperament differences or that there is a consistent microbial signature.
