The available evidence supports a **correlation**, but not definitive causal mediation.

- In the strongest direct study (**PMID:27515367**), liraglutide improved islet viability and graft function while reducing **CCL2 and IL-6 secretion, macrophage chemotaxis/activation, and macrophage infiltration**. This directly links improved graft performance with reduced inflammatory activity.
- Other studies support additional mechanisms. Liraglutide reduced early graft β-cell apoptosis and improved glucose control (**PMID:18511515**), while GLP-1 agonists protected islets from cytokine-induced injury (**PMIDs:21669040, 26794660**).
- A newer scaffold-based transplantation study also found improved graft outcomes alongside protection from inflammatory injury (**PMID:40956865**), although scaffold-related effects confound attribution.
- No study identified here selectively blocked liraglutide’s anti-inflammatory effects, restored inflammatory signaling, or otherwise demonstrated that inflammation reduction was necessary for the graft benefit. Antioxidant, anti-apoptotic, cytoprotective, and insulinotropic effects may contribute in parallel.

Thus, liraglutide’s improvement of islet graft function is **associated with and plausibly partly supported by anti-inflammatory properties**, especially during early engraftment, but the relationship is not proven to be exclusively or causally anti-inflammatory.

FINAL DECISION: YES
