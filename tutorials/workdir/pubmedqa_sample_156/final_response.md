The study is:

- **McLemore MR et al.** “Women know best—findings from a thematic analysis of 5,214 surveys of abortion care experience.” *Women’s Health Issues*. 2014;24(6):594–599.  
- **PMID:** 25442704  
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.whi.2014.07.001

### Findings

Researchers analyzed open-ended responses from **5,214 abortion-care surveys** collected across **22 U.S. clinical sites**. Participants were asked whether their experience was better, about the same, or worse than expected and invited to describe it in their own words.

- **3,600 women** reported experiences better than expected.
- **136 women** reported experiences worse than expected.
- Two broad categories shaped reported experiences:
  - **Clinic-level factors**, such as waiting times, pain control, pain management, and staff interactions.
  - **Patient-level factors**, including expectations and individual circumstances.
- Women reporting negative experiences generally attributed them to **modifiable aspects of clinic care**, particularly inadequate pain management and delays before or during the appointment.

### Interpretation

The study supports the idea that women are uniquely positioned to report how abortion care is actually experienced and which aspects require improvement. Their accounts identified concrete quality-of-care problems that may not be captured by clinical safety measures alone.

However, “women know best” should not be interpreted as meaning that every individual patient account is sufficient for judging medical safety or clinical effectiveness. The study supports **respecting and systematically incorporating women’s experiences into quality improvement**, alongside clinical and safety data.
