Yes. Histologic evidence supports that basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF/FGF-2) enhances the angiogenic response associated with transmyocardial laser revascularization (TMLR).

- In a canine chronic-ischemia study (PMID: 10752546), adding bFGF increased large-vessel number approximately twofold, proliferating vascular-cell density by 50%, and estimated vascular cross-sectional area approximately threefold. The authors concluded that bFGF enhanced post-TMLR angiogenesis, primarily by enlarging vessels rather than increasing total vessel number.
- A porcine study (PMID: 12414042) found increased capillary and arteriole numbers with combined rhFGF-2 and TMLR compared with ischemic controls. However, it did not clearly demonstrate statistically greater capillary or arteriole density than TMLR alone.
- Thus, the strongest evidence supports structural histologic enhancement—especially larger vessels and greater vascular proliferation—while evidence for an unequivocal additive increase in capillary density over TMLR alone is limited. The canine study also found no additional regional blood-flow benefit from bFGF.

FINAL DECISION: YES
