**Yes, with an important qualification:** transcriptome sequencing and comparative analyses have revealed candidate mechanisms for sustained latex flow in *Hevea brasiliensis*, but most are associations or hypotheses rather than experimentally confirmed causes.

The most direct study compared latex from CATAS8-79 and PR107, clones differing in latex regeneration and flow duration. It reported 6,726 differentially expressed unigenes and proposed roles for ethylene, laticifer-wall lignin, antioxidants, glucanases, and rubber-particle aggregation in flow duration. Jasmonate and carbohydrate metabolism, along with HMGR/HRT-related activity, were linked mainly to latex regeneration. These findings nominate mechanisms; they do not establish causality (PMID: 25928745; evidence: `call_XLZyqXAPBakpNbHPnk9MmROd`).

Other studies provide supporting, but less direct, evidence. Ethephon treatment increased flow duration and reduced plugging index, with transcriptomic changes implicating water circulation, redox balance, sugar metabolism, and actin dynamics (PMID: 26985821; `call_S3xy2pGWUXuxUpDdBnj2zD97`). Focused gene studies support possible roles for aquaporin-mediated water transport and thiol/glutathione-related redox buffering (PMIDs: 25927524 and 27419133; `call_wzBZTpdIjH2SFp3cPd71hNU2`, `call_OE7ApnTIG3PoBqH9z5KoZBgU`). Yield and regeneration studies also implicate carbohydrate use and stress/ROS responses, but do not specifically compare long- and short-duration flow (PMIDs: 24066172 and 27555864; `call_Ih65U78lP0bdn5pv0FAX0nh1`, `call_Lt9DtiPs2j2qYP6PpbEFMiEt`).

**Bottom line:** transcriptomic comparisons suggest that water balance, ethylene responses, carbohydrate supply, redox protection, cell-wall properties, and reduced latex coagulation may contribute to longer flow. Direct comparisons by flow duration are limited, so these should be treated as candidate mechanisms rather than proven explanations.
