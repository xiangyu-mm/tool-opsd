Yes—**with an important qualification**.

Two prospective studies in preterm infants found that red blood cell transfusion:

- Increased splanchnic/intestinal tissue oxygenation measured by NIRS.
- Reduced fractional tissue oxygen extraction, consistent with improved oxygen delivery.
- Did **not** significantly change measured resting superior mesenteric artery blood-flow velocities.

In the study of 20 extremely preterm infants (PMID:26643925), splanchnic tissue oxygenation increased by 14.6% and tissue hemoglobin index by 32.3%, while mesenteric peak systolic and diastolic velocities were unchanged. A second study of 59 preterm infants (PMID:27509230) reported similar findings across different postnatal ages.

However, the effect is not universally flow-neutral. Another cohort (PMID:32108891) found decreased celiac artery flow and impaired postprandial SMA-flow augmentation after transfusion, alongside an increase in postprandial splanchnic oxygen extraction. Thus, transfusion appears to improve resting intestinal oxygenation primarily by increasing blood oxygen content, but it may alter feeding-related intestinal hemodynamics.

FINAL DECISION: YES
