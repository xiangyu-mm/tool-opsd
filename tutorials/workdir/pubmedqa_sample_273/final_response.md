Yes, with an important distinction.

A primary human dental-pulp study (Ghazaryan et al., 2016; **PMID: 27497510**) found that recombinant complement C3a:

- **Mobilized dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs)** and increased their proliferation.
- **Specifically guided pulp-fibroblast recruitment** in a microfluidic C3a-gradient chemotaxis assay.
- Increased proliferation of both DPSCs and pulp fibroblasts.
- Was associated with **C3aR/C3AR1 expression** in cultured pulp fibroblasts, STRO-1-positive DPSCs, and human tooth sections.

The key qualification is that C3a **did not specifically direct DPSC chemotaxis** in the study; it mobilized them without demonstrating directional recruitment. In contrast, directional recruitment was specifically demonstrated for pulp fibroblasts. C3aR expression supports involvement of the receptor, but receptor-blocking or loss-of-function experiments were not reported, so receptor necessity was not definitively proven.
