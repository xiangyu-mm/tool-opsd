Evidence from the longitudinal SEARCH study (1,142 youth with recent-onset type 1 diabetes, followed for two years) supports this statement.

Adiposity was assessed using BMI z-score, while lipid outcomes included total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, non-HDL-C, and triglycerides. Changes in adiposity were associated with favorable changes only in HDL-C and triglycerides—not across the full lipid profile. The effect was stronger among youth with higher baseline adiposity, and the authors described weight loss as an effective but limited approach to treating dyslipidemia.

Therefore, change in adiposity minimally affected the overall lipid profile, although it had some favorable effects on HDL-C and triglycerides.
