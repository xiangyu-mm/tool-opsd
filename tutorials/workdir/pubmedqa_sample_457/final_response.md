**Conclusion: No—the combined strategy was not shown to be superior.**

The relevant study is Mina et al., *JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions* (2016; PMID: 27491601). It pooled **8 randomized trials involving 27,491 patients with acute coronary syndromes** and analyzed outcomes by access site and anticoagulant.

- Bivalirudin reduced major bleeding versus heparin in **femoral-access** patients (OR 0.51, 95% CI 0.46–0.60), but not significantly in **radial-access** patients (OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.45–1.26).
- Radial access reduced major bleeding versus femoral access among **heparin-treated** patients (OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.77), but not significantly among **bivalirudin-treated** patients (OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.65–1.41).
- The analysis found no differences in major adverse cardiovascular events or all-cause mortality between anticoagulants regardless of access site.

The authors concluded that combining bivalirudin with radial access provided **no additional benefit**. A caveat is that these were stratified comparisons within trials, not a dedicated randomized trial directly comparing the combined strategy with each alternative; the abstract also does not report a formal interaction test.
