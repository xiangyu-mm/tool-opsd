Yes. Evidence from Colombia indicates that platelet abnormalities are associated with clinical complications in malaria:

- In a mixed cohort of 862 patients with *P. falciparum*, *P. vivax*, or mixed infection, patients with complications had lower platelet counts and plateletcrit and higher platelet distribution width (PDW). Thrombocytopenia was associated with liver dysfunction and occurred alongside severe anemia, neurologic and pulmonary complications, renal impairment, and severe hypoglycemia (PMID: 25075486; evidence: `call_w43YEBPxspvMsnQvgmBmoKSF`, `call_sqbgANmWyihXQtd2Sl2zGhgU`).
- In a separate Colombian *P. vivax* study, severe thrombocytopenia occurred in 54% of severe cases, alongside hypoglycemia and liver/kidney failure (PMID: 33615000; evidence: `call_KGY1TMUQTyyzxyuYQmjdLxHy`).

However, the available reports do not provide species-stratified effect estimates or establish that platelet abnormalities independently predict complications after adjustment. Thus, the association is supported for Colombian malaria overall, but its comparative strength in *P. vivax* versus *P. falciparum* remains uncertain.

FINAL DECISION: YES
