The evidence supports **yes**: low-dose radiotherapy can reduce PBMC adhesion to endothelium in vitro.

Key findings:

- **PBMC irradiation:** 0.1–0.5 Gy reduced adhesion of viable human PBMCs to activated endothelioma cells to approximately **70% of control** after 4 hours, associated with loss of leukocyte **L-selectin** (PMID:10738086; search evidence `call_D2RaGDLe1iIUU9wgNV8Wo5FW`).
- **Endothelial irradiation:** Approximately 0.3–0.7 Gy reduced PBMC adhesion to activated human endothelial cells, with reductions evident at 4 and 24 hours (PMIDs:12020443, 12194755).
- The effect was **dose- and time-dependent**: adhesion could increase at intermediate time points, such as 12 hours, indicating a biphasic response (PMID:15057429).
- Findings were replicated in an endothelial cell line and primary human dermal microvascular endothelial cells at approximately 0.5 Gy; redox signaling was implicated (PMID:26051282).

The qualification is that “selective” is best supported as a preferential anti-adhesive effect rather than nonspecific loss of cell viability. The studies do **not** establish strong selectivity for one PBMC subset over another, such as T cells versus monocytes.

FINAL DECISION: YES
