Yes. The evidence indicates that classification of breast cancer patients by the conventional three-tier Nottingham Prognostic Index (NPI) identifies significant differences in several immunohistochemical markers.

The strongest direct study was a cohort of 120 breast carcinoma patients (PMID:25082024), which found:

- **ER:** positivity decreased with increasing NPI; **P < 0.01**
- **PR:** positivity decreased with increasing NPI; **P < 0.01**
- **HER2/neu:** overexpression increased in moderate/poor NPI groups; **P = 0.01**
- **Ki-67:** high proliferation increased with NPI, while low Ki-67 was concentrated in the good-NPI group; **P < 0.01**
- **Bcl-2:** more frequent in good/moderate NPI groups, although a marker-specific p-value was not available.
- **p53:** evaluated, but a clear NPI-associated direction or significant result could not be confirmed from the available report.

A separate study also found significantly higher **CD74** expression with higher NPI scores (PMID:27834468), although this did not directly demonstrate differences in ER, PR, or HER2 expression across NPI categories.

Overall, NPI classification highlights a more favorable immunophenotype in lower-risk groups—more ER/PR positivity and lower proliferation—and a less favorable phenotype in higher-risk groups—more HER2 overexpression and high Ki-67. However, NPI incorporates tumor size, grade, and nodal status, so these associations may partly reflect the biological features already embedded in the index.

FINAL DECISION: YES
