Yes. A direct human FDG-PET study found that active cocaine abusers had a blunted brain response to acute alcohol intoxication compared with controls.

- **Study:** Volkow et al., *Life Sciences* (2000), PMID: **10737368**
- **Participants:** 9 active cocaine abusers and 10 controls
- **Challenge:** Oral alcohol, 0.75 g/kg; plasma alcohol levels were equivalent between groups
- **Finding:** Alcohol reduced whole-brain glucose metabolism by **17±10%** in cocaine abusers versus **26±6%** in controls.
- **Regional result:** Alcohol-induced metabolic responses were also attenuated in reported **limbic regions**, cingulate gyrus, medial frontal cortex, and orbitofrontal cortex.

The evidence is direct but limited by the small sample and the fact that the abstract does not identify specific limbic structures such as the amygdala or hippocampus. The result concerns a blunted metabolic response, not necessarily reduced subjective intoxication.

FINAL DECISION: YES
