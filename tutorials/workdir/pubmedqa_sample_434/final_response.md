The available evidence supports **yes**.

- In the strongest direct cohort, **634 patients** initially classified with nonspecific upper-extremity pain were followed. Only **1 patient (0.16%)** had definite, symptom-corresponding discrete pathology identified and successfully treated surgically.
- Other suspected diagnoses were possible or speculative and explained only a fraction of symptoms.
- Nonspecific arm pain is clinically defined by the absence of objective findings or a clear correspondence between symptoms and a specific pathophysiologic process.
- Mechanistic studies suggest that some patients may have altered sensory processing, sensitization, or muscle changes, but these findings are heterogeneous and do not establish a single discrete lesion.
- The evidence is limited by retrospective designs, small mechanistic studies, and incomplete assessment of rare causes such as vascular disease.

Thus, **discrete pathophysiology appears uncommon**, although “nonspecific” does not mean the pain lacks all biological mechanisms.

FINAL DECISION: YES
