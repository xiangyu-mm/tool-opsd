**Yes—long-term estrogen replacement was associated with better measured exercise capacity in the population you asked about, but the evidence does not establish that estrogen caused the improvement.**

The most directly relevant study, published in 2000, examined 248 postmenopausal women without known coronary artery disease. Women who currently or previously used hormone replacement therapy had significantly higher maximal oxygen uptake and anaerobic threshold than never-users. The association was not explained by age or physical-activity level in the authors’ analysis, which also considered other potential confounders. ([PMID: 10740161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10740161/))

The broader evidence is not uniformly positive: a small randomized, placebo-controlled study of 23 women found that 90 days of oral estradiol did not improve exercise capacity. That short trial tested treatment over months, however, rather than the association with long-term use. ([PMID: 16390780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16390780/))

**Bottom line:** Long-term HRT use was associated with improved exercise-capacity measures in women without known CAD, but this observational finding is not proof of a causal benefit.

FINAL DECISION: YES
