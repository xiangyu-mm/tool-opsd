**Yes—if fluid balance is judged from the fluid chart alone.** The study most directly addressing this question found clinically important disagreement between recorded fluid balance and body-weight change in colorectal surgical patients.

Tolstrup and Brandstrup studied **113 ASA I–III patients undergoing elective colorectal surgery**, comparing cumulative nurse-charted fluid balance with daily weight change over six postoperative days. The mean discrepancy was under 0.4 kg/L on days 1–4, but rose to **1.2 kg/L on day 5** (*p* < 0.002) and **2 kg/L on day 6** (*p* < 0.0001). Agreement was uncertain throughout: Bland–Altman limits were wide—over 6 to nearly 10 kg/L—even though correlations were moderate to strong. The authors concluded that fluid charts should not be used alone; body weight and clinical judgment are important adjuncts.

**Interpretation and caveat:** This supports incomplete assessment when relying on charted inputs and outputs alone, especially later in the postoperative period. It does not prove that every clinical assessment is inadequate: the study was descriptive, included elective patients from two trials, and used weight change as a comparison measure rather than a definitive measure of fluid status.
