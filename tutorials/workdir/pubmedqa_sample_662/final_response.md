The evidence supports **yes**.

A study of metropolitan areas in **Harbin and Chongqing, China** (PMID: 25086705) used distributed lag non-linear models and found significant associations between both extreme cold and extreme heat and diabetes mortality:

- **Harbin:**  
  - Extreme cold: RR 1.223 (95% CI 1.054–1.418)  
  - Extreme heat: RR 1.343 (95% CI 1.080–1.670)
- **Chongqing:**  
  - Extreme cold: RR 1.201 (95% CI 1.006–1.434)  
  - Extreme heat: RR 1.811 (95% CI 1.083–3.027)

The effects occurred over different lag periods, indicating that mortality risk could be delayed after exposure. The study was observational and city-specific, so it demonstrates an association rather than definitive causation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
