Yes. The primary study (PMID: 25443366, 2014) evaluated a standardized iridoid fraction from *Barleria prionitis* leaves in rats receiving 50, 100, or 200 mg/kg orally for five days.

The fraction showed dose-dependent gastroprotective effects in several experimentally induced ulcer models:

- Pylorus ligation: 18.67–66.26% protection  
- Aspirin-induced ulcers: 24.65–63.25% protection  
- Cold-restraint stress: 20.77–59.42% protection  
- Ethanol-induced ulcers: 16.93–77.04% protection  

It also reduced acid-pepsin secretion and increased mucin and mucosal glycoproteins. In the stress model, it reduced ulcer index and lipid peroxidation while increasing catalase activity. The authors concluded that the fraction had anti-ulcerogenic and antioxidant potential.

The evidence is from animal experiments, and the retrieved record does not establish the exact strain, group sizes, or comparator-drug details.

FINAL DECISION: YES
