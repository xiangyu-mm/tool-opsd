The case report by Cuperman et al. directly addressed this question:

- **Study:** *Silent polymorphisms in the RYR1 gene do not modify the phenotype of the p.4898 I>T pathogenic mutation in central core disease: a case report.*
- **PMID:** 25084811
- **DOI:** 10.1186/1756-0500-7-487
- The affected patient carried the pathogenic **RYR1 p.Ile4898Thr (p.I4898T)** variant, along with multiple additional silent/synonymous polymorphisms.
- Relatives carrying the exon 98 synonymous variant but not p.I4898T had no pathological phenotype.
- The patient’s central core disease phenotype was within the range previously observed for individuals with the same pathogenic mutation.
- The authors therefore concluded that the silent polymorphisms, including **c.14256A>C**, did **not** act as phenotype modifiers of p.I4898T.

This conclusion is based on a single-family case report, so it does not exclude modifier effects in other genetic or clinical contexts. However, for the reported case, the answer is no.

FINAL DECISION: NO
