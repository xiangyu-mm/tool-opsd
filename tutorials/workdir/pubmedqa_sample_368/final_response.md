**Conclusion: No—not as a stand-alone mortality predictor based on the most directly applicable evidence.** In a prospective multicenter cohort of 400 hemodynamically stable patients aged ≥65 with acute PE, echocardiographic right-ventricular dysfunction was **not associated with 30-day mortality after adjustment** (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.31–2.58; PMID 27498122). The study authors concluded that TTE should not be used alone for risk stratification in this group.

There is some supportive but less directly applicable evidence: another acute-PE cohort found RV diameter and TAPSE <16 mm associated with in-hospital mortality, but did not report results specifically for stable elderly patients (PMID 33999379). Thus, echo findings may contribute to risk assessment, but the evidence does not show that echocardiography reliably predicts mortality on its own in hemodynamically stable elderly patients.
