## Evidence summary

A directly relevant study evaluated this intervention:

- **Study:** Wang et al., *American Journal of Clinical Pathology* (2016), **PMID: 27510715**.
- **Setting:** Harris Health System, a safety-net system serving underserved patients with suspected SLE.
- **Intervention:** A one-click SLE laboratory-testing algorithm with automated reflex testing, clinical-pathologist interpretation, and pathology-based triage before rheumatology evaluation.
- **Comparator:** The pre-intervention referral process.
- **Baseline access problem:** Rheumatology waiting time was approximately **6 months**, and **80%** of patients did not have a complete laboratory workup available at their first rheumatology visit.
- **Outcome:** After implementation, rheumatology waiting time decreased by **50%**, indicating improved access to specialty care.

However, the evidence is limited because it appears to be an observational pre/post quality-improvement evaluation rather than a randomized or controlled study. It did not report referral-completion rates, sample size, or an absolute post-intervention wait time. Thus, the intervention demonstrated improved timeliness of access, but evidence that it increased completed specialty evaluations is insufficient.

FINAL DECISION: YES
