**Yes—early postoperative PTH monitoring can expedite discharge when it is used in a risk-guided management protocol.**

Several cohort studies reported shorter stays:

- In a retrospective sequential cohort of 107 patients, using day-1 PTH and calcium to guide management was associated with a **median stay of 1 day versus 4 days** with serial calcium and symptom monitoring (p<0.0001). The PTH-guided group also had fewer hypocalcemic symptoms and no intravenous calcium use (PMID: **25091178**).
- In a 697-patient retrospective study, a 1-hour PTH protocol was associated with **mean length of stay of 0.89 versus 1.98 days** and fewer overnight observations (53.7% versus 97.4%). Symptomatic hypocalcemia rates were similar between groups (PMID: **27779965**).
- An earlier cohort reported a mean stay of **1.64 versus 2.20 days** with 4-hour PTH-guided management; over half of the protocol group left on postoperative day 1, with no readmissions reported (PMID: **18573120**).

**Caveat:** These findings are mainly from observational or before-and-after studies, not definitive randomized comparisons. PTH should inform a clinical pathway—often alongside calcium levels, symptoms, and supplementation—not serve as a stand-alone guarantee of safe discharge. The results support faster discharge for selected patients, but outcomes depend on the protocol and local follow-up arrangements.
