**Yes.** Human studies generally find that higher circulating retinol-binding protein 4 (RBP4) is associated with greater angiographic coronary disease burden:

- In a 305-patient angiographic CAD cohort, RBP4 increased with the number of narrowed vessels and independently correlated with CAD severity in multivariable analysis (β=0.297; overall model R²=0.422, p<0.001; **PMID: 25142320**).
- Among 55 patients with acute coronary syndrome, RBP4 correlated positively with Gensini score (r=0.286, p=0.034) and SYNTAX I score (r=0.403, p=0.002; **PMID: 34616228**).
- A subclinical-hypothyroidism cohort reported positive associations with stenosed-vessel number and Gensini score (**PMID: 31278889**).
- A STEMI study found positive correlations between RBP4 and SYNTAX score within double- and three-vessel disease subgroups, although RBP4 did not differ significantly across vessel-count groups (**PMID: 33198863**).

Overall, the direction of findings supports an association with lesion burden or complexity. The evidence is observational, and many reported severity associations are unadjusted; it does not establish causation or a specific link to plaque vulnerability.
