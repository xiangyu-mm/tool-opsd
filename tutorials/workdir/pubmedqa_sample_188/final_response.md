Yes—available evidence indicates that position-related injury is uncommon in robotic gynecologic surgery, though it can occasionally be severe.

- In the most directly relevant cohort, **7 of 831 robotic gynecologic procedures (0.8%; 95% CI 0.3%–1.7%)** involved a position-related injury (PMID **25449565**).
- Reported injuries included minor head contusions, lower-extremity neuropathies, one brachial plexus injury, and one large ecchymosis; **no long-term sequelae** were reported.
- Broader robotic-surgery reviews report positioning-injury or postoperative neuropathy rates of roughly **0.8%–6.6%**, but these estimates involve heterogeneous procedures and definitions and are not specific to gynecologic surgery (PMID **30697565**).
- Serious complications such as rhabdomyolysis, compartment syndrome, and ocular injury have been reported, but primarily as **isolated case reports without reliable incidence denominators** (PMIDs **20206458**, **27331136**, **38186052**).

Thus, position-related injury appears **uncommon overall**, but risk may increase with prolonged procedures, steep Trendelenburg, lithotomy positioning, obesity, hypotension, or prior surgery.
