The available evidence supports **yes**.

Two human cervical-carcinoma cohorts found that higher pretreatment peritumoral interstitial fluid flow velocity (`v0`), estimated using Gd-DTPA dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, was associated with poorer outcomes:

- **2014 cohort:** 62 evaluable patients with stage IB–IIIB disease. Higher `v0` predicted worse disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). In multivariable analysis including stage, tumor volume, and lymph-node status, `v0` was the only variable retaining independent prognostic information.
- **2018 cohort:** 54 patients treated with concurrent cisplatin chemoradiotherapy. High `v0` was again associated with worse DFS and OS. High `v0` combined with a large low-enhancing tumor volume identified a particularly poor-prognosis group, with approximately **13% 5-year DFS and OS**, compared with **100%** for patients with low `v0` and small low-enhancing volume.

However, the evidence is limited by small observational cohorts, likely restricted generalizability, and lack of readily available hazard ratios, external validation, and proof of treatment-independent causality. Thus, `v0` is a **promising prognostic biomarker**, especially in locally advanced cervical carcinoma treated with chemoradiotherapy, but its routine clinical utility remains unconfirmed.

FINAL DECISION: YES
