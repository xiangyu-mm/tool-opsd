Yes—PKCε (PRKCE) is supported as a **negative regulator of PVAT-derived vessel formation**, specifically in the context of PVAT progenitor differentiation and in-vitro tubulogenesis.

The direct study (PMID:25433270) found that:

- VEGF-induced differentiation of mouse PVAT-derived mesenchymal vessel progenitors reduced PKCε.
- PKCε overexpression or activation reduced vascular markers including SMA/α-SMA and PECAM/CD31.
- PKCε activation completely abolished tubule formation in collagen-gel assays.
- The proposed mechanism involved interference with the PAK1 pathway.

However, this evidence is limited to one direct study and does not establish inhibition of PVAT-driven neovascularization in an intact in-vivo model. Moreover, in non-PVAT endothelial models, PKCε activation promoted endothelial proliferation, migration, sprouting, and VEGF/FGF-2 angiogenic signaling (PMID:23880610). Thus, PKCε is not a universal anti-angiogenic factor; its effect is context-dependent.
