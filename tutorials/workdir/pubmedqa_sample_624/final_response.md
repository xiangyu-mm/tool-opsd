## Conclusion

**Yes—current observational evidence indicates that contralateral prophylactic mastectomy (CPM) does not provide a demonstrated overall-survival or breast-cancer-specific survival benefit in young women with ER-negative breast cancer.**

Key evidence:

- **National Cancer Data Base cohort (PMID: 25081341):**  
  14,627 women aged ≤45 years with stage I–II breast cancer were analyzed. After adjustment for demographic, tumor, treatment, and comorbidity factors:
  - Overall CPM versus unilateral mastectomy: **HR 0.93, p=0.39**
  - ER-negative subgroup: **HR 1.12, p=0.32**
  - Thus, there was no statistically significant survival improvement, including among ER-negative patients.

- **Population-based cohort of women aged ≤35 years (PMID: 30607124):**  
  Breast-cancer-specific mortality was similar in women who did and did not undergo CPM, with no observed mortality benefit.

- **Why some earlier studies suggested benefit:**  
  A SEER analysis reported better disease-specific and overall survival with CPM (PMID: 25047478), but the authors judged this likely to reflect **selection bias**—healthier, more advantaged, or otherwise lower-risk patients may have been more likely to choose CPM. This is especially important because CPM prevents future cancer in the opposite breast but does not usually reduce the risk of metastasis from the original cancer, which is the major determinant of mortality.

Therefore, the most accurate interpretation is **no proven survival benefit**, rather than definitive proof that CPM can never benefit any individual patient. CPM may still be considered for selected patients with very high contralateral-breast cancer risk, such as some BRCA1/2 carriers, but that is a different question from survival benefit in the general young ER-negative population.

FINAL DECISION: YES
