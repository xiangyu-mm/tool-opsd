**Answer: Yes, with an important qualification.** A study developed a serum ELISA for a titin fragment attributed primarily to MMP-2 cleavage and tested it in dexamethasone-treated rats and a 56-day human bed-rest study (PMID: 25077715). In both settings, fragment levels changed over the course of atrophy-associated exposure. In the human study, levels fell by 11.9% after one day of bed rest, then rose gradually to 16.4% above baseline by day 47.

This supports the fragment as a **time-dependent indicator of atrophy-related muscle turnover**, but not as a simple, monotonic measure of muscle loss: the early decrease followed by a later increase means a single measurement—or a rise alone—does not straightforwardly quantify atrophy. The evidence comes from specific experimental models, so it does not by itself establish broad clinical validity across causes of muscle wasting.
