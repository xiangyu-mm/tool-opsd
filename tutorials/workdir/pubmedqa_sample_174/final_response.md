Yes. The primary study by Brewer et al. (2014, PMID: 25432266) directly tested Arabidopsis **DMR1** mutants against both pathogens.

- **dmr1-2** showed increased resistance to both *Fusarium culmorum* and *F. graminearum* in silique infection assays.
- The same allele also showed reduced fungal colonization of rosette leaves.
- For *F. culmorum*, additional alleles (**dmr1-3, dmr1-4, and dmr1-5**) enhanced silique resistance, although effects varied by allele and tissue.
- The mutants accumulated **L-homoserine**, consistent with impaired homoserine-kinase activity; exogenous L-homoserine also reduced infection.
- The evidence is specific to Arabidopsis tissues and does not establish equivalent field resistance in cereal crops.

Thus, DMR1 mutations—most clearly **dmr1-2**—do confer enhanced resistance to both *F. culmorum* and *F. graminearum*, with allele- and assay-dependent variation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
