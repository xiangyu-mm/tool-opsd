The evidence supports **yes**: a study of 10 healthy adults and 20 people post-stroke found that plantarflexion moment was significantly related to contralateral step length during the split-belt post-adaptation period. In both groups, moment decreased for the fast limb and increased for the slow limb from mid-stance to toe-off. The authors also noted that the change could increase paretic plantarflexion moment when the paretic step is longer, but decrease it when the step is shorter (PMID: 25074249).

This supports plantarflexion moment as a **contributor associated with** the step-length after-effect. The available evidence does not establish that it causes or predicts the after-effect, or that the moment–step-length relationship differs between groups.

FINAL DECISION: YES
