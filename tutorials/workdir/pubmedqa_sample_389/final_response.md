### Conclusion

**Preoperative statin use at radical prostatectomy has not been shown consistently to be associated with biochemical recurrence, and the most direct study found no significant association with pathologic upgrading.**

The most directly relevant study was a 2016 cohort of 3,042 men undergoing radical prostatectomy. Statin use at surgery was not significantly associated with:

- **Biochemical recurrence:** adjusted HR 1.06 (95% CI 0.86–1.31).
- **Pathologic upgrading** among men whose biopsy showed Gleason 3+3=6: adjusted OR 0.78 (95% CI 0.58–1.04).

Other studies make the recurrence evidence mixed: one SEARCH cohort reported lower recurrence among statin users (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.50–0.97), but another cohort found no association, and their pooled estimate was also null (HR 1.00, 95% CI 0.80–1.19). Some favorable reports have retrospective designs or exposure definitions that combine preoperative and postoperative use, so they do not establish a benefit from taking statins at the time of surgery.

**Interpretation:** The evidence does not demonstrate that preoperative statin use is associated with either outcome. This is not proof that there is no effect; the evidence is observational, and individual statins have not been adequately compared for these outcomes.
