**Yes.** Direct high-speed imaging studies indicate that vocal-fold/glottal oscillation changes across the tenor passaggio. Reported differences include changes in **open quotient** and oscillatory **periodicity/regularity**. The specific pattern depends on whether the singer transitions to falsetto or maintains stage voice above the passaggio, and is also influenced by vowel, loudness, and nasalance.

For example, a study of seven professional tenors using high-speed imaging at 20,000 frames per second found increased glottal-area open quotient across transitions, with greater open quotient in falsetto than in stage voice above the passaggio; it found no major fundamental-frequency jumps (PMID: 27499033). Other studies reported vowel-dependent stability (PMID: 28040341), greater periodicity in stage voice than in modal-to-falsetto transitions (PMID: 31668917), and loudness-dependent changes in open quotient (PMID: 33455852).

The evidence is direct but based on relatively small groups of professional singers. An earlier rigid-laryngoscopy study was often unable to assess the vocal-fold cycles because supraglottic structures obstructed the view (PMID: 25017997). So, the findings support changes in oscillatory characteristics, but not one universal pattern for every singer or passaggio strategy.
