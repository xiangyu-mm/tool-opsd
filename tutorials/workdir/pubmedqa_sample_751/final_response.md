Yes. A 2000 study of 70 kidney transplant recipients measured serum GALβ1-4GlcNAc α2-6-sialyltransferase (ST6GalI/ST6GAL1) immediately before transplantation.

- DGF was defined as requiring posttransplant dialysis.
- ST6GalI levels were higher in recipients who developed DGF: **3735 ± 228 U** versus **2933 ± 83 U** in those without DGF.
- The difference was statistically significant (**P < 0.0001**).
- ST6GalI level and cold ischemic time were reportedly independent risk factors in multivariable analysis.

Thus, increased pretransplant ST6GAL1 was associated with and predicted dialysis-defined DGF in that cohort. However, the evidence is limited to one older study without reported discrimination metrics, validated cutoff, or external validation, so its value as a broadly applicable clinical biomarker remains uncertain.

FINAL DECISION: YES
