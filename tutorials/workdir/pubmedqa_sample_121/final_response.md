The cluster-randomized trial by Perrin et al. (PMID: 25445827) supports **yes**.

- **Design:** 22 pediatric practices; 948 children aged 3–12 years with persistent asthma.
- **Intervention:** Parent telephone peer coaching for 12 months.
- **Primary morbidity outcome:** At 12 months, children receiving coaching had **20.9 more symptom-free days per child** than those receiving usual care (95% CI, 9.1–32.7).
- **Acute-care outcomes:** There was no overall reduction in emergency-department visits at 12 months, but by 24 months ED visits were reduced by **0.28 per child** (95% CI, −0.50 to −0.02). Reductions were particularly evident among Medicaid-insured children, including fewer ED visits and hospitalizations.

Thus, the trial showed reduced asthma morbidity overall, especially asthma impairment measured by symptom-free days, although effects on acute healthcare use were not uniform across all groups and time points.

FINAL DECISION: YES
