Yes, in the specific MHC-disparate model tested.

In Sonoda et al. (Transplantation, 2000; PMID: 10755569), ICAM-1-deficient mice receiving B10.D2 corneal grafts showed:

- 100% long-term graft acceptance versus 58% in wild-type recipients (P=0.006).
- No donor-specific alloantigen DTH, indicating suppressed host allosensitization.

However, this protection was mismatch-dependent: ICAM-1 deficiency did not improve survival of fully mismatched BALB/c or minor-antigen-mismatched BALB.b grafts. Thus, it supports suppression of sensitization and rejection for the tested B10.D2 MHC disparity, but not universal prevention of corneal allograft rejection.

FINAL DECISION: YES
