The evidence supports **yes**, particularly in cardiac ventricular myocytes.

- **NOX2 is required:** Angiotensin II increased NOX2-dependent ROS and cellular arrhythmia susceptibility; these effects were reduced or absent in gp91phox/NOX2-deficient cells (PMID:25073061; `call_PfXqJaJuVAydMvsAbP7rde1p`).
- **PKA pathway:** PKA inhibition prevented Ang II-induced increases in peak L-type Ca²⁺ and Na⁺ currents, supporting a NOX2–ROS–PKA branch that alters membrane excitability. Redox-sensitive PKARIα/PKA signaling also contributed to Ang II-stimulated Ca²⁺ current, although direct NOX2-mediated PKA oxidation was not fully demonstrated (`call_ubtczVDSRxohcMVNwypex6Ll`).
- **CaMKII pathway:** CaMKIIδ deletion prevented Ang II-induced diastolic sarcoplasmic-reticulum Ca²⁺ leak, supporting a NOX2–ROS–CaMKII branch that promotes afterdepolarization-related triggered activity (PMID:25073061; `call_PfXqJaJuVAydMvsAbP7rde1p`).
- **Independent support:** In atrial myocytes, Ang II-induced ROS, Ca²⁺ overload, and arrhythmic events were suppressed by NOX2 inhibition or deficiency, although PKA and CaMKII were not directly tested there (PMID:29625277; `call_Wwq3MAQXX09WWNdxYevZWgJx`).
- **Important qualification:** In vivo NOX2-overexpression data indicate that the mechanism is context-dependent and does not universally operate through PKA/CaMKII-mediated RyR2 changes (PMID:33483749; `call_eU5BaySheOcmlnW7v6BpYuJ6`).

Thus, the strongest conclusion is that **NOX2 mediates Ang II-dependent cellular arrhythmogenesis through parallel PKA-dependent ionic-current changes and CaMKII-dependent SR Ca²⁺ leak**, with the most direct evidence coming from the 2014 ventricular-myocyte study.

FINAL DECISION: YES
