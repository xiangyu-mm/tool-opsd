**Yes—alterations of the lamina cribrosa (LC) have been observed alongside peripapillary retinoschisis (PPRS) in both glaucoma and pachychoroid-spectrum disease, but the evidence establishes association, not causation.**

The central evidence is a retrospective, single-institution case series of 16 selected eyes with PPRS and abnormal LC findings on enhanced-depth imaging OCT: eight eyes had glaucoma and eight had pachychoroid-spectrum disease. In glaucoma, the abnormalities were central or peripheral focal LC defects; in pachychoroid disease, they were LC disinsertions or peripheral focal defects. Central LC defects corresponded to inner-layer retinoschisis, while disinsertions and peripheral defects corresponded to outer-layer retinoschisis. The small, selected series cannot establish how common this association is or whether LC changes cause PPRS. **(PMID: 27506483)**

The pachychoroid finding needs particular qualification. A later cross-sectional study assessed pachychoroid patients **without PPRS**: focal LC defects occurred in 6/180 patients (3.3%), versus 0/236 controls, a difference that was not statistically significant (P=0.318). Thus, LC defects can occur in pachychoroid disease without retinoschisis; this supports possible co-occurrence, not a specific or proven causal link. **(PMID: 35047360)** A glaucoma case report also described PPRS corresponding to an LC defect on OCT, consistent with the case-series observation. **(PMID: 32011583)**

**Bottom line:** the reported association is present in both disease settings, but its strength and causal meaning remain uncertain.

FINAL DECISION: YES
