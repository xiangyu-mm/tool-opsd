The most likely diagnosis is **primary hyperfibrinolysis (paraneoplastic hyperfibrinolysis associated with advanced prostate cancer)**.

Supporting features:

- Extensive spontaneous hematomas and hemarthroses
- Markedly reduced fibrinogen
- Elevated D-dimer
- Prolonged PT and aPTT
- Normal platelet count, arguing against typical consumptive DIC
- Near-exact match to a published prostate cancer case of primary hyperfibrinolysis (PMID: 29320623)

Acquired hemophilia would usually cause an isolated prolonged aPTT with normal PT and fibrinogen. DIC remains a differential, but preserved platelets and the characteristic prostate-cancer-associated fibrinolytic pattern favor primary rather than secondary hyperfibrinolysis.
