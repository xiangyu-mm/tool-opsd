The relevant study is **PMID: 25433917**, conducted in the mass-flowering shrub *Rhododendron ferrugineum*.

- Researchers examined **28 natural patches** differing in floral display and likely pollinator/mate availability.
- In sparse patches, **open seed set was relatively higher**, consistent with reproductive assurance through selfing when pollination opportunities were limited.
- However, estimated **lifetime inbreeding depression was approximately 0.90**, indicating that selfed progeny achieved only about **10% of the lifetime fitness** of outcrossed progeny.
- Thus, selfing improved early reproductive assurance but imposed severe later-life fitness costs. The authors concluded that these costs strongly counteracted—and should evolutionarily oppose—the benefit of selfing.

The evidence is based on natural patch comparisons, emasculation/pollination treatments, progeny-array estimates of selfing, and comparisons of progeny versus adult fitness; it was not a simple controlled self-versus-outcross experiment with directly reported paired fitness means.

FINAL DECISION: YES
