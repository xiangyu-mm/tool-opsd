Yes. Human studies indicate that stress-related symptoms and inflammatory biomarkers are associated with bioimpedance measures, particularly:

- **Lower phase angle** with higher CRP, TNF-α, IL-6, and other inflammatory markers.
- **Higher extracellular water (ECW)** and lower intracellular water with chronic stress-related symptoms and higher inflammatory burden.
- **Higher evening cortisol** and lower morning cortisol associated with higher ECW and lower phase angle.
- Associations with perceived stress, HRV, leukocyte counts, and raw resistance/reactance are weaker or more context-dependent.

Evidence is strongest for **inflammation versus phase angle** and for **stress-related symptoms/cortisol versus ECW, intracellular water, and phase angle**. However, most studies are observational or cross-sectional, and associations vary with BMI, hydration, nutrition, disease severity, and bioimpedance device.

FINAL DECISION: YES
