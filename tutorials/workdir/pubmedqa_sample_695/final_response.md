Yes—current evidence supports an interaction between aging and alcohol exposure that alters hepatic DNA hydroxymethylation (5hmC).

- In a direct 2×2 mouse study (PMID:25070523), 4-month-old and 18-month-old male C57BL/6 mice received control or ethanol Lieber–DeCarli diets for 5 weeks.
- Ethanol reduced global hepatic 5hmC in young mice, from **0.29 ± 0.06% to 0.22 ± 0.01% of cytosines** (*p*=0.004), but did not significantly reduce 5hmC in old mice.
- The formal **age-by-alcohol interaction was significant** (*p*=0.009), indicating effect modification rather than merely independent additive effects.
- In the same study, old mice had lower hepatic **Tet2** and **Tet3** expression than young mice (*p*<0.005), providing a plausible age-related mechanistic correlate, although a TET-expression interaction was not established.
- Age-only studies show inconsistent directions of hepatic 5hmC change with aging, and the direct interaction evidence is limited to bulk liver from male mice. Comparable human liver or purified-hepatocyte studies were not identified.

Thus, the evidence supports an age-dependent alcohol effect on hepatic 5hmC, but its relevance to humans and specific liver cell populations remains uncertain.

FINAL DECISION: YES
