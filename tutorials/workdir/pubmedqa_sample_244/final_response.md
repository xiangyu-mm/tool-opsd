Yes—**serum α-hydroxybutyrate (α-HB) was associated with elevated 1-hour OGTT glucose and early-phase β-cell dysfunction** in the relevant human study.

- **Study:** Varvel et al., *BMJ Open Diabetes Research & Care* (2014), PMID: **25452875**
- **Population:** 217 adults at increased risk for diabetes; 75-g OGTT with serial glucose and insulin measurements.
- **Elevated 1-hour glucose endpoint:** ≥155 mg/dL.
- **Findings:**
  - Participants with higher α-HB levels (>3.9 µg/mL) had greater glucose exposure during OGTT.
  - They showed a reduced initial insulin-response slope, interpreted as impaired early insulin secretion.
  - Adding α-HB to age, sex, BMI, fasting glucose, HbA1c, and fasting insulin increased the AUC for predicting 1-hour glucose ≥155 mg/dL from **0.82 to 0.86** (*p*=0.015), with a net reclassification improvement of **52%** (*p*<0.0001).

The main limitation is that this was a **cross-sectional** study, so it supports biomarker-based prediction/discrimination during the OGTT—not proof that α-HB independently predicts future β-cell failure or diabetes. Also, early β-cell dysfunction was inferred from insulin kinetics rather than a standard insulinogenic-index result.

FINAL DECISION: YES
