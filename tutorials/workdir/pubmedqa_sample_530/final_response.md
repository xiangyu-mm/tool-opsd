Yes. In cultured human coronary vascular smooth muscle cells, urokinase’s protease-containing C-terminal fragment induced proliferation through a pathway requiring:

1. Plasmin activity  
2. Metalloprotease/ADAM activity  
3. HB-EGF shedding  
4. EGFR transactivation  

EGFR inhibition with AG1478 and EGFR knockdown both blocked proliferation, while plasmin or metalloprotease inhibition prevented EGFR phosphorylation and the proliferative response (PMID: 25082749). Supporting studies in rat smooth muscle cells also found that uPA-induced proliferation depended on plasmin and EGFR signaling (PMID: 15874933). A related human-cell study confirmed ADAM- and HB-EGF-dependent EGFR transactivation, although it measured migration rather than proliferation (PMID: 19394555).

Important qualification: the evidence supports the protease → ADAM → HB-EGF → EGFR sequence, but the key human proliferation study reported that uPAR was not required. Thus, EGFR transactivation by this form of triple-membrane signaling is required, but uPA–uPAR engagement is not necessarily the initiating step.

FINAL DECISION: YES
