Yes. Direct evidence from two rat studies supports that melatonin prevents or attenuates radiation-related oxidative stress and periodontal tissue breakdown in experimental periodontitis:

- **2017 study (PMID: 27510437):** In irradiated rats with experimental periodontitis, melatonin significantly reduced oxidative stress index, 8-hydroxy-2′-deoxyguanosine, malondialdehyde, and C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen. It also reduced alveolar bone destruction and periodontal attachment-level damage compared with irradiated, untreated rats.  
  *Source: `call_x9zP1nfXy8LTOtXA8NSWLGKi`*

- **2024 study (PMID: 39614289):** In rats given ligature-induced periodontitis and 5 Gy irradiation, melatonin at 10 mg/kg for 15 days improved TOS/TAS, inflammatory and apoptotic markers, RANKL/OPG-related remodeling, and significantly limited buccal, lingual, and furcal alveolar bone loss.  
  *Sources: `call_Lp8ZpBUsFq5lQdZ0myLvb8Ng`, `call_fF6YWxRq4YylPbue3bkBIYVd`*

The evidence is consistent but limited to preclinical animal studies; it does not establish clinical efficacy in humans.
