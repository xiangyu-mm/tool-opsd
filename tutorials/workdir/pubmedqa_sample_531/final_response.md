**Yes.** Research has evaluated and compared microwave-oven disinfection with chemical disinfection for dental gypsum or stone casts.

- A 2014 study of contaminated Type III dental-stone casts reported that **900 W microwave treatment for 5 minutes** and **0.5% sodium hypochlorite** each reduced bacterial counts by about 5 log₁₀ and were considered equivalent (PMID: 25083033; source: `call_6NqZ6AaI8idxeON9tCyggXja`).
- A 2024 study comparing microwave treatment with chemical immersion and spray on Type IV casts found microwave treatment more effective microbiologically. It increased surface roughness, but none of the methods significantly changed dimensional accuracy (PMID: 38317034; source: `call_ozDEbKVtRzNOpXciBB2hHL4v`).
- Other studies also compared microwave disinfection with sodium hypochlorite or other agents, with effectiveness varying by study conditions and microorganism (PMIDs: 16335173, 24082984, 39657937; sources: `call_6W81xLQ5zqIbEyPsyGXKYjXn`, `call_bqHQqshZDN8u7URPxa3q4cEe`, `call_yLVRBBRoSOdzA1ncdPImsDNp`). The 2005 study’s chemical treatment was applied to impressions before casting, so that comparison was not like-for-like.

Overall, the literature supports direct comparisons for **microbial disinfection**. Evidence directly comparing the methods for hardness, strength, or surface-detail preservation is limited.

FINAL DECISION: YES
