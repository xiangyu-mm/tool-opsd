Yes. A primary goat study (PMID: 10730741) used cranial bypass to separately administer propofol to the torso/spinal circulation or cranial circulation while recording lumbar dorsal-horn neuronal activity during noxious hindlimb mechanical stimulation.

- Torso propofol significantly depressed responses:
  - 1 mg/kg: 500 ± 243 to 174 ± 240 impulses/min at 1 minute
  - 3.75 mg/kg: 478 ± 204 to 91 ± 138 impulses/min at 1 minute
- Cranial propofol produced no significant depression.

Because spinal/torso exposure reduced dorsal-horn responses while cranial exposure did not, the experiment supports a direct spinal depressant effect, with little or no indirect supraspinal contribution. The study was conducted under constant isoflurane anesthesia, so “direct” refers to the differential spinal-versus-cranial effect rather than proof of a specific cellular molecular mechanism.

FINAL DECISION: YES
