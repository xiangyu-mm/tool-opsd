Yes. Human studies indicate that endurance exercise modulates heat-shock protein (HSP) expression in circulating leukocytes, especially HSP70/HSP72 and, less consistently, HSP27.

Key evidence:

- **Acute endurance exercise:** Running at 75% VO₂max increased leukocyte HSP27 and inducible HSP70 mRNA/protein, with larger responses in warmer conditions (28°C versus 18°C) [call_mKG7VqjpqRCPuNSUp6v2icI9].
- **Prolonged exercise:** A 6-hour ultramarathon increased HSP70 mRNA in blood mononuclear cells immediately after exercise, although HSP27 mRNA did not change [call_9Hd9HSTcDOMWMQg0aClVEzu3].
- **Quantified PBMC response:** After one moderate aerobic bout, PBMC HSP70 mRNA increased 1.59-fold and HSP27 mRNA 1.49-fold at 3 hours. At 24 hours, HSP70 and HSP27 proteins increased 3.40-fold and 1.81-fold, respectively [call_vzKCF8x75ugxkOPWSgu7ELK9].
- **Training adaptation:** Ten days of exercise heat acclimation increased basal PBMC HSP72 by 17.7% and HSP90 by 21.1%, but reduced ex-vivo HSP72 inducibility. A year-long training season also increased intracellular lymphocyte HSP70 in a subgroup of athletes [call_uxPoHiz7Ec4uHV540nCCgSsK; call_zwVXF09vCGFGjiBRB6OfNEtB].
- **Modifiers:** Responses vary with exercise intensity and duration, heat exposure, training status, sex-hormone status, supplementation, disease, and sampling time. In chronic-fatigue-syndrome patients, some leukocyte HSPs decreased after exercise rather than increased [call_5RaikkWFcSHCG1h1U9XwTQ92].

Overall, the most defensible conclusion is that endurance exercise produces a generally transient leukocyte HSP response—early mRNA induction followed by later protein increases—but chronic training can elevate basal expression, prime responses, or blunt inducibility. Thus, modulation is well supported, while the direction and magnitude are context dependent.
