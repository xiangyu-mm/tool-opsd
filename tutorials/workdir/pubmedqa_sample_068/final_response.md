**Yes—with an important qualification.** The prospective international cohort study (PMID: 25429159; DOI: 10.2967/jnumed.114.145326) concluded that a **single interim PET scan could not distinguish chemoresistant lymphoma from disease that would ultimately achieve complete remission**, and therefore could not guide risk-adapted treatment.

However, interim PET was **associated with outcomes at the group level**: two-year event-free survival was 58% for interim-PET-positive patients versus 90% for PET-negative patients (HR 5.31, 95% CI 3.29–8.56). Among interim-PET-positive patients, some later reached PET-negative complete remission, illustrating why a positive interim scan alone did not establish treatment failure.

FINAL DECISION: YES
