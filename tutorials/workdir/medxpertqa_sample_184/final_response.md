A competent adult has the right to refuse blood transfusion, even if refusal may result in death; therefore, the father’s refusal should be respected.

An unconscious child with life-threatening hemorrhage cannot provide consent. Parents generally make decisions for minors, but they cannot refuse immediately necessary, life-saving treatment when doing so places the child at substantial risk of death. In this emergency, physicians should not delay treatment to await the mother or obtain a court order. The emergency doctrine permits transfusion and surgery to proceed in the child’s best interests.

Thus, the son should receive packed red blood cells and emergency surgery, while the father should not be transfused against his wishes.
