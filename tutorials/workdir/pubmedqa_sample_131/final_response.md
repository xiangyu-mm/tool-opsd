Yes. The available evidence suggests that fragmented QRS is associated with greater PVC frequency in patients without overt cardiac disease, but the evidence is limited and observational.

The most directly relevant study was a retrospective cohort of 282 patients described as having no overt structural heart disease. fQRS prevalence increased with PVC counts of <120, 120–720, and >720 per 24 hours: **26.2%, 46.2%, and 65.9%**, respectively. In regression analysis, fQRS was a borderline predictor of frequent PVCs (**OR 2.11, 95% CI 1.00–4.45; p=0.05**) (PMID: 25430411; source: `call_2eUwe2gzaUY7uneIa9sjDHAd`).

A second retrospective study of 199 patients also found fQRS more often among those with PVC burden >10% than among those with burden <10% (**47.5% vs 30.6%; p=0.015**). However, the retrieved report did not establish that all participants lacked structural heart disease or provide an adjusted fQRS estimate (PMID: 34493030; source: `call_6KQacX2BaiuGsJPLOATS3Pib`).

**Bottom line:** Findings support an association, not causation. Small retrospective samples, borderline adjusted evidence in the directly applicable study, and incomplete reporting of ECG criteria and disease screening limit confidence.

FINAL DECISION: YES
