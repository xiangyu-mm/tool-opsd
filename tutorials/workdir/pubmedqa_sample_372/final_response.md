Research supports that *Cryptosporidium parvum* rhomboid1 (CpROM1) has activity associated with CpGP900 cleavage.

- In the key 2016 study (PMID: 27502595), CpROM1 interacted with CpGP900 in yeast two-hybrid assays.
- Co-expression of CpROM1 and CpGP900 in mammalian cells produced a CpGP900 cleavage product; CpGP900 alone did not.
- CpROM1 was subsequently localized to micronemes, where CpGP900 is also found, providing biological plausibility.
- Independent work confirmed that endogenous GP900 is cleaved during secretion, but did not prove CpROM1 is the responsible enzyme.
- Definitive physiological proof is still lacking because no purified-enzyme assay, catalytic-mutant control, genetic perturbation, or mapped cleavage site was reported.

Thus, CpROM1 is experimentally supported as a CpGP900-cleaving rhomboid protease, although the evidence is strongest in a heterologous-cell system rather than a fully validated native-parasite mechanism.

FINAL DECISION: YES
