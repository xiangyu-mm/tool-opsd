The matching study was a retrospective cohort of **88 patients** with metastatic renal cell carcinoma who underwent metastasectomy between **2003 and 2014**, during the era of modern targeted systemic therapy (**PMID:27520319**).

Key findings:

- Preoperative **CRP >5 mg/L** was associated with significantly worse overall survival.
- Median overall survival:
  - **CRP >5 mg/L:** 50.67 months
  - **CRP ≤5 mg/L:** 135.47 months
- CRP remained statistically significant in multivariable analysis (**P=0.038**), alongside resection-margin status (**P=0.015**).
- The PubMed abstract does not provide the adjusted hazard ratio or 95% confidence interval.
- The authors concluded that elevated preoperative CRP was an **independent predictor of overall survival** after metastasectomy, while noting the need for larger multicenter validation.

Therefore, in this study, preoperative CRP was an independent prognostic factor, although the evidence is retrospective and should not be interpreted as establishing causality.
