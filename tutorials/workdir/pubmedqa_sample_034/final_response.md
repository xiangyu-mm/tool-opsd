The evidence supports the statement **“blood viscosity, but not shear stress, associates with delayed flow-mediated dilation.”**

The key study was a 2015 observational study of 148 adults (PMID: **25428725**). Brachial FMD was assessed at 50 seconds, 2 minutes, and 3 minutes after cuff release. Participants were classified as early dilators, late dilators, or non-dilators.

- **Blood viscosity:** Significantly higher in late dilators and identified as an independent predictor of FMD timing/pattern.
- **Shear stress:** The early post-ischemia shear-stress stimulus, quantified as shear-stress area under the curve during the first 40 seconds, did not differ significantly among groups and was not associated with the FMD pattern.
- **Peak FMD:** Did not significantly differ between early and late dilators, indicating that the association concerns delayed timing rather than necessarily reduced maximal dilation.

Other studies show that shear stress can influence or dynamically change during FMD, but they do not contradict the more specific finding that shear-stress magnitude was not associated with delayed FMD in the key study. The evidence is therefore direct but largely based on one principal observational study rather than a broad replicated literature.
