The evidence supports the claim.

A prospective study of 65 patients with short-segment Barrett’s oesophagus undergoing impedance–pH monitoring during PPI therapy found:

- **Impaired chemical clearance was associated with progression:** patients who developed incident dysplasia had substantially lower post-reflux swallow-induced peristaltic wave (PSPW) indices than those who did not (15% vs 32% at surveillance; 12% vs 30% at baseline; **P=0.001**). A PSPW index below 26% predicted dysplasia with 75% accuracy.
- **Inadequate acid suppression was not associated with progression:** gastric and oesophageal acid exposure times did not differ significantly between groups, despite PPI treatment and reported symptom control.

The main limitation is that the outcome was incident dysplasia at 3-year surveillance rather than invasive cancer, and the conclusion is based principally on one cohort study (PMID:25087846).

FINAL DECISION: YES
