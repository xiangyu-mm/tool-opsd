**Yes.** Adult mouse studies support that KLF5 is required for proper maintenance of intestinal crypt proliferation, particularly in Lgr5-positive stem cells.

In adult inducible intestinal epithelial Klf5-knockout mice, crypt proliferation markers fell within days and crypt apoptosis increased. Proliferation partially recovered later, but remained below wild-type levels. In a separate Lgr5-lineage deletion model, targeted stem cells showed reduced proliferation and increased apoptosis; many were eventually lost, although proliferation re-emerged in transit-amplifying cells. These findings support a real requirement for normal crypt homeostasis, while indicating that compensatory or surviving progenitors can restore some proliferative activity.

Evidence cited in the search results includes adult inducible-knockout studies (PMIDs **21763241**, **24440658**, **25069574**, **25460247**) and an inducible intestinal stem-cell study (PMID **24626089**). Constitutive deletion also showed severe proliferation defects, but is less directly informative about adult maintenance because it includes developmental effects.
