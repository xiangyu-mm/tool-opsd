Yes. A human study directly assessed both markers in Frozen Shoulder Syndrome (adhesive capsulitis):

- **β-catenin:** Protein levels were significantly increased in frozen-shoulder tissue compared with rotator-cuff-tear tissue, measured by Western blot.
- **IGF2:** **mRNA expression** was significantly increased in primary fibroblasts derived from frozen-shoulder tissue compared with rotator-cuff-derived fibroblasts.

Study: *“IGF2 expression and β-catenin levels are increased in Frozen Shoulder Syndrome,”* Clinical and Investigative Medicine (2014), **PMID: 25090267**.

Important qualification: the study measured **IGF2 transcript**, not IGF2 protein, and did not clearly isolate a separate synovial-tissue compartment. Thus, the evidence supports increased IGF2 expression in disease-derived fibroblasts and increased β-catenin protein in frozen-shoulder tissue.

FINAL DECISION: YES
