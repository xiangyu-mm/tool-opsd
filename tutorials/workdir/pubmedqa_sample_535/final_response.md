Evidence supports an association between desert dust—specifically Asian dust (Kosa)—and short-term acute myocardial infarction (AMI) risk in Western Japan.

- **Fukuoka study (Western Japan; PMID: 25074374):** A time-stratified case-crossover study of 3,068 AMI hospitalizations found that Asian-dust events occurring **0–4 days before admission** were significantly associated with AMI incidence, particularly exposure **4 days before admission**.
- **Kumamoto replication (PMID: 29020374):** Asian dust one day before onset was associated with AMI (**OR 1.46, 95% CI 1.09–1.95**), with a stronger association for non-ST-elevation MI.
- **Nationwide Japanese study (PMID: 31950372):** Exposure two days before admission was associated with myocardial infarction with nonobstructive coronary arteries (**OR 1.65, 95% CI 1.18–2.29**), although no association was observed for MI with established coronary artery disease.

These are observational case-crossover studies, so they support a **short-term triggering effect** rather than proving that desert dust causes long-term underlying coronary disease. Nevertheless, the directly relevant Western Japan study and subsequent independent evidence support answering yes.

FINAL DECISION: YES
