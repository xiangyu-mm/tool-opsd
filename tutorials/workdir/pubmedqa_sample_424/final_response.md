**Yes.** A controlled study in neonatal piglets found that lipid-emulsion formulation affected both ileal microbiota and mucosal host gene expression during 14 days of total parenteral nutrition (TPN). Piglets received equivalent lipid doses of SMOFlipid, which contains long-chain omega-3 PUFAs, or predominantly omega-6 Intralipid; sow-fed piglets served as controls.

The microbiota differed between the two TPN groups (*P*<.05). SMOFlipid-treated piglets’ communities were more similar to sow-fed controls by UniFrac distance, while the individual taxa changed in different directions: Intralipid was associated with increased *Parabacteroides* and SMOFlipid with increased Enterobacteriaceae. TPN also increased expression of mucin, claudin 1, β-defensin 2, and interleukin 8; those increases were smaller with SMOFlipid than with Intralipid (*P*<.05).

**Interpretation:** Formulation matters, but the microbial shifts are not uniformly beneficial or harmful, and differences in gene expression do not by themselves establish a clinical benefit. This conclusion rests on a single piglet study and should not be assumed to apply directly to human neonates.

**Source:** “Lipid Emulsion Formulation of Parenteral Nutrition Affects Intestinal Microbiota and Host Responses in Neonatal Piglets,” *JPEN* (2017), PMID: 27495286.
