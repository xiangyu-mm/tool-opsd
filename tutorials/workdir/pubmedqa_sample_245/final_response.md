**Yes—available evidence indicates that receipt of preventive care differs by insurance status, although the pattern depends on the service and comparison.**

- A U.S. community health-center study found that ACA Medicaid expansion reduced uninsured diabetes-related visits and increased preventive-care access; HbA1c screening rose in several patient groups (PMID: 30413546).
- In a national sample of adults with diagnosed diabetes, trends differed across insurance subgroups: dental visits declined among uninsured adults, while cholesterol testing declined among Medicaid-insured adults (PMID: 37844212).
- In a large FQHC cohort, privately insured patients had higher adjusted checkup completion than Medicaid patients (aRR 1.15, 95% CI 1.10–1.19) (PMID: 38100007).

These studies support a difference by insurance status, but do not show a uniform advantage for one insurance group across every diabetes preventive service. **The evidence is suggestive rather than a definitive point-of-care comparison across all services.**
