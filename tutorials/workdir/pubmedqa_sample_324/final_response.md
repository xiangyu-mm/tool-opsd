**Yes, frequent bleach use at home was associated with increased odds of non-allergic asthma in the directly relevant study.** In an EGEA follow-up of 607 women, self-reported bleach use 4–7 days per week, compared with non-use, was associated with non-allergic asthma (adjusted OR 3.3, 95% CI 1.5–7.1) and non-allergic adult-onset asthma (4.9, 2.0–11.6). No association was observed for allergic asthma. The estimate for current asthma overall was smaller and borderline (1.7, 1.0–3.0). [PMID:27492540; search evidence: `call_kIrvPEGH4bIdKzbPJ9AS6qUQ`, `call_5cQf3rRoh5Wob66J3apBRGV5`]

A larger study of 12,758 women found higher odds of non-allergic asthma with frequent use of household irritants, but assessed bleach together with ammonia, solvents, and acids; it therefore supports the broader irritant-exposure finding, not a bleach-specific effect. [PMID:32829252; search evidence: `call_7X9DCRSAoR2VB4AX1NuPxOXp`, `call_GvsL29LDypo9vMg7wNue5wZR`]

These are observational associations and do not establish that bleach caused asthma. The bleach-specific result relies principally on one study, and its available abstract does not provide the full list of adjustment variables.
