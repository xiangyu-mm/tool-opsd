## Conclusion

No. The available evidence does **not** support self-reported physical activity before a quit attempt as a protective factor against relapse **for everyone**.

Key evidence:

- In a randomized trial of 392 smokers receiving cessation counseling and nicotine patches, adding an individualized physical-activity program did **not** improve prolonged abstinence at 7 weeks, 6 months, or 12 months compared with wellness counseling (PMID: 37273658).
- A greater increase in strength-training minutes during treatment predicted later abstinence, but this was an association with **change in activity**, not proof that baseline self-reported activity universally prevents relapse.
- Results may differ by activity type, amount, population, motivation, mental health, social support, and cessation treatment; therefore, any apparent benefit is not universal.

Thus, physical activity may be helpful for some smokers or as part of a broader cessation plan, but it cannot be considered a protective factor against relapse for all.

FINAL DECISION: NO
