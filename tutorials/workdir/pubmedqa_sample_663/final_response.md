### Conclusion

Yes—there is direct primary evidence that tumor-cell α9β1 integrin signaling promotes breast-cancer growth and lymphatic metastasis through recruitment or activation of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs).

- In **PMID:25099519**, blocking tumor-cell α9β1 in orthotopic MDA-MB-231 xenografts reduced tumor growth, lymphatic metastasis, CAF recruitment, and host-derived osteopontin (OPN).
- CAF abundance correlated with tumor size.
- Co-inoculation with wild-type fibroblasts enhanced tumor growth, whereas **OPN-deficient fibroblasts did not**, supporting a functional CAF-derived OPN pathway.
- A separate study, **PMID:22545097**, showed that α9β1 cooperates with tumor-cell VEGF-D to promote lymphangiogenesis and lymphatic metastasis, but that study did not establish CAF involvement.

Thus, the **α9β1 → CAF recruitment/activation → CAF-derived OPN → tumor growth and lymphatic metastasis** pathway is supported in an orthotopic xenograft model. However, the broader claim that α9β1 universally acts through a specific ligand such as tenascin-C or through CAF-derived VEGF-C/D has not been demonstrated.
