Yes. Preoperative white matter lesions were independently associated with worse long-term survival after internal carotid endarterectomy.

- **2014 study (PMID: 25076957):** Among 353 CEA patients followed for a median of 67 months, WML severity remained independently associated with long-term mortality after adjustment for age, diabetes, peripheral arterial disease, ICA stenosis severity, and surgical indication: **HR 3.83, 95% CI 1.17–12.5, p<0.05**.
- **2020 study (PMID: 32331995):** In 1,038 symptomatic CEA patients, imaging-reported cerebral small-vessel disease—including WMLs—independently predicted 3-year cardiovascular death: **adjusted HR 2.52, 95% CI 1.12–5.67, p=0.026**.

These are observational cohort findings, so they demonstrate independent prognostic association after statistical adjustment, not causation. The evidence supports WMLs as a long-term mortality-risk marker after CEA.

FINAL DECISION: YES
