The evidence supports that anxiety is common and generally more prevalent among people with Tourette syndrome than among people without tic disorders.

- A 2023 meta-analysis of 12 studies involving 3,812 people with Tourette syndrome estimated pooled anxiety prevalence at **53.5%** (95% CI 39.9–66.6%), though estimates varied by age, setting, and diagnostic method (source: `call_VkzPy1BWmO4Q0fq4I6AYBala`).
- A large structured-interview study found increased anxiety risk in Tourette syndrome compared with unaffected family members (**OR 1.4**, 95% CI 1.0–1.9), even after accounting for OCD and ADHD (source: `call_lzu3O0jHgLfoQ7X6x3ZbADXS`).
- OCD and ADHD are frequent co-occurring conditions and can inflate or complicate anxiety estimates; anxiety should not be considered interchangeable with either condition.
- Evidence for persistent/chronic motor or vocal tic disorder without Tourette syndrome is more limited, and the available studies do not establish a definitive comparison with tic-free controls (source: `call_QHLkXKqFAQpdOcmSD5IP77tR`).
- Specific anxiety subtypes, such as generalized anxiety disorder or social anxiety disorder, were not consistently quantified.

Therefore, the best-supported conclusion is that clinically significant anxiety is prevalent and appears increased in Tourette syndrome, while evidence for other chronic tic disorders is suggestive but less conclusive.
