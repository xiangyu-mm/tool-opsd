Yes. The evidence supports that extracellular histones induce phosphatidylserine (PS) exposure in human red blood cells and can produce a procoagulant phenotype.

- In **PMID:25069624**, isolated human RBCs exposed to a natural histone mixture showed dose-dependent annexin-V binding, indicating PS externalization. At approximately **20 μg/mL**, about **14%** of cells were annexin-V positive.
- Histone-treated RBCs had increased **prothrombinase activity** and accelerated fibrin formation in plasma-recalcification assays, directly demonstrating a procoagulant phenotype.
- **Histone H4** was the main active subtype tested; other individual histones were largely ineffective under the same conditions.
- A second study, **PMID:31302949**, independently found dose-dependent annexin-V-positive PS exposure and eryptosis in isolated human RBCs. The response occurred without detectable hemolysis or membrane leakage, arguing against simple RBC lysis as the explanation.
- The evidence is strongest for direct, non-lytic, H4- or histone-mixture-induced PS scrambling. However, reversibility after histone removal was not established, and the second study did not directly measure coagulation.
