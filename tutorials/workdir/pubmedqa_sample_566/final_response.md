The evidence supports **yes, prostasin may contribute to early-onset severe preeclampsia by suppressing trophoblast invasion**, but it does not establish that this mechanism causes the disease in people.

A case-control study found higher placental prostasin in 19 early-onset severe preeclampsia cases (≤34 weeks) than in late-onset severe preeclampsia and control groups. In transwell assays, recombinant prostasin inhibited trophoblast invasion and migration; blocking prostasin with an antibody increased MMP2 and MMP9 secretion by HTR-8/SVneo cells. These findings support the proposed mechanism, while the patient data remain observational and the cell assays do not prove an in-vivo causal effect (Yang et al., *J Perinatol.*, 2015; PMID: 25078863; doi:10.1038/jp.2014.136).
