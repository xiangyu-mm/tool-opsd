Stomal maturation does **not appear to increase** the rate of persistent tracheocutaneous fistulas.

Evidence from three pediatric retrospective studies found no statistically significant increase in fistula formation with maturation:

- In the strongest adjusted study of 179 children, maturation was not independently associated with TCF after accounting for age and tracheostomy duration (multivariable **p = 0.089**).
- Another study found TCF in **53%** of patients with maturation versus **45%** without maturation (**P = 0.44**).
- A third found **10.2%** versus **12.8%**, respectively.

In contrast, **longer tracheostomy duration** was a more consistent risk factor; dependence for more than 24 months was associated with increased TCF risk (**RR 2.52, P < 0.005**). Younger age at tracheostomy and tracheobronchomalacia were also reported risk factors.

The evidence is primarily pediatric, and direct adult comparative evidence is limited. Thus, maturation may be correlated with high-risk patients or longer cannulation, but it has not been shown to independently cause more fistulas.

FINAL DECISION: NO
