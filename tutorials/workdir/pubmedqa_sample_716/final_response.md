## Research assessment

A pilot study is scientifically justified, but it should be designed as a **prospective feasibility and safety study**, not as a definitive efficacy trial.

### Evidence

- The only direct human study identified was a retrospective series of **10 eyes with severe glaucoma** undergoing drainage implant surgery plus injectable crosslinked/reticulated hyaluronic acid gel (PMID:25096285; source: `call_8z6MwLEMwXqJoFauoMrijfV2`).
- Mean IOP decreased from **38.5 ± 10.7 to 13.0 ± 5.0 mmHg** over two years, with reported success in 80% using a ≥40% reduction criterion.
- However, the study was **small, uncontrolled, and retrospective**, so the improvement cannot be separated from the effect of drainage implant surgery itself.
- Related HealaFlow studies in deep sclerectomy and trabeculectomy provide only indirect support. They suggest feasibility but do not establish benefit in Ahmed, Baerveldt, Molteno, or other tube implants (`call_xIpT2GAnfsYoAGQSb1x4XyNP`; `call_SyxDsrEHWqZXJY07hllLppgs`; `call_v4mq04RJf32f5c6mSbZM0lvF`).
- No additional direct human comparative study was identified. Effects on medication burden, capsule fibrosis, hypotony, infection, tube complications, corneal health, and reoperation remain uncertain.

### Recommended study

A suitable pilot should:

- Prospectively enroll carefully selected severe or refractory glaucoma patients.
- Specify the exact hyaluronic-acid product, concentration, dose, placement, and timing.
- Include a contemporaneous tube-only comparator if feasible.
- Follow patients for at least 24 months.
- Measure IOP, medication burden, hypotony, inflammation, infection, tube/plate exposure or obstruction, encapsulation, corneal endothelial status, visual outcomes, and reoperation.
- Include regulatory and institutional review because peri-implant use may be outside the product’s approved indication.

Thus, the evidence supports investigating the concept under close monitoring, but not routine clinical adoption or a large confirmatory efficacy trial.
