Yes. The relevant prospective ALSPAC birth-cohort study was:

- **Title:** *Maternal and childhood psychological factors predict chronic disabling fatigue at age 13 years*
- **PMID:** 25448612
- **Sample:** 5,657 children; 110 (1.9%) met criteria for chronic disabling fatigue.
- **Outcome:** Fatigue lasting at least 3 months, sufficiently disabling to prevent school attendance or normal hobbies/sport/leisure, with no other identified cause.

### Findings

Maternal psychological symptoms predicted increased risk:

- **Maternal anxiety:** adjusted OR **1.19** per episode (95% CI 1.09–1.31)
- **Maternal depression:** adjusted OR **1.24** per episode (95% CI 1.11–1.39)

Child psychological factors were also associated initially:

- **Child psychological problems:** adjusted OR **1.19** per problem (95% CI 1.00–1.41)
- **Upsetting life events:** adjusted OR **1.22** per event (95% CI 0.99–1.58)

However, after adjustment for maternal anxiety and depression, the child-specific associations weakened and were no longer clearly statistically significant:

- Psychological problems: OR **1.12** (95% CI 0.93–1.33)
- Upsetting events: OR **1.19** (95% CI 0.94–1.52)

Thus, maternal anxiety and depression were fairly consistent prospective predictors, whereas the apparent effects of childhood psychological problems and upsetting events were partly explained by maternal psychological factors. Because this was an observational cohort study, the findings demonstrate prediction/association rather than causation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
