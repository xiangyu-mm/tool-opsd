A simplified risk-reduction method is:

1. **Inspect the hex driver** and confirm the tip is fully engaged and undamaged.
2. **Attach a secure dental floss ligature or manufacturer-approved tether** to the driver handle before placing it intraorally. Keep the free end controlled by the operator or assistant.
3. **Maintain direct visual and hand control**—do not leave the driver resting in the mouth or pass it over the open throat.
4. If safely feasible, place a **large, retrievable gauze throat screen** as a secondary backstop. Do not pack gauze deeply, and do not use a small or fraying piece.
5. Use a **rubber/dental dam** instead of or in addition to gauze when the implant location and access permit, particularly for posterior sites or multiple small components.
6. Follow the implant manufacturer’s instructions: a tether must not interfere with torque delivery, sterility, driver engagement, or create an entanglement hazard.

The best simple approach is therefore **direct control plus a secure tether**, with gauze or a dental dam as an additional barrier when appropriate. The literature supports these as practical precautions, but there is no validated protocol specifically for hex drivers during stage-two implant uncovering. If the instrument disappears or may have been inhaled, stop treatment and arrange urgent medical assessment; aspiration is more serious than simple swallowing.
