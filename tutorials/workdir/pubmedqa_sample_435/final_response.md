## Conclusion

Yes—**in the reported in-vitro HepG2 hepatocellular carcinoma model**, combining the JAK2 inhibitor **AG490 (tyrphostin AG490)** with dendritic-cell-activated **AFP-specific CD8⁺ T cells** enhanced antitumor activity compared with AFP-specific T cells alone.

Reported effects included:

- Reduced HepG2 cell viability  
- Increased tumor-cell apoptosis  
- G1 cell-cycle arrest  
- Increased IL-2, IL-6, IFN-γ, TNF-α, perforin, and granzyme B  
- Increased BAX expression  

The authors interpreted the mechanism as **Fas/FasL-signal independent** because Fas and FasL expression reportedly did not increase, while BAX and perforin/granzyme B-associated cytotoxicity increased.

However, the evidence has an important limitation: the study did **not clearly demonstrate Fas/FasL independence using anti-Fas/anti-FasL blocking or neutralization-rescue experiments**. Thus, the conclusion is supported by expression and pathway-associated findings, but not definitively proven. The evidence also primarily demonstrates **in-vitro enhancement**, not a definitive combination-treatment survival or tumor-growth comparison in vivo.

**Key source:** Wang et al., *OncoTargets and Therapy* (2016), PMID: **27499636**, DOI: **10.2147/OTT.S108385**.

FINAL DECISION: YES
