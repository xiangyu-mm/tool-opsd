**Yes.** The evidence supports κ-opioid receptor mediation of nitrous oxide’s antinociceptive effect in mice:

- In a knockout study, nitrous oxide’s antinociceptive effect was **almost completely abolished** in κ-opioid receptor knockout mice, while its hypnotic effect was preserved (PMID: [25086587](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25086587/)).
- In a pharmacological study, the κ-selective antagonist nor-binaltorphimine significantly suppressed nitrous oxide antinociception; a δ-selective antagonist did not (PMID: [20157832](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20157832/)).
- Earlier work also found that κ-receptor antagonist administration into either the brain or spinal space reduced nitrous oxide analgesia (PMID: [2157601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2157601/)).

Together, the genetic and antagonist findings support a role for κ-opioid receptors in the **antinociceptive effect**, not necessarily all effects of nitrous oxide.

FINAL DECISION: YES
