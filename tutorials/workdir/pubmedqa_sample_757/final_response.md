Yes. In rats, acute carotid occlusion–induced ischemia altered activation–flow coupling during forepaw stimulation:

- In PMID **23032485**, graded ischemia was produced using unilateral or bilateral carotid occlusion. Somatosensory evoked potentials and metabolic responses remained relatively coupled, but the **cerebral blood-flow response was attenuated more strongly than the electrical activation response** as ischemia worsened—evidence of activation–flow uncoupling.
- In PMID **15893740**, carotid occlusion combined with cortical compression produced progressive flow reduction. The forepaw-evoked flow response declined even while an activation–flow response remained detectable.

Thus, acute carotid occlusion does not necessarily abolish the response, particularly with mild ischemia, but it can reduce the flow component disproportionately to neural activation and thereby alter the coupling relationship.

FINAL DECISION: YES
