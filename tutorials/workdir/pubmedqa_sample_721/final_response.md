## Synthesis

The available evidence indicates that **serum testosterone generally decreases with age in women with PCOS**, including during the transition from reproductive age into the 40s and beyond.

Key evidence:

- A **13-year prospective study** found that total testosterone declined more in women with PCOS than in matched controls. However, PCOS participants still had higher total testosterone, free-androgen index, and DHEAS at follow-up.
- Several age-stratified studies found higher testosterone-related androgen measures in younger PCOS women, with age-related reductions in total testosterone, DHEAS, and/or free-androgen index.
- After menopause, women with PCOS continued to have higher testosterone, DHEAS, and free-androgen index—and lower SHBG—than matched women without PCOS. Thus, levels may decline without completely resolving PCOS-associated hyperandrogenism.
- Evidence for **directly measured free testosterone** is limited; many studies use free-androgen index instead. Most studies are observational, and assay methods and menopause status are inconsistently reported.

Therefore, the best interpretation is that testosterone **does decrease with age in PCOS**, but the decline is variable and does not necessarily normalize androgen levels relative to women without PCOS.
