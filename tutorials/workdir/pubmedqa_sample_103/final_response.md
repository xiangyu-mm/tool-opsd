Yes. International observational studies of nutritional support in mechanically ventilated burn patients have been conducted.

### Key evidence

- **Heyland et al., Burns, 2015 — PMID: 25445003**  
  The exact matching study analyzed **90 mechanically ventilated burn patients** from the International Nutrition Survey (2007–2011).  
  - 88/90 received enteral nutrition.
  - Enteral feeding began at a median of 17 hours.
  - 57% experienced feeding interruptions.
  - Mean deficits were 943 kcal/day and 49 g protein/day.
  - Larger energy and protein deficits were associated with higher 60-day mortality after severity adjustment.

- **Clinical Nutrition, 2020 — PMID: 32336526**  
  A prospective international multicenter observational study of **283 mechanically ventilated patients** across **14 burn ICUs**.  
  - Enteral nutrition was used on 90.5% of patient-days.
  - Mean energy and protein adequacy were approximately 65%.
  - Only 35.3% achieved at least 80% of energy requirements and 34.3% achieved at least 80% of protein requirements.

- **J Trauma Acute Care Surg, 2024 — PMID: 39225723**  
  A multinational observational study across **54 burn centers in 18 countries**, including **493 mechanically ventilated adults**.  
  - Lower calorie delivery was associated with increased six-month mortality.
  - Receiving 25% rather than 100% of prescribed calories was associated with adjusted HR 3.89 for mortality.
  - Protein adequacy was not significantly associated with mortality or functional outcomes.

Thus, the answer is clearly affirmative. These studies are observational and demonstrate substantial gaps between prescribed and delivered nutrition, with associations between inadequate energy delivery and worse outcomes. They do not, however, establish causality.

FINAL DECISION: YES
