Preoperative prostate mp-MRI provides **limited reliable staging information as a stand-alone test in routine clinical practice**.

Evidence supports this conclusion:

- A meta-analysis of 75 studies found only moderate sensitivity for extracapsular extension (57%), seminal-vesicle invasion (58%), and overall T3 disease (61%), despite high specificity.
- A real-world multicenter study reported particularly low sensitivity across staging endpoints: approximately 3–49% at one institution and 17–24% in a regional cohort.
- For pelvic lymph-node metastases, pooled sensitivity was only 40%, so a negative MRI cannot reliably exclude nodal disease.
- Recent multicenter data found MRI sensitivity for seminal-vesicle invasion as low as 16%.
- Clinical benefit is uncertain: randomized and propensity-adjusted studies did not show consistent reductions in positive margins, recurrence, or functional complications.

However, “little information” should not mean “no value.” mp-MRI can identify gross extracapsular or seminal-vesicle invasion, provide anatomical information for treatment planning, and has relatively high specificity. Its findings should therefore be combined with PSA, biopsy grade, clinical stage, nomograms, pathology, and—when indicated—PSMA-PET.

Thus, the claim is substantially correct when referring to **stand-alone routine staging**, particularly for ruling out microscopic local or nodal disease.
