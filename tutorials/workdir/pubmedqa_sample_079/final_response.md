**Yes.** A primary study in rat hearts and cultured rat cardiomyocytes directly tested catalytically inactive α-enolase. ENO1 silencing attenuated doxorubicin-induced apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction, whereas overexpressing either wild-type or catalytically inactive ENO1 increased apoptosis and reduced ATP. The authors also implicated AMPK dephosphorylation (PMID: 25446184).

A second study reported that mitochondrial α-enolase interacts with VDAC1 and can stabilize mitochondrial membranes; doxorubicin displaced it from mitochondria, alongside activation of intrinsic cell-death signaling (PMID: 24361255). Together, these findings support a catalytically independent role, although the mitochondrial-protective and pro-apoptotic observations suggest its effects may depend on localization or context.
