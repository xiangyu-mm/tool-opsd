## Evidence summary

Yes—topical bFGF (FGF2) improved secondary lymphedema in the rat-tail model reported by Onishi et al. (PMID:25426379).

Compared with saline, topical bFGF was associated with:

- Reduced tail volume.
- Earlier decline in indocyanine-green fluorescence, consistent with improved lymphatic drainage.
- Faster reduction of subcutaneous and deep tissue swelling.
- Increased VEGF-C mRNA and protein.
- Increased histologic lymphatic-vessel density at postoperative day 14.

These findings provide convergent evidence that the improvement was associated with lymphangiogenesis, rather than reflecting only nonspecific reduction of swelling. However, the study did not block FGF2/VEGF-C signaling or experimentally prevent lymphangiogenesis. Therefore, lymphangiogenesis was supported as the mechanism but not proven to be necessary or causally sufficient.

FINAL DECISION: YES
