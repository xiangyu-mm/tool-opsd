## Conclusion

Yes—**in a qualified sense**.

The 2015 paper *Comprehensive analysis suggests simple processes underlying EEG/ERP – demonstration with the go/no-go paradigm in ADHD* (PMID: 25445244) reports that an assumption-light, comprehensive analysis separated the averaged EEG/ERP signal into **two spatiotemporally distinct processes**:

- one plausibly related to **attention**
- one more plausibly related to **perception**

This supports the idea that apparently complex EEG/ERP waveforms in the ADHD go/no-go paradigm may reflect a **small, parsimonious set of underlying neurophysiological processes**, rather than each waveform feature representing an independent mechanism. A related methodological study reached a similar conclusion and reported that such process-based EEG markers could differentiate ADHD from typical development (PMID: 23805094).

However, the evidence does **not** establish that ADHD EEG/ERP abnormalities are universally simple, reducible to a single process, or fully explained by standard components such as N2 or P3. The accessible abstract does not provide detailed component statistics, numerical group differences, or verified source-localization/time-frequency results. Thus, the strongest conclusion is that comprehensive analysis **suggests parsimony**, not that it proves a single simple mechanism.

FINAL DECISION: YES
