**Yes—CKD-EPI appears useful for early risk prediction in this population.** A 2017 study of 348 cancer patients aged 65 or older undergoing contrast-enhanced CT found that 50 (14.4%) developed contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN). Among patients whose serum creatinine was below 1.5 mg/dL, CKD-EPI identified pre-existing renal insufficiency in 47 patients (13.5%). That subgroup had higher odds of a creatinine rise greater than 0.5 mg/dL after contrast (OR 15.02, 95% CI 5.24–43.07). The reported association was stronger than with MDRD (OR 13.73) or Cockcroft–Gault (OR 5.03), and CKD-EPI was reported as more useful than serum creatinine alone. This larger creatinine rise was also associated with longer hospitalization and higher in-hospital mortality. [PMID: 27491499](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27491499/)

**Caveat:** This conclusion rests on a single study; the abstract does not establish external validation or provide standard predictive-performance measures such as sensitivity, calibration, or discrimination. CKD-EPI should therefore be viewed as a useful pre-scan risk-assessment aid—not a guarantee that CIN will occur or proof that contrast caused subsequent kidney injury.

FINAL DECISION: YES
