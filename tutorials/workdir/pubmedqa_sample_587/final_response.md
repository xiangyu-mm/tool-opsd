Yes. The primary study PMID:25092916, titled *“Phosphoproteomic profiling reveals IL6-mediated paracrine signaling within the Ewing sarcoma family of tumors,”* directly supports this conclusion.

Key evidence:

- EWS/FLI1 knockdown in Ewing sarcoma family tumor cells was followed by mass-spectrometry phosphoproteomic and phosphotyrosine profiling.
- STAT3 phosphorylation was increased predominantly in neighboring cells that retained EWS/FLI1, indicating a non-cell-autonomous effect.
- Conditioned-medium experiments showed that knockdown cells released soluble factor(s) capable of activating STAT3 in other tumor cells.
- Cytokine analyses and ligand/receptor inhibition implicated IL-6 in this signaling.
- The signaling had a functional consequence, providing protection from apoptosis.

Thus, the study supports **IL-6-associated paracrine signaling between Ewing sarcoma tumor cells**, particularly from EWS/FLI1-deficient cells to neighboring Ewing sarcoma cells.

Important limitation: the evidence does not establish signaling between tumor cells and stromal, immune, endothelial, or other microenvironmental cells. It also provides stronger evidence for IL-6-associated STAT3 activation than for every component of a fully demonstrated IL-6R–JAK1/2–STAT3 cascade, because direct IL6R abundance, JAK1/JAK2 phosphorylation, and in vivo microenvironmental validation were not clearly established in the available evidence.

The related 2024 study PMID:38906855 concerns JAK1/STAT6/GAS6/TAM signaling but does not independently demonstrate IL-6-mediated paracrine signaling.

FINAL DECISION: YES
