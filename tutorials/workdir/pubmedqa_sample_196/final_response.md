**Yes—available evidence indicates that global or widespread cortical thinning generally normalizes after weight restoration.** In a longitudinal adolescent/young-adult study, global cortical thickness increased during approximately three months of restoration and was no longer different from controls at follow-up (PMID: 26876474; source: `call_ZGwNDpFAAWstOcX3SOLlZlSG`). In another study, widespread thinning observed during acute illness was absent at weight-recovered follow-up about a year later (PMID: 35025002; source: `call_FyKz1GQQvjkLU3wxR8RcPLge`).

Long-term recovered participants also had cortical thickness comparable to controls in a cross-sectional study (PMID: 25433902; source: `call_NKaWsgZdggMsMRtnxyc5ijVZ`), though that comparison cannot establish within-person recovery. Some studies report residual localized or non-cortical abnormalities, so normalization of global cortical thickness does not mean every brain measure necessarily normalizes.

FINAL DECISION: YES
