The best direct evidence suggests **no**.

A study of **721 children on Ishigaki Island** (PMID: **25086748**) examined eight Japanese FLG loss-of-function variants under the island’s warm, humid subtropical climate:

- FLG-LOF frequency in children with atopic dermatitis: **7.9%**
- Frequency in non-AD children: **6.1%**
- Difference: **not statistically significant** (*P* = 0.174)

The authors concluded that FLG loss-of-function mutations were **not always a predisposing factor** for atopic dermatitis in this subtropical population. This does not establish that FLG variants have no biological effect, but it indicates that their association with clinically apparent AD was not detectable in this Ishigaki cohort.

This finding is climate- and population-specific. Other Japanese studies found associations between variants such as **3321delA** and **S2554X** and AD, so FLG mutations can confer risk in some populations. The Ishigaki evidence therefore supports attenuation or non-detection of that risk under the island’s subtropical conditions rather than universal absence of FLG-related susceptibility.

FINAL DECISION: NO
