Yes. In a randomized study of patients with chronic heart failure undergoing 6 months of exercise training, celiprolol 200 mg/day enhanced several metabolic benefits compared with placebo:

- **Insulin sensitivity:** increased **33% with celiprolol** versus **17% with placebo**; the increase was statistically significant only in the celiprolol group.
- **Serum lipids:** the rise in fasting **HDL cholesterol was significantly greater** with celiprolol. Exercise increased HDL by approximately **12% overall**, with improved HDL/total-cholesterol and HDL/LDL ratios.
- **Limitation:** **total cholesterol did not change**, so the augmentation applied mainly to insulin sensitivity and favorable HDL-related measures, not all lipid parameters.

The evidence comes from the clinical study **“Celiprolol augments the effect of physical exercise on insulin sensitivity and serum lipid levels in chronic heart failure” (PMID: 10742707)**. The sample was small, and some participants were already receiving another beta-blocker, so the result should be interpreted cautiously.

FINAL DECISION: YES
