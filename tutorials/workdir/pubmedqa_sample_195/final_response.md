Yes—human liver evidence indicates that cholesterol negative-feedback regulation is impaired in Alagille syndrome (ALGS).

The key study examined 23 patients, including liver tissue from 5 patients. ALGS livers showed:

- Increased hepatic cholesterol.
- Persistent maturation of SREBP2, despite cholesterol accumulation; normally, cholesterol suppresses SREBP2.
- Increased expression of cholesterol uptake/synthesis regulators, including LDLR and HMGCR.
- Reduced CYP7A1 expression, impairing the classical conversion of cholesterol to bile acids.
- Increased hepatic bile acids and altered transporter/regulatory responses.

Together, these findings support defective sterol-mediated feedback and abnormal cholesterol handling in ALGS. The abnormalities may be partly driven by cholestasis and impaired bile-acid export.

A narrower conclusion about the canonical FXR–FGF19–CYP7A1 pathway is less certain. Human ALGS studies did not directly establish defective FGF19 production, FGFR4/β-Klotho signaling, or FXR responsiveness; increased SHP expression suggests that some bile-acid feedback signaling remains active. Thus, the overall cholesterol feedback loop appears impaired, but the precise molecular defect is not fully defined.
