**Yes—with qualifications.** Human studies support an **adjusted association** between plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) and NAFLD, while leptin and adiponectin levels and their associations with NAFLD vary by sex and age.

- In a Taiwanese case-control study of 210 people with MRI/MRS-defined NAFLD and 420 matched controls, PAI-1 was the only measured factor that remained independently associated with NAFLD in multivariable analysis (OR 1.39, *P*=0.039). The abstract does not provide the full adjustment set or confidence interval. [Search evidence: `call_bHUR04CzSHSkljLkfolQCBuL`; PMID 25091195]
- In the prospective CARDIA analysis, higher PAI-1 was associated with later CT-defined NAFLD: adjusted ORs were 2.16 and 2.71 per standard-deviation increase in log PAI-1 at two assessments. The displayed abstract does not specify all covariates. [Search evidence: `call_upfd1tcZLgE6MMnGPvEUwxuc`; PMID 32090434]
- In the Taiwanese study, women had higher leptin and adiponectin than men overall. NAFLD-associated leptin/adiponectin patterns differed by sex and, among women, were apparent mainly from age 45 onward. [Search evidence: `call_bHUR04CzSHSkljLkfolQCBuL`]

“Independent” here means statistically associated after adjustment for measured covariates; it does **not** establish that PAI-1 causes NAFLD. The adipokine patterns also should not be treated as universal across ages or populations.

FINAL DECISION: YES
