## Conclusion

Yes—**timing is decisive** for hypertonic saline (HTS) attenuation of polymorphonuclear neutrophil (PMN) cytotoxicity.

### Key evidence

- **Before PMN priming or activation:** In isolated human PMNs, 180 mM Na⁺ HTS given before PAF priming or before fMLP activation reduced β₂-integrin expression, superoxide production, and elastase release. The greatest suppression occurred when HTS preceded priming.  
  *PMID: 10744274; source: `call_F6y0YzHRNNSJW518eAMJA0Gb`*

- **After PMN activation:** HTS given after priming and activation did not suppress cytotoxicity; it **increased superoxide and elastase release**. This is the strongest direct evidence that treatment timing can reverse the effect of HTS.  
  *PMID: 10744274; source: `call_F6y0YzHRNNSJW518eAMJA0Gb`*

- **Hemorrhagic-shock model:** HTS could not suppress neutrophils already stimulated with fMLP. In hemorrhaged mice, delayed HTS after initial lactated Ringer’s resuscitation produced more lung injury than early HTS administration.  
  *PMID: 10909888; source: `call_7PFgSWycTWoXOiuBw3LpPh8l`*

- **Mechanistic support:** HTS administered before mesenteric-lymph-mediated PMN priming reduced superoxide production, endothelial adherence, and PMN-mediated endothelial cytotoxicity. Post-activation treatment was not directly tested in this study.  
  *PMID: 12095130; source: `call_QSaO6YhSS4dXw02zovoM3dnD`*

- **Transient effect:** HTS-induced suppression is reversible when cells return to normotonicity and can be restored by repeated HTS exposure, suggesting a limited therapeutic window rather than permanent PMN inhibition.  
  *PMID: 11331449; source: `call_gNodDCNGN6IETBd00WfGY3eV`*

### Interpretation

The evidence supports a narrow timing window:

1. **Pre-priming:** strongest attenuation of PMN cytotoxicity.  
2. **After priming but before activation:** still protective.  
3. **After activation:** ineffective and potentially cytotoxicity-enhancing.  
4. **Delayed administration in vivo:** may fail to prevent, or possibly worsen, inflammatory lung injury.

Thus, HTS is not simply immunosuppressive regardless of when it is administered; its effect depends substantially on whether PMNs have already been primed or activated.

FINAL DECISION: YES
