Yes. In a cross-sectional study of 830 guardians of children presenting with suspected malaria in Oromia, Ethiopia, children whose guardians believed blood might be covertly tested for HIV had a longer median delay before presentation: 3 days versus 2 days (p<0.001). Concern was also associated with higher odds of presentation after at least 3 days (OR 1.73; the abstract’s upper confidence limit appears as “270,” likely a formatting error for 2.70).

This supports an association, not proof that the concern caused the delay. The study is Haji et al., *Malaria Journal* (2014), PMID 25098338, DOI: 10.1186/1475-2875-13-301.
