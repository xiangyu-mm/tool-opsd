### Findings

Yes, available evidence supports DUSP6 as a predictor of **initial progestin sensitivity** in atypical endometrial hyperplasia (AEH), although the evidence is limited and not yet sufficient to establish it as a routine clinical biomarker.

- In a 2015 human study of AEH and endometrial carcinoma treated with progestins, pretreatment DUSP6 immunohistochemical expression was significantly higher in progestin-sensitive AEH than in resistant AEH. Progestin was effective in 89% of AEH cases, and DUSP6 increased after treatment in sensitive—but not resistant—cases (PMID: 25451692; source: `call_HErIEdYpbA6iYpc06td8XYsa`).
- A retrospective study of 36 women with AEH or early endometrial cancer treated with hysteroscopic resection plus a levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine device found that weak pretreatment DUSP6 was associated with treatment resistance or lack of regression: RR 16, P=0.0074. Reported performance was sensitivity 80%, specificity 90%, and AUC 0.85 (PMID: 34837386; source: `call_4PvuwkjTtvxu8ygo4S5yrCng`).
- DUSP6 was not associated with recurrence after an initial response in that study, so the evidence supports prediction of **initial response**, not relapse risk.
- Mechanistic evidence from endometrial cancer cells is compatible with a role in hormone-response signaling but does not independently validate DUSP6 as a clinical predictor (PMID: 23419500; sources: `call_iMwrE6FYoaP7PzJReY9QSDoi`, `call_GvEm7svT0QCnHyW9BGjDUTYe`).

Important limitations include small retrospective cohorts, immunohistochemical rather than standardized molecular testing, incomplete information about the progestin used in the 2015 study, and limited direct evidence in formally designated EIN cohorts.

FINAL DECISION: YES
