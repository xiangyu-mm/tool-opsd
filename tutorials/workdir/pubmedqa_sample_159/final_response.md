Yes. The strongest evidence is a direct in-vitro study in rat patellar-tendon-derived tenocytes (PMID: 25433272), which compared triamcinolone acetonide (TA), lidocaine, and their combination. Both agents alone reduced tenocyte viability and altered morphology, while **TA plus 1% lidocaine caused significantly lower viability and more pronounced morphological damage than either agent alone**. The authors described this as synergistic potentiation.

Supporting evidence shows that:

- **Lidocaine alone** can impair tendon-cell proliferation, collagen I/III production, and extracellular-matrix function (PMID: 35955918).
- **TA alone** can reduce viability and increase apoptosis in human rotator-cuff-derived cells, particularly at higher or repeated exposures (PMID: 25477418; PMID: 27965219).
- A canine tendon-explant study found toxicity from 1% lidocaine but not from TA under its specific conditions; however, it did not test the combination (PMID: 30035090).

Thus, the available evidence supports potentiation, but the conclusion is based primarily on one direct combination study. The TA concentration and exposure duration in that study were not available, and clinical relevance may depend on dose, exposure time, tendon source, and whether isolated cells or intact tendon tissue is considered.

FINAL DECISION: YES
