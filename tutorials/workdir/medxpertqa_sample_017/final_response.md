Naloxone is a **synthetic N-allyl derivative of oxymorphone** and acts as a competitive opioid receptor antagonist, especially at the μ-opioid receptor.

Why the other choices are false:

- **B:** It is an antagonist, not a partial agonist.
- **C:** It has a short half-life and may require repeat dosing.
- **D:** It is metabolized primarily by the liver.
- **E:** It reverses opioid—not benzodiazepine—respiratory depression.
- **F:** It binds reversibly, not irreversibly.
- **G:** It crosses the blood-brain barrier and acts centrally.
- **H:** It is used in, not contraindicated because of, opioid use disorder.
- **I:** Its effects result from antagonism of opioid effects.
- **J:** Oral naloxone undergoes substantial first-pass metabolism; the statement is not the best true characterization compared with A.

**FINAL ANSWER: A**
