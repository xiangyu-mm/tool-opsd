Yes. Molecular characterization studies of domestic-cat parvoviruses in India have reported evidence consistent with newer genetic variants:

- **2017 study (PMID: 27502089):** Among 106 cats, VP2 sequencing identified two CPV-2a strains from Mumbai and Puducherry that the authors described as new variants, along with three FPV strains. Their distinct amino-acid substitutions and independent clustering suggested ongoing evolution.
- **2022 study (PMID: 35493752):** Full-length VP2 analysis in southern Indian domestic and shelter cats identified three previously unreported mutations, a “new CPV-2a/FPV” sequence, and a potential CPV-2a/FPV recombinant. This provides the strongest evidence for emerging molecular diversity.
- **2026 study (PMID: 42654988):** Found genetically diverse FPV circulating among Chennai cats, including several substitutions and one divergent sequence, although it did not establish a clearly novel named variant.

Thus, the answer is **yes**, particularly based on the 2017 and 2022 studies. However, the evidence mainly concerns novel VP2 sequence features and a potential recombinant; confirmation of a new antigenic lineage or biologically significant variant would require complete-genome, broader epidemiological, and phenotypic studies.

FINAL DECISION: YES
