**Yes—there is evidence of a tendency, but it is not uniform.** In a study of 21 typical and 10 atypical bronchopulmonary carcinoids, apoptosis was low overall (mean apoptotic index 0.18%), yet an index above 0.5% occurred significantly more often in atypical tumors (*P* < 0.008). Ki-67 proliferation rates were also low overall, but were highest in atypical tumors; the apoptotic and Ki-67 indices were positively correlated (*P* < 0.01). (PMID: 10738217)

The pattern is not consistent across every small cohort: another study of 16 typical and 4 atypical pulmonary carcinoids found greater proliferative capacity in atypical tumors but significantly more apoptosis in typical tumors. (PMID: 9762544)

**Conclusion:** Atypical carcinoids show a reported tendency toward increased proliferative activity and, in one directly relevant study, increased apoptotic activity; “tendency” is more accurate than treating this as a universal finding.
