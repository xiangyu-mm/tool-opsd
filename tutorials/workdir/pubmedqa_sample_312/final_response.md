Yes. The strongest direct evidence is a 2016 mock corneal-graft study (PMID:27519149) in which collagen cross-linking significantly increased the stress needed to separate paired corneal sections:

- Cross-linked grafts: **0.691 ± 0.12**
- Untreated controls: **0.236 ± 0.09**
- Paired **p = 0.0087**

A separate ex vivo keratoplasty study (PMID:23960934) also found increased graft attachment strength after UVA–riboflavin cross-linking, including higher burst pressure and separation strength. A newer collagen-gel study supports cohesive interface formation but lacks a definitive quantitative adhesion comparison.

Therefore, collagen cross-linking appears to increase adhesion in mock and ex vivo corneal graft models.

FINAL DECISION: YES
