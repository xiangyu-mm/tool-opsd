**Yes—with an important qualification.** The best-supported example is the SOD3 **R213G (rs1799895)** variant. It weakens extracellular-matrix and vascular binding: reported binding to aorta was about 10-fold lower ex vivo, and vascular tissue levels were about 2.5–3-fold lower than for wild-type EC-SOD. In knock-in mice, EC-SOD shifted from lung and vascular tissue toward plasma and bronchoalveolar fluid. The protein-distribution effect is therefore well supported mechanistically. (PMIDs 16087794, 20600835, 25085920; search evidence: `call_zPFlcXFSV74KCpELyh8dGE74`, `call_dYgknRkWoSsErvSeRGJ8xGsc`, `call_YOQlTTJqSZY8NsBzRItorhif`)

Whether that redistribution changes **human disease risk** is less certain and appears to depend on the affected compartment. Studies suggest possible protection from some allergic airway outcomes, but reduced tissue-bound EC-SOD may impair vascular protection; experimental work found greater susceptibility to hypoxic pulmonary hypertension. Human associations are mixed and not uniformly replicated, so the evidence does not support a simple, general increase in cardiopulmonary risk. (PMIDs 28878123, 25085920; search evidence: `call_D3KZtSXYHsoWVCvn998TCWhJ`, `call_46aDVxpy8POP9830SVEbSPbk`)

This conclusion applies to **R213G**, not automatically to **Ala40Thr/Ala38Thr**: direct evidence that Ala40Thr alters EC-SOD distribution or binding is insufficient. (Search evidence: `call_uenQnRgtYCUxHjFNLkA6tGGw`, `call_hJfmdPZpCiZ9NbX5FrDNSPhi`)

FINAL DECISION: YES
