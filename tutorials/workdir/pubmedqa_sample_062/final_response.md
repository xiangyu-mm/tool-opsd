Evidence indicates that sexual activity does **not** predispose patients with GERD to additional reflux episodes.

A prospective study monitored 21 GERD patients with 48-hour Bravo esophageal pH monitoring; 15 were included in the final analysis. Participants had intercourse on one night and abstained on another, after comparable meals. Reflux time and the number of acid reflux events during the 30- and 60-minute periods surrounding intercourse were not significantly different from matched periods without intercourse (Bor et al., 2014; PMID: **25452843**).

Limitations include the very small sample, six exclusions, short monitoring period, controlled timing after a refluxogenic meal, and evaluation of only the missionary position. Thus, some individuals may still experience symptoms during sexual activity, but current direct evidence does not show that intercourse increases objective reflux episodes in GERD overall.
