Yes. A study of liver tissue from 34 people with primary biliary cirrhosis found that after ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) treatment, immunohistochemical labeling for **HSP70** decreased significantly in bile ducts and hepatocytes (P<0.01). The study also reported a decrease in ubiquitin labeling, which is not itself a heat-shock protein.

This supports a reduction in HSP70 expression after treatment, though the abstract does not provide the size of the effect or establish that UDCA directly targets HSPs. **Sakisaka et al., “Ursodeoxycholic acid reduces expression of heat shock proteins in primary biliary cirrhosis,” PMID: 10726964 (2000).**

FINAL DECISION: YES
