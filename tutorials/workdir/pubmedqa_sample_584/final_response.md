**Yes.** A prospective single-center study of 154 people screened to exclude pathological constipation or incontinence found that age and gender influenced some anorectal manometry measures, but not all.

- **Gender:** Men had greater anal sphincter length than women (4.23 vs 3.85 cm; *p*<0.001) and higher maximum squeeze pressure (190.18 vs 116.49 mmHg; *p*<0.001). Maximum resting pressure did not differ significantly by gender (*p*=0.93).
- **Age:** Maximum resting pressure decreased with age in both men and women (*p*<0.001 for both). Maximum squeeze pressure decreased with age in women (*p*<0.001), but not significantly in men. Anal sphincter length was not associated with age (*p*=0.707).

Thus, age and gender are relevant when interpreting **some** manometric parameters; the effects are parameter-specific. This conclusion is based on one single-center study: *“Anorectal manometric parameters are influenced by gender and age in subjects with normal bowel function”* (2014; PMID: 25092560).
