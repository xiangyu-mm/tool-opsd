Yes. Primary in vivo studies support this conclusion:

- In an aganglionic rat jejunum model, transplanted GFP-labeled skin-derived precursor cells (SKPs) engrafted and formed GFP-positive structures resembling enteric ganglia between the longitudinal and circular muscle layers (Wagner et al., PMID: 25092099).
- A related study found SKP-derived clusters that co-expressed mature neuronal and glial markers and contained markers of neurotransmitter synthesis, consistent with enteric neuroglial differentiation and ganglion formation (PMID: 25487489).

The evidence demonstrates ganglion-like structures in vivo, although it was obtained in an experimental rat model and does not by itself establish fully functional, clinically effective enteric ganglia.

FINAL DECISION: YES
