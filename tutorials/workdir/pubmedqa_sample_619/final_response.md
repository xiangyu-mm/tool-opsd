Yes. Direct comparative planning studies—primarily in Hodgkin lymphoma—generally show that involved-nodal radiation therapy (INRT) delivers lower doses to critical organs at risk than involved-field radiation therapy (IFRT).

- In a 20-patient mediastinal Hodgkin lymphoma study, INRT reduced the planning target volume from **1,705 to 1,015 cm³** while maintaining similar target dose. Nearly all organ-at-risk dose-volume parameters improved by approximately **20–50%** with INRT (PMID: **22079733**).
- Reported mean doses were lower with INRT than IFRT for every listed organ:
  - **Heart:** 17.94 vs 9.19 Gy with 3D-RT; 13.76 vs 7.42 Gy with IMRT
  - **Spinal cord:** 23.93 vs 13.78 Gy; 19.16 vs 11.55 Gy
  - **Lungs:** 10.62 vs 8.57 Gy; 12.77 vs 9.64 Gy
  - **Breasts:** lower mean doses bilaterally with INRT under both techniques
- A separate pediatric Hodgkin lymphoma comparison found significantly lower mean doses with INRT for the **kidneys, heart, thyroid, parotids, and lungs**, along with lower breast V50% (PMID: **25082095**).
- Modern GHSG quality-assurance data support the feasibility of INRT and report improved thyroid doses, although complete paired dose-volume data were not provided in the abstract (PMID: **36896214**).

The evidence is mainly retrospective treatment replanning rather than randomized clinical comparison. The degree of sparing depends on accurate identification of the initially involved nodes, imaging and contouring quality, and the radiation technique. IMRT may reduce high-dose exposure but can increase low-dose “bath” to some organs, so not every dose metric necessarily improves.

Thus, INRT generally lowers critical-organ doses compared with IFRT while maintaining target coverage, particularly for mediastinal Hodgkin lymphoma.

FINAL DECISION: YES
