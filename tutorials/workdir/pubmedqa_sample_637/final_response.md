**Verdict: broadly yes, with an important qualification.** A comparative phylogeny and synteny study concluded that the Dact family arose from a **chordate-specific ancestor**. It proposed that Wnt regulation was part of the family’s ancestral functions, while a role in TGF-β signaling emerged later in the Dact2/4 lineage—not that every Dact paralog necessarily regulates both pathways in the same way (PMID: 25099342).

Developmental expression data in chicken limbs are consistent with Dact proteins acting where Wnt and TGF-β/BMP signaling are relevant, and the authors proposed that they may integrate those pathways (PMID: 23417991). Later work also describes Dact/Dapper/Frodo proteins across vertebrate groups, while showing that paralogs such as Dact-4 can be unusually divergent and have a restricted distribution (PMID: 33186756).

Thus, “chordate-specific regulators at the intersection” is a reasonable summary of the family’s reported origin and proposed pathway role, but it oversimplifies the **evolutionary and functional differences among paralogs**.

FINAL DECISION: YES
