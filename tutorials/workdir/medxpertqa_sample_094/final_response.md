The best diagnosis is **cardiac myxoma**, most likely a **left atrial myxoma** (**G**).

A left atrial myxoma can intermittently obstruct the mitral valve, producing:

- Dyspnea, orthopnea, pulmonary crackles, and a soft mid-diastolic apical murmur resembling mitral stenosis
- An early diastolic **tumor plop**, caused by the mobile mass moving toward or through the mitral valve
- Constitutional symptoms such as intermittent fever, fatigue, and malaise, often mediated by IL-6

Subacute endocarditis could explain fever and fatigue, but it would not typically cause a tumor plop or intermittent functional mitral obstruction. Rheumatic mitral stenosis would more characteristically cause a fixed opening snap and persistent diastolic rumble.

FINAL ANSWER: G
