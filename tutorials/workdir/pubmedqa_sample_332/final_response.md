Yes. The primary study (PMID: 27502396) reported that uPAR directly interacts with both uPA and IGF1R in TNBC cells, demonstrated by immunoprecipitation and proximity-ligation assays. Combined knockdown of uPAR with either uPA or IGF1R additively reduced TNBC-cell viability, migration, and invasion. In 174 TNBC tissue samples, these complexes also correlated with higher histological grade and invasion-related cathepsins.

Thus, the evidence supports a tumor-promoting role for uPAR complexes with uPA and IGF1R, although the tissue correlations are associative and the functional evidence is primarily from cell models.

FINAL DECISION: YES
