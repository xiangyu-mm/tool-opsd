Yes. In the Poznan Prospective Study (PMID: 25090481), 79 patients with newly diagnosed type 1 diabetes completed a 5-day intensive teaching program and a 20-question diabetes-knowledge test, followed for 11 years.

Subclinical macroangiopathy was assessed using right common carotid artery intima–media thickness (IMT). Higher baseline diabetic knowledge was associated with lower subsequent IMT:

- Correlation: **r = −0.27, p = 0.017**
- Multivariable regression: **β = −8, 95% CI −16 to −1, p = 0.037**
- Adjusted for sex, age, smoking, hypertension, diabetic kidney disease, mean follow-up LDL cholesterol, and HbA1c.

Therefore, the study reported that baseline diabetic knowledge after the 5-day teaching program was an **independent predictor** of later subclinical macroangiopathy. This indicates an independent statistical association, not proof that the teaching program itself caused the reduced vascular burden.

FINAL DECISION: YES
