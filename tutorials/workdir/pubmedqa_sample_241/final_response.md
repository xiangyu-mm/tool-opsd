**Yes, conditionally.** Primary-care surveillance can improve disease-incidence estimates, especially for timely monitoring of community trends and epidemic signals. In one evaluation, a sentinel network covering 2.6% of the population produced COVID-19/acute respiratory infection estimates that tracked population-wide registration reasonably well.

However, routine primary-care records do not automatically measure true population incidence. They can miss undiagnosed cases, reflect who seeks care, and vary in completeness by disease and case definition. For example, primary-care coeliac-disease records substantially undercounted estimated seroprevalence; prostate-cancer records were earlier than some other sources but incompletely captured cases.

**Conclusion:** These systems are most useful when practices are representative, denominators and case definitions are clear, algorithms are validated, and estimates are calibrated with laboratory or registry data. Used alone, they may improve timeliness without improving the completeness or accuracy of absolute incidence estimates.

FINAL DECISION: YES
