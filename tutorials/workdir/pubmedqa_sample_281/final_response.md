Yes. The available preclinical evidence supports regulation of arterial remodeling in angiotensin II–induced hypertension:

- **HDAC4:** In Ang II-infused mice, phosphorylated HDAC4 increased in renal arteries and aorta. Class II HDAC inhibition with **MC1568** reduced blood pressure, arterial remodeling, vascular smooth-muscle-cell proliferation, DNA synthesis, and hypertrophy.
- **GATA6:** Ang II increased arterial GATA6 protein. GATA6 was reported to interact with HDAC4, and GATA6 overexpression increased vascular smooth-muscle-cell size and number.
- **HDAC4–GATA6 pathway:** The study proposed a **CaMKIIα/PKD1/HDAC4/GATA6** mechanism underlying vascular hypertrophy and hyperplasia (PMID: **27512969**).
- **HDAC8:** In a separate Ang II hypertensive mouse study, selective HDAC8 inhibition with **PCI34051** reduced vascular hypertrophy and GATA6 mRNA, as well as blood pressure and vascular inflammation (PMID: **31223486**).

Thus, HDACs—particularly **HDAC4**, with additional evidence for **HDAC8**—and GATA6 appear to regulate arterial remodeling in this animal model. The evidence is mechanistic and preclinical; it does not by itself establish clinical benefit in humans.

FINAL DECISION: YES
