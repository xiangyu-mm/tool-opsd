The most likely cause is **insulinoma (J)**.

During hypoglycemia, insulin should be nearly undetectable. Instead, she has:

- Elevated/inappropriately detectable insulin
- Elevated C-peptide and proinsulin, indicating **endogenous insulin secretion**
- Suppressed β-hydroxybutyrate, reflecting insulin-mediated inhibition of ketogenesis
- Fasting-predominant episodes, which favor insulinoma over NIPHS

Exogenous insulin would suppress C-peptide and proinsulin. Hypopituitarism may cause hypoglycemia but would not produce this pattern of endogenous hyperinsulinism. Sulfonylurea exposure can mimic insulinoma biochemically and should be excluded with a drug screen, but among the choices, insulinoma is the best answer. Her galactorrhea and headaches warrant separate pituitary evaluation and may represent a coexisting process.

FINAL ANSWER: J
