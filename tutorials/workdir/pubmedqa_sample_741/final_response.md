Yes. The directly relevant study was Suzuki et al. (2000), **“Hyperthermia prevents metabolic and cerebral flow responses to hypoxia in the fetal sheep”** (PMID: **10732315**).

- In normothermic near-term fetal sheep, 30 minutes of hypoxia decreased brain temperature relative to core temperature. This was interpreted as evidence of reduced cerebral oxygen consumption (cerebral hypometabolism) together with increased cerebral blood flow.
- After 1 hour of intrauterine warming, fetal core and brain temperatures increased by approximately **0.66°C** and **0.61°C**, respectively.
- During subsequent hypoxia, the normal fall in brain temperature relative to core temperature no longer occurred, indicating that hyperthermia prevented these compensatory metabolic and cerebral-flow responses.
- The authors concluded that fetal hyperthermia leaves the brain less protected against hypoxic injury.

This evidence supports prevention of the characteristic hypoxia-induced cerebral hypometabolic and increased-flow response, although the study inferred these responses from brain-temperature measurements rather than directly measuring both cerebral metabolism and flow.

FINAL DECISION: YES
