**Yes—one small study found increased polysulfone dialyzer ultrafiltration capacity and improved measured dialysis performance during intradialytic glucose infusion.**

In a 2000 study of six non-diabetic chronic dialysis patients across 48 post-dilution haemodiafiltration sessions, continuous 50% glucose infusion (40 mL/h) was compared with no infusion. With glucose, the ultrafiltration coefficient (Kuf) increased from **22.8 ± 2.2 to 32.1 ± 3.9 mL/h/mmHg** (P<0.0001). Urea and creatinine reduction ratios also rose, and double-pool Kt/V increased from **1.65 ± 0.24 to 1.86 ± 0.24** (P<0.005). No detrimental effects were reported during the study sessions.

**Important qualification:** Kuf is an operational measure of ultrafiltration capacity; these results support increased dialyzer permeability/performance under the studied conditions, but do not establish a general membrane mechanism or justify routine glucose infusion. The evidence comes from a small study, so broader effectiveness and safety remain uncertain.
