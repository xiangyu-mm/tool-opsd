Yes. The available evidence supports troponin-I as a predictor of delayed or subsequently detected atrial fibrillation after acute ischemic stroke/TIA.

- **Most direct study (PMID:25440352):** In 185 stroke/TIA patients, delayed AF—defined as AF detected after an initially sinus-rhythm admission ECG—occurred in **30%** of patients with abnormal admission troponin-I versus **6.1%** with normal troponin-I. Abnormal troponin-I independently predicted delayed AF (**OR 5.8, P=.037**).
- **Supporting study (PMID:41226937):** Admission hs-troponin-I ≥ the 99th percentile independently predicted AF detected after ischemic stroke during follow-up.
- **Limitations:** The direct study had only 10 delayed-AF events, did not report a confidence interval or standardized troponin-I cutoff, and the broader literature is heterogeneous. A systematic review found limited troponin-specific evidence, inconsistent reporting, and substantial risk of bias.

Thus, troponin-I is a plausible risk-enrichment marker for intensified rhythm monitoring, but it is not yet a sufficiently validated standalone prediction tool.

FINAL DECISION: YES
