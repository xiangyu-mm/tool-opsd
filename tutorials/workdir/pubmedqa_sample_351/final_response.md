Yes. The most directly relevant evidence is a prospective observational cohort study (PMID: 27502204, *Sleep & Breathing*, 2017) involving 190 patients with coronary artery disease.

- OSA severity was measured using the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI).
- Serial LDL-C measurements were obtained every 3–6 months over approximately 53 months.
- Visit-to-visit LDL-C variability was quantified using variation independent of the mean (VIM).
- Higher AHI was correlated with greater LDL-C variability: **r = 0.183, p = 0.016**.
- After adjustment for diabetes and hyperlipidemia, each 10-unit increase in AHI was associated with a **3.8% increase in LDL-C variability** (95% CI, 0.1%–7.4%; p = 0.044).

Thus, greater OSA severity was independently associated with greater visit-to-visit LDL-C variability in patients with CAD. However, the evidence is limited to a single observational study and does not establish causality or show that OSA treatment reduces LDL-C variability.

FINAL DECISION: YES
