Yes—**with an important qualification**.

Actinic keratosis (AK) is generally regarded as the **most common clinically recognized premalignant lesion associated with invasive cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC)** on chronically sun-damaged skin. “AK I” refers to the **mildest histologic grade**, with atypical keratinocytes mainly limited to the basal and immediate suprabasal layers, approximately the lower third of the epidermis.

However:

- AK I is **not itself invasive SCC**.
- Most individual AK lesions do not progress to SCC.
- Invasive cSCC may also arise from SCC in situ/Bowen disease or without a clinically recognized precursor.
- The evidence supports AK as the most common precursor **as a category**, but does not necessarily prove that AK I specifically is more common than all other AK grades among lesions associated with invasive SCC.

This interpretation is supported by pathology and dermatology literature, including PMIDs 32772261, 34357052, 36232651, 38068343, 38396897, and 39133637.

FINAL DECISION: YES
