Yes. The directly relevant study (Arya et al., *Journal of Vascular Surgery*, 2014; PMID: 25441012) analyzed 306,438 vascular-surgery patients treated at safety-net versus nonsafety-net public hospitals.

- Safety-net hospitals had significantly longer hospital stays and higher hospital charges.
- Unadjusted mortality was higher at safety-net hospitals (OR 1.28; 95% CI, 1.13–1.46).
- After adjustment for patient and clinical factors, the mortality difference was no longer statistically significant.
- The study included surgery for peripheral arterial disease, carotid stenosis, and nonruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm.

Thus, safety-net hospital care was associated with higher resource use/cost but similar adjusted in-hospital mortality.

FINAL DECISION: YES
