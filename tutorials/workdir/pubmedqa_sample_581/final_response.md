The most directly relevant study found **no significant association** between distal colorectal polyp type (conventional adenoma, hyperplastic polyp, or serrated polyp) and synchronous proximal advanced serrated lesions. Those advanced lesions included sessile serrated lesions with dysplasia, lesions ≥10 mm, or traditional serrated adenomas; 52% of patients with proximal advanced serrated lesions had no distal polyps (source: `call_oL0j4cZkH7GAc7D3RGzxRCdE`).

There are qualifications: distal sessile serrated lesions were associated with *any* proximal sessile serrated lesion in a secondary analysis, and small distal hyperplastic polyps were associated with subsequent SSLs in one cohort (OR 2.23, 95% CI 1.18–4.00). But neither finding establishes an association with proximal **advanced** serrated lesions. Follow-up evidence also points to proximal or large SSLs—not distal SSLs—as markers of later advanced neoplasia (`call_Cmx9VmG0KHj0ktGvZe66dIWs`; `call_wtJZIqnEAo8h3SMQOEyXxO6f`).

Overall, distal findings are **not established as reliable indicators** of proximal advanced serrated lesions.
