Yes—based on a direct human study (PMID: 25093845).

- The study examined peripheral blood mononuclear cells from 15 active smokers.
- DNA double-strand breaks were assessed using γ-H2AX foci.
- After **one month of smoking cessation**, median γ-H2AX foci decreased from **4.44 to 0.28 foci/cell** (p<0.05), reaching a level comparable to nonsmokers (**0.37 foci/cell**).
- This supports reversal of the **measured population-level DSB signal** after cessation.

The study does not prove that every pre-existing break was individually repaired, and it was small and focused on young healthy smokers. Nevertheless, the available direct evidence supports the conclusion that smoking cessation can substantially reverse DSB burden in human mononuclear cells within about one month.

FINAL DECISION: YES
