Yes. Evidence from prospective cohorts of women indicates that **NT-proBNP**, a B-type natriuretic peptide, improves cardiovascular disease risk prediction beyond traditional risk factors.

- **Women’s Health Initiative (PMID: 25443700):** In 3,813 women followed for a median of 9.9 years, adding NT-proBNP increased the C-statistic from **0.765 to 0.774** (*P* = 0.0003). It also improved reclassification (**NRI 0.08**, *P* < 0.0001; **IDI 0.0105**, *P* < 0.0001).
- **Rotterdam Study (PMID: 20083731):** In sex-stratified analyses, adding NT-proBNP improved prediction in women, with a **13.3% net reclassification improvement** (95% CI 5.9–20.8%, *P* < 0.001).

The improvement in discrimination was statistically significant but modest; gains in risk reclassification were more substantial. The strongest direct evidence concerns **NT-proBNP**, rather than BNP itself, but it supports an incremental predictive value for B-type natriuretic peptide testing in women.

FINAL DECISION: YES
