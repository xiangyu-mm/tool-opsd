## Conclusion

Yes. The most directly relevant economic evaluation compared Lynch syndrome tumor testing for colorectal cancer diagnosed at **≤50 years** versus **≤70 years**:

- Raising the threshold to 70 detected approximately **four times as many Lynch syndrome cases**.
- The number needed to test per detected carrier increased from **19 to 42**.
- Testing cost per detected carrier increased from **€4,837 to €9,437**.
- When cascade testing of relatives was included, the incremental cost was only **€2,703 per additional life-year gained**, which was considered highly cost-effective under Dutch thresholds (Mvundura et al., PMID:25081898).
- A systematic review found additional economic evaluations supporting universal or age-targeted testing up to age 70.
- An Australian lifetime microsimulation similarly found universal tumor-testing strategies to be cost-effective, with ICERs generally within the modeled willingness-to-pay range.

The result depends substantially on:

- cascade testing and surveillance of relatives;
- adherence to colonoscopic surveillance;
- local testing and healthcare costs;
- the tumor-testing algorithm used;
- assumptions about Lynch syndrome cancer risk and prevention.

Thus, the evidence supports expanding **tumor-first testing** from age 50 to 70 for colorectal cancer patients, but the evidence is model-based and country-specific rather than definitive clinical-trial evidence.

FINAL DECISION: YES
