**Yes, in a preclinical mouse model, with a caveat about mechanism.** In a TNBS-induced colitis study, human umbilical cord-derived MSC treatment improved survival and clinical, macroscopic, and histologic outcomes. Treatment was also associated with increased CD5⁺ B cells and regulatory T cells and reduced Th1/Th17 cells and inflammatory cytokines. Transferred CD5⁺ B cells themselves improved colitis; the cells inhibited T-cell proliferation and produced IL-10. These results support a functional contribution by CD5⁺ B regulatory cells, but do not establish that they are the sole or necessary mediator of the MSC benefit. This is animal evidence, not evidence of efficacy in people.

Source: Chao et al., *Stem Cell Research & Therapy* (2016), PMID **27515534**, DOI **10.1186/s13287-016-0376-2**. A 2019 correction reported a duplication error within Figure 3 (PMID **30665468**).
