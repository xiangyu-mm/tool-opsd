Yes. In *Physcomitrium patens* (formerly *Physcomitrella patens*), plasma-membrane-targeted PIN proteins—especially PpPINA and PpPINB—have strong causal evidence for regulating moss shoot development.

- The primary study **PMID:25448003** showed that two PIN proteins are targeted to the plasma membrane in leafy gametophore shoots.
- Auxin treatments and auxin-transport inhibitors disrupted apical activity, leaf initiation, leaf shape, and shoot tropisms.
- PIN mutants phenocopied these developmental effects, linking PIN-mediated auxin transport to shoot development rather than merely correlating PIN expression with it.
- **PMID:37052186** provides additional mechanistic support: CNIH2 controls PINA trafficking and polar plasma-membrane localization, and disrupting this pathway alters auxin-dependent growth.
- PpPINC has a clearer role in reproduction and sporophyte retention, so the strongest shoot-development conclusion applies to PpPINA/PpPINB.

Thus, plasma-membrane PIN localization and activity are necessary components of auxin-dependent shoot development in this moss, although plasma-membrane targeting alone has not been shown to be sufficient.

FINAL DECISION: YES
