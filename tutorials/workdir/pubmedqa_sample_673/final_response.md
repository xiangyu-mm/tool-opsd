Yes. **Somatic ERCC2 mutations were associated with greater response to cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy** in muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma. In a small retrospective study, mutations were found in **9 of 25 responders and 0 of 25 nonresponders** (36% vs 0%; Fisher exact *P* = 0.009; Van Allen et al., 2014, PMID: 25096233).

This supports a correlation, but not yet a validated treatment-selection test: the cohort was small and selected across response groups, and the result does not by itself show that ERCC2-mutant tumors benefit more from cisplatin than from alternative treatments. The proposed mechanism—impaired nucleotide-excision repair of cisplatin-induced DNA damage—is biologically plausible.

FINAL DECISION: YES
