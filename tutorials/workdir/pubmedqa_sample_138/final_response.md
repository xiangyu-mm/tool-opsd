### Evidence summary

A prospective study directly addressed this question:

- **Study:** MrOS Sweden cohort, *JACC* 2014, PMID: **25443702**
- **Population:** 2,416 men aged **69–81 years**
- **Follow-up:** Approximately **5 years**
- **Outcome:** 302 incident coronary heart disease (CHD) events
- **Biomarkers:** Baseline serum dehydroepiandrosterone (**DHEA**) and dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (**DHEAS**)

Per standard-deviation increase in baseline hormone concentration:

- **DHEA:** Age-adjusted hazard ratio **0.82** (95% CI 0.73–0.93)
- **DHEAS:** Age-adjusted hazard ratio **0.86** (95% CI 0.77–0.97)

Thus, **lower levels of both DHEA and DHEAS were associated with higher 5-year CHD risk**. The DHEA association remained significant after adjustment for conventional cardiovascular risk factors, testosterone, estradiol, C-reactive protein, and renal function. The association was also unchanged after excluding early follow-up events, reducing concern about reverse causality.

This is observational evidence from one highly relevant elderly-male cohort, so it supports prediction/association rather than proving causation. The evidence for DHEAS is specifically age-adjusted in the reported results, whereas DHEA retained significance with broader adjustment.

FINAL DECISION: YES
