Yes. The evidence supports that UBC9/UBE2I is required for **efficient cardiomyocyte protein quality control**, particularly during proteotoxic, ischemic, or metabolic stress.

- **UPS and aggregate clearance:** UBC9 knockdown reduced proteasomal degradation measured with a GFPu/CL1 reporter and increased protein aggregation in cardiomyocytes. In the CryAB(R120G) proteotoxicity model, UBC9 overexpression reduced aggregates, whereas knockdown worsened them (PMID: **25097219**; source: `call_5ni1j3rulUYgy2OxBwW3AnyZ`).
- **Autophagic clearance:** UBC9 gain or loss altered autophagic flux. Cardiac UBC9 overexpression reduced aggregates and improved fibrosis, hypertrophy, cardiac function, and survival in CryAB(R120G) mice (PMID: **27142163**; source: `call_IW1uBzbjJZSwd9DyqKXW8IAE`).
- **Mitophagy:** Cardiomyocyte-specific Ubc9 knockout aggravated diabetic cardiomyopathy and impaired mitophagy, while UBC9 overexpression was protective (PMID: **40210187**; sources: `call_hBlFsaRnG5LRqB9g82SKGMWV`, `call_lcrViwPjgKuMwonNQNLmIDbw`).

The main qualification is that the evidence demonstrates a requirement for **efficient stress-adaptive proteostasis**, not necessarily absolute indispensability under all baseline conditions. Also, some effects—especially on mitophagy—may be independent of UBC9’s canonical SUMO-conjugation activity.

FINAL DECISION: YES
