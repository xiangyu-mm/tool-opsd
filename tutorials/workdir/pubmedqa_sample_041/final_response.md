Yes—acute L-dopa administration can alter alpha and beta EEG power in parkinsonian patients.

The most direct evidence is from Babiloni et al. (PMID:25452725): in 24 patients with Parkinson disease, oral levodopa/carbidopa (250/25 mg) increased eyes-closed resting **absolute alpha and beta power** approximately 60 minutes after dosing, especially over centro-temporo-parietal scalp regions. Beta-power increases were also associated with lower rigidity and bradykinesia.

However, the effect is not uniform across all EEG measures:

- Resting scalp **absolute alpha and beta power**: increased in the direct acute-dose study.
- Resting cortical **beta coherence**: decreased with dopaminergic medication in another study.
- Task-related cortical **beta power/modulation**: increased or normalized during executive and auditory tasks.
- Subthalamic beta power measured invasively with LFPs: acute levodopa reduced low-beta activity, while alpha changes were not significant.

Thus, alpha and beta EEG power can reflect acute L-dopa administration, but the direction and interpretation depend on recording site, resting versus task conditions, and whether the outcome is local power, coherence, or stimulus-related modulation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
