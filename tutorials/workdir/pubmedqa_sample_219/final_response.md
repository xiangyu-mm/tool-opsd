Yes. The direct experimental study by Khazaei et al. (PMID: 25422754, 2014) measured arcuate KiSS-1 mRNA in lactating rats using real-time PCR.

- **Litter size:** With continuous suckling, dams nursing 5 pups had significantly higher arcuate KiSS-1 mRNA than dams nursing 10 or 15 pups (**P < 0.0001**). Thus, larger litters were associated with lower KiSS-1 expression.
- **Suckling intensity:** After 6 hours of pup separation, 2.5 minutes of suckling produced higher KiSS-1 mRNA than 5 or 7.5 minutes (**P < 0.0001**). Thus, stronger/longer suckling inhibited KiSS-1 expression.
- The authors reported that the effect of suckling intensity was more pronounced than the effect of litter size.

The cross-condition comparisons involving pup separation should be interpreted cautiously because separation itself can affect gene expression, but the within-condition comparisons directly support inhibition by increased litter size and suckling intensity.
