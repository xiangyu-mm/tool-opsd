Yes. The directly relevant study was Xu et al. (2014; PMID: 25087999), which examined 514 normal-weight Chinese men with normal liver enzymes.

Key findings:

- NAFLD participants had lower serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D3] than non-NAFLD participants: **13.46 ± 4.65 vs 15.65 ± 5.89 ng/mL**, respectively (**P = 0.002**).
- Serum 25(OH)D3 was inversely correlated with fatty liver index after adjustment for age and BMI (**r = −0.108, P = 0.014**).
- Logistic regression found an inverse association with NAFLD (**OR 0.937 per 1-ng/mL increase; 95% CI 0.884–0.993; P = 0.028**).

Thus, lower serum vitamin D was associated with a higher likelihood or burden of NAFLD even in normal-weight Chinese men with normal liver enzymes. However, this was an observational study using ultrasound, so it does not establish causality, and only one clearly matching study was identified.
