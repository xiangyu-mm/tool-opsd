The reaction is **hydrolysis of a methyl ester to a carboxylic acid**:

\[
\mathrm{R{-}CO_2Me \rightarrow R{-}CO_2H}
\]

The decoded reactant contains a **methyl ester**, while the product contains the corresponding **carboxylic acid**. Plausible reagent systems include:

### Most likely conditions

1. **Lithium hydroxide**
   - LiOH, aqueous THF or methanol/water
   - Then acidification with HCl or another acid  
   - Common for ester hydrolysis in medicinal chemistry because it is often relatively mild and selective.

2. **Sodium hydroxide**
   - NaOH, aqueous methanol, ethanol, or THF/water
   - Acid workup with HCl  
   - Produces the carboxylate initially, which is protonated during workup.

3. **Potassium hydroxide**
   - KOH in aqueous ethanol or methanol
   - Followed by acidic workup.

### Alternative conditions

4. **Hydrochloric acid**
   - Aqueous HCl, optionally in methanol, ethanol, or THF/water
   - Heat may be used.

5. **Sulfuric acid**
   - Dilute aqueous H₂SO₄, often with heating.

6. **Trimethyltin hydroxide**
   - Me₃SnOH in an organic solvent such as dichloromethane or methanol
   - A useful milder, more selective ester-hydrolysis reagent when other ester or amide groups must be preserved.

A representative procedure would be:

> **LiOH·H₂O, THF/H₂O or MeOH/H₂O, room temperature to gentle heating, followed by acidification with dilute HCl.**

The most plausible single answer is therefore **aqueous LiOH followed by acidic workup**, with **NaOH or KOH** as common alternatives.
