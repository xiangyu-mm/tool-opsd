The transformation is **reduction of an ethyl ester to a primary alcohol**: the ester carbonyl carbon becomes the `CH₂OH` carbon. It is not ester hydrolysis, which would give a carboxylic acid or carboxylate.

Potential reagents include **LiAlH₄** or **LiBH₄**, both strong hydride reagents commonly used for ester reduction. A related database record lists **NaBH₄ in ethanol** for a difluoropyridyl ester-to-alcohol conversion, but reports only **1.0% yield**. That is a weak precedent, not evidence of a reliable method; the record’s reagent/catalyst field lists ethanol as the solvent. **(Search evidence: `call_kg8famkDz5YqA5yxMYCoxDsI`.)**

Therefore, **LiAlH₄ or LiBH₄ are plausible candidates**, while NaBH₄/ethanol is a reported but apparently low-yield precedent.
