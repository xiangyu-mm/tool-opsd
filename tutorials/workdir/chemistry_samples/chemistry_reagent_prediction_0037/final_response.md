The transformation is **hydrolysis of a methyl ester**:

\[
\mathrm{R{-}C(=O)OCH_3 \rightarrow R{-}C(=O)OH}
\]

Suitable reagent systems include:

### Most likely options

- **LiOH, THF/H₂O or MeOH/H₂O**, followed by acidification with HCl  
  - Often the preferred mild and chemoselective option.
- **NaOH, aqueous MeOH or EtOH**, followed by acidic workup  
  - Standard ester saponification conditions.
- **KOH, aqueous EtOH or H₂O**, followed by acidification  
  - Stronger basic hydrolysis; useful if the ester is less reactive.

### Alternative acidic hydrolysis

- **Aqueous HCl**, often with heating
- **Dilute H₂SO₄ in water/alcohol**, with heating

For this substrate, a reasonable first choice would be:

> **LiOH, THF/H₂O, room temperature to mild heating, then HCl or another acid for workup**

The substrate contains an aryl chloride, ether, alkyne, and hydrazone-like functionality. These groups are generally compatible with mild ester saponification, whereas strongly acidic or strongly heated conditions may cause unwanted decomposition or hydrolysis elsewhere.
