### Reaction interpretation

The transformation is most consistent with **hydrolysis of an ethyl ester to a carboxylic acid**:

\[
\mathrm{R{-}C(=O)OCH_2CH_3 \rightarrow R{-}C(=O)OH}
\]

The hydroxamate, heterocycle, stereocenter, and remaining side chain appear unchanged. This is therefore **ester saponification**, not oxidation or general deprotection.

### Most likely reagents

**Primary proposal:**

- **Lithium hydroxide monohydrate (LiOH·H₂O)**
- **Water**
- **1,4-Dioxane** as a cosolvent
- Followed by **acidification**, such as dilute HCl or another suitable aqueous acid, to give the free carboxylic acid

A representative condition would be:

> LiOH·H₂O, H₂O/1,4-dioxane, room temperature or mild heating, approximately 2 h; acidify during workup.

### Plausible alternatives

Other standard ester-hydrolysis systems could also produce this conversion:

- **NaOH, aqueous THF or ethanol/water**
- **KOH, aqueous methanol or ethanol**
- **Aqueous LiOH in THF/water**
- For milder acidic hydrolysis: **HCl or H₂SO₄ in aqueous alcohol**, although this is less likely if the scaffold contains acid-sensitive groups
