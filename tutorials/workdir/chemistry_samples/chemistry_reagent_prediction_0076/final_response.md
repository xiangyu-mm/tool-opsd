The heteroaromatic scaffold and stereocenter are retained. No new C–C or C–N bond is formed.

## Recommended reagent systems

### 1. Trifluoroacetic acid — preferred first option

**Reagents/conditions:**

- TFA, neat or 20–50% in dichloromethane
- 0 °C to room temperature
- Approximately 0.5–3 h, monitoring by LC–MS or TLC
- Concentrate and co-evaporate with toluene or dichloromethane to remove residual TFA

Example:

```text
Substrate
TFA/DCM, 0 °C → rt, 1–3 h
```

This is generally the best initial screen for a complex molecule containing a tert-butyl ester and acid-sensitive heteroaromatic functionality.

### 2. Hydrogen chloride in dioxane or ethyl acetate

**Reagents/conditions:**

- 4 M HCl in dioxane, typically 2–10 equivalents
- Room temperature
- 1–4 h

Example:

```text
Substrate
4 M HCl in dioxane, rt, 1–4 h
```

This can be useful if isolation as the hydrochloride salt is acceptable. The product may require neutralization or ion-exchange workup to obtain the free carboxylic acid.

### 3. HCl in dichloromethane or ethyl acetate

A less concentrated HCl solution can also be used:

```text
HCl in EtOAc or DCM, rt
```

This is useful when minimizing excessive acidity or controlling the reaction rate.

### 4. Formic acid

**Reagents/conditions:**

- 80–100% formic acid
- Room temperature to mild heating
- Several hours

Formic acid is a milder alternative, although it may require longer reaction times than TFA or HCl.

### 5. Stronger acid conditions, if necessary

If the ester is unusually resistant:

- TFA with a small amount of anisole or another cation scavenger
- Concentrated sulfuric acid, used cautiously and only on a small scale

These stronger conditions increase the risk of decomposition, sulfonation, or degradation of the heteroaromatic scaffold and should not be the initial choice.

## Practical recommendation

I would begin with:

```text
TFA/DCM = approximately 1:1
0 °C addition, then room temperature
1–3 h
```

Use a dilute solution if solubility permits. After completion, remove the solvent and excess TFA, then triturate or purify to obtain the carboxylic acid.

### Important considerations

- The tert-butyl ester should be cleaved readily under acidic conditions.
- Basic hydrolysis with LiOH, NaOH, or KOH is possible in principle, but it is less attractive here because it may require longer exposure and can affect sensitive heteroaromatic or imide-like functionality.
- Avoid oxidative or hydrogenolytic conditions; neither is needed for this transformation.
- The stereocenter is not expected to be affected under mild, short-duration acid treatment, although prolonged strongly acidic heating should be avoided.
