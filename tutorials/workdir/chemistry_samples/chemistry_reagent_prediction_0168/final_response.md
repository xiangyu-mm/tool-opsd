### Transformation identified

The reaction converts a **tertiary amide into a tertiary amine**:

\[
\mathrm{R{-}N(CH_3){-}C(=O){-}CH_2R'}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\mathrm{R{-}N(CH_3){-}CH_2{-}CH_2R'}
\]

Thus, the carbonyl oxygen is removed and the amide carbonyl carbon becomes a methylene group.

### Most likely reagents

**Primary candidate:**

- **Lithium aluminium hydride, LiAlH₄**
- Solvent: dry **THF or diethyl ether**
- Followed by aqueous or mildly basic workup

Typical schematic conditions:

\[
\mathrm{LiAlH_4,\ THF/Et_2O,\ reflux\ or\ room\ temperature;\ then\ H_2O/NH_4Cl}
\]

### Other plausible alternatives

1. **Borane complexes**
   - **BH₃·THF**
   - **BH₃·SMe₂**
   - Often useful for selective reduction of amides to amines.

2. **Red-Al**
   - Sodium bis(2-methoxyethoxy)aluminium hydride
   - A strong hydride alternative to LiAlH₄.

3. **Diisobutylaluminium hydride, DIBAL-H**
   - Less likely for this product because DIBAL-H more commonly reduces amides to aldehydes or partially reduced products rather than fully converting them to amines.

### Ranked proposal

| Rank | Reagent | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **LiAlH₄** | Most conventional and likely for complete tertiary-amide reduction |
| 2 | **BH₃·THF or BH₃·SMe₂** | Highly plausible, potentially more chemoselective |
| 3 | **Red-Al** | Chemically suitable strong-hydride alternative |
| 4 | **DIBAL-H** | Less likely for the shown complete amide-to-amine conversion |
| 5 | Catalytic hydrogenation | Unlikely without specialized catalysts and conditions |

**Best single prediction:** **LiAlH₄ in dry THF or ether, followed by aqueous workup.** Because the substrate contains aryl fluoride, aryl chloride, an ether, and sulfur-containing heteroaromatic functionality, **BH₃·THF** would be a particularly reasonable alternative if chemoselectivity was important.
