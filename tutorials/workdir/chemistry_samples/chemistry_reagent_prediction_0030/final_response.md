The reaction is most consistent with **base-promoted SN2 N-alkylation**.

The first reactant is a primary **3-chloropropyl oxazolidinone**, and the second is an **azaheteroaryl-substituted piperazine**. The terminal chloride is displaced by the free secondary piperazine nitrogen.

### Most likely reagents and conditions

**Likely reaction mixture:**

- **Potassium carbonate, K₂CO₃** — base and HCl scavenger  
- **Potassium iodide, KI**, or **sodium iodide, NaI** — optional iodide promoter for in situ chloride-to-iodide exchange  
- **1-Butanol** — supported as a solvent/additive in related reaction records  
- **Heat**, likely approximately **50–90 °C**

A practical alternative would be:

- **K₂CO₃ or Cs₂CO₃**
- Catalytic or stoichiometric **KI/NaI**
- **DMF, DMSO, or acetonitrile**
- Heating until consumption of the alkyl chloride

### Proposed transformation

\[
\text{3-chloropropyl oxazolidinone}
+
\text{azaaryl-piperazine}
\rightarrow
\text{3-(piperazin-1-yl)propyl oxazolidinone}
\]

The key bond change is:

\[
\mathrm{C-Cl \rightarrow C-N(piperazine)}
\]

### Most likely reagent set

**K₂CO₃, KI, and 1-butanol, with heating**

If the piperazine starting material was supplied as an HCl salt, additional carbonate base would be needed to liberate the free amine. A transition-metal catalyst is not expected for this transformation.
