The reaction is most consistent with **hydrolysis (saponification) of an ethyl ester to a carboxylic acid**.

### Structural change

\[
\mathrm{EtOOC{-}CH_2{-}N(Me){-}C(O){-}C{\equiv}CPh}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{HOOC{-}CH_2{-}N(Me){-}C(O){-}C{\equiv}CPh}
\]

The ethoxy group is removed from the ester; the N-methyl group and the tertiary amide remain unchanged.

### Most likely reagents

- **NaOH**
- **H₂O**
- **MeOH** as a cosolvent  
- Acidic workup, such as **dilute HCl**, to convert the carboxylate salt to the free acid

A representative procedure would be:

> NaOH in a methanol/water mixture, followed by acidification with dilute HCl.

### Plausible alternatives

Other bases that could perform the same transformation include:

- **LiOH·H₂O**, often in THF/H₂O or MeOH/H₂O
- **KOH**, in aqueous methanol or ethanol
- **Na₂CO₃ or K₂CO₃**, under milder hydrolysis conditions, depending on substrate reactivity

The directly supported reagent set is **aqueous NaOH in MeOH/H₂O**, followed by acidification. This is ester hydrolysis, not ester-to-amide conversion, N-dealkylation, or acyl transfer.
