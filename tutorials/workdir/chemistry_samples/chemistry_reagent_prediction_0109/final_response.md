The reaction is a **carboxylic acid → primary alcohol reduction**:

\[
\mathrm{Ar{-}C(=O)OH \rightarrow Ar{-}CH_2OH}
\]

The aryl bromide and aryl fluoride should ideally remain unchanged.

## Recommended reagent

**Borane–tetrahydrofuran complex, BH₃·THF**

Typical conditions:

- **Reagent:** BH₃·THF, approximately 1–1.5 equivalents relative to the carboxylic acid  
- **Solvent:** THF  
- **Temperature:** 0 °C initially, then room temperature  
- **Workup:** Careful quench with methanol or aqueous alcohol, followed by standard aqueous workup

A practical alternative is:

**BH₃·SMe₂** in THF or another suitable ether solvent.

### Why this is preferred

Borane is particularly effective for reducing carboxylic acids and is generally more chemoselective than strong hydrides. Under appropriate conditions, aryl **C–Br** and **C–F** bonds are expected to remain intact.

## Other possible reagent systems

| Reagent system | Suitability | Comments |
|---|---:|---|
| **BH₃·THF** | Best first choice | Efficient acid-to-alcohol reduction; generally compatible with aryl halides |
| **BH₃·SMe₂** | Best alternative | Similar reactivity; often easier to handle commercially |
| **LiAlH₄, THF or Et₂O** | Effective fallback | Strong, reliable reduction, but less chemoselective and requires careful anhydrous handling |
| **NaBH₄/I₂** | Possible alternative | Can reduce acids, but substrate and condition dependent |
| **BH₃·THF followed by oxidative or aqueous workup** | Preferred screening condition | Useful if the substrate contains additional reducible groups |

## Recommended order of screening

1. **BH₃·THF, THF, 0 °C → room temperature**
2. **BH₃·SMe₂, THF, 0 °C → room temperature**
3. **LiAlH₄, anhydrous THF or ether, low temperature → room temperature**

Avoid beginning with LiAlH₄ if the molecule contains other sensitive groups, such as additional carbonyls, nitro groups, alkenes, or heteroatom-containing functionalities.

**Best concise answer:** use **BH₃·THF in THF**, beginning at 0 °C and allowing the reaction to warm to room temperature, followed by careful aqueous or alcoholic quench.
