## Conclusion

**BRAF mutation analysis—particularly BRAF V600E testing—is a valid tool to implement, but only as part of a sequential Lynch syndrome work-up, not as a standalone diagnostic test.**

Its appropriate use is:

1. Perform tumor mismatch-repair immunohistochemistry or microsatellite-instability testing.
2. If the tumor shows **MLH1/PMS2 loss**, perform **BRAF V600E testing and/or MLH1 promoter-methylation analysis**.
3. A positive BRAF V600E result strongly favors a sporadic MLH1-deficient colorectal cancer and may reduce unnecessary germline testing.
4. A negative BRAF result does **not** establish Lynch syndrome and does not exclude a sporadic MLH1-deficient tumor; proceed according to family history, age, MLH1 methylation, germline testing, and possibly somatic tumor sequencing.

BRAF testing has little value for tumors with isolated MSH2, MSH6, or PMS2 loss, where germline evaluation is generally more appropriate. The revised Bethesda guidelines themselves are a clinical selection tool and may miss germline mismatch-repair mutation carriers; therefore, BRAF testing should not replace comprehensive tumor and germline evaluation.

The evidence indicates high specificity but incomplete sensitivity. Published Western estimates identify BRAF V600E in approximately 69–78% of MLH1-methylated sporadic colorectal cancers, while markedly lower frequencies have been reported in some populations, including 15/155 MLH1-loss tumors in one Chinese cohort. This population variation limits use of a fixed negative predictive value (sources: `call_Aa4YdlsDFqBPO9CDCIZVafS8`, `call_UrMFtuVePJApkHHtXSUiF1pq`, `call_DDsA8WqWr8dyDsAwPhhKfs8A`).

Furthermore, tumor triage can occasionally provide false reassurance. Germline Lynch syndrome has been reported in patients whose tumors showed MLH1 promoter methylation, particularly when there was a strong family history; clinical context should therefore override an apparently sporadic tumor result when suspicion remains high (`call_dWMPqO7azSi6s2IFXksaMLwv`, `call_8b8ItG6969wdLFF4Oo0dXoQ0`).

Thus, BRAF V600E analysis is best regarded as a **reflex triage test for MLH1-deficient colorectal tumors**, helping distinguish likely sporadic disease from cases requiring germline investigation. It is clinically useful and appropriate to implement, provided its limitations are recognized.
