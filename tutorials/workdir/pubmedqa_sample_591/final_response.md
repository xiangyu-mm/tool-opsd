## Conclusion

**Long-term or repeated soap use can repeatedly raise skin-surface pH and stress the skin barrier, but current human evidence does not show that it permanently changes the skin’s underlying pH-maintenance mechanism.**

In a randomized infant study, alkaline soap (pH 9.5) raised skin pH by **0.45 units** ten minutes after washing, compared with 0.19 after water; it also removed more surface fat. The study did not measure how quickly pH recovered. Reviews indicate that skin pH commonly returns toward its usual acidic range over several hours after washing, while repeated cleansing can contribute to lipid loss and barrier stress. Acidity also supports barrier-lipid processing and antimicrobial defenses, so repeated alkalinization may temporarily interfere with these functions.

The important limitation is that direct long-term human studies measuring pH recovery and the skin’s acidifying mechanisms during ordinary alkaline-soap use are sparse. pH-balanced syndets generally appear less disruptive than traditional alkaline soap, but evidence of a permanent “reset” of the skin’s pH regulation has not been established.
