Yes. The study identified was a five-year retrospective longitudinal study of 359 patients with type 2 diabetes:

- **Citation:** Pan et al., *Journal of Diabetes and Its Complications* (2014); PMID **25073934**, DOI **10.1016/j.jdiacomp.2014.06.015**.
- Patients with diabetic-retinopathy progression had higher mean glycated albumin than those without progression (**22.66 ± 5.92% vs 19.83 ± 5.18%; P < 0.01**).
- In multivariable logistic regression, mean glycated albumin remained independently associated with progression (**OR 1.087; P = 0.039**), alongside mean HbA1c, baseline retinopathy status, and eGFR.
- The reported optimal glycated-albumin cutoff was **21.85%**.

Thus, serum glycated albumin predicted diabetic-retinopathy progression within this cohort, although the evidence is observational and retrospective, and the study did not establish causality or external predictive validity.
