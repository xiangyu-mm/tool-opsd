## Conclusion

Yes—available evidence supports that bone marrow stromal cells inhibit activation of cirrhotic liver fat-storing cells through adrenomedullin secretion, but the evidence is limited.

### Key evidence

A directly relevant 2015 study reported that rat bone marrow stromal-cell (BMSC) conditioned medium:

- Reduced proliferation and activation of cultured cirrhotic fat-storing cells, a model of hepatic stellate cells.
- Reduced angiotensin II, angiotensin II type-1 receptor, and p47-phox signaling.
- Involved BMSC-derived adrenomedullin.
- Used recombinant adrenomedullin supplementation and adrenomedullin-specific siRNA, providing gain- and loss-of-function testing.

**Primary source:** PMID:25445161, *Digestive Diseases and Sciences* (2015), “Bone Marrow Stromal Cells Inhibit the Activation of Liver Cirrhotic Fat-Storing Cells via Adrenomedullin Secretion.”

### Important limitations

- The evidence comes from a single directly matching in-vitro rat-cell study.
- The accessible record does not provide effect sizes, dose-response data, or detailed results showing how completely adrenomedullin knockdown reversed the BMSC effect.
- In-vivo confirmation in cirrhotic animals or humans was not established.
- Therefore, the study supports an adrenomedullin-mediated mechanism, but does not prove that adrenomedullin is fully necessary and sufficient in all settings.

FINAL DECISION: YES
