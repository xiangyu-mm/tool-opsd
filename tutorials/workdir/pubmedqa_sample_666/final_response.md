Yes. A prospective DCCT/EDIC study (PMID: 25088746) found that **lower baseline plasma levels of selected very-long-chain ceramide species** were associated with subsequent development of nephropathy in people with type 1 diabetes.

Key evidence:

- **Population:** 497 participants with type 1 diabetes, initially with normal albumin excretion.
- **Follow-up:** 14–20 years.
- **Outcomes:** 141 developed microalbuminuria and 65 developed macroalbuminuria.
- **Finding:** Higher baseline very-long-chain ceramide levels were associated with **lower odds of later macroalbuminuria**, implying that decreased levels were associated with increased nephropathy risk.
- The association remained after adjustment for treatment group, retinopathy, sex, HbA1c, age, baseline albumin excretion, lipids, diabetes duration, and ACE inhibitor/ARB use.

This supports an association, not necessarily a causal relationship. The abstract identifies the finding as applying to “select” very-long-chain ceramide species but does not specify the individual species.

FINAL DECISION: YES
