## Conclusion

Yes. The available literature generally supports an **independent statistical association** between diagnostic ureteroscopy before radical nephroureterectomy and subsequent intravesical recurrence.

Key evidence includes:

- Meta-analyses found higher recurrence after preoperative ureteroscopy, with pooled hazard ratios of **1.56** and **1.44** (both statistically significant) [call_KnpPrhS71dIoh0JijxSQgRnQ; call_ihmFIYtbqugJFs1goDGLtEKx].
- A 453-patient cohort found preoperative ureteroscopy independently associated with recurrence after adjustment: **HR 1.413, 95% CI 1.015–1.965** [call_ECSK7WG0IXlwGevO4FNHRk1K].
- The association appears strongest for **ureteroscopic biopsy**, with adjusted **HR 1.40**, whereas ureteroscopy without biopsy was not significant [call_rY1sjdM5MZOCA7UU6443FPKI].
- Delayed nephroureterectomy after ureteroscopy was associated with recurrence: **HR 1.98, 95% CI 1.08–3.65** [call_tcRB9NBfaxJQI6R890EZg7yU].
- A prospective multicentre registry also reported increased recurrence after preoperative ureteroscopy: **HR 1.705, 95% CI 1.082–2.688** [call_MKQIfGDOL1T28w0GUtvfXnC8].

However, the evidence is observational. At least one large cohort found no significant independent association, and ureteroscopy is preferentially used in diagnostically difficult or potentially higher-risk tumors. Therefore, **confounding by indication cannot be excluded**, and the evidence supports association rather than definitive causation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
