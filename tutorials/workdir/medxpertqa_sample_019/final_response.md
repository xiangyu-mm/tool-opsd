The most likely diagnosis is **glycogen storage disease type I (von Gierke disease)**.

The key findings are:

- Severe fasting intolerance due to inability to maintain blood glucose
- Doll-like facial fullness
- Protuberant abdomen with marked hepatomegaly
- Thin limbs and growth impairment
- Milky blood from severe hypertriglyceridemia/lipemia

GSD type I results from impaired conversion of glucose-6-phosphate to free glucose, usually due to **G6PC deficiency** in GSD Ia. This blocks both glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis and causes hypoglycemia, glycogen accumulation, hepatomegaly, and hyperlipidemia. Lipoprotein lipase deficiency can cause milky plasma but does not explain the characteristic fasting hypoglycemia and glycogen-storage phenotype.
