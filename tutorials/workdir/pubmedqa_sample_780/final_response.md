Yes. In healthy volunteers, paroxetine affects both the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of metoprolol.

- **Pharmacokinetics:** Paroxetine, a potent CYP2D6 inhibitor, substantially increased metoprolol exposure:
  - In an 8-volunteer study, mean AUC increased from **169 to 1,340 ng·h/mL for R-metoprolol** and from **279 to 1,418 ng·h/mL for S-metoprolol**—approximately **8-fold and 5-fold**, respectively. Both Cmax and half-life approximately doubled (PMID **10741632**).
  - In a 15-volunteer crossover study, paroxetine increased R- and S-metoprolol AUC approximately **3- to 4-fold** across immediate-release and extended-release formulations and approximately doubled elimination half-life (PMID **21923449**).
  - A separate 10-volunteer study similarly found approximately **4- to 5-fold increases** with immediate-release metoprolol and **3- to 4-fold increases** with extended-release metoprolol (PMID **20400652**).

- **Pharmacodynamics:** Paroxetine enhanced and prolonged β-blockade. It increased the reduction in **exercise heart rate** and **exercise systolic blood pressure**; the exercise-heart-rate effect AUC increased by approximately **46%** in the 8-volunteer study (PMID **10741632**). The interaction may be more clinically pronounced with immediate-release metoprolol because of higher peak S-metoprolol concentrations.

Thus, in healthy volunteers, paroxetine increases metoprolol concentrations and intensifies/prolongs its heart-rate and blood-pressure effects. These findings support caution with coadministration because of possible excessive β-blockade, bradycardia, or hypotension.

FINAL DECISION: YES
