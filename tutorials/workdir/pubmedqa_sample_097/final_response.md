Yes. Available evidence indicates that pelvic infection after IUD placement is uncommon, including with same-day placement in eligible patients.

- In a WHO analysis of 22,908 IUD insertions, overall PID incidence was **1.6 per 1,000 woman-years** (Farley et al., *Lancet*, PMID: 1347812).
- Risk was temporarily higher during the first 20 days after insertion (**9.7 vs 1.4 per 1,000 woman-years** thereafter), but the absolute risk remained low.
- Systematic reviews of immediate postabortion IUD insertion found **similar infection rates** compared with no IUD or delayed insertion, without evidence of a clinically important infection excess.
- Placement should generally be deferred in patients with **current purulent cervicitis, untreated gonorrhea/chlamydia, or active pelvic infection**.

Thus, infection is a rare complication when appropriate screening and eligibility criteria are followed, although the retrieved evidence does not establish a precise absolute risk difference between same-day and delayed placement.

FINAL DECISION: YES
