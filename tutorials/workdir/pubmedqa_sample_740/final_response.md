Two direct pig cardiopulmonary-bypass studies found that biologically variable pulsation improved preservation of jugular venous oxygen saturation during rewarming from approximately 28°C:

- **Biologically variable vs conventional pulsatile CPB:** mean exposure to SjvO₂ <50% was **3.6±7.3 vs 82±96 %·min** (*p*=0.004), with cumulative exposure **42±87 vs 983±1158 %·min** (*p*=0.004). PMID: **10735686**; source: `call_k2lINlD96sZAy3QkSvRVbrqr`.
- **Biologically variable vs apulsatile CPB:** mean exposure to SjvO₂ <50% was **1.9±3.6 vs 164±209 %·min** (*p*=0.021), with cumulative exposure **23±45 vs 1,796±2,263 %·min** (*p*=0.020). PMID: **9456096**; source: `call_lupDTHauHxbFxdmSfIySYwHz`.

Temperature, blood gases, hemoglobin, and relevant perfusion variables were generally comparable, supporting an effect associated with biologic variability rather than simply different rewarming conditions. However, the direct evidence is from **animal studies**. Human studies provide only indirect support for pulsatility or higher pump flow and do not directly test biologically variable pulsation during rewarming.
