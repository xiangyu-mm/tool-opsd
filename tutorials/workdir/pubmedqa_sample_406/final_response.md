Yes—PTSD appears to influence the **intrathecal cytokine response** to painful stimulation in combat veterans. In a small study of 21 male combat veterans (10 with PTSD, 11 controls), capsaicin was injected into the quadriceps and cerebrospinal-fluid cytokines were measured over 110 minutes. IL-1β rose significantly only in the PTSD group, and the IL-10 response was delayed compared with controls. TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-8 increased in both groups.

The evidence available here does **not** establish whether PTSD changed pain ratings or temporal summation between groups. So the cytokine finding supports an influence on the response, but the nociceptive component remains unresolved from the retrieved results. Study: PMID 27490714, *Psychoneuroendocrinology* (2016).

FINAL DECISION: YES
