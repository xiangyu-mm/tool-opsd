The evidence indicates that **excess adiposity does not regulate or explain the altered mononuclear-cell (MNC) cytokine response to glucose ingestion in PCOS**.

- In the directly relevant study (PMID: **25078146**), normal-weight women with PCOS—both those with normal abdominal adiposity and those with excess abdominal adiposity—failed to suppress MNC-derived **TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β** after a 75-g oral glucose load.
- Normal-weight controls, in contrast, suppressed these cytokines.
- Obese participants, with or without PCOS, also showed impaired suppression, indicating that obesity can independently produce a similar inflammatory phenotype.
- A related weight-matched study (PMID: **24576416**) likewise concluded that the systemic inflammatory response was independent of obesity, although chronic inflammation, abdominal fat, and hyperandrogenism may contribute.
- An earlier study (PMID: **15985479**) suggested adiposity contributes to inflammation and insulin resistance, but did not show that it explains the PCOS-specific cytokine abnormality.

Thus, adiposity may modify inflammatory responses and correlate with cytokine measures, but the abnormal response in PCOS persists independently of excess adiposity.
