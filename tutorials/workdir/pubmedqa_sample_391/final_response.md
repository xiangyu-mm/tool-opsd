Yes—**invasive aortic pulse wave velocity (aPWV) appears to predict blood-pressure response after renal sympathetic denervation (RDN)**, with lower baseline aPWV associated with greater BP reduction.

Evidence includes:

- **2016 cohort (n=58 analyzed):** responders had lower aPWV than nonresponders (14.4±4.4 vs 17.7±4.5 m/s; *p*=0.009). Invasive PWV was the only significant multivariable predictor; higher stiffness predicted poorer response (PMID:27497368; source `call_2wJT3Z6oFhzEFCgSy7euM98R`).
- **2022 prospective study (n=80):** patients with aPWV below **14.4 m/s** had greater 3-month reductions in 24-hour and daytime systolic BP than those above the threshold (source `call_lx4Gq1TkMYYdKAupBx80VRqM`).
- **2018 small study (n=32):** invasive PWV showed predictive discrimination for response, with ROC AUC 0.849 (*p*=0.004), though the sample was small and the cutoff was not independently validated (PMID:29744617; source `call_pEVgcTIZM1w2G9Vr2WM7KYHT`).

However, the evidence is limited by small observational cohorts, variable responder definitions, and lack of robust external validation. The approximately **14.4 m/s** threshold remains investigational. Importantly, the studies primarily predict **short-term BP response**, not mortality, cardiovascular events, renal outcomes, or long-term clinical benefit.

Therefore, invasive aPWV is a **promising predictor of RDN BP response**, but not yet a definitively validated treatment-selection biomarker.

FINAL DECISION: YES
