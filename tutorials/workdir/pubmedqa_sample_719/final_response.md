The evidence supports **yes**.

- In co-transfected **HEK293T cells**, dopamine D1 receptors (D1R/DRD1) and corticotropin-releasing hormone receptor type-2α (CRHR2α/CRF2α) were reported to **co-immunoprecipitate**, supporting physical association.
- Their interaction was functionally consequential: D1R agonists induced intracellular **Ca²⁺ mobilization** in the co-expression system, dopamine reduced CRHR2α cell-surface expression, and simultaneous receptor activation produced **ERK1/2 signaling occlusion**.
- Rat medial prefrontal-cortex synaptosomes showed receptor co-localization and convergent ERK signaling, supporting physiological relevance, although this does not by itself prove direct receptor-receptor binding.
- The strongest direct evidence is therefore biochemical co-immunoprecipitation from cells expressing both receptors, combined with altered trafficking and signaling. No FRET/BRET or comparable quantitative live-cell proximity assay was identified.
- A separate study supports complex formation for **CRHR2β**, but that isoform-specific result should not be conflated with CRHR2α.

Thus, D1R and CRHR2α appear to form a functionally interacting receptor complex in living-cell expression systems, with some limitation regarding the precise in-cell structural demonstration.

FINAL DECISION: YES
