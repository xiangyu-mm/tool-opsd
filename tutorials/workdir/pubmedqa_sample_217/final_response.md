Evidence indicates that 448 kHz electrical stimulation **can promote proliferation of human mesenchymal stem cells**, specifically human adipose-derived stem cells.

- **Study:** Hernandez-Bule et al., 2014, PMID: 25427571.
- **Protocol:** 448-kHz capacitive-resistive stimulation, subthermal current density of 50 μA/mm², applied for 5 minutes followed by 4 hours off.
- **Findings:** Increased proliferation rate, more cells in the S/G2/M phases of the cell cycle, and increased PCNA and ERK1/2 expression.
- **Additional finding:** The stimulation did not prevent subsequent adipogenic, chondrogenic, or osteogenic differentiation.
- **Limitation:** The direct evidence is from adipose-derived MSCs in vitro. The available evidence does not establish the same effect in human bone-marrow-derived MSCs, and the primary abstract does not report an absolute proliferation increase.

Thus, the answer is positive for human adipose-derived MSCs under the tested conditions, but broader generalization should be cautious.

FINAL DECISION: YES
