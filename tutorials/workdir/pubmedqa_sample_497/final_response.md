Yes. The strongest direct evidence comes from an in vivo pig-liver study (PMID: 25092576) that measured plasmid DNA, transgene mRNA, and protein after hydrodynamic eGFP delivery. Although retrograde injection generated substantial transgene mRNA, protein production was lower than expected, leading the authors to conclude that inefficient mRNA translation limited gene-transfer efficacy.

However, this is not the only limitation:

- Delivery route and hydrodynamic mechanics strongly affect hepatocyte access and expression.
- Intraportal delivery performed better than intrabiliary delivery in one porcine study (PMID: 35211639).
- A replication-capable non-integrating vector increased vector genomes and luciferase expression approximately tenfold.
- Pig-specific protein secretion may further reduce circulating therapeutic protein (PMID: 27695064).
- Other studies demonstrate durable protein expression in pig liver, arguing against a universal translation block.

Thus, low RNA translation is a demonstrated contributor to reduced efficacy in pig liver, but the overall limitation is multifactorial and also involves delivery, intracellular access, vector persistence, and protein secretion.

FINAL DECISION: YES
