Yes. A primary study in *Phaseolus vulgaris* (common bean; PMID: 25429887) found that treating foliage with the aboveground fungal pathogen *Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*—either live pathogen or pathogen fragments—significantly reduced:

- Rhizobia-induced root nodule formation
- Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal (AMF) colonization

The study linked these belowground effects to systemically induced plant defenses, particularly increased polyphenol oxidase activity in roots. Thus, the evidence supports a top-down inhibitory effect of an aboveground pathogen on both rhizobial and AMF symbioses in common bean.

FINAL DECISION: YES
