## Conclusion

Yes—available evidence supports that **TLK2 amplification/overexpression can induce genomic instability by impairing the DNA-damage-induced G2/M checkpoint**, particularly in human breast-cancer models.

### Evidence

- The key experimental study, **PMID:27489360**, reported that TLK2 amplification/overexpression:
  - impaired Chk1/Chk2-mediated checkpoint signaling,
  - produced a G2/M checkpoint defect,
  - delayed DNA repair, and
  - increased chromosomal instability.

- In TCGA breast tumors, TLK2 expression correlated with genome-wide copy-number alteration burden (**Spearman R = 0.393, P < 0.001**). This supports the relationship in tumors but is correlational rather than independently causal.

- Independent work supports TLK2’s role in recovery from DNA-damage-induced G2 arrest through ASF1A-dependent chromatin restoration and reactivation of pro-mitotic gene expression. However, that study does not by itself establish that TLK2 overexpression causes premature mitotic entry.

### Qualification

The causal evidence is strongest for the specific breast-cancer models studied and is based principally on one mechanistic study. It should not automatically be generalized to every cancer type. Also, TLK2 depletion can cause replication stress and DNA damage through different mechanisms, so TLK2 loss and amplification are not equivalent perturbations.

FINAL DECISION: YES
