Yes. The best direct evidence is a 2017 human study of 67 clival chordomas (PMID: 27506406):

- **Dural penetration and prognosis:** Tumors with more extensive dural penetration (Type II, n=34) had worse outcomes than those with limited penetration (Type I, n=33). Dural penetration correlated with overall survival (*P* = 0.032) and progression-free survival (*P* = 0.022). Mean overall survival was longer for Type I tumors (*P* = 0.046), and among completely resected patients, mean progression-free survival was also longer (*P* = 0.007).  
  **Source:** `call_OR5HnonnSLOsTtYqPzOodLro`

- **PDGFR-β expression:** Type II tumors with more serious dural penetration had higher platelet-derived growth factor receptor-β (**PDGFRB/PDGFR-β**) expression than Type I tumors. Expression was assessed by immunohistochemistry, quantitative RT-PCR, and Western blotting.  
  **Sources:** `call_OR5HnonnSLOsTtYqPzOodLro`, `call_FCYhJTPng6fq6EkShKX9KSZO`

- **Limitations:** This is observational evidence from one principal comparative cohort. The retrieved abstract does not fully define the pathological or radiological boundary between Type I and Type II penetration. A separate 12-patient transcriptomic study found broad molecular differences according to dural penetration, but did not independently report PDGFRB-specific expression or survival results (PMID: 32533822; source `call_eKma3mLRNALxWbsqeIhXAo5J`).

Therefore, more extensive dural penetration is associated with poorer prognosis and higher PDGFR-β expression in clival chordoma, but causality and independent replication remain unproven.

FINAL DECISION: YES
