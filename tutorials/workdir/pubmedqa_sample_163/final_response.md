## Evidence summary

Yes. Preoperative proteinuria predicts postcardiotomy acute kidney injury even when baseline glomerular filtration is preserved.

- **Direct evidence:** In a cohort of 1,246 cardiac-surgery patients restricted to **eGFR >60 mL/min/1.73 m²**, preoperative dipstick proteinuria was independently associated with **all AKI stages and dialysis-requiring AKI** after adjustment. AKI occurred in 34.8%, and 2.9% required renal replacement therapy.  
  Source: `call_dfKotNDlD4MTUPFHoaPh5t7v`

- **Supportive preserved-function cohort:** Among 616 patients with mean eGFR approximately 96, heavy proteinuria was independently associated with AKI (**adjusted OR 3.14, 95% CI 1.26–7.77**).  
  Source: `call_y7lH8JAnAQESxOtQtlwQU4Wt`

- **Incremental prediction:** In a prospective cohort using urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, adding albuminuria to a clinical model increased the AUC from **0.67 to 0.70**, with 29% continuous net reclassification improvement. However, this study did not specifically isolate patients with eGFR ≥60.  
  Source: `call_s4U03H3eG0kjY7TaEMMaT61V`

- **Broader evidence:** A meta-analysis found preoperative proteinuria associated with postoperative AKI overall (**adjusted OR 1.65**) and in the cardiac-surgery subgroup (**adjusted OR 1.69, 95% CI 1.39–2.06**), though not specifically restricted to preserved eGFR.  
  Source: `call_qj6mhAbkbZSr5oi10ZsVWPpS`

### Interpretation

Proteinuria is best regarded as an **independent risk predictor or marker of occult renal vulnerability**, even when eGFR is normal or near-normal. The evidence is strong for association with postoperative AKI, including severe AKI, but more limited for proving that proteinuria substantially improves a fully validated risk model beyond eGFR and standard clinical variables.

FINAL DECISION: YES
