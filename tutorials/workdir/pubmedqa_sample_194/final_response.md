Yes. In PC12 cells, S43126’s antiproliferative and cell-death effects were shown to be **partly mediated** by:

- **ROS:** S43126 increased intracellular ROS by >2.5-fold; N-acetylcysteine reduced ROS and attenuated downstream effects.
- **Stress-activated MAPKs:** Inhibitors of ERK, JNK, and p38 MAPK reduced the antiproliferative response.
- **Caspases:** Caspases 3, 8, and 9 were activated, while the pan-caspase inhibitor z-VAD-fmk reduced S43126-induced cell death and proliferation loss.

These inhibitor-rescue experiments support functional involvement rather than simple correlation. However, the evidence is pharmacologic and indicates **partial**, not exclusive or definitively causal, mediation; the study did not establish the precise pathway order or prove that any individual kinase or caspase was indispensable.

Source: PMID:25443718, *Pharmacological Reports* (2014).

FINAL DECISION: YES
