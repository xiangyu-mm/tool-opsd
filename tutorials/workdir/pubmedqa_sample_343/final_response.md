**Yes—in preclinical research, ADAM17 is supported as a tumor promoter and potential therapeutic target, but the Western-diet-specific claim needs qualification.**

The primary study, Grivennikov et al. (*Clinical Cancer Research*, 2017; PMID: 27489286), reported increased colonic ADAM17 and related pathway components with Western-diet exposure. It proposed a CXCL12–CXCR4–ADAM17–TGFα–EGFR signaling route. Separate experiments strengthen the tumor-promoter case: colonocyte-specific ADAM17 deletion reduced EGFR signaling and tumor development in an AOM model, while inhibitor studies reduced tumor-related signaling or tumor multiplicity in other models.

**Important caveats:** the dietary comparison did not itself establish that ADAM17 caused Western-diet-induced tumor burden, nor did it demonstrate increased ADAM17 catalytic activity. Some inhibitor evidence is not specific to ADAM17. These are preclinical findings, not proof of therapeutic benefit in patients.

**Bottom line:** ADAM17 is a promising preclinical target and tumor-promoting factor, but its diet-specific causal role and clinical value remain unproven.
