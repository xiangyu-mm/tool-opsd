**Yes—soft-drink consumption was associated with depressive symptoms in a study of adults in China.** A cross-sectional survey of 3,667 adults in Tianjin found that, compared with the lowest intake category, the adjusted odds of elevated depressive symptoms (Zung Self-Rating Depression Scale score ≥50) were higher in the middle category (OR 1.43, 95% CI 1.01–2.01) and highest category (OR 2.00, 95% CI 1.15–3.37), with a significant trend (p<0.01). Similar patterns were reported using other symptom cutoffs (PMID: 25451447).

This supports an **association**, not a causal conclusion: the study was cross-sectional, so it cannot determine whether soft-drink intake preceded depressive symptoms or rule out residual confounding. A separate Chinese college-student study also reported higher depression symptom scores with frequent soft-drink intake, but it is less representative of adults overall (PMID: 31097051).
