Yes. A prospective cohort study directly examined this question:

- **Study:** PMID 25084165, *PLOS ONE* (2014)
- **Population:** 2,131 active, healthy employed adults; 34% women
- **Design:** Three assessments over approximately 3.7 years
- **Exposure:** Increase in insomnia symptoms from the first to second assessment
- **Outcome:** New-onset back pain at the third assessment
- **Adjusted result:** Increased insomnia symptoms predicted a **40% higher odds** of subsequent back pain (**OR 1.40, 95% CI 1.10–1.71**) after adjustment for socioeconomic factors, health, lifestyle, and anthropometric variables.
- **Directionality:** The study did not find evidence that pre-existing back pain predicted later increases in insomnia symptoms.

Thus, increased insomnia symptoms appear to predict the onset of back pain among employed adults. Because this was an observational cohort study, it supports temporal prediction but does not prove that insomnia causes back pain.

FINAL DECISION: YES
