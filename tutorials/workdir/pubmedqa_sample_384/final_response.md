**Yes—experimental evidence supports this.** In a rat study, CNP reduced the tachycardia produced by stellate-ganglion stimulation and reduced norepinephrine release from isolated atria. The response to externally applied norepinephrine remained intact, consistent with an action on sympathetic neurotransmission rather than reduced responsiveness of the heart. An NPR-B antagonist prevented the reduction in norepinephrine release; CNP also reduced calcium signaling in stellate neurons. (PMID: 27496871)

In the same study, rats with neuron-specific dominant-negative NPR-B had higher heart rates and blood pressure, and heart-rate variability suggested a shift toward sympathoexcitation. A sympatholytic treatment reduced these measures. These results support a role for neuronal CNP–NPR-B signaling in restraining sympathetic drive.

**Qualification:** The evidence is principally from animal experiments, including isolated-tissue and in-vitro tests. It supports inhibition of cardiac sympathetic neurotransmission and modulation of sympathetic autonomic tone, but does not establish that CNP–NPR-B broadly suppresses every component of autonomic function in humans.
