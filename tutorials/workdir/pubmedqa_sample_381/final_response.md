Yes—but the association appears modest, and the evidence does not establish that moderate activity alone causes clinically significant remodeling.

In a study of 715 healthy adults, those with higher accelerometer-measured moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) had higher left-ventricular mass index and left-atrial volume index than those with lower MVPA. Cardiac function did not differ between groups (PMID: 37993041). Other studies also report structural differences across activity levels, but they are observational and do not isolate the effect of moderate-intensity activity from vigorous activity or total exercise volume.

Overall, routine moderate activity may be associated with small physiological changes in cardiac structure. The available evidence is limited on whether moderate activity itself causes these changes, and it should not be conflated with the more pronounced remodeling reported in high-volume endurance athletes.

FINAL DECISION: YES
