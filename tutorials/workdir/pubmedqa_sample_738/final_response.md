Yes. Direct evidence indicates that vasoactive agents can modulate monocyte migration across glomerular endothelial cells, although the evidence base is limited.

- **Histamine increased transmigration** across cultured glomerular endothelial layers. At \(10^{-8}\) M, migration increased from **1,012 ± 37 to 1,711 ± 163 cpm/well** (\(P<0.001\)); it also increased migration in Boyden-chamber assays. Cimetidine reduced this effect, implicating an **H₂-receptor-dependent mechanism**.
- **PGE₂ (dinoprostone) inhibited migration** dose-dependently, including basal, histamine-stimulated, and IL-1β-stimulated migration.
- **Dibutyryl-cAMP** also inhibited basal and stimulated migration, supporting involvement of cAMP signaling.
- **Angiotensin II and endothelin-1** did not significantly alter migration in the tested assays.

These findings come primarily from one direct in-vitro study, **PMID:10736970**, using cultured glomerular endothelial layers and monocyte/monocyte-derived macrophage migration assays. The study demonstrates modulation but does not fully establish whether the effects result from altered endothelial adhesion molecules, barrier permeability, chemokine production, or direct changes in leukocyte motility. Evidence for other vasoactive agents, such as nitric oxide donors, bradykinin, vasopressin, and catecholamines, was not identified in the direct glomerular-endothelial model.

FINAL DECISION: YES
