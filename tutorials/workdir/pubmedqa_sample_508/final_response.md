The evidence **favors an EPO-independent contribution**, particularly when prolonged rupture is accompanied by intra-amniotic inflammation or chorioamnionitis—but it does not prove that NRBC elevation is never EPO-driven.

- In the most direct PROM study, cord-blood EPO was **not associated with duration of membrane rupture**. The investigators proposed cytokine-mediated erythropoiesis, but the cohort was small (40 mother–infant pairs), and the reported abstract does not provide enough NRBC-by-duration data to establish the mechanism.
- In a preterm cohort, NRBC counts were associated with cord IL-6 and early-onset sepsis, but not with EPO after adjustment. In another preterm cohort including PPROM, histologic chorioamnionitis was associated with increased NRBCs; that study could not distinguish direct inflammatory effects from EPO mediation.
- EPO can still contribute when fetal hypoxia or placental vascular pathology is present. Therefore, these findings do not exclude an EPO-mediated component in an individual pregnancy.

**Conclusion:** It is reasonable to say prolonged rupture-associated NRBC elevation appears *more consistent with an inflammatory, potentially EPO-independent response* than with a rise in EPO caused simply by longer rupture duration. The evidence is limited, and “not EPO-driven” is too categorical.

FINAL DECISION: YES
