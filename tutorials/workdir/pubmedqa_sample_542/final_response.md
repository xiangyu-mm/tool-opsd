The evidence supports **yes**, with an important qualification.

The direct study by Slipicevic et al. (2014; PMID: 25093290) evaluated advanced FIGO III–IV ovarian serous carcinoma effusions. WEE1 nuclear protein was assessed by immunohistochemistry in 287 effusions, including 109 post-chemotherapy recurrence effusions.

Key findings in the post-chemotherapy subgroup:

- High WEE1 expression was defined as >25% positive tumor cells.
- High WEE1 was associated with poorer overall survival on univariate analysis (**p = 0.004**).
- WEE1 remained independently associated with poor survival in multivariable Cox analysis adjusted for clinicopathologic variables (**p = 0.003**).
- WEE1 expression was higher in post-chemotherapy recurrence effusions than in pretreatment effusions (**p = 0.002**).

Thus, the study supports WEE1 as a **novel independent prognostic marker of poor survival in post-chemotherapy ovarian serous carcinoma effusions**. However, the conclusion is based primarily on one primary cohort; an exact hazard ratio was not available in the accessible record, and no independent validation cohort specifically examining this setting was identified. The result should therefore be considered promising but not definitively established across broader ovarian carcinoma populations.

FINAL DECISION: YES
