Yes—available observational evidence supports an association between obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and increased colorectal neoplasia risk:

- A 2022 meta-analysis reported increased colorectal cancer incidence among people with OSA: **HR 1.70 (95% CI 1.48–1.96)** across four adjusted studies (PMID: **36636076**).
- Another 2022 meta-analysis reported increased risk of **combined colorectal neoplasms**—colorectal cancer and adenoma—among people with sleep apnea: **RR 1.75 (95% CI 1.56–1.97)** (PMID: **36092341**).
- However, a large Taiwanese cohort found a non-significant colorectal cancer association: **adjusted HR 1.17 (95% CI 0.82–1.68)** (PMID: **37835421**).
- A separate, adenoma-only increased risk has not been clearly established from the retrieved evidence, and these observational findings do not prove causation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
