Yes—**STin2 VNTR in the serotonin-transporter gene SLC6A4** was reported to be associated with comorbid tobacco-use and mood disorders.

- **Primary study:** PMID **25451436**
- **Design:** Four groups—never-smokers (n=113), mood disorder without tobacco-use disorder (n=62), tobacco-use disorder without mood disorder (n=90), and comorbid patients (n=95).
- **Finding:**  
  - **STin2.12 allele:** positively associated with comorbidity; OR **3.07** (95% CI 1.41–6.68).  
  - **STin2.10/10 genotype:** negatively associated/protective; OR **0.34** (95% CI 0.16–0.74).  
  - Associations persisted after adjustment for demographic and socioeconomic variables.
- Mood disorders included **major depression and bipolar disorder**.

A related case-control study also found STin2.12 associated with tobacco-use disorder alone, while STin2.10/10 was protective (PMID **24968820**). However, the evidence appears limited, and the association should not be interpreted as proof of causation.

Importantly, **STin2 refers to the SLC6A4 serotonin transporter VNTR**, not an SLC6A2/norepinephrine-transporter variant.  

FINAL DECISION: YES
