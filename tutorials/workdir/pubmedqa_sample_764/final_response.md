The relevant study is a population-based case-control study from Northern Ireland (PMID: **10729388**), including **259 myocardial infarction cases** and **259 population-based controls**.

- CagA-positive *H. pylori* infection was initially more common among cases than controls: **56.4% vs 47.9%**.
- The crude odds ratio was **1.41 (95% CI 1.00–1.99)**.
- After adjustment for age, sex, number of siblings, smoking, and socioeconomic factors, the association was substantially reduced and became non-significant: **adjusted OR 1.16 (95% CI 0.79–1.70)**.
- The authors concluded that infection with virulent CagA-positive strains, like *H. pylori* infection overall, **was not associated with myocardial infarction**.

Thus, this study does not support an independent association between virulent *H. pylori* strains and ischaemic heart disease/myocardial infarction.

FINAL DECISION: NO
