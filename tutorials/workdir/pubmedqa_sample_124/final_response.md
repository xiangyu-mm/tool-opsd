The available evidence supports the proposition.

- **Primary study:** PMID **25452390** reported that glioma-derived versican, particularly V0/V1 isoforms, promotes tumor expansion.
- Silencing **VCAN** in glioma cells reduced microglial **MT1-MMP**, decreased intracerebral tumor growth, and prolonged survival in mice.
- In organotypic brain slices, the growth-promoting effect required the presence of microglia.
- Recombinant VCAN V1 induced MT1-MMP in wild-type microglia but not in **Tlr2-knockout** microglia.
- A TLR2-neutralizing antibody reduced glioma-induced MT1-MMP and ex vivo tumor growth.
- A complementary study, PMID **24014382**, showed that TLR2 deficiency itself reduces glioma growth and MT1-MMP expression.

Thus, the supported mechanism is:

**Glioma-derived VCAN → TLR2 signaling in glioma-associated microglia/brain macrophages → MT1-MMP induction → extracellular-matrix remodeling and glioma expansion.**

The main limitations are that VCAN-specific causality is concentrated in one preclinical study, most functional data are from mouse and ex vivo models, and direct validation in human glioma-associated macrophages remains limited. Therefore, the evidence supports the claim as a contributory mechanism, but not necessarily as the sole or clinically dominant pathway.

FINAL DECISION: YES
