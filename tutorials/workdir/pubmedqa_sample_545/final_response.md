## Conclusion

**Yes—probably for intraoperative sentinel-node localization and detection, but not yet proven to improve long-term clinical outcomes.**

The relevant evidence concerns **freehand SPECT (fhSPECT)**, an intraoperative 3-D imaging/navigation system:

- **Prospective feasibility study, 23 oral-cancer patients:** fhSPECT mapped **98%** of sentinel nodes identified preoperatively, including difficult floor-of-mouth tumors; final nodal-stage accuracy was **100%**. However, all patients also underwent elective neck dissection, and there was no randomized comparator (PMID: **25077931**).
- **66 sentinel-node procedures:** fhSPECT detected **94%** of sentinel nodes and was considered helpful in **24%** of procedures. Missed nodes were disproportionately in level I and near the injection site, indicating that the technique does not eliminate the shine-through problem (PMID: **25524387**).
- **Blinded comparison in 50 cT1–T2 N0 oral-cancer patients:** the observed false-negative rates were **26.3%** with lymphoscintigraphy, **15.8%** with SPECT/CT, and **5.3%** with intraoperative fhSPECT (PMID: **30236825**). All methods still missed some positive nodes.

### Interpretation

The studies suggest that intraoperative 3-D imaging can:

- Improve three-dimensional localization of sentinel nodes
- Help identify nodes that are difficult to localize with a gamma probe alone
- Potentially reduce false-negative biopsy results
- Facilitate sentinel-node surgery in anatomically challenging sites

But the evidence is limited by small sample sizes, nonrandomized designs, heterogeneous comparisons, and lack of demonstrated effects on survival, recurrence, complications, quality of life, or need for neck dissection. Therefore, it is best viewed as a **promising adjunct that improves technical performance**, rather than a definitively proven improvement in overall patient outcomes.

FINAL DECISION: YES
