**Probably not.** H3F3A glycine-34 mutations are reported in about **96% of giant cell tumour of bone (GCTB)** in a 412-bone-tumour study (PMID: 27499898). In contrast, a study of **58 sporadic giant cell lesions of the jaw** reported gain-of-function mutations in **KRAS, FGFR1, or TRPV4** in 42 cases (PMID: 30385747). This supports distinct molecular profiles rather than a shared, highly prevalent H3F3A alteration.

**Caveat:** The search results available here did not expose the jaw study’s H3F3A-specific testing denominator or explicit negative result. So the evidence supports “not shared as a common hallmark,” but does not let me claim that H3F3A mutations never occur in sporadic jaw lesions.
