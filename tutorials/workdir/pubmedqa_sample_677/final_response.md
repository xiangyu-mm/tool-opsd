### Findings

The study is:

- **Gladstone BP, Vach W.** “Choice of non-inferiority (NI) margins does not protect against degradation of treatment effects on an average—an observational study of registered and published NI trials.”  
- **PLOS ONE. 2014;9(7):e103616.**
- **PMID:** 25080093  
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pone.0103616  
- **Full text:** PMCID PMC4117500

The investigators assembled NI trials reflecting current practice:

- 112 trials with binary outcomes  
- 38 trials with continuous outcomes  
- 24 trials with survival outcomes  

For each trial, they translated the selected NI margin into a simulated **conditional probability that the new treatment was actually harmful given that the trial declared it non-inferior**. This directly operationalized the risk of degradation.

Key results:

- Overall median modeled probability of degradation: **56%**
- Interquartile range: **45%–62%**
- Only approximately **two-fifths** of trials had a degradation probability below 50%
- Therefore, in most examined trials, the selected margin did not provide even better-than-even protection against declaring a potentially harmful treatment non-inferior.

The authors concluded that prevailing NI-margin choices **did not sufficiently protect against average degradation** and proposed an additional “third hurdle” for margin selection, beyond conventional considerations of assay sensitivity and preservation of a fraction of the active-control effect.

This is an observational, simulation-based assessment rather than a randomized comparison of margin-setting strategies, so it demonstrates inadequate protection under the study’s assumptions—not that every individual NI trial was invalid. Nevertheless, the study’s answer to the stated question is explicit and supported by its quantitative results.
