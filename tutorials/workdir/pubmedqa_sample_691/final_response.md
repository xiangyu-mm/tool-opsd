**Yes.** The primary study by Lee et al. reports that RRP1B modulates metastasis-associated transcription through chromatin regulation. In MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells and HeLa cells, RRP1B occupied chromatin regions associated with transcriptional repression; at repressed loci it co-occupied sites with TRIM28/KAP1 and CBX5/HP1α, and those sites showed increased H3K9me3. RRP1B upregulation also induced global changes in histone methylation. The authors describe RRP1B upregulation as associated with metastasis suppression.

This supports a role for RRP1B in regulating metastasis through histone methylation and heterochromatinization. It does **not** establish that RRP1B itself is a histone methyltransferase.  
**Source:** Lee et al., *Molecular Cancer Research* (2014), PMID: 25092915; DOI: 10.1158/1541-7786.MCR-14-0167.

FINAL DECISION: YES
