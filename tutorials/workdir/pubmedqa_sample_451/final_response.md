Yes. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) studies of biliary tract cancer have identified associations between tumor somatic alterations and chemotherapy outcomes, particularly for gemcitabine–cisplatin or other gemcitabine–platinum regimens.

- **ARID1A alterations:** In a 2023 targeted-NGS study of 193 advanced biliary tract cancers, ARID1A alteration independently predicted primary resistance/progression during first-line gemcitabine–cisplatin (OR 3.12, *p* = 0.046). Functional experiments also supported increased cisplatin resistance in ARID1A-mutant bile-duct cancer models (PMID:37139666).
- **CDKN2A, TP53, and ARID1A:** An earlier NGS survey of 183 patients found that combined alteration patterns—especially CDKN2A plus TP53 mutations and ARID1A mutations—were associated with poor progression-free and overall survival during gemcitabine–platinum treatment (PMID:27495988). However, no single gene was independently validated as a predictive marker.
- Other NGS findings, including **KRAS, ERBB2, BRAF, FGFR2, IDH1, and TP53**, have mainly indicated prognosis or potential targeted-treatment options rather than established cytotoxic chemotherapy resistance.
- The evidence is retrospective and not yet sufficient to use these alterations routinely to select or withhold chemotherapy. Gallbladder-specific resistance associations remain especially limited.

Thus, NGS surveys do reveal a clinically relevant association—most convincingly involving **ARID1A and gemcitabine–cisplatin resistance**—but the finding remains investigational rather than a fully validated predictive biomarker.

FINAL DECISION: YES
