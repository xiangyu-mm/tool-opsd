Yes. The strongest direct evidence is a post hoc analysis of the 96-week PIVENS trial involving 222 adults with biopsy-confirmed NASH and paired liver biopsies (PMID: 25429853):

- **HDL-C:** increased by 2.9 mg/dL in participants whose NASH resolved, versus a 2.5 mg/dL decrease without resolution (**P < 0.001**).
- **Triglycerides:** decreased by 21.1 mg/dL with NASH resolution, versus 2.3 mg/dL without resolution (**P = 0.03**).
- **LDL-C and non-HDL-C:** decreased over time in both groups, but there were **no significant resolution-specific differences**.

Thus, the proposed pattern is supported: NASH resolution was associated with improved HDL-C and triglycerides, but not with additional improvement in LDL-C or non-HDL-C. However, this conclusion is based mainly on one direct resolution-stratified analysis; broader NASH treatment trials show heterogeneous, treatment-specific lipid effects and do not establish this as a universal rule.

FINAL DECISION: YES
