### Evidence summary

Yes—**subsensory vibratory-noise insoles appear to improve some short-term balance and gait-related outcomes in healthy older adults**, but the evidence is limited.

- **Primary study:** A randomized, single-blind crossover study of 12 healthy adults aged 65–90 years used piezoelectric insoles delivering stochastic-resonance noise at 70% or 85% of each participant’s sensory threshold (PMID:25450133).
- **Balance:** Both stimulation levels significantly reduced postural-sway area.
- **Mobility/gait:** Timed Up and Go performance improved, and temporal walking variability decreased.
- **Supporting study:** A related analysis involving 12 healthy elderly participants found significant reductions in all eight measured quiet-standing sway parameters with subsensory foot noise (PMID:16287079).

### Limitations

The evidence is based primarily on a very small sample and closely related studies. Benefits were measured during or shortly after stimulation; there is no convincing evidence for lasting effects after removing the insoles, improved gait speed across populations, or prevention of actual falls. Therefore, the conclusion applies mainly to **acute laboratory measures of balance and selected gait/mobility outcomes**, not established long-term clinical benefit.

FINAL DECISION: YES
