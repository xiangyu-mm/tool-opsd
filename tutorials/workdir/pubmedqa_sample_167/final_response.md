Yes. **18F-FDG PET/CT is used to assess treatment response in sarcoidosis**, particularly when disease activity is uncertain, multisystem disease is present, or cardiac sarcoidosis is being monitored.

### Evidence

- In a 30-patient cohort with active chronic sarcoidosis, median SUVmax decreased from **8.46 to 4.90** after approximately 12 months of treatment. PET changes agreed with patient-reported clinical status, supporting its use as an adjunct to treatment monitoring (source: `call_O7qvz3vwLToFg2Me7iATgc2A`).
- In a prospective 27-patient corticosteroid study, PET responders had fewer relapses than nonresponders (**14.2% vs 61.5%, p=0.018**). However, PET response did not perfectly correspond to clinical remission (source: `call_fjOSQNksNY37YLqF3XQ9lO1u`).
- A retrospective 23-patient study found that reduced SUVmax after glucocorticoid therapy was associated with higher clinical remission and lower recurrence, although the study was small and heterogeneous (source: `call_jJRxs95zatX2HjMHFNd9CeZ9`).
- Serial PET/CT is particularly useful in **cardiac sarcoidosis**, where it can demonstrate suppression or recurrence of myocardial inflammation during immunosuppression. However, links to major clinical outcomes remain uncertain, and evidence is observational (sources: `call_xCcGZqcD3HTN8sWNT8cYFGDn`, `call_eG0pEsuzsICVu5Bl3RJbhM2r`).
- A systematic review concluded that FDG-PET/PET-CT can assess inflammatory activity and monitor treatment response, but emphasized the need for stronger evidence. Recent imaging guidance likewise describes treatment-response monitoring as useful but insufficiently standardized (sources: `call_TH7c5t5cDdiztXdlydbL9sJq`, `call_oNQNg7ERCCSXyS5Zs3EW7Vtt`).

### Important limitations

PET/CT should not be used as a standalone measure of treatment success. Its limitations include:

- Small, mostly observational studies
- No universally accepted PET treatment-response threshold
- Imperfect agreement between metabolic response, symptoms, lung function, and organ recovery
- FDG uptake that is not specific for sarcoidosis
- Radiation exposure and cost
- Technical challenges with physiologic myocardial uptake in cardiac studies

Therefore, PET/CT is best interpreted alongside symptoms, physical findings, pulmonary function tests, laboratory data, cardiac MRI/echocardiography, and organ-specific assessments. It is most useful when the result is likely to change treatment decisions.
