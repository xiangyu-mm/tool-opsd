Yes. **AKAP9 (aKAP9/Yotiao) is supported as a genetic modifier of congenital LQT1**, based on a primary human study:

- **Chorley et al., 2014, PMID: 25087618** studied a South African founder cohort in which 168 individuals carried the same **KCNQ1 p.Ala341Val** mutation.
- Several common **AKAP9 variants/haplotypes** were associated with differences in QTc, cardiac-event risk, and disease severity among KCNQ1 carriers:
  - rs11772585 T/TACT: **218% higher cardiac-event risk**
  - rs7808587 GG: **74% higher risk**
  - rs2282972 T/CATT: **53% lower risk**
  - rs2961024 GG/CGCG: age-dependent QTc effects
- Because the primary KCNQ1 mutation was effectively held constant, these findings support a modifier effect rather than merely an association with LQTS susceptibility.

This should be distinguished from **LQT11**, in which pathogenic AKAP9 variants independently cause long-QT syndrome by disrupting AKAP9–KCNQ1 channel regulation. The modifier evidence is clinically meaningful but is based primarily on one founder-population study, with limited evidence of broad independent replication.

FINAL DECISION: YES
