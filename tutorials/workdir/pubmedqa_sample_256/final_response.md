**Yes—glycemic variability was associated with higher estimated 10-year cardiovascular risk** in the study. It included 240 people with type 2 diabetes, HbA1c ≤7.0%, and no prior cardiovascular disease. Over 72 hours of CGM, standard deviation of blood glucose (SDBG) and mean amplitude of glycemic excursions (MAGE) rose across Framingham risk categories (p<0.01). MAGE independently predicted membership in the high estimated-risk category (OR 1.623, 95% CI 1.198–2.316; p<0.001).

**Important qualification:** This was an association with *Framingham-predicted* risk, not evidence that CGM variability predicted cardiovascular events observed over the subsequent 10 years.  
Source: Tang et al., 2016, *Clinica Chimica Acta*. PMID: 27502250.
