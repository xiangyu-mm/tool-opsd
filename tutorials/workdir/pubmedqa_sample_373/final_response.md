No. This case report provides direct evidence that negative serology does **not** exclude active infectious syphilis.

- **Report:** PMID 27495107; DOI: 10.1097/MD.0000000000004520.
- The patient had a history of latent syphilis and cerebral infarction.
- Both **nontreponemal testing** and **specific antitreponemal IgM** were negative.
- Nevertheless, cerebrospinal fluid transmitted infection to a seronegative New Zealand rabbit; motile *Treponema pallidum* was detected and genetically confirmed as strain type 14d/f.
- Benzathine penicillin was ineffective, while aqueous crystalline penicillin G—appropriate for neurosyphilis—was associated with disease regression.

Thus, in this patient, negative nontreponemal and antitreponemal IgM results failed to exclude active, infectious CNS syphilis. The evidence is a single case report and does not establish how frequently this occurs, but it demonstrates that clinical suspicion and additional evaluation remain necessary when neurologic or other compatible findings are present.

FINAL DECISION: NO
