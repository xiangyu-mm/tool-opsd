**Conclusion: Yes.** Evidence supports an intrinsic circadian clock in the intervertebral disc that is sensitive to age and at least some cytokines, and whose disruption is linked to degeneration.

- **Intrinsic clock:** Mouse disc explants showed sustained, approximately 24-hour PER2 rhythms for more than five days in both annulus fibrosus and nucleus pulposus; human disc cells also oscillated. The study additionally identified hundreds of rhythmic disc transcripts (PMID 27489225; source: `call_WBc0I1o52RG2WRcnMcFmcVa4`).
- **Age and cytokines:** Circadian amplitude declined with mouse age. IL‑1β abolished rhythms in the tested experiment, whereas TNF‑α did not—so the cytokine effect should not be generalized to every inflammatory mediator (PMID 27489225; source: `call_WBc0I1o52RG2WRcnMcFmcVa4`).
- **Degeneration:** Disc-cell-specific Bmal1 deletion caused age-related degeneration in mice; follow-up knockout work found disrupted rhythmic transcripts and age-related annulus-fibrosus matrix changes (PMIDs 27489225, 37495193; sources: `call_WBc0I1o52RG2WRcnMcFmcVa4`, `call_YyahZ8piyyR3Obv50kNQoW3D`). Degenerated human and rat nucleus pulposus tissue also showed reduced clock markers, while experimental clock disruption impaired matrix or inflammatory homeostasis (PMIDs 34934193, 35922862; sources: `call_9I1dd0xbImpSrzsXZ6lD2Hvz`, `call_Du8DhtPdqnOgUi6ZIOrRXEgW`).
