Yes—**for eligible tuberculosis patients, an integrated community TB-HIV adherence model provided a viable alternative to routine daily DOT in Cape Town**, although it was a de-intensified model rather than complete elimination of supervision.

The key Cape Town study (PMID: **27510244**) evaluated 70 TB clinics and compared:

- **Previous approach:** daily DOT  
- **Integrated model:** two weeks of DOT and treatment-literacy sessions, followed by self-administered TB treatment, weekly community-health-worker home visits, and HIV-care/ART support

Results were comparable:

- **TB treatment success:** 82.8% with daily DOT vs 82.5% with the integrated model  
- **Default:** no significant difference  
- **Mortality:** no significant difference  
- **ART initiation:** improved from 67% to 74% with the integrated model

A related South African study (PMID: **21276302**) similarly found no significant deterioration in TB cure or treatment success with lay-health-worker adherence support.

However, the evidence has limitations: the Cape Town study was retrospective and pre/post rather than randomized, and the intervention retained initial DOT. Therefore, it supports replacing **prolonged routine daily DOT** with structured community support in appropriately selected, stable patients—not abandoning all DOT or using the model universally. Patients at high risk of nonadherence or poor follow-up may still need more intensive observation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
