**Yes—with an important caveat.** Damage patterns at the head–stem taper junction can help researchers understand how material is lost, especially when morphology is combined with quantitative surface measurements and mechanical modeling.

Retrieval studies use visual damage scoring, maximum-depth measurements, and cross-sectional imaging to characterize taper damage. One matched study of 120 head–stem pairs found no significant difference in fretting-corrosion scores between microgrooved and smooth tapers (head *P* = .14; stem *P* = .35; PMID: 28111124). Modeling work has also compared predicted material removal under millions of simulated gait cycles with damage observed on retrieved components, supporting investigation of interacting mechanical and chemical processes (PMID: 36354540).

The caveat is that appearance alone cannot reliably determine how much loss came from fretting versus corrosion, establish a unique cause, or quantify total loss. Damage patterns are best treated as **mechanistic clues**, interpreted alongside metrology and modeling—not as proof by themselves.
