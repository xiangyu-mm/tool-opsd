## Evidence summary

- **Direct prospective evidence is negative.** In the most relevant study, two prospectively followed renal-transplant cohorts were evaluated (TDS, 68 patients; RITS, 22 patients). sCD30 levels did not distinguish patients who later developed rejection after tacrolimus discontinuation from non-rejectors, and the 3-month sCD30 level did not predict late acute rejection (>3 months).  
  **Source:** PMID:25446808; search evidence `call_jmKgZTZJlQRoNCjWKeay0W0y`.

- **Safe immunosuppression tapering was not supported.** The same study directly examined immunosuppression reduction/discontinuation and found no association between sCD30 and rejection risk. Thus, sCD30 could not identify patients in whom tacrolimus could be safely tapered or withdrawn.  
  **Source:** PMID:25446808; `call_jmKgZTZJlQRoNCjWKeay0W0y`.

- **Some weaker studies suggest associations, but not validated prediction.** A small cohort of 45 recipients found that failure of sCD30 to decline by day 4 was associated with both early and late rejection over 5 years. However, it lacked validated discrimination measures, adjusted estimates, and external validation.  
  **Source:** PMID:28694560; `call_1eY3QYqVBLEU7piQKMSXDSo2`.

- **Other evidence concerns different outcomes or composite markers.** A multianalyte signature containing sCD30 was associated with long-term graft loss, but this does not establish sCD30 as an independent predictor. A meta-analysis found associations mainly with **early** acute rejection and emphasized heterogeneity across studies.  
  **Sources:** PMID:25478957, PMID:32256486; `call_zP9ZrlmEN68ONy77acTmQwCF`, `call_X7IXf8CMzUZR8bj6CuOwA2bn`.

## Conclusion

Soluble CD30 may reflect immune activation and may show inconsistent associations with early rejection or long-term graft outcomes. However, the strongest directly relevant prospective evidence does **not** support sCD30 as a reliable predictor of late acute rejection, and no evidence demonstrates that it can safely guide immunosuppression tapering or withdrawal. It should not be used alone for that clinical decision.
