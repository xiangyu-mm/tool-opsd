Yes. The 2015 goat study directly reported that as AF became persistent, fractionated electrograms co-localized with areas of localized slow conduction or block and were associated with connexin disorganization—greater Cx40 heterogeneity and Cx43 lateralization—without a detectable change in connective-tissue content (fibrosis). Fractionation increased over three 4-week AF periods, reaching 17% during pacing and 23% during AF, while connective-tissue content and net conduction velocity did not change.

This supports the proposed fibrosis-independent mechanism in the goat model. “Caused by” is the authors’ conclusion, but the findings establish co-localization and association rather than definitive experimental proof that connexin changes alone cause fractionation.

**Source:** PMID 25444850, *Heart Rhythm* (2015), “Fractionation of electrograms is caused by colocalized conduction block and connexin disorganization in the absence of fibrosis as AF becomes persistent in the goat model.”

FINAL DECISION: YES
