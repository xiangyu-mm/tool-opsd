Yes. The relevant longitudinal study was Erlandson et al. (PLOS ONE, 2014; PMID: 25436612), conducted in the Multicenter AIDS Cohort Study.

- Participants included 270 HIV-positive men and 247 HIV-negative men, followed for at least 5 years, with a median follow-up of approximately 7.5 years.
- Participants reported changes in regional body fat involving the face, legs, and abdomen.
- Health-related quality of life was assessed using the SF-36 Physical Component Summary (PCS) and Mental Component Summary (MCS).
- Self-reported body-fat change was associated with longitudinal decline in PCS.
- This association remained statistically significant after adjustment for demographic factors and comorbidities, including conditions such as dyslipidemia, depression, hypertension, and diabetes.
- The association was specific to physical quality of life; body-fat change was not significantly associated with MCS.
- The study therefore supports self-reported body-fat change as a **marker** of declining physical health-related quality of life with aging, independent of comorbidity.

The evidence supports an association, not proof that body-fat change directly causes physical quality-of-life decline. The available report also does not provide precise regression coefficients or confidence intervals.

FINAL DECISION: YES
