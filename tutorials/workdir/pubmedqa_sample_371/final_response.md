Yes. The available preclinical evidence supports that oral L-citrulline supplementation protects female mice against diet-induced NAFLD.

- In female C57BL/6J mice receiving a Western-style diet, oral L-citrulline at **2.5 g/kg body weight for 6 weeks** reduced hepatic triglycerides, hepatic neutrophil accumulation, and PAI-1 protein compared with the diet alone (PMID: **27496089**; source: `call_q5bs7fW9UgSylMTAbwklHJ3Z`).
- A second study found that the same oral dose attenuated progression of early diet-induced NAFLD/NASH after disease induction; this benefit was lost with intestinal arginase inhibition, supporting a mechanism-dependent effect (PMID: **33550112**; sources: `call_mA7Nbrpazk8l2A1nLAc6XjQU`, `call_A9tymjD4rc1EKuj44daHHl3S`).

Thus, the evidence supports protection against steatosis and inflammatory liver injury in female mice. However, it does not clearly establish prevention of hepatic fibrosis, and these findings cannot be assumed to apply directly to humans.

FINAL DECISION: YES
