Yes—available evidence supports that **STAT3 activation in hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) functionally cross-talks with and amplifies TGF-β1 signaling**, promoting liver injury-associated fibrosis.

Key evidence:

- In chronic hepatitis B tissue, DEN-fibrotic rats, and HSC experiments, STAT3 activation increased TGF-β1 expression. STAT3 knockdown or JAK2 inhibition reduced TGF-β1 and weakened TGF-β1-induced profibrogenic and anti-apoptotic effects (**PMID:25092172**).
- HSC-specific STAT3 deletion reduced fibrosis in bile-duct-ligation and CCl₄ models. STAT3 directly promoted type-I collagen production, although this study did not prove a direct STAT3–TGF-β1 molecular interaction (**PMID:22687286**).
- Pharmacologic STAT3 inhibition reduced HSC activation markers, including α-SMA, Col1a1/collagen I, and TIMP1, and attenuated TAA- and CCl₄-induced fibrosis. It also reduced TGF-β1-associated HSC activation (**PMID:30935693**).
- S3I-201 reduced TGF-β1/TGFβRII expression and TGF-β1-induced Smad2/3 phosphorylation, supporting functional cooperation between STAT3 and canonical TGF-β/Smad signaling (**PMID:30206312**).

The most appropriate interpretation is **functional pathway cooperation or positive feedback**, rather than established physical “crosslinking” between STAT3 and TGF-β1. STAT3 activation enhances HSC survival, myofibroblast activation, collagen production, and extracellular-matrix accumulation. These effects can exacerbate chronic liver injury and fibrosis.

Important qualification: STAT3 is cell-type and context dependent. HSC STAT3 appears predominantly profibrotic in these models, whereas STAT3 in hepatocytes may have regenerative or protective effects in some settings. Therefore, the conclusion applies specifically to **STAT3 activation in HSCs**, not necessarily to global hepatic STAT3 activation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
