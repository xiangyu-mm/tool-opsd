**Conclusion:** Yes—alveolar macrophages can causally promote progression of EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma in experimental models. Depleting them reduced tumor burden, and a separate study found that tumor-associated alveolar macrophages can provide cholesterol that sustains tumor EGFR signaling and growth. In human tumors, an activated alveolar-macrophage signature was associated with poorer survival, but that is observational evidence. The findings support macrophages as contributors, not necessarily the sole driver or a proven universal dependency in patients.

**Key studies**
- [PMID 27496865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27496865/): In a mutant-EGFR mouse model, alveolar-macrophage expansion tracked tumor progression, and macrophage depletion reduced tumor burden. The human survival analysis was an association.
- [PMID 32125091](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32125091/): In an inducible EGFR-mutant mouse model, targeting alveolar macrophages reduced tumor growth more than targeting interstitial macrophages; CD8 T-cell infiltration also increased.
- [PMID 37131637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37131637/): Tumor-derived GM-CSF and macrophage PPARγ support cholesterol transfer to tumor cells, helping sustain EGFR phosphorylation and tumor growth in experimental models.

FINAL DECISION: YES
