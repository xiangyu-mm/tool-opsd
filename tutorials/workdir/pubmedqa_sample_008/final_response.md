The available clinical evidence supports a protective association, but not definitive independent prognostic validity.

- In Gaya et al. (PMID:25444981), 134 patients with clinically high-grade T1 bladder cancer were assessed by immunohistochemistry.
- ΔNp63 was lost in 90/134 tumors (67.2%).
- Progression to muscle-invasive disease occurred in **21.1% of ΔNp63-loss tumors versus 0% of tumors retaining ΔNp63** (*p*<0.001).
- The cohort had substantial follow-up (median 62.1 months), and most patients underwent repeat TURBT and BCG-based management.
- The result concerns **progression**, not merely recurrence, and is consistent with retained ΔNp63 being favorable.
- However, the evidence is limited principally to one cohort. The available report does not provide a multivariable-adjusted hazard ratio, nor separate analyses for cystectomy, metastasis, or cancer-specific mortality. Thus, it does not establish that ΔNp63 is independently protective after adjustment for all standard prognostic factors.
- This finding should not be generalized automatically to total p63 or TP63 expression.

Overall, retained ΔNp63 expression appears to be a protective prognostic marker for progression in clinical high-grade T1 bladder cancer, while confirmation in larger, independently validated, multivariable-adjusted cohorts is needed.
