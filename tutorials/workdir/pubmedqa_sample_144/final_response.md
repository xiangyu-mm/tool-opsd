Yes. The relevant common polymorphism is **MSLN rs1057147 (c.*69G>A)** in the gene’s 3′ untranslated region.

Evidence indicates that:

- **miR-611 represses the G allele preferentially:** in reporter assays, miR-611 reduced activity of the MSLN 3′-UTR construct carrying **G**, but not the **A** allele. This supports an allele-dependent miR-611 binding/regulatory effect, although the study described it as “most likely” affecting the binding site rather than directly demonstrating physical binding.
- **Soluble mesothelin-related peptide (SMRP) differs by genotype:** in a validation cohort of 759 subjects without mesothelioma, **GG individuals had the lowest SMRP levels**, whereas **A-allele carriers (GA/AA) had higher levels** (PMID: **25436799**).
- The original study also found an association between rs1057147 G>A and SMRP among **59 healthy asbestos-exposed individuals** (PMID: **19858537**).
- A later asbestos-exposed cohort replicated the direction of effect among participants without mesothelioma: heterozygotes and two-variant-allele carriers had higher serum SMRP than wild-type individuals (p<0.001; PMID: **32187018**).

Thus, the **G allele is associated with stronger miR-611-mediated repression and lower soluble mesothelin**, while the **A allele is associated with weaker repression and higher soluble mesothelin**. The published abstracts do not provide complete genotype-specific numerical SMRP values, and some replication cohorts were asbestos-exposed rather than entirely unselected healthy populations.

FINAL DECISION: YES
