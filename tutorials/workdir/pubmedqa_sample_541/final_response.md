Yes. Direct evidence indicates that flavones and flavonoid-rich fractions from *Erythrina falcata* modulate fear memory in rats.

- **de Oliveira et al. (PMID: 25096710)** tested *E. falcata* stem-bark extracts, fractions, and purified **vitexin, isovitexin, and diosmetin-6-C-glucoside** using one-trial step-down inhibitory avoidance. These preparations enhanced acquisition/retention of conditioned fear memory. Some fractions also increased resistance to extinction, while the purified compounds eventually underwent extinction rather than permanently blocking it.  
  Sources: `call_6g3Yr6V4Jvt4MP68R2SAsaIL`, `call_XQMfm1aOMcw9iJ4QOOeKRJGC`

- A follow-up study (PMID: **26778988**) tested the flavonoid-rich **FfB fraction** in conditioned-suppression paradigms. FfB influenced fear-memory acquisition and spontaneous recovery, with pharmacological evidence implicating GABA-A, NMDA/GluN2B, 5-HT1A, and ERK-related hippocampal mechanisms.  
  Sources: `call_ZddOwYNFUHyyRz4ihsBoxuKo`, `call_4vGoXiNWWg37ZCYXWzD7ahOz`

Thus, the evidence concerns genuine aversive-memory outcomes—acquisition, retention/retrieval, extinction, and spontaneous recovery—not merely general anxiety or nonspecific cognition. However, the studies do **not** establish that erythrinin/erythrinine specifically is responsible, and the available records did not provide reliable dose details.

FINAL DECISION: YES
