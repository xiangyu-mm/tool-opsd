The most likely diagnosis is **squamous-cell carcinoma of the lung (B)** causing **PTH-related protein (PTHrP)-mediated humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy**.

- Calcium is markedly elevated, while PTH is suppressed, indicating **PTH-independent hypercalcemia**.
- In a patient with a lung mass, the classic cause is tumor secretion of **PTHrP**.
- PTHrP-mediated hypercalcemia is most strongly associated with **squamous-cell carcinoma**, particularly in heavy smokers.
- The **central** location of the mass also favors squamous-cell carcinoma, whereas adenocarcinoma is more often peripheral.
- Hypercalcemia can cause the patient’s acute confusion and disorientation.

Small-cell carcinoma can present centrally but only rarely causes PTHrP-mediated hypercalcemia.

**FINAL ANSWER: B**
