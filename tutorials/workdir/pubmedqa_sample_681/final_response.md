Yes—**with an important qualification**.

The strongest directly relevant evidence is **PMID:37108353**, which used WGBS and transcriptome data from multiple goose breeds and developmental stages. It reported:

- **Promoter/TSS methylation:** generally inversely associated with transcription; earlier demethylation of the **MyoD1 promoter** accompanied earlier expression.
- **Gene-body methylation:** positively associated with expression, particularly toward regions near transcription termination sites.

This pattern is consistent with **complementary regulatory roles**: promoter methylation may influence transcription initiation, whereas gene-body methylation may be associated with active transcription or transcriptional elongation.

However, the result is primarily **correlational**, and the direction of these associations is context-dependent. For example, another WGBS study in Northern pike found no gene-body methylation–expression correlation and even observed a sex-specific positive association between promoter methylation and expression. Thus, WGBS can reveal complementary regional patterns, but it does not establish a universal mechanism or causality by itself.

FINAL DECISION: YES
