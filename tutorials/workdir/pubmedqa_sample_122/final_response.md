A directly relevant study (Jin et al., 2015; PMID: 25429008) tested field pea in phosphorus-deficient Vertisol under:

- Deficient vs. adequate phosphorus: 15 vs. 60 mg P kg⁻¹ soil
- Ambient vs. elevated CO₂: approximately 380–400 vs. 550–580 ppm
- Drought imposed at flowering

The study found that:

- **Adequate phosphorus** increased soluble sugars, leaf inorganic phosphate, and root distribution in deeper soil layers during drought.
- **Elevated CO₂** reduced stomatal conductance and transpiration, while increasing soluble sugars, relative leaf water content, and water-use efficiency.
- **Combined adequate phosphorus and elevated CO₂** produced the greatest water-use efficiency.
- The authors concluded that phosphorus and elevated CO₂ **interactively enhanced periodic drought tolerance**, through improved water conservation, deeper rooting, and maintenance of leaf phosphorus status.

The evidence directly supports enhanced **physiological drought tolerance and water relations**. However, the available evidence does not establish that the treatments increased biomass or seed yield.

FINAL DECISION: YES
