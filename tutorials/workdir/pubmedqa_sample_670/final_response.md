Yes. The strongest direct evidence is a 2014 survey of 246 Australian dentists (adjusted response rate 40.1%; PMID: 25091082):

- Only **3.7%** reported using a published dental-anxiety screening scale.
- Approximately **half** directly asked patients about dental anxiety.
- **56.5%** cited lack of awareness as the main reason for not using a published scale.
- Only about **one-third** reported undergraduate training in dental anxiety.
- **41.7%** rated that training as good or better.
- Nearly **37%** expressed interest in further training.

This indicates that, at least in 2014, informal questioning was much more common than validated screening, and a substantial training need existed. However, the evidence is self-reported, based on a modest response rate, and does not establish current Australian practice. The older 1991 Victorian study assessed anxiety-management strategies but did not measure formal screening or training needs.

FINAL DECISION: YES
