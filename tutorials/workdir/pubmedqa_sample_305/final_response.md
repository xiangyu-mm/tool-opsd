## Findings

The study **“Obesity Does Not Modify the Risk of Differentiated Thyroid Cancer in a Cytological Series of Thyroid Nodules”** (PMID: 27493887) evaluated **4,849 patients** undergoing fine-needle aspiration for thyroid nodules.

- BMI groups ranged from normal weight to grade-3 obesity.
- Suspicious or malignant cytology (Thy4/Thy5) occurred in:
  - Normal weight: **6.8%**
  - Overweight: **6.3%**
  - Grade-1 obesity: **6.3%**
  - Grade-2 obesity: **4.0%**
  - Grade-3 obesity: **4.2%**
- The differences were not statistically significant (**p = 0.29**).
- Sex-specific analyses were also nonsignificant.
- Among women with grade-2/3 obesity, suspicious or malignant cytology was actually less frequent (**OR 0.51, 95% CI 0.284–0.920; p = 0.009**).

Later thyroid-nodule studies similarly found no meaningful association between BMI/obesity and malignancy risk, although broader case-control studies of papillary thyroid cancer have reported positive associations between obesity and cancer risk. Those studies address general cancer susceptibility rather than obesity-related modification of risk among patients already selected for thyroid-nodule cytology.

A limitation is that the target study used **Thy4/Thy5 cytology as the principal endpoint**, rather than confirmed histologic DTC for every patient, and the available report does not provide a formal BMI interaction test. Nevertheless, the observed data and authors’ conclusion do not support obesity as a modifier of DTC risk in this cytological series.

FINAL DECISION: NO
