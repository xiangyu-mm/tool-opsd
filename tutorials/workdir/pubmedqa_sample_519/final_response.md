Yes. The primary study (PMID: 25088559) provides direct evidence that **Banderuola (Bnd) regulates asymmetric cell division in Drosophila**.

Key evidence:

- Bnd was identified in an in vivo live-imaging RNAi screen for defects in **Numb segregation** in sensory organ precursor cells.
- Bnd depletion disrupted **cell polarity, spindle orientation, and asymmetric protein localization** in sensory and neural precursor cells.
- Genetic and biochemical experiments indicated that Bnd physically and functionally interacts with **Discs-large (Dlg)** to establish opposing cortical domains.
- Combined Bnd and *dlg* knockdown strongly enhanced defects and caused extensive brain tumor formation, supporting a functional role in asymmetric division and tumor suppression.

The evidence establishes Bnd as an asymmetric-cell-division regulator, although the precise molecular steps downstream of its interaction with Dlg remain incompletely defined.

FINAL DECISION: YES
