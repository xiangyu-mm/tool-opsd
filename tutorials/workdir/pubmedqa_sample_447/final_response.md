## Conclusion

Yes—**estrogen-containing contraception is generally associated with higher total circulating 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] concentrations**, particularly among combined oral-contraceptive users. However, the association is modest, heterogeneous, and not consistently significant.

### Evidence

- In a winter study of 66 women, oral-contraceptive users had mean plasma 25(OH)D concentrations of **83 ± 40 nmol/L**, compared with **59 ± 22 nmol/L** among nonusers—approximately **39% higher after adjustment** for age and vitamin-D intake (PMID: 9627916; source: `call_5g8OoaMNdOZvHyUmFgFSzVkN`).
- Other observational studies found that oral-contraceptive use was independently associated with higher 25(OH)D or lower odds of hypovitaminosis D, including an odds ratio of **0.45** for hypovitaminosis D in one cohort (PMID: 25906790; sources: `call_fZcHFWXvCGPZSyk0yGVimmmO`, `call_TuU70hgJd8blT6YZpkBAeKgd`).
- A formulation-specific clinical study found smaller effects: total 25(OH)D increased approximately **4–7%** with estrogen-containing formulations, while levonorgestrel alone had no significant effect (source: `call_PcN9WxCy0PgtJFoZW6Bo3b6a`).
- The likely mechanism is an estrogen-related increase in **vitamin-D-binding protein**, which can raise measured total 25(OH)D. In one study, vitamin-D-binding protein increased **19.1%**, total 25(OH)D increased **4.4%**, but bioavailable 25(OH)D decreased **18.2%** (source: `call_PcN9WxCy0PgtJFoZW6Bo3b6a`).
- Results are not universal: some older and small studies found no significant difference, and one PCOS study observed a nonsignificant decline in 25(OH)D during estrogen-containing contraceptive treatment (sources: `call_CcakNKvLLzMGuKp1IXzeagLM`, `call_nf01IBhKUWhspWAjRUDXdZmo`, `call_K50rhJDVTPikaAX8FutGqqoX`).

Therefore, estrogen-containing contraception appears to increase **total measured 25(OH)D on average**, but this may partly reflect increased binding protein rather than an increase in biologically available vitamin D. The evidence should not be interpreted as proof that these contraceptives improve vitamin-D status in every user.
