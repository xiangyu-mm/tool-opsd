Yes—primarily based on preclinical evidence.

- In hypercholesterolemic pigs, a 10-day pomegranate-polyphenol diet prevented impaired coronary endothelium-dependent relaxation. The effect was associated with Akt/eNOS activation, preserved nitric-oxide signaling, and reduced oxidative stress (PMID: 25444379).
- Ex-vivo porcine coronary studies provide stronger mechanistic support: red-wine polyphenols, grape extracts/juice, and EGCG increased Akt and eNOS phosphorylation, NO/cGMP production, and coronary relaxation. PI3K/Akt inhibitors, NOS inhibitors, and antioxidant interventions reduced these effects (PMIDs: 15623569, 16962569, 19155632, 20117080).
- Human trials show improved peripheral endothelial function and NO bioavailability after flavanol-rich chocolate, but they did not directly assess coronary endothelial function or Akt/eNOS activation (PMIDs: 22173910, 29329924).

Thus, polyphenol-enriched diets can plausibly prevent or attenuate coronary endothelial dysfunction through a redox-sensitive PI3K/Akt/eNOS mechanism, but direct clinical confirmation in human coronary vessels remains lacking.

FINAL DECISION: YES
