The cited study was a 2-year randomized, placebo-controlled trial followed by a 2-year single-blind extension in 61 elderly adults with mild cognitive impairment (PMID: 25093483).

- Renal function was evaluated using abbreviated MDRD and CKD-EPI estimates.
- Across the 4-year period, neither measure showed a statistically significant decline:
  - aMDRD: **P = .453**
  - CKD-EPI: **P = .213**
- The abstract reported no specific signal for acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease progression, or other adverse renal events.
- The authors concluded that chronic low-dose lithium did not impair renal function in this cohort.
- However, the available abstract does not provide arm-specific creatinine/eGFR values, lithium concentrations, retention numbers, confidence intervals, or urinary concentrating-function results. The sample was also small, and adverse events overall were more common with lithium.

Thus, this particular trial does **not** support renal impairment from long-term, low-dose lithium in elderly participants, although it cannot exclude uncommon or longer-term kidney toxicity.

FINAL DECISION: NO
