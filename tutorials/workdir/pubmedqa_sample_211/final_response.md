**Yes.** Two months after surgery, creatinine clearance in sham-operated ApoE-deficient females was 140 ± 26 μL/min, similar to sham-operated controls (171 ± 17 μL/min). Ovariectomy reduced clearance in ApoE-deficient mice to 61 ± 10 μL/min—a decrease of about 56% (p<0.05). This supports a protective, delaying effect of endogenous ovarian hormones on renal functional decline in this model.

The finding does not identify estrogen as the sole protective hormone: ovariectomy removes ovarian hormones more broadly. Also, plasma urea was elevated in both ApoE-deficient groups, so the evidence for protection is specific to the reported functional measure rather than every renal outcome.
