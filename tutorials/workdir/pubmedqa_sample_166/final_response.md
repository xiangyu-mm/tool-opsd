Yes. The directly relevant prospective study by Bajaj et al. (2015; PMID: 25445772) evaluated 118 patients with cirrhosis without overt hepatic encephalopathy.

- Cognitive reserve was estimated using the Barona Index.
- HRQOL was measured with the Sickness Impact Profile.
- Approximately 49% had covert hepatic encephalopathy; mean MELD was about 9.
- In regression analyses adjusting for covert hepatic encephalopathy and MELD score, cognitive reserve remained significantly associated with:
  - Total HRQOL impairment: **P < .001**
  - Psychosocial impairment: **P < .001**
  - Physical impairment: **P < .03**

Higher cognitive reserve was associated with better HRQOL, independent of covert hepatic encephalopathy and liver disease severity. However, this was a single observational cohort, so the findings demonstrate an independent statistical association rather than causality; adjusted effect sizes and confidence intervals were not reported in the available abstract.

FINAL DECISION: YES
