Yes. The best direct evidence is a small human study (PMID:25443761) comparing high-intensity intermittent exercise at:

- **Terrestrial altitude:** 4090 m, after 6 days of acclimatization  
- **Simulated altitude:** acute normobaric hypoxia at FiO₂ 12% (approximately 4090-m equivalent)

Using lung-ultrasound B-lines as an indirect marker of pulmonary interstitial edema:

- At terrestrial altitude, B-lines increased from **7 ± 4 at rest to 17 ± 5 immediately after exercise** (*P* < .001), with an increase in every participant.
- During simulated altitude, B-lines increased only **2 ± 2** (*P* < .05).
- B-lines returned to baseline within 4 hours.

A related study using moderate—not high-intensity intermittent—exercise also found more B-lines at terrestrial than simulated altitude, providing additional supportive evidence. However, the direct evidence is limited to a small study, and B-lines are an indirect measure rather than a definitive quantification of pulmonary edema.

FINAL DECISION: YES
