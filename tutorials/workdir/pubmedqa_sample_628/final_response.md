**Yes.** Evidence in *Anopheles stephensi* supports ABC transporters contributing to defense against permethrin, particularly ABCG4:

- **Gene-specific functional evidence:** ABCG4 silencing with siRNA increased permethrin-associated larval mortality; antisense Vivo-Morpholino experiments were consistent with this result. At 24 hours, reported mortality increased from 45.6% with permethrin alone to 64% and 58.4% with the two siRNA treatments. (*Malaria Journal*, 2019; PMID: 31462239.)
- **Pharmacological evidence:** The ABC-transporter inhibitor verapamil lowered the permethrin LD₅₀ in larvae from 0.137 to 0.025 mg/L. This supports transporter involvement but does not identify a specific transporter. (*Parasites & Vectors*, 2014; PMID: 25073980.)
- **Supporting expression evidence:** Permethrin exposure induced ABC transporter transcripts, including ABCG4, in larvae and adults. Expression changes alone do not establish function. (PMIDs: 25073980, 25504146, 28302529.)

Taken together, the gene-silencing result and inhibitor effect support a role in permethrin defense, while the strongest gene-specific evidence points to ABCG4.

FINAL DECISION: YES
