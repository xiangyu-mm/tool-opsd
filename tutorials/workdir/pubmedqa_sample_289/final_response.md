**Yes—mood was associated with the concordance between subjective and objective sleep-duration measures in the study identified.**

Baillet et al. studied 45 older adults, measuring sleep with actigraphy over at least three consecutive nights and subjective sleep duration and mood using ecological momentary assessment. They found a significant difference between subjective and actigraphic sleep-duration estimates (p<0.001), and mood status at awakening statistically explained variation in that discrepancy (p=0.020). The abstract does not specify the direction of the effect.

This was a small pilot study, so it supports an association rather than proving that mood causes the disagreement.  
**Citation:** Baillet M, et al. “Mood Influences the Concordance of Subjective and Objective Measures of Sleep Duration in Older Adults.” *Front Aging Neurosci.* 2016;8:181. PMID: 27507944. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnagi.2016.00181

FINAL DECISION: YES
