**Yes.** In male mice, MT1/Mel1a deficiency was associated with **more time spent feeding**, but not greater total food intake, body weight, or general locomotor activity. It also reduced nocturnal **Pomc** expression in the arcuate nucleus and pituitary pars distalis. The pars intermedia showed no genotype difference, so the pituitary effect was region-specific. Melatonin increased Pomc expression in wild-type mice but not MT1-deficient mice, supporting an MT1-dependent effect.

This conclusion is based on the directly relevant study by Jilg et al., *Neuroendocrinology* (2017), PMID: 27490331. In short, deficiency affects feeding pattern and POMC expression, but does not increase overall food consumption.
