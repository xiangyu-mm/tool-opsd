**Yes—pre-transplant impedance measures were associated with earlier allograft injury in small retrospective studies.**

- In a 30-recipient cohort, prolonged bolus clearance and greater distal and proximal reflux episodes were associated with shorter time to biopsy-defined acute rejection or lymphocytic bronchiolitis (PMID [25444368](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25444368/)).
- In a 32-recipient cohort, overall and recumbent bolus reflux exposure and bolus-clearance time were associated with early injury; corresponding acid-pH measures were not significant (PMID [26290637](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26290637/)).

**Caveat:** These were small, retrospective studies. Their early-injury outcomes were rejection/lymphocytic bronchiolitis; they do not establish that impedance measures predict primary graft dysfunction specifically, or prove causation.

FINAL DECISION: YES
