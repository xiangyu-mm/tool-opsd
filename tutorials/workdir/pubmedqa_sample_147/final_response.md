The evidence supports a qualified **yes**.

- **Direct study:** Hofmann et al. (*Radiology*, 2015; **PMID: 25423144**) implanted human rectus-abdominis muscle precursor cells into nude mice and used serial 4.7-T MRI relaxometry—T1, T2, T2*—plus ADC measurements.
- During tissue maturation, MRI values shifted toward those of mature skeletal muscle:
  - T1: approximately **2242 → 1386 ms**
  - T2: approximately **224 → 32 ms**
  - T2*: approximately **33.3 → 10.8 ms**
  - ADC: approximately **1.53 → 1.39 × 10⁻³ mm²/s**
- Differentiation was independently supported by **immunofluorescence, Western blotting, and contraction assays**, with fibroblast, prostate-cancer-cell, and collagen controls.
- A contemporaneous commentary (**PMID: 25710336**) interpreted the findings as noninvasive, label-free detection of implanted muscle precursor-cell differentiation.
- Other MRI studies, such as ferritin-engineered C2C12 myoblast tracking (**PMID: 20643023**), demonstrate graft localization or size estimation but do not establish differentiation-specific imaging.

The important limitation is that T1, T2, and T2* changes are not intrinsically specific to differentiation; edema, inflammation, extracellular matrix, necrosis, iron, and tissue injury can also alter relaxation. Therefore, relaxometry is best viewed as a **noninvasive characterization or monitoring biomarker when combined with biological validation**, not as standalone proof of differentiation. Evidence is strongest for transplanted human muscle precursor cells and is more limited for endogenous satellite cells.
