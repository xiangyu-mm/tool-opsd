Yes—based on the available direct comparative evidence, MRSA ST22-IV was more stress-tolerant than ST228-I across the tested panel.

The key study (PMID:25428923; source `call_FiYHz6l5cZ8IzPafNQCwmxp3`) reported that ST22-IV had superior survival or resistance under:

- Oxidative stress  
- Autolytic conditions  
- High osmolarity  
- Acidic conditions  
- Alkaline conditions  
- Desiccation  
- Heat shock  

Thus, ST22-IV showed an advantage across multiple host- and environment-relevant stresses. However, the accessible evidence did not provide effect sizes, exposure conditions, or isolate-level reproducibility. It also did not directly test—or establish superiority for—antimicrobial peptides, disinfectants, or starvation (`call_1gstHaNT40D4tUfX2euUIHQu`).

A later study of 98 MRSA strains found approximately a one-log reduction after one week of dehydration and no overall epidemic-versus-nonepidemic difference; it referenced prior ST22-IV/ST228-I findings but did not independently provide a quantitative direct comparison (`call_jw9mRxNxPpAXcBueZenm7HQg`, `call_HORIvb39Rv7St6CGhnPeMJsm`).

Therefore, the best-supported interpretation is that ST22-IV is more resistant to **multiple specifically tested stresses**, but not necessarily every possible host or environmental stress.

FINAL DECISION: YES
