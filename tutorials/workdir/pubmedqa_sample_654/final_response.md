Yes. Confocal laser endomicroscopy has demonstrated acute, food-associated changes in the intestinal—particularly duodenal—mucosa of a subset of patients with IBS.

Key evidence:

- In a 2014 study, direct duodenal exposure to diluted food antigens caused changes within 5 minutes in 22 of 36 IBS patients, including increased intraepithelial lymphocytes, epithelial gaps/leakage, and widened intervillous spaces. These changes were absent in CLE-negative IBS patients and non-IBS controls (PMID: 25083606).
- In a 2019 study, 76 of 108 IBS patients showed CLE responses to food components. Responders had increased intraepithelial lymphocytes, increased claudin-2, reduced occludin, and eosinophil degranulation, consistent with acute epithelial-barrier disruption (PMID: 31100380).
- A later multicentre study found that CLE had limited sensitivity and specificity for diagnosing wheat sensitivity, so it is not sufficiently validated as a routine diagnostic test (PMID: 34544843).

Thus, CLE can show food-associated mucosal reactions in IBS, but the response occurs only in a subset of patients and the technique’s diagnostic accuracy remains limited.

FINAL DECISION: YES
