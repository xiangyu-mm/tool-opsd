## Conclusion

Yes. **CD64 expression—particularly neutrophil CD64 (nCD64), encoded by FCGR1A—is increased in severe and complicated acute pancreatitis.**

### Clinical evidence

- In a human study of **21 mild and 10 severe acute pancreatitis patients**, peripheral-blood leukocyte CD64 mRNA was higher in severe disease and decreased during remission (source: `call_WKwgBhDUDiOWUW9vjwwmir0N`; supporting study details: `call_rb60UX6IxfcLFTFtikUDrD2k`).
- In a cohort of **234 patients**, CD64 index and CD64-positive neutrophils were higher in infected than non-infected pancreatitis and increased further in patients with sepsis. For pancreatitis-associated infection, CD64 showed **82.14% sensitivity and 88.51% specificity** (`call_bIgHEYKm5hRt41RFUpjJ3TB8`).
- In two independent cohorts totaling **302 patients**, admission nCD64 was higher in patients who developed infected pancreatic necrosis. Reported performance was:
  - Training cohort: **AUC 0.859**
  - Validation cohort: **AUC 0.919**
  - Reported odds ratio: **2.971** (`call_FwPLCHFR84RBhbl8Lu3Eqq2O`; `call_9IcMbWP3Xck9KX1FZwJL0r80`)
- A separate 302-patient study associated higher nCD64 with **SIRS, ARDS, multiple-organ failure, and mortality**. A cutoff of 1.45 identified a low-risk group with no observed deaths in the study cohorts, although the available report did not provide complete adjusted effect estimates (`call_lc2Js88h7BBMnH3ongWOwoz7`; `call_g6WwbIepPHc9EWMcnbTM8qXK`).

### Clinical significance

Increased nCD64 appears useful for:

1. **Early severity assessment** in acute pancreatitis.
2. Identifying patients at risk for **infected pancreatic necrosis**.
3. Supporting diagnosis of **abdominal infection and sepsis**.
4. Estimating risk of **organ failure, ARDS, and death**.

However, it should be interpreted as an **adjunctive biomarker**, not a stand-alone diagnostic or prognostic test. Limitations include mostly retrospective or single-center studies, different CD64 assays, limited external validation, and the need to integrate nCD64 with clinical assessment, imaging, APACHE II/SOFA, CRP, and procalcitonin.
