Yes. A 2014 Malawi study of HIV-positive adults with HBV sub-genotype A1 starting first-line stavudine/lamivudine/nevirapine found evidence of both high HBV replication and treatment-emergent resistance.

Among HBeAg-positive participants, 96% had baseline HBV DNA above 2,000 IU/mL. During follow-up, 24 of 96 participants with available data (25%) acquired HBV resistance-associated mutations. At six months, the lamivudine-resistance mutation M204I was detected in 16 of 17 samples using deep sequencing; by 12 months, all viremic patients had multiple resistance and compensatory mutations on the same HBV genomes.

The results support the claim in this cohort. Interpretation is tempered by substantial loss to follow-up: only 41% of the initially HBsAg-positive group remained at 12 months. **Source:** Aoudjane et al., *Clinical Infectious Diseases* (2014), PMID: 25100867; doi:10.1093/cid/ciu630.
