Yes. The evidence indicates that iTRAQ-based quantitative proteomics and phosphoproteomics reveal developmental changes in central-metabolism-related proteins during wheat grain development.

- **Lv et al. (2014, PMID: 25427527)** analyzed developing wheat grains using iTRAQ proteomics and TiO₂-enriched phosphoproteomics. Of 1,146 identified proteins, 421 changed by at least twofold across development. Proteins involved in starch biosynthesis and storage accumulated at later stages, while proteins involved in protein turnover and photosynthesis showed contrasting patterns.
- The same study identified **23 differentially phosphorylated proteins**, including **ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase** and **sucrose synthase**, directly implicating phosphorylation-based regulation of starch and carbohydrate metabolism.
- A second iTRAQ study of wheat grains at 4, 8, and 12 days after anthesis (**PMID: 26779988**) found 1,762 proteins with at least 1.5-fold differences. These proteins were enriched in carbohydrate, lipid, and nitrogen metabolism, supporting broader metabolic remodeling during early grain development.

The strongest evidence concerns **starch/carbohydrate metabolism and its phosphorylation regulation**. However, these studies primarily measured protein abundance and phosphorylation; they did not directly establish changes in metabolite concentrations, metabolic flux, glycolytic flux, or TCA-cycle activity.

FINAL DECISION: YES
