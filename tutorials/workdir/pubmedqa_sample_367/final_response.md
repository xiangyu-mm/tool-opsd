## Conclusion

**No increased risk has been demonstrated**, particularly in adults undergoing dilation of selected benign esophageal strictures.

The strongest evidence was a retrospective study of 2,216 dilation sessions:

- Major adverse events: **0.5%** with rule-of-3 non-adherence vs **1.6%** with adherence; *P* = .18.
- Perforation: **0%** vs **0.7%**; *P* = .18.
- Overall, adverse events and perforation were more strongly associated with **malignant strictures** than with rule-of-3 violation.

A small additional study of 12 patients undergoing progressive dilation beyond the rule reported no serious adverse events, but it had no comparator and was too small to establish safety.

The rule of 3 appears to be primarily an **expert-consensus practice convention**, not a threshold validated by prospective trials. Therefore, the available evidence does **not** show that non-adherence increases adverse-event risk, but it cannot exclude risk in malignant, severely complex, caustic, angulated, or otherwise high-risk strictures. These findings should not be interpreted as evidence that unrestricted dilation is safe.
