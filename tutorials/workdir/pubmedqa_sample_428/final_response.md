**Answer: Yes, with an important qualification.** The evidence supports ASM as a negative regulator of **peripheral, induced Treg generation** in the studied experimental settings. It does not establish that ASM suppresses **thymic Treg development** generally.

In mouse studies, ASM deficiency or pharmacological inhibition was associated with increased Treg frequency; one study also reported greater induced-Treg output from ASM-deficient T cells stimulated in vitro with TGF-β and IL-2. However, the increased Treg *proportion* may partly reflect conventional T-cell loss and Tregs’ relative resistance to inhibitor-associated cell death. Thus, these findings support a negative regulatory effect, but do not show that every increase in Treg frequency represents increased Treg production.

Key evidence: *Journal of Immunology* (2016), PMID: 27638864; *Biological Chemistry* (2018), PMID: 29908119. The literature search also identified a human-focused inhibitor study (PMID: 33898989), but the details retrieved were insufficient to assess its methods or effect size.
