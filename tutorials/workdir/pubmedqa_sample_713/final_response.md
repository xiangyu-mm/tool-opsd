Yes—published UAE studies report allergic-rhinitis prevalence and environmental triggers, but the estimates vary substantially by population and how rhinitis was defined.

**Prevalence findings**
- In a 2019 ISAAC questionnaire survey of schoolchildren in Dubai and the Northern Emirates, reported allergic-rhinitis prevalence was **46.5% in children aged 6–7** and **51.3% in those aged 13–14**. These were questionnaire-based findings, not necessarily physician-confirmed diagnoses. Hay fever was reported less often: 22.1% and 19.9%, respectively (PMID: 34703522; source: `call_O7EfjVStiGSlXcEu4JZ7SVir`).
- In an Al-Ain survey of 6,543 participants aged 13 and older, self-reported prevalence was **36% crude** and **32% adjusted for age and sex** (PMID: 19735489; source: `call_998og24Hm2gvtihrYbFItMHH`).
- A population-based survey across all seven Emirates found **7%** screened positive for allergic-rhinitis symptoms or diagnosis among 1,229 participants (PMID: 25097721; source: `call_XR1GUWDxRcuenLIQkNolLw4u`).
- An older all-Emirate school survey reported **14.9% hay fever**; that outcome should not be treated as identical to allergic rhinitis (PMID: 10900696; source: `call_LF9sRGlr0ONx2xnvxLtnp9uu`).

**Reported triggers and associated factors**
In the seven-Emirate survey, participants reported symptom aggravation from **dust (59%)**, **grass/pollen (44%)**, and **proximity to animals (21%)**; 71% reported environmental triggers. Symptoms were most often reported in winter, then spring (PMID: 25097721; source: `call_OO8rJaMrYPvU3TpRnFTMLRZl`). The Al-Ain study also found associations with family history, Arab origin, younger age, female sex, and higher education (PMID: 19735489; source: `call_VHML67PWNyM3dpDi7yfR7xOM`).

**Bottom line:** UAE studies identify dust, pollen/grass, and animals as reported aggravators, but they do **not** establish one reliable national prevalence figure. Estimates differ with age group, location, survey period, and questionnaire definition; several measure symptoms or hay fever rather than clinically confirmed allergic rhinitis.
