The British Familial Breast Cancer Group study supports screening women with a significant family history of breast cancer.

In its pooled UK audit of 8,783 women, screening detected cancers at rates comparable to routine NHS screening in older women, and screen-detected tumors had more favorable pathological features. Subsequent FH01 studies similarly found that annual mammography detected most cancers at screening and that tumors were smaller and less likely to be node-positive.

However, the evidence was observational, without a randomized unscreened control group or directly measured mortality benefit. The estimated survival advantage was modeled rather than definitively demonstrated.

Therefore, the results support offering annual mammographic surveillance to carefully risk-defined women—particularly those aged 40–49—but do not constitute definitive randomized proof of mortality reduction.
