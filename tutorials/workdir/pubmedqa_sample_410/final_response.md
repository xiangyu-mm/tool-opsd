## Conclusion

**Yes, with an important qualification.** The SLC39A8 missense variant **rs13107325 (A391T; ZIP8 Thr391)** has evidence of association with Crohn’s disease and of **selective, context-dependent changes** in gut microbial composition. The evidence does **not** establish a consistent, broad microbiome shift across all carriers or populations.

- **Crohn’s disease:** In a 2019 human study, the variant was found in 21/171 Crohn’s disease cases (12.3%) versus 30/476 controls (6.3%), with **OR 2.1, P=0.01**. The study reported replication of the Crohn’s association. [PMID: 30703110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30703110/)
- **Gut microbiome:** That same study found no adjusted association with overall microbiome composition or microbial richness, although some individual microbial taxa had suggestive findings with limited replication. A later analysis of a pediatric Crohn’s cohort reported **reduced Veillonella sequence variants in ileal mucosa** among variant carriers, supporting a more specific association in a disease and tissue context. [PMID: 38289995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38289995/)

Mechanistic mouse studies also report microbiome shifts, but those are supportive biological evidence—not proof of a general effect in humans. [PMIDs: 33139556](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33139556/), [39322808](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39322808/)
