**Yes.** The most directly relevant study was a retrospective cohort of 214 patients who had curative resection of localized primary gastric GIST without adjuvant therapy. Tumors were classified as intramural, endoluminal, exoluminal, or mixed. **Exoluminal or mixed type independently predicted recurrence** (HR 3.7, *p*=0.043), alongside tumor size >3.5 cm and mitotic rate >5/50 HPF (PMID: 25075057).

A population-based cohort of 295 resected gastric GISTs also found poorer 5-year recurrence-free survival for **transmural** tumors than for luminal or exophytic tumors (71% vs 96%; HR 8.45, 95% CI 3.69–19.36; *p*<0.001; PMID: 33651216). This supports a relationship between growth configuration and recurrence, although its classification is not identical to the first study’s.

**Caveat:** The evidence is observational, and a later study of 2–5 cm gastric GISTs found growth pattern associated with progression-free survival by Kaplan–Meier analysis, but not among the independent predictors in its multivariable model (PMID: 37120543). Thus, macroscopic type is a prognostic factor in some cohorts, but it should be interpreted alongside established tumor-risk features and the specific type classification used.
