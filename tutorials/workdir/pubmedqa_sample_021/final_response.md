Yes. The strongest evidence indicates that severe maternal undernutrition—typically approximately 50% caloric restriction during gestation—can induce an accelerated reproductive-aging phenotype in adult female rat offspring.

Key findings include:

- **PMID:25439841:** By 8 months, 80% of offspring exposed to late-gestation food restriction had disrupted estrous cycles, predominantly persistent estrus. They also had cystic ovarian changes, fewer corpora lutea, altered LH/FSH and sex-steroid levels, and hypothalamic reproductive-axis changes.
- **PMID:29351580:** Gestational caloric restriction caused persistent estrus, fewer antral follicles and corpora lutea, more follicular atresia, and increased ovarian apoptosis by postnatal day 65.
- **PMID:25810471:** Prenatal undernutrition was associated with reduced ovarian follicle number, increased ovarian ER stress and follicular apoptosis, and reduced ovarian vascular density—findings consistent with accelerated ovarian aging.
- **PMID:36408965:** Not all models showed overt senescence; despite altered KNDy neuroendocrine programming, estrous cyclicity and pulsatile LH secretion remained comparable with controls.

Thus, the evidence supports premature reproductive senescence or accelerated reproductive aging, especially as manifested by early persistent estrus, follicle depletion, ovarian pathology, and altered reproductive endocrine signaling. However, direct evidence for irreversible menopause, permanent infertility, and the independent effect of lactation-only undernutrition remains limited.

FINAL DECISION: YES
