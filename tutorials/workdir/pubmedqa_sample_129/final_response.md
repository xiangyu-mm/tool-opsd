Yes. A PubMed-indexed study (PMID: 25423132) directly examined this question in C6 glioma cells.

- Cells were treated with GDNF at 70 ng/mL.
- GDNF increased β-catenin phosphorylation at Tyr-654.
- Phospho-β-catenin increased in cytoplasmic and nuclear fractions.
- Immunocytochemistry supported β-catenin nuclear translocation.
- The mechanism was linked to N-cadherin/Src signaling; Src inhibition reduced the phosphorylation response.

Thus, the available direct evidence supports that GDNF promotes β-catenin Tyr-654 phosphorylation and subsequent nuclear translocation in C6 glioma cells. This conclusion is based primarily on one experimental glioma-cell study and may not establish that the effect occurs uniformly across all glioma subtypes.

FINAL DECISION: YES
