**Yes—with an important qualification.** Mouse studies report endocrine-pancreas abnormalities in male offspring, but the consequences differ by generation and model.

- **Male F1:** One multigenerational study found enlarged islets and increased alpha- and beta-cell mass, altered cell distribution and weak Pdx1 staining, alongside hyperinsulinemia, elevated fasting glucose and glucose intolerance (PMID: 25441581). Another found mitochondrial and insulin-granule abnormalities in islets even though measured glucose tolerance and stimulated insulin secretion were comparable to controls (PMID: 31773193). A separate study reported a reduced beta-cell fraction in male newborns, while adult males were similar to controls on chow and showed only transient glucose intolerance after high-fat-diet rechallenge (PMID: 35111136).

- **Male F2:** The multigenerational study found persistent islet remodeling, increased alpha- and beta-cell mass, weak Pdx1 staining and hyperinsulinemia. However, F2 males did **not** show significant fasting hyperglycemia or glucose intolerance (PMID: 25441581).

These results support endocrine-pancreas impairment or remodeling, but **do not establish that male F2 progeny consistently develop systemic glucose dysregulation**. The evidence is from mouse studies; the models often continued high-fat-diet exposure during pregnancy and/or lactation, so effects cannot always be attributed solely to pregestational obesity. Directly relevant human evidence was not identified.

FINAL DECISION: YES
