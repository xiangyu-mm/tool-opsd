**Yes.** *Equisetum arvense* has been reported to modulate inflammatory immunocompetent cells—specifically, human lymphocytes in vitro.

- A 2014 study reported that standardized horsetail extract dose-dependently reduced activated T-cell proliferation and activation markers, including IL-2-related measures. The study did not detect apoptosis or necrosis as the explanation for reduced proliferation (PMID: **25088216**).
- A 2018 study also found reduced lymphocyte activation and proliferation in vitro. Its results implicated both silica and isoquercitrin in the effects (PMID: **29202511**).

This supports **in-vitro immunomodulation**, particularly suppression of lymphocyte activation; it does not establish that horsetail has clinical anti-inflammatory effects or the same effects in people taking it.

FINAL DECISION: YES
