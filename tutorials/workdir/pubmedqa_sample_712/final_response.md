## Evidence summary

**APOBEC1 can induce somatic/genomic DNA mutations, but this effect is context-dependent.**

- In a primary study (**PMID: 25085003**), rat APOBEC1 increased:
  - Loss of an integrated EGFP reporter in AID-deficient DT40 cells.
  - Imatinib-resistant human CML clones carrying mutations in **BCR-ABL1**.
- These findings support APOBEC1 as a genomic mutator under experimental overexpression conditions.
- However, an earlier study (**PMID: 14559972**) found no APOBEC1-induced somatic hypermutation or class switching in tested mammalian cells, indicating that the activity is not universal.
- Additional work (**PMID: 33330905**) found limited APOBEC1-associated genomic DNA damage, supporting off-target DNA activity but not necessarily proving a broad mutation process in vivo.

**A compatible mutational signature has been reported in esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC).**

- The key EAC study (**PMID: 25085003**) reported an AID/APOBEC-like mutation pattern in EAC that resembled APOBEC1’s experimentally observed sequence preference.
- EAC cohorts also show canonical APOBEC-associated signatures, particularly **COSMIC SBS2** and **SBS13**:
  - **SBS2:** mainly C>T mutations in a TC context.
  - **SBS13:** mainly C>G and C>A mutations in a TC context.
- These signatures are compatible with APOBEC activity, but they are **not APOBEC1-specific**. Current enzyme-attribution evidence more strongly implicates **APOBEC3A and/or APOBEC3B** for canonical SBS2/SBS13 in human cancers.

## Conclusion

The answer is **yes** if “compatible” is meant literally: APOBEC1 has experimental somatic DNA-mutagenesis activity, and an APOBEC1-compatible mutation pattern has been reported in EAC. However, the EAC mutations cannot currently be assigned specifically to APOBEC1 rather than APOBEC3A/APOBEC3B.

FINAL DECISION: YES
